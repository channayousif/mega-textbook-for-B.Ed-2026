# Self-assessment and giving feedback

## The self-assessment checklist

Every topic page in a per-topic unit ends with a "Self-assessment checklist" -
a short list of "I can..." statements for that topic. Once you're signed in,
each item is a live checkbox: tick it once you're confident you can do what
it says.

Your ticks are saved to your account and follow you across devices - tick an
item on your phone, and it shows ticked the next time you open the same
topic on a laptop, once you're signed in there too. The Progress area of
your dashboard shows a separate completion fraction for these checklists,
alongside (not combined with) your usual unit-coverage fraction - ticking
every item on a topic never marks the whole unit "studied" for you; that
still needs your own separate action.

If you tick items while signed out, they're saved to that browser only, with
a "sign in to sync across devices" note above the list. The next time you
sign in on that same browser, those local ticks are copied into your account
automatically, just once - so ticking something else on another device
afterwards is never overwritten by an old, already-merged local tick.

If a topic's wording changes later, any item whose text no longer matches
what you previously ticked shows up unticked again - your other, unchanged
ticks on the same topic are left exactly as you left them.

## Giving feedback on a page

On a topic page, a unit's opening page, its assessment page, its
teacher-notes page, or a course-level review page, signed-in readers see a
"Give feedback" button. Use it to leave a comment on the page as a whole, or
select a specific sentence first and then click the button to attach your
comment to exactly that passage - your quote is shown back to you before you
submit.

This is separate from a teacher's "Suggest improvement" control, which is
about the book's content more broadly - use "Give feedback" for a comment
tied to something specific on the page you're reading. The curriculum owner
reviews everything you submit and may follow up by revising the page; you
can see your own feedback items and their status by returning to the same
account, but you can't edit a comment once it's sent.
