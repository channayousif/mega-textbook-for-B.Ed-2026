# Reviewing and certifying a unit

Every unit on this platform passes through a set of review gates before it is
published. Two of them are judgement calls that a person has to make: **G3**,
the English review, and **G5**, the Urdu review. The **reviewer** capability is
what lets someone other than the curriculum owner make those calls.

Like verified-teacher status, it is granted only by an admin, and it is not
something you can switch on for yourself. Apply from **Review content** on
your dashboard. Your grant names the B.Ed track, the teaching-licence track,
or one B.Ed course, and records your qualification evidence. The licence
track is a topic list with no course codes.

## What the capability unlocks

The **Review workbench** lists topics and formal units within your grant.
For each, record the criterion results and specific comments, then recommend
approval or improvement. The admin reads your recommendation and records the
decision. Licence heading-page reviews are advisory while a topic-list
evidence contract is still being developed.

The existing **Review queue** prepares Git evidence for G3/G5. It lists only
formal units within your scope.
Open a unit from there and you get what you need to review it - the English
text for a G3, and for a G5 the English and the Urdu side by side, so you are
comparing rather than reading each in turn.

When preparing formal evidence you choose one of three outcomes:

- **Certify** - the unit passes this gate.
- **Request revision** - it goes back for rework. Two rounds of this are
  allowed before the unit is escalated instead.
- **Escalate** - the decision is not yours to make. It reaches the curriculum
  owner, who owns review policy and escalations.

## What it does not unlock

Nothing else. Holding the reviewer capability does not let you:

- write or edit any unit,
- grant a capability to anyone, including yourself,
- change anyone's role,
- see answer keys or any other restricted teaching material (that is the
  separate verified-teacher capability),
- or change a review someone else has already certified.

A reviewer judges work. A reviewer does not change work, and does not decide
who else may judge it.

## A G5 needs its G3 first

You will not be offered a G5 on a unit whose G3 is still open, and the system
will refuse a G5 certification that does not name the accepted G3 it was made
against.

The reason is worth knowing rather than working around: an Urdu review is a
comparison against a specific English version. If the English has not itself
been accepted, there is nothing settled to compare against, and a G5 made
against a moving target tells nobody anything. If the queue will not offer you
a G5 you were expecting, its G3 is what is missing.

## Formal evidence produces two files, not a status change

This is the part that surprises people. When you prepare certification, nothing on the site
changes. Instead you get two downloads: the certification itself, which records
what you checked and what you found, and a single line for the unit's tracker.

Both are committed to the project's repository through its ordinary review
process, the same way the content itself is. That is deliberate. A review is
evidence, and evidence that can be changed quietly afterwards is not worth
much; putting it in version control means every certification is permanent,
attributable and readable long after the fact, including by you.

It also means the curriculum owner stays in the loop for the commit - but
committing a batch of certifications takes minutes, where reviewing the units
they represent takes days. That difference is the entire point of the
capability.

## Before your first review

Two things:

1. **You are recorded.** Your initials, the courses and stages you are
   qualified for, and the evidence you were qualified on are written down in
   the project's reviewer record. Your certifications carry those initials.
2. **You are qualified on real units first.** You review two or three units the
   curriculum owner has already reviewed, without seeing their findings, and
   the two sets are compared. Agreement matters; passing a unit that the owner
   failed matters more. That comparison is what qualification means here, and
   it is recorded alongside your entry.

If you think you should hold a grant and do not, apply from your dashboard or
contact your platform administrator. A grant can be revoked without changing
past Git evidence.
