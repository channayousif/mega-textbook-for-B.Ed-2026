# maslach2016 - verification excerpt

Maslach, C., & Leiter, M. P. (2016). Understanding the burnout experience: recent research and
its implications for psychiatry. *World Psychiatry, 15*(2), 103-111.
DOI 10.1002/wps.20311. Retrieved 2026-09-18 from PMC4911781 (PubMed Central, open access).

**Provenance:** verbatim publisher text, quoted from the open-access full text.

## The three dimensions (section: DEFINING BURNOUT)

> "The three key dimensions of this response are an overwhelming exhaustion, feelings of
> cynicism and detachment from the job, and a sense of ineffectiveness and lack of
> accomplishment."

Elaborated in the same section as:

> exhaustion: "wearing out, loss of energy, depletion, debilitation, and fatigue"
> cynicism: "negative or inappropriate attitudes towards clients, irritability, loss of
> idealism, and withdrawal"
> inefficacy: "reduced productivity or capability, low morale, and an inability to cope"

## Demands and resources, not workload alone (section: CAUSES AND OUTCOMES)

> "The JD-R model focuses on the notion that burnout arises when individuals experience
> incessant job demands and have inadequate resources available to address and to reduce those
> demands."

The paper situates this across six organisational domains: workload, control, reward, community,
fairness and values.

## What this supports

EFMP-302 Unit 5 topic-01 and fig-U5-2: the three named dimensions, and the claim that burnout is
a demand-resource imbalance held over time rather than a quantity of work. Both are supported
directly.
