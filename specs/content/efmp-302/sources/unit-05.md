# Sources consulted - EFMP-302 Unit 5 (Issues and Challenges in the Teaching Profession)

Per `specs/007-content-depth-standard/contracts/sources-consulted.md`. Every key cited in
`specs/content/efmp-302/coverage/unit-05.md` appears here, and every key here is cited in prose
and in a per-topic `## Further reading` section.

## Verification method, changed for this unit

Two G3 reviews of Units 2 and 3 found citation defects that registry metadata could not have
caught: an author's initials invented, a conclusion kept after its source was disowned, a
framework attributed to a paper containing a different model, and a conclusion drawn from a failed
search. Registry metadata verifies that a citation exists and is bibliographically accurate. **It
does not verify that the source supports the claim.**

So for this unit every source was verified by **reading its abstract and full bibliographic
record** through OpenAlex or Crossref, not only by confirming that the DOI resolves, and the scope
of each is recorded below and restated in the prose wherever the source is used. Three sources
carry scope limits that materially affect what they can support, and the unit states them to the
learner rather than hiding them in this file.

## Deliberate absence of sources

**Six of twelve sub-topics carry `no-external-source`.** That is a high proportion and it is
honest rather than lazy. The sub-topics concerned describe Pakistani working conditions, the
multilingual classroom, responsible digital conduct, and the control-and-influence distinction.
No verifiable source was found for the Pakistan-specific claims, and the unit says so in the
prose: Topic 5.1 states plainly that its multilingual practices are "reasoned practice consistent
with the general point about cognitive load, not findings", and the unit's `index.mdx` carries a
standing note on the evidence.

The alternative would have been to attach a plausible-looking citation to a claim it does not
support, which is precisely the defect the Unit 2 and Unit 3 reviews found.
