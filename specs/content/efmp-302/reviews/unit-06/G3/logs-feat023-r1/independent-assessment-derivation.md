## RRQs - content I expect a full-credit answer to contain

1. Training course = event with certificate, may contribute, is not development; qualification
   certifies a past threshold; appraisal requirement is employer-imposed and produces
   compliance; CPD practitioner-driven, continuous, measured by changed practice.
2. Four reasons: knowledge base changes; pupils change; initial preparation cannot be
   sufficient by construction (3-4 years vs 30); alternative is drift not stability. Drift =
   unexamined practice slowly adapts to convenience (lighter marking, shorter explanations,
   same three pupils answering) with nobody deciding.
3. Six principles: sustained, embedded, content/pupil-focused, collaborative,
   feedback-supported, chosen. Rules out (any three): one-day event; never touching your own
   room; the generic workshop; attending alone; only being told things; being sent on a
   course. One-day workshop fails "sustained" regardless of content.
4. Stages: preservice, new teacher, experienced. Transitions: responsibility transfers at the
   end of preservice; alone with a class at the start of the new teacher stage; mechanics
   automatic / attention freed entering the experienced stage. NOTE ON THE STEM: the stem says
   "explain why the new-teacher transition frees attention rather than adding knowledge",
   but topic-02 locates freed attention at the transition INTO the experienced stage
   ("mechanics have become automatic. Attention is freed"); at the new-teacher transition
   attention is consumed, not freed. The prose that fits "frees attention rather than adding
   knowledge" is Imran (knows wait time, cannot use it) -> experienced stage. I will check
   the mark scheme's reading of this.
5. Sana: preservice, everything new at once, attention consumed by mechanics, no spare
   attention. Imran: new teacher, survival, no automatic routines, cannot hold the room.
   Same symptom, different stage-level reason.
6. Inverse arrangement: attention free -> highest capacity; nothing external requires change
   (classes run, results hold) -> lowest pressure. Follows: development at this stage must be
   self-driven, needs genuine challenge and honest observation.
7. Three conversions: decide before leaving the single thing to try; agree with one colleague
   to each try it and compare in a fortnight; decide what to look at to know whether it
   worked. Principles supplied per topic-03: sustained, collaborative, feedback-supported.
8. Hennessy 2022 (LMIC technology review): opportunities limited, often unsustained,
   frequently not informed by research evidence; technology has real potential for formal
   programmes and informal peer learning; outcomes mixed. Two Pakistani constraints from:
   data cost, device access, electricity.
9. Six blocks: gap stated specifically; evidence the gap is real; one goal; the activity;
   evidence that would show change; review date. Prevents: unobservable goals; a list of
   virtues; the commonest failure; drift in route choice; March having nothing to look at;
   quiet abandonment. September-fixed evidence = success criteria decided before outcomes are
   known, so the plan can visibly fail rather than be rationalised.
10. Plan for an appraiser is written to look good; Unit 4's separation of development from
    judgement; an honest gap statement cannot survive an audience that judges; product is
    compliance, not development.
