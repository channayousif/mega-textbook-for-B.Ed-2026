## Course Description

The guide describes this course as a foundational understanding of Information and
Communication Technology (ICT) and its applications for students across arts, sciences, and
social sciences disciplines. It introduces fundamental concepts of computer literacy, computer
systems, hardware and software components, operating systems, internet usage, cybersecurity,
and ethical issues related to digital technologies. The course emphasizes practical use of ICT
tools for academic, professional, and everyday purposes, while also exploring emerging
technologies and their impact on society and future careers (guide lines 389-395, paraphrased).

## Course-wide items

**Course Learning Outcomes** (verbatim from the guide, lines 397-412):

1. Explain the concept and importance of computer literacy in modern society.
2. Classify computers based on functionality and size.
3. Identify major hardware and software components of computer systems.
4. Describe basic operating system concepts.
5. Apply safe and responsible practices for cybersecurity and privacy.
6. Demonstrate ethical use of ICT and avoid plagiarism.
7. Use internet tools and digital communication platforms effectively.
8. Explain emerging technologies and their societal impact.

**Teaching strategies** (from the guide): Lectures, Interactive discussions, Question-answer
sessions, Demonstrations, Case study analysis.

**Practical work** (from the guide): Hands-on computer exercises, Individual assignments,
Group assignments, Internet research tasks, Presentations.

**Assessment criteria:** guide-silent (no table in the guide). The pipeline default applies:
60% summative / 40% formative (Constitution Art. III.7). Unit-end 10/10/5 banks are summative
self-study; per-topic checks and tasks are formative.

## Unit 1: Introduction to Computer Literacy and ICT

Weeks 1-3 (derived). Unit Spec (G1) for `docs/semester-1/gict-300/unit-01/`.

- **CLO refs**: course outcomes 1, 2 (and 8's societal-impact thread where it touches the
  internet's impact).
- **Key terms**: Computer literacy, ICT, computer generations, classification, digital divide.
- **Topics**: what computer literacy means and why it matters; how computers evolved and their
  five generations; how computers are classified (by functionality, and by size and power);
  how computers and the internet change society; where ICT is applied across fields.
- **Worked-example / activity concepts**: given a school office task list, sort which tasks
  need ICT and which do not; given photographs of five devices, classify each computer by size
  and power and justify the choice.
- **Assessment blueprint**: formative on defining computer literacy and classifying computers;
  summative includes one Analyze-or-higher item on the impact of ICT on a named Sindh school.

## Course review plan

- **Course summary points**: computer literacy as a foundation for teachers; the
  hardware/software/OS stack every classroom computer rests on; safety, privacy, and ethics as
  everyday practices; the internet's tools and the technologies emerging next; ICT's role in
  Pakistani classrooms specifically.
- **Practice-question mix**: roughly 24 MCQs (4 per unit), 18 RRQs (3 per unit), 12 ERQs (2
  per unit), sampling every topic and every CLO at least once.
- **Practicum project ideas**:
  - ICT lab audit - survey the practicum school's ICT hardware and software; simple
    checklist; bring back the completed audit with recommendations.
  - Lesson plan with ICT - design one lesson that uses a specific ICT tool; teach or rehearse
    it; bring back the plan plus a short reflection.
  - Digital safety workshop - run a 20-minute safe-internet session for a class; poster or
    slides; bring back the materials used and pupil responses.
  - Citation mini-lesson - teach younger pupils how to avoid copying; before/after examples;
    bring back the examples and what changed.
  - Emerging technology briefing - prepare a one-page briefing for the head teacher on one
    emerging technology's fit for the school; bring back the briefing and the head's remarks.
