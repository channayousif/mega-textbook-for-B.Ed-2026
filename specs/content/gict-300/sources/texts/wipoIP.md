## The page's definition and structure (verified)

The page defines intellectual property as referring to "creations of the mind, such as
inventions; literary and artistic works; designs; and symbols, names and images used in
commerce." It explains that IP protection aims to balance innovators' interests with the
public interest so creativity and innovation can flourish. Its "Types of IP" section
describes six categories: patents (exclusive rights for inventions), copyright (rights
over literary and artistic works), trademarks, industrial designs, geographical
indications, and trade secrets. The page also links IP to education, business, and
frontier technologies including AI.

Used for: the WIPO framing of IP as a balance between innovators and the public interest,
and the types of IP (U5-03).
