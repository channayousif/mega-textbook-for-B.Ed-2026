## 2. Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs 1-10)

All RRQ stems were solved independently and compared against the mark schemes in `unit-assessment.mdx`:

- **RRQ-01 (Understand)**: Explain why typing speed alone does not make a person computer literate. Solved: Typing is only one mechanical input skill; computer literacy encompasses file handling, information evaluation, digital communication, and safety. Matches model answer (2 marks: 1 for typing being partial, 1 for naming other skills).
- **RRQ-02 (Understand)**: Three skills of computer literacy matched to school tasks. Solved: Operating devices (starting PC for attendance), handling files (saving lesson plans in organized folders), communicating/safety (emailing parents securely). Matches model answer (6 marks: 1 per skill, 1 per task).
- **RRQ-03 (Analyze)**: Head teacher's claim that smartphone ownership equals staff computer literacy. Solved: Right that smartphones give exposure to ICT tools; wrong in conflating device ownership and casual consumption with workplace digital competence (spreadsheets, file management, safety). Matches model answer (3 marks: 1 for valid point, 2 for analytical critique).
- **RRQ-04 (Understand)**: Impact of the stored-program concept. Solved: Stored-program architecture placed instructions in the same memory as data, allowing machines to be reprogrammed via software rather than physical rewiring. Matches model answer (2 marks: 1 for instructions in memory, 1 for programmatic switching).
- **RRQ-05 (Understand)**: Five computer generations and their defining technologies. Solved: 1st: vacuum tubes; 2nd: transistors; 3rd: integrated circuits; 4th: microprocessors; 5th: artificial intelligence / ultra large scale integration. Matches model answer (5 marks: 1 per generation).
- **RRQ-06 (Analyze)**: Comparison of 1980s IBM PC and modern tablet with voice assistant. Solved: Both are 4th generation in physical construction (microprocessors); the tablet incorporates 5th-generation AI software and natural-language interaction, showing evolution in software paradigm alongside hardware. Matches model answer (3 marks).
- **RRQ-07 (Understand)**: Analog vs digital computers with examples. Solved: Analog computers measure continuous physical variables (e.g. slide rule, mercury thermometer, speedometer); digital computers count discrete binary states (e.g. laptop, smartphone). Matches model answer (3 marks: 1 for continuous vs discrete, 2 for examples).
- **RRQ-08 (Apply)**: Classification on size-and-power axis for weather forecasting and washing machine. Solved: Weather-forecasting system is a supercomputer (requires massive parallel floating-point performance for national simulations); washing machine controller is an embedded computer (dedicated microprocessor embedded within a dedicated appliance). Matches model answer (4 marks: 1 class + 1 justification per device).
- **RRQ-09 (Understand)**: Digital divide explanation and Pakistan example. Solved: The gap in ICT access and capabilities; in Pakistan, illustrated by urban households with home high-speed fibre broadband vs rural families sharing a single low-bandwidth 2G/3G mobile phone. Matches model answer (3 marks: 1 definition, 2 for concrete example).
- **RRQ-10 (Analyze)**: Online homework portal with uneven submissions due to shared home phones. Solved: Technology deployed without considering existing socioeconomic disparities exacerbates the digital divide; schools must provide lab access or alternative offline pathways. Matches model answer (3 marks: 1 identifying divide, 1 analyzing device sharing, 1 policy implication).
