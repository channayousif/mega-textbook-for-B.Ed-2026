## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. Prompt: Which learning domain principally concerns knowledge and thinking in UNESCO's ESD framework?
   - Independent solution: B (Cognitive)
   - Analysis: Cognitive learning concerns knowledge, understanding and thinking processes. Socio-emotional concerns values, feelings and relations, while behavioural concerns action. Institutional is not one of UNESCO's three individual learning domains.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Remember.

2. Prompt: Pupils listen to a younger child's difficulty reaching a shared tap and explain why the plan should consider it. Which domain is most directly evidenced?
   - Independent solution: C (Socio-emotional, through considering another's needs)
   - Analysis: Listening empathetically to another child's access needs and valuing fair distribution of water represents values, empathy and human relationships, which constitute the socio-emotional domain.
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Apply.

3. Prompt: A group compares what might happen to a school garden in a dry month and a wet month before choosing plants. Which competency is most directly practised?
   - Independent solution: A (Anticipatory thinking)
   - Analysis: Evaluating and comparing potential future scenarios, trajectories and consequences before acting directly exemplifies UNESCO's anticipatory thinking competency.
   - Key: A. Match. Bloom: Apply.

4. Prompt: A teacher adds water-saving work to an existing mathematics lesson. Which planning choice best supports an ESD approach?
   - Independent solution: D (Connect quantities, fair access and a feasible reporting action)
   - Analysis: Effective ESD integrates knowledge (quantities), social consideration (fair access) and action (reporting) within existing subject curriculum without degrading academic rigor or relying on superficial displays.
   - Key: D. Match. Bloom: Apply.

5. Prompt: What best describes the relationship between EE and ESD?
   - Independent solution: B (They overlap, while ESD explicitly links sustainability dimensions)
   - Analysis: EE and ESD are historically connected and overlapping traditions with shared concern for environment and participation; ESD explicitly emphasizes interconnected environmental, social, and economic choices.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Understand.

6. Prompt: A lesson asks pupils to identify local birds and explain how litter harms them. The description says nothing about costs or access. Which judgement is best supported?
   - Independent solution: C (It shows an EE emphasis; a broader ESD claim needs more evidence)
   - Analysis: The described task demonstrates environmental observation and harm prevention (EE emphasis). Absence of description does not prove the teacher rejected social aspects, but claiming an ESD emphasis without evidence of connected dimensions is unsupported.
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Apply.

7. Prompt: Pupils compare litter reduction, affordability and access before proposing a lunch-container rule. What most strongly supports an ESD interpretation?
   - Independent solution: A (The connected concerns influence their proposed rule)
   - Analysis: Genuine ESD learning is evidenced when multi-dimensional considerations (environmental waste, economic cost, social access) actively shape decisions, rather than merely appearing as passive poster decorations.
   - Key: A. Match. Bloom: Apply.

8. Prompt: Which change is strongest evidence of transformative learning in a trainee's account?
   - Independent solution: D (Revising an assumption about storage after examining supply evidence)
   - Analysis: Transformative learning fundamentally involves critically reflecting on and revising underlying habits of mind or unexamined assumptions based on evidence and alternative perspectives.
   - Key: D. Match. Bloom: Understand.

9. Prompt: A tank starts with 50 litres, receives 25, supplies 20 and leaks 5. What is its end stock?
   - Independent solution: B (50 litres)
   - Analysis: In a stock-and-flow balance: Ending Stock = Initial Stock + Inflow - Outflows. Here: 50 + 25 - (20 + 5) = 75 - 25 = 50 litres.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Apply.

10. Prompt: Which description contains a complete balancing feedback loop for a water shortage?
    - Independent solution: C (A low level prompts a report, an adult arranges delivery, and the level recovers)
    - Analysis: A feedback loop requires a circular causal chain returning to influence the initiating state. A shortage (low level) triggers reporting and delivery, which increases water level and reduces shortage, forming a closed balancing loop.
    - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Apply.

9. Prompt: Given tank starts with 90 L, receives 30 L, supplies 25 L, leaks 10 L, distinguish stock from flows and explain why leak reduction alone may not guarantee supply.
   - Derived solution: Calculation: 90 + 30 - 25 - 10 = 85 L end stock. Stock: volume in tank (85 L). Flows: delivery (inflow), consumption and leakage (outflows). Reducing leaks saves water but if delivery fails or demand surges, shortages persist due to supply delays and external constraints. 4 marks (calculation 1, stock defined 1, flows defined 1, supply uncertainty explained 1).
   - Key comparison: Matches model answer and mark scheme. Bloom: Analyze.

10. Prompt: Trace a low-level report through delivery back to tank level, explaining one delay and one check.
    - Derived solution: Low tank level triggers report -> caretaker receives report -> supplier dispatches water -> delivery enters tank -> water level rises (closing loop). Delay: supplier response time or transport lag. Check: comparing tank level logs before and after scheduled delivery. 4 marks (loop sequence 1, return effect 1, delay identified 1, check identified 1).
    - Key comparison: Matches model answer and mark scheme. Bloom: Analyze.
