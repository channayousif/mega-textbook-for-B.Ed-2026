## RRQ independent solutions

1. Commitment states an intended direction (environmental learning within schooling); implementation
   arranges and supports opportunities (an aligned water lesson with materials). Matches the key.
2. A poster establishes a communication resource; it does not establish understanding or changed
   water use. Further evidence: ask pupils to compare two responses and inspect reasons. Matches.
3. Environmental: what water is wasted or available. Social: can all pupils reach safe water
   fairly. Economic: what resources and ongoing costs the response needs. Matches.
4. The inference from one omitted topic to whole-policy failure is unsupported. Bounded claim: no
   relevant discussion was observed in this lesson; check the planned sequence first. Matches.
5. Record issuer, date and section; separate wording from application; check the applicable class
   outcome and mentor arrangements; do not treat a historical national commitment as current local
   practice. Matches.
6. A textbook change alters one resource; curriculum reform changes aims, teaching experiences and
   assessment together. A waste lesson moving from copied definitions to compared options with
   reasons assessed changes more than the book. Matches.
7. A revised question naming two alternatives and asking for a reason using access and cost aligns
   with a comparison outcome. Matches.
8. Plan an outcome and model question; rehearse with peers; review observation notes with a mentor;
   revise a question that elicited only slogans and try it again. Matches.
9. Attendance counts participation, not changed teaching. Compare observed questioning or lesson
   plans before and after support with the same criterion; state the sample. Matches.
10. Picture cards instead of handling refuse (safety); oral explanation and meaningful roles
    (participation); collect each pupil's reason for a choice (evidence). Matches.

  Evaluate), states a bounded judgement and follow-up (0-2). Demand is Analyze and Evaluate.
- ERQ 3 (Create): rubric requires a locally verifiable outcome and coherent sequence (0-3, Create),
  explicit task-assessment alignment (0-3, Analyze), a worked example with reasons and a condition
  (0-2), and safety and participation adaptations (0-2). Demand is Create and Analyze.
- ERQ 4 (Evaluate): rubric distinguishes participation from competence (0-2), evaluates the lecture
  proposal (0-3, Evaluate), designs a rehearsal-feedback-revision extension (0-3, Create), and
  separates recommendations from verified local outcomes (0-2). Demand is Evaluate and Create.
- ERQ 5 (Create): rubric links policy interpretation, curriculum and preparation (0-3, Analyze),
  compares two actions and defends a feasible choice (0-3, Evaluate), uses aligned learning evidence
  and proportionate follow-up (0-2), and states responsibilities, inclusion and limits (0-2).
  Demand is Analyze and Evaluate.

The five ERQs cover the blueprint: two on policy (ERQ 1-2), two on curriculum and teacher education

not inflated by the verb label: ERQ 2 asks for analysis of a difference and a bounded judgement,
which is Analyze work, not a recall task.
