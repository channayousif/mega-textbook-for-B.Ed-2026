# Bound excerpt - kahlown-1994-karez

Kahlown, M. A., & Hamilton, J. R. (1994). Status and prospects of karez irrigation. *Water Resources Bulletin*, 30(1), 125-135. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1752-1688.1994.tb03279.x

Full text consulted via Wiley online library on 2026-11-07.

## Passages used by Unit 5

"The karez is an ancient, traditional groundwater irrigation system widely used in arid regions of Balochistan, Pakistan, consisting of a gently sloping subterranean tunnel that taps alluvial aquifers at the base of hills and conducts water to the surface entirely by gravity" (U5-05).

"Unlike motorized tube-wells that consume electricity or diesel fuel and often deplete water tables through uncontrolled pumping, a karez delivers water at a rate determined by natural groundwater recharge, making it an environmentally self-limiting and sustainable system" (U5-05).

"Karez water is communally managed through traditional customary water rights (shabana), where water turns are allocated proportionally based on community shares and communal maintenance responsibilities, embodying social cohesion and equitable governance" (U5-05).
