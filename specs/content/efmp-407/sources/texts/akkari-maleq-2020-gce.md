## Chapter 1: Introduction: Global Citizenship Education: A New Paradigm for Educational Systems? (pp. 1-14)

"Global Citizenship Education (GCE) has emerged in recent years as a central concept in the international educational debate. Under the auspices of UNESCO and integrated into target 4.7 of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), GCE aims to empower learners of all ages to assume active roles, both locally and globally, in building more peaceful, tolerant, inclusive and secure societies." (p. 2)

"A critical approach to GCE moves beyond soft notions of global awareness or mere international tourism. It questions structural inequalities, historical injustices, and the reproduction of power hierarchies within national and global education systems. Critical GCE emphasizes social justice, human rights, and the capacity of learners to critically analyze global issues from diverse cultural and geopolitical perspectives." (p. 5)

"Far from erasing or undermining national identity and local civic commitment, meaningful global citizenship education roots itself in local communities. Learners who understand local challenges - such as water scarcity, poverty, or environmental degradation - develop empathy and civic agency that connect directly to global responsibilities." (p. 8)

## Dimensions and Pedagogy of GCE (pp. 9-11)

"Effective global citizenship education requires interactive, learner-centred pedagogies:
1. Dialogic and participatory learning: encouraging students to articulate different viewpoints, engage with controversial social issues, and build consensus.
2. Critical inquiry: investigating the root causes of systemic challenges rather than merely discussing surface symptoms.
3. Action-oriented projects: connecting classroom deliberation with tangible community initiatives, enabling students to experience citizenship as an active practice." (pp. 9-10)
