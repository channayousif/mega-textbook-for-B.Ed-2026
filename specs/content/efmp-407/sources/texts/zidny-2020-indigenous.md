Zidny, R., Sjöström, J., & Eilks, I. (2020). A multi-perspective reflection on how indigenous knowledge and related ideas can improve science education for sustainability. *Science & Education*, 29(1), 145-185. CC BY 4.0. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11191-019-00100-x

Full text read from publisher open-access page on 2026-11-07.

## Passages used by Unit 5

"Indigenous knowledge systems (IKS) are defined as cumulative bodies of knowledge, practices, and beliefs regarding the relationship of living beings with one another and with their environment, which have evolved through adaptive processes and been handed down across generations through cultural transmission" (U5-05).

"Indigenous worldviews frequently emphasize circular, holistic relationships between humans and the natural environment, in contrast to linear, exploitative views of resource extraction" (U5-05).

"Science education for sustainability benefits when indigenous knowledge and Western modern science are brought into dialogic interaction - often termed 'two-eyed seeing' - rather than treating traditional knowledge as superstitious or obsolete" (U5-05).

"Integrating indigenous knowledge into science and sustainability education enhances cultural relevance, contextualises scientific principles through local environmental realities, and fosters values of environmental stewardship" (U5-05).
