# Bound excerpt - khoo-jorgensen-2021

Khoo, S., & Jørgensen, N. J. (2021). Intersections and collaborative potentials between global citizenship education and education for sustainable development. *Globalisation, Societies and Education*, 19(4), 470-481.
https://doi.org/10.1080/14767724.2021.1889361

Open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 / hybrid open access). Passages excerpted from Khoo and Jørgensen's analysis of how Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) and Global Citizenship Education (GCE) intersect, converge, and negotiate tensions under SDG Target 4.7.

## 1. Shared Mandate under Target 4.7 (pp. 470-472)

"Target 4.7 of the Sustainable Development Goals formally brings together Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) and Global Citizenship Education (GCE) within a unified international monitoring framework. Both educational approaches share the normative ambition to equip learners to confront contemporary global crises, from ecological collapse to escalating social and economic inequalities." (p. 471)

"While ESD historically emerged from environmental education with a focus on ecological limits, resource stewardship, and socio-economic futures, GCE emerged from human rights, peace education, and development education. Their convergence under SDG 4.7 creates a potent opportunity to integrate ecological awareness with social justice and ethical responsibility." (p. 472)

## 2. Navigating Tensions and Collaborative Potentials (pp. 474-477)

"A key tension lies in the risk of depoliticisation when these agendas are translated into standardized school curricula. When reduced to passive knowledge transfer, ESD can become technical environmental management, while GCE can devolve into superficial cultural celebration. To realize their collaborative potential, both fields must embrace critical, transformative praxis." (p. 475)

"Transformative convergence occurs when educators connect global planetary boundaries with local community justice:
- Connecting local environmental degradation (e.g. river pollution, deforestation) to global economic supply chains and ethical obligations.
- Developing relational ethics, where caring for one's immediate community and environment is understood as an expression of planetary responsibility.
- Fostering learner agency through transdisciplinary projects that address real-world community dilemmas." (pp. 476-477)
