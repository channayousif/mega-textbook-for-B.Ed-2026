Notes for whoever renders these.

- `fig-U6-1` (concept-map) and `fig-U6-3` (flowchart) are the unit's schematics for Art. III.10.
  Both must stay readable at phone width; `fig-U6-3` has six stages and two return arrows, so it
  should be drawn as a vertical flow on a narrow viewport rather than a horizontal one that forces a
  pinch-zoom.
- **Nothing in these prompts carries meaning in colour alone.** `fig-U6-5`'s three rings are
  distinguished by their labels and their contents, not by hue, and the two arrows leaving and
  entering ring 3 are distinguished as bold and dashed. Keep that, because a reader on a monochrome
  print handout gets the whole figure.
- `fig-U6-1`'s four branches are the four culture markers and the ring around them is the teacher's
  own micro-behaviour. The ring must visibly enclose all four branches rather than sit beside them:
  the point of the figure is that the teacher's behaviour conditions all four, and a renderer who
  draws the ring as a fifth branch has inverted the argument.
- `fig-U6-4` is the unit's densest table at ten rows and five columns. It is the one figure here that
  may need to be split for a narrow viewport; if it is split, split it by level (classroom rows
  together, system rows together) and keep the footer counts correct for the whole, not the part.
- `fig-U6-6`'s fourth column is "What they must not be asked to do". That column is the figure's
  pedagogical point and must not be dropped for space; if something has to go on a small screen, drop
  the second column instead.
- Short quoted fragments inside `fig-U6-4` and `fig-U6-6` that reference the statute (`s. 9(1)`,
  `s. 9(2)`, `s. 6(11)`) are section references rather than quotations, and the statutory wording
  they gesture at is bound at `specs/content/efmp-305/sources/texts/sindh2018.md`. A renderer must not
  expand a section reference into a paraphrase of the provision.
- For the Urdu variants at G6: the six statutory terms named in `concepts/unit-06.md`'s translator
  instruction apply to figure labels as well as to prose, and the English term stays in parentheses
  on first use inside a figure too.
