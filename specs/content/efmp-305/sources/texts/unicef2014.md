## Printed-page locators and paraphrased support

These notes replace the interrupted draft's long verbatim extracts. They are verification
locators, not a substitute for reading the source.

- Page 8, section II: collaboration involves families, schools and communities. It depends
  on relationships and institutional conditions rather than a universal recipe.
- Page 10, section II: external help should be proportionate to need. Consider both the
  benefit of additional help and the instruction or social contact missed during withdrawal.
- Page 12, section III, Figure 1: a general participation ladder distinguishes symbolic
  involvement from shared power. It is not a Pakistan-specific measurement instrument.
- Page 13, section III: families may advocate, support learning, partner with schools, help
  other families or participate in governance. The booklet attributes these options to UNESCO.
- Pages 14 and 16: families contribute knowledge of a child's learning and communication.
- Section V: negative school experiences, limited options, poverty and fear of community
  stigma can obstruct involvement. Families may also have mixed views of inclusion.
- Section VI: extended-family carers can be important partners; classroom aides should
  support access without becoming a permanent attachment to one pupil.
