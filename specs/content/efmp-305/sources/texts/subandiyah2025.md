## Abstract (OpenAlex)

```text
This study examined the impact of DI on student engagement in learning in Bahasa Indonesia across
three middle schools in Indonesia. [...] In this quasi-experimental mixed-methods design, 180
students - one experimental group using DI and one control group using traditional teaching - were
covered. [...] The results reveal that DI significantly impacted academic performance and learning
behaviors. Moreover, the experimental group produced a substantially higher result in the
post-test (M = 82.45) than the control group (M = 64.12) and demonstrated more active and
initiative behavior in class. However, no significant difference was found in class attendance,
indicating that DI's influence was limited to academic and behavioral engagement rather than
overall effectiveness in improving attendance.
```

## Method: what the differentiation consisted of (full text)

```text
The experimental group had DI implemented in need, learning style and level of student academic
ability. The teachers used comprehensive development with a broad stroke of flexible grouping to
ensure dynamic and varied ability criteria to achieve the aims.

Furthermore, within the flexible grouping, the tasks implemented in the treatment class were
tailored to each student's various learning styles and developmentally appropriate difficulty
levels. These tasks consist of individual and group projects designed to achieve students'
cognitive, affective and psychomotor participation. Formative assessment occurred frequently to
keep tabs on student progress.

This study was conducted at three junior high schools located in three major cities in Indonesia:
Bandung (West Java), Semarang (Central Java) and Surabaya (East Java).
```
