## Abstract

```text
This classroom action research, following the Kemmis and McTaggart model, aimed to describe the
implementation of the Student Teams Achievement Division (STAD) cooperative learning model,
examine its effect on slow learners' mathematics learning outcomes, and describe their learning
activity across two cycles. The study involved 26 sixth-grade students (14 boys, 12 girls) at an
inclusive elementary school in East Kalimantan, Indonesia, including six slow learners. [...]
Mathematics achievement increased substantially, with students reaching the minimum mastery
criterion rising from 27 percent at baseline to 92.31 percent in Cycle I and 100 percent in Cycle
II, alongside a rise in class mean score from 52.31 to 93.00.
```

## Results and conclusion (full text)

```text
Across both cycles, peer-tutoring behavior was consistently the lowest-scoring student-activity
indicator, even as it increased in absolute terms between cycles.

Collectively, these parallel gains indicate that structured, scaffolded, peer-mediated
instruction can meaningfully support slow learners within a regular inclusive classroom rather
than requiring their separation from it.
```

## What this supports, and what it does not
