## Section 1.5, Social exclusion and social cohesion (p. 12, paras 26 and 28)

```text
26. Education is not only about the individual, it has a societal role [...] In a country with
alarming inequities of income and opportunities, reducing the social exclusion needs to be one of
the principle objectives of the Policy. The educational system in Pakistan is accused of
strengthening the existing inequitable social structure as very few people from the public sector
educational institutions could move up the ladder of social mobility.

28. Uneven distribution of resources and opportunities and apprehensions of sliding down on the
scale of poverty promote social exclusion.
```

## Section 2.4, Overarching priorities, Policy Actions 3, 4 and 5 (pp. 19-20)

This is the whole of what the 2009 policy says at system level about inclusion and about learners
with disabilities. It is quoted here in full, in sequence, with the surrounding actions shown so a
reviewer can confirm nothing has been omitted between them.

```text
Policy Actions:

1. Provinces and Area Governments shall affirm the goal of achieving universal and free primary
education by 2015 and up to class 10 by 2025.

2. Provincial and Area Governments shall develop plans for achieving these targets, including
intermediate enrolment targets and estimates of the required financial, technical, human and
organizational resources.

3. The plans shall also promote equity in education with the aim of eliminating social exclusion
and promoting national cohesion. Greater opportunities shall be provided to marginalised groups of
society, particularly girls.

4. To achieve the commitments of Government of Pakistan towards Education for All (EFA) and the
MDGs, inclusive and child-friendly education shall be promoted.

5. Special measures shall be adopted to ensure inclusion of special persons in mainstream
education as well as in literacy and TVE programmes.

6. Governments shall improve quality of educational provision at all levels of education.
```

## Annex, Section B, Equity in education (pp. 66-67, paras 9 and 10)

```text
9. The averages for Pakistan, noted above, mask large differences in access across gender, ethnic
minorities, provinces, regions and rural-urban divides. This results in weaker performance on
equitable distribution of educational opportunities. It is common knowledge, as well as a proven
```

## What this settles

Supports U5-1, and sets a hard limit on what U5-1 may claim:

- The policy does commit, at system level, to promoting inclusive and child friendly education
  (Action 4) and to special measures for the inclusion of "special persons" in mainstream
  education (Action 5). Unit 5 quotes Action 5 and uses the policy's own dated term as evidence of
  how the vocabulary has moved since 2009.
- The policy's equity language (Action 3) names marginalised groups and girls; its own annex
  concedes that "discrimination exists in the education system in various forms" and attributes
  the inequity to "poor implementation and social customs".
- **The policy's inclusion content is two sentences long.** A full-text search of this PDF for
  "inclusive", "disabilit", "special needs" and "special education" returns the abbreviation list,
  the two policy actions quoted above, section 1.5 on social exclusion, one reference to the
  Ministry of Social Welfare and Special Education as an actor, and a stipend provision aimed at
  child labourers. There is no inclusive-education chapter, no definition of inclusive education,
  no disability data, no named duty on a school and no accountability mechanism. Unit 5 asserts
  exactly this and the assertion is the pedagogical point of the topic: it is what makes the
  difference between a policy statement and a legal duty concrete rather than abstract.
- The annex's own disparity data are gender, rural/urban and provincial. Disability is not
  disaggregated anywhere in it, which the unit states.

**What it does not settle.** Nothing about implementation after 2009, nothing about the 18th
Amendment's devolution of education to the provinces (which the unit mentions as context and
sources from the Sindh Act's own preamble, where the provincial subject point is recited), nothing
about the 2017 or 2018 policy documents, and nothing about any provincial policy. Any
implementation claim in this unit is carried by `virani2022` or `parveen2022` and labelled as
teacher-reported.

**Counting claim.** The "two sentences" claim is a claim about absence, which is the kind a
reviewer should test rather than accept. The test is reproducible: search this exact PDF
(sha256 above) for the four strings named, and compare the hit list with the one given above.
