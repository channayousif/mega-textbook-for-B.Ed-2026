`specs/content/efmp-305/coverage/unit-05.md` appears here, and every key here is cited both in
prose and in a per-topic `## Further reading` section.

The course guide carries **no** "Text Books and References" section, so no source for this unit is
`guide-required`. Under `D-2026-0053` item 2 the author binds verified real works chosen for
relevance to the course outcomes, labelled author-selected. The content-spec front matter declares
`open_access_floor:` / `default: 1`. The floor is met by `ainscow2020b` and by three further
registry-verified open-access Pakistani studies bound below, so no escalation to `specs/gaps.md` is
needed for this unit.

## Unverifiable sources

The three primary texts (`sindh2018`, `ict2020`, `nep2009`) are **not** in this list. They were
retrieved in full, read, and bound as committed excerpts with checksums, so a reviewer can check
support offline and reproducibly. That is the whole reason this unit is safe to publish: the only
claims it makes about what a document requires are grounded in text that travels with it.

The six remaining keys are unverifiable at G1 in the sense the depth gate means: the bibliographic
record and, where a licence exists, the licence have been checked, but **no full text was retrieved
from this authoring host** and no excerpt is bound. Support is bibliographic or abstract level only,
per `D-2026-0001`; a reviewer must verify each specific claim against the source text at G2.

- `parveen2022`: supports U5-4 and U5-5. CC BY-NC 4.0, so G2 should retrieve and bind an excerpt
  without difficulty. **Scope cautions the prose must respect:** a questionnaire survey of 191
  **pre-service** teachers on teacher-training programmes at **one university in Lahore**, using
  the ATIES and CIES instruments. These are not in-service teachers, not Sindh, and not a national
  sample. Topic 5.3 states the sample and the city in the same paragraph as the findings, cites the
  study for the **pattern** (favourable in general, reluctant in specific places, concerned about
  time, other parents and buildings) and reproduces **no number** from it except the sample size.
  The unit must never present it as evidence of national prevalence.
- `virani2022`: supports U5-4. CC BY 4.0, gold open access. **Scope cautions:** an exploratory
  qualitative study with **fifteen** purposively selected primary school teachers, five of whom had
  pupils with special needs. This is an account of what a small number of teachers said, not a
  measurement. The prose names the number and treats the attitude finding as the authors' own
  characterisation rather than as an established fact about Pakistan. The study's point about the
  parent-teacher relationship is carried forward to Unit 6 rather than used here.
- `anis2025`: supports U5-4. CC BY 4.0. **Scope cautions:** an **instrument-development** study,
  300 elementary teachers from public and private schools, principal axis factoring with varimax
  rotation. The only claim bound to it is the **four-factor structure** and the inference that
  attitude, preparedness, resources and collaboration are not separable in this context. It is not
  cited for any teacher's attitude level, for any variance figure, or for anything about pupils.
  This is the row whose use is most abstract, and a G2 reviewer should confirm the factor names are
  reported as given here before accepting the inference.
- `ainscow2020b`: supports U5-5. CC BY-NC 4.0 and the unit's open-access floor. **Scope cautions:**
  a conceptual and comparative paper drawing on international experience, not an empirical study
  and not about Pakistan. The quoted phrase about the field remaining confused as to the actions
  needed is from the abstract. The claim that school culture and collaboration matter alongside
  resources is the author's own well-known argument but is bound here at abstract level, so G2
  should pin it to the body text before a reviewer is asked to accept it as the paper's finding.
- `unCRPD`: supports U5-1 and U5-2. A public international instrument with no registry record. Same
  record as Unit 1's. Its Article 24 page was not retrieved on this pass; however, the two facts the
  unit takes from it that could mislead if wrong - the adoption year and Pakistan's ratification
  date of 25 October 2011 - are recited inside the Sindh Act's own preamble, which **was** retrieved
  and is bound. G2 should still retrieve Article 24 itself, because Unit 1 leans on its substance
  more heavily than this unit does.
- `unesco2020`: supports U5-1 and U5-5. Free to read, no open licence, no full text retrieved.
  Shared record with Units 1 and 2. The two claims bound to it are framing claims about the global
  character of the policy-to-practice gap, deliberately pitched at what the report's title and remit
  establish. No chapter, page or figure from it is cited anywhere in this unit, and G2 should refuse
  any later edit that adds one without a retrieval.

- **No further verification is owed on the three primary texts for the claims this unit makes.**
  The excerpts are bound with checksums and each records its own limits. What a reviewer should
  still do is adversarial rather than bibliographic: check that the unit never upgrades the Sindh
  Act's "shall endeavor" into an absolute duty, never attributes a federal provision to Sindh, and
  never states an implementation fact that the excerpts explicitly disclaim.
- **Amendments and subordinate legislation were not checked.** The statutes were retrieved as
  published on the pages named and no search was made for amending Acts, rules, notifications or
  case law. The unit's teacher notes tell the tutor to check the Department's own page before relying
  on a provision in a real case. If the curriculum owner wants the amendment history established,
  that is a separate legal-research task and must not be assumed to have been done here.
