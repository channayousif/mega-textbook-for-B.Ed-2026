2. Prompt: What is the main difference between a national ideology and a national identity?

## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

- RRQ 1: Why Pakistan Studies is not only history; real place example.
  - Derived solution: Must state multidisciplinary coverage (geography, economy, civics, culture) and illustrate with a multi-dimensional locus (e.g. Sukkur Barrage on Indus: geography of river, economic irrigation, historical colonial construction, inter-provincial constitutional water accord).
  - Supplied model: Matches 4-mark scheme (1 non-history areas, 1 real place, 2 multidimensional links).
- RRQ 2: Difference between ideology and religion, using Ideology of Pakistan.
  - Derived solution: Religion = faith and worship; ideology = social/political order and governance principles. Ideology of Pakistan draws on Islam (justice, consultation) while incorporating democracy and civic equality.
  - Supplied model: Matches 3-mark scheme (1 religion, 1 ideology, 1 synthesis).
- RRQ 3: Two teacher responsibilities and concrete classroom actions.
  - Derived solution: Any two of Accuracy, Fairness, Connection, Thinking with specific pedagogic application.
  - Supplied model: Matches 4-mark scheme (2 naming, 2 concrete actions).
- RRQ 4: Textbook omitting Balochistan and KP from national identity chapter.
  - Derived solution: Analytically diagnoses marginalisation and alienation of pupils from omitted provinces; weakens national cohesion. Suggests concrete inclusion of regional languages and cultural contributions.
  - Supplied model: Matches 4-mark scheme (1 exclusion diagnosis, 1 integration analysis, 1 accuracy critique, 1 remedy).
- RRQ 5: Role of Sir Syed and 1906 Muslim League in Two-Nation Theory growth.
  - Derived solution: Sir Syed highlighted distinct socio-educational interests after 1857 without demanding separate state; 1906 Muslim League secured separate electorates, recognising distinct political status.
  - Supplied model: Matches 4-mark scheme (2 Sir Syed, 2 League/electorates).
- RRQ 6: Three reasons for significance of Two-Nation Theory.
  - Derived solution: Basis for Pakistan demand; unified diverse regional Muslim populations; demonstrated inadequacy of majoritarian safeguards; founding ideological rationale.
  - Supplied model: Matches 3-mark scheme (1 per reason).
- RRQ 7: Refuting claim that Two-Nation Theory proves mutual hatred.
  - Derived solution: Separates political self-determination from personal enmity; cites Jinnah's 11 August speech and Constitution Articles 20 and 36.
  - Supplied model: Matches 5-mark scheme (1 analytical separation, 2 evidence citations, 1 explanation, 1 expression).
- RRQ 8: Difference between Islamic state in Constitution and a theocracy.
  - Derived solution: Theocracy is non-consensual rule by clergy; Pakistan Constitution vests sovereignty in Allah, exercised by people through elected representatives.
  - Supplied model: Matches 3-mark scheme (1 definition, 1 elected role, 1 contrast).
- RRQ 9: Three features of Islamic state and constitutional references.
  - Derived solution: E.g. Sovereignty in Allah (Preamble), Authority as trust (Preamble), Equality (Article 25), Minority protection (Articles 20, 36).
  - Supplied model: Matches 6-mark scheme (1 per feature, 1 per location).
- RRQ 10: Apply four source-reading steps to 11 August 1947 extract.
  - Derived solution: (1) Who/when (Jinnah, 11 Aug 1947); (2) Audience (Constituent Assembly & diverse public); (3) What it says (freedom of worship, equal citizenship); (4) Meaning (faith does not determine civic rights).
  - Supplied model: Matches 4-mark scheme (1 per step, analytical inference required for step 4).
