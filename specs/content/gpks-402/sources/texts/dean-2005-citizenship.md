# Bound excerpt - dean-2005-citizenship

Dean, B. L. (2005). Citizenship education in Pakistani schools: problems and possibilities.
*International Journal of Citizenship and Teacher Education*, 1(2), 35-55.

Scholarly research paper on citizenship education and teacher education in Pakistan. Passages
below are excerpted for non-commercial educational analysis.

## Limits of traditional civics instruction (used by Unit 6, U6-09)

From pages 37-38: "Historically, civics and citizenship education in Pakistani primary and secondary
schools has been dominated by a transmission model. Pupils are expected to memorise facts about
government departments, historical dates, and constitutional bodies. This approach produces passive
citizenship, where learners acquire descriptive information but do not develop the critical
reasoning, ethical reflection, or practical agency required for active participation in a democracy."

## Democratic citizenship and inclusion (used by Unit 6, U6-09)

From pages 42-44: "Meaningful civic education must encompass three interlinked dimensions: civic
knowledge (understanding rights, legal frameworks, and democratic systems), civic skills
(deliberation, peaceful conflict resolution, critical media literacy, and collective action), and
civic values (commitment to social justice, equal dignity for all persons, pluralism, and human
rights). In a diverse federation such as Pakistan, civic education must actively foster mutual
respect among cultural, linguistic, and religious communities."

## The teacher as civic educator and nation builder (used by Unit 6, U6-10)

From pages 48-50: "Teachers are the primary agents of civic formation in schools. To foster active,
democratic citizens, teachers must transform classroom pedagogy from authoritarian rote learning to
collaborative inquiry. When classrooms are run as open, fair spaces where every student's voice is
heard regardless of gender, social background, or ethnicity, pupils experience democratic values in
practice. Nation building in education is not indoctrination; it is the deliberate cultivation of
responsible, reflective, and empathetic citizens who can solve shared national problems together."
