## Verified chronological evidence

- Before a new constitution, the independence arrangements continued government under the adapted Government of India Act, 1935.
- Liaquat Ali Khan introduced the Objectives Resolution on 7 March 1949; the Constituent Assembly adopted it on 12 March.
- Governor General Ghulam Muhammad dissolved the first Constituent Assembly on 24 October 1954. The second Assembly followed in 1955. One Unit combined the western wing in that period.
- The first Constitution passed on 29 February 1956, received assent on 2 March and came into force on 23 March. It established a parliamentary arrangement, with the Prime Minister heading government, the President as head of state and a unicameral Parliament. The two wings had equal representation.
- Iskander Mirza abrogated it and declared martial law on 7 October 1958; Ayub Khan displaced him later that month.
- The 1962 framework concentrated executive authority in the President, retained provincial institutions, used one central chamber and an indirect electoral college of Basic Democrats.
- Martial law returned under Yahya Khan on 25 March 1969. The 1970 general election used adult franchise and population. Political conflict preceded the separation of East Pakistan and creation of Bangladesh.
- The Assembly adopted the Interim Constitution and appointed a Constitution Committee on 17 April 1972. The draft report was presented on 31 December.
- The permanent Constitution passed unanimously on 10 April 1973, was authenticated on 12 April, and came into force on 14 August. Its parliamentary and two-House framework differed from the preceding presidential arrangement.
- The later history records military-rule interruptions after 1977 and 1999 and subsequent restorations and amendments.

## Limits

The page contains questionable original Senate seat totals and simplified historical interpretation. Unit 3 does not reproduce those figures or its claims about the inevitability of partition. Use it for chronology alongside the primary constitutional documents, not as a sufficient account of every cause of 1971. Classroom analogies and pedagogical inferences in the unit are the author's own.
