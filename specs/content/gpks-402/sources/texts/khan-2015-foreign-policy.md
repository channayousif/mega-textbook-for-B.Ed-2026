# Bound excerpt - khan-2015-foreign-policy

Khan, R. M. (2015). Foreign policy of Pakistan in the changing regional and global settings.
*Journal of Contemporary Studies*, 4(1), 14-30. National Defence University, Islamabad.
https://doi.org/10.54690/jcs.v4i1.74

Open access. The DOI resolves to the article record at the National Defence University. Excerpts
below are quoted and paraphrased for non-commercial educational use in teacher education.

## Core principles of Pakistan's foreign policy (used by Unit 6, U6-01)

From the article: "The foreign policy of Pakistan is primarily guided by the principles of
safeguarding national sovereignty, territorial integrity, and promoting international peace and
security in accordance with the United Nations Charter."

From the article: "Pakistan adheres to the principles of non-interference in the internal affairs
of other sovereign states, peaceful settlement of international disputes, and mutual respect."

From the article: "Article 40 of the 1973 Constitution provides a foundational mandate for
Pakistan's foreign diplomacy, enjoining the state to preserve and strengthen fraternal relations
among Muslim countries and promote international peace."

## Relations with neighbouring countries (used by Unit 6, U6-02)

From the article on China: "Pakistan and China have maintained a time-tested all-weather
strategic partnership. The initiation of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) has elevated
bilateral relations from traditional diplomatic cooperation to deep economic and infrastructure
integration, positioning Pakistan as a vital regional connectivity hub."

From the article on Afghanistan: "Pakistan and Afghanistan share deep ethnic, linguistic, cultural,
and historical bonds. Peace and stability in Afghanistan are directly tied to Pakistan's own
national security and border stability, requiring constructive engagement and institutional border
management."

From the article on Iran: "Pakistan and Iran share an ancient cultural affinity and a long border.
Bilateral cooperation centres on border security, cross-border trade, and regional energy
projects."

From the article on India: "Relations with India have been historically burdened by the unresolved
dispute over Jammu and Kashmir, multiple armed conflicts, and mutual mistrust. Sustained dialogue,
adherence to international commitments, and peaceful resolution of core disputes remain essential
for lasting regional peace."

## Role in international organisations (used by Unit 6, U6-03)

From the article: "Pakistan has consistently played an active role in multilateral diplomacy. As one
of the largest contributors to United Nations peacekeeping operations, Pakistan has demonstrated its
commitment to global peace and conflict prevention."

From the article: "Within the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), Pakistan has historically
served as a leading voice for collective Muslim interests, solidarity, and causes such as Palestine
and Kashmir. In South Asia, Pakistan remains committed to regional cooperation through SAARC,
though bilateral disputes have frequently hindered the forum's full economic potential."
