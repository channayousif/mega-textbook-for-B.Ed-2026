## Course-wide items

- **Course Objectives (guide, verbatim, `3rd 2026.txt:19-27`)**: at the end of the course, prospective teachers are expected to be:
  1. Familiar with the four language skills - Listening, Speaking reading and writing
  2. Identify and prepare activities for developing four skills
  3. Apply modern methods and approaches in teaching of English
  4. Prepare lesson plans of Prose, Poetry, Composition and Grammar
  5. Effective use of audio visual aids.
  6. Measure and evaluate the students' progress during teaching of English As a foreign I second language
- **Teaching strategies (guide, `:103-106`)**: lecture; discussions; question-answer; brainstorming.
- **Practical work (guide, `:108-111`)**: group work; group assignments; individual assignment; presentations.
- **Assessment criteria (guide, `:113-117`)**: class test; mid-term test; evaluation of assignments; class attendance; class participation and performance. No weights are given, so the Constitution Art. III.7 default (60% summative / 40% formative) applies.
- **Recommended resources**: see `## Reading list` below (cited by reference only, never reproduced - Constitution Art. III.5).

## Course Description

From the course guide (paraphrased, `:17-18`): the course prepares prospective teachers to teach English in Grades I to VIII. They become familiar with the English curriculum and its expected student learning outcomes, use the language skills to vary their teaching methods and promote active learning, make and use teaching and learning materials, and plan English lessons and activities.

## Drafting notes for intake

Recorded by the drafter so the intake evaluator can test them; none is settled here.

1. **Units with four or more topics.** Following the guide's numbering makes Units 1 (4 topics), 2 and 6 (5), 3 (6) and 5 (7) larger than any approved unit. Two blueprint defaults cannot hold for them: the per-topic formative floor (>= 3 items per topic) gives 12 to 21 formative items, beyond `D-2026-0072`, which rules only on three-topic units; and a 10-item MCQ or RRQ band cannot give 6 or 7 topics two items each, so Units 3 and 5 state ">= 1 per topic". Both need an owner ruling, or a different partition.
2. **Unit 4 is one guide line.** The guide's section 4 has a single item (`:72`). Two checklist rows (U4-02, U4-03) are derived from objective 3 and the mapped readings under `D-2026-0053` item 4; each row names its source.
3. **Dated media.** The guide names cassette players, video cassettes, VCRs, filmstrips and overhead projectors (`:35-36`, `:86-88`). The checklist keeps the guide's items; the parenthesised modern equivalents (audio recordings, video, projector) are the drafter's reading of the same idea and need owner confirmation.
4. **Repeated items.** Vocabulary (1.2.5 and 2.4) and pronunciation (1.2.2 and 2.5) each appear twice in the guide: once as part of the speaking skill, once as a teaching task. Both rows are kept, with that distinction.
5. **"Word building" under methods** (3.6, `:69`) is a vocabulary technique, not a method; it stays where the guide puts it.
6. **Reading list.** All five guide books are print. Two are local Pakistani texts not found in Open Library; Nolasco is misprinted as "Nohand (1993)"; Cook's edition and year disagree.
7. **Objective 6 wording.** "As a foreign I second language" (`:27`) is reproduced verbatim; it reads as "as a foreign or second language".
