## Evidence notes (original paraphrase)

DiBattista and Kurzawa report a statistical item analysis of 1198 multiple-choice items on sixteen classroom tests in various disciplines. The mean item discrimination coefficient was +0.25, and more than 30 per cent of items had unsatisfactory coefficients below +0.20. Of the 3819 distractors, 45 per cent were flawed either because fewer than 5 per cent of examinees selected them or because their selection was positively rather than negatively correlated with test scores. The authors conclude that there is considerable room for improvement in the quality of many multiple-choice tests and suggest that instructors conduct an item analysis and modify distractors that impair discriminatory power.
