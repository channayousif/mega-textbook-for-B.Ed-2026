## Evidence notes (original paraphrase)

Koca, Stavre and Kaçani examine how English grammar is taught communicatively in Albanian pre-university classrooms. They review the shift from a form-centred approach, which taught rules in isolation, to a communicative approach, which at first paid little attention to form, and then to an integrated approach that draws learners' attention to form within meaningful communication. They adopt Larsen-Freeman's three-dimensional model of grammar: form (accurate use), meaning (the message encoded) and pragmatic use (application in context). Their mixed-methods study, a questionnaire and structured interviews with EFL teachers, finds that teachers are aware of communicative methods but find them hard to implement because of limited resources, insufficient training and traditional curricula. Most teachers report that integrated grammar teaching helps pupils use language in real situations, and they recommend combining implicit and explicit grammar teaching and integrating grammar with listening, speaking, reading and writing.
