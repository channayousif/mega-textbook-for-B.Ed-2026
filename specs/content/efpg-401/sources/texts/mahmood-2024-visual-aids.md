## Evidence notes (original paraphrase)

Mahmood, Bukhari and Bahoo report a quasi-experimental study of visual aids in English teaching at secondary level in district Bahawalnagar, Pakistan. A sample of 30 students was taught English with visual aids under an experimental pre-test and post-test design, and their achievement was compared with a control group taught by the traditional method. The visual-aid group achieved significantly better post-test scores; the traditional group improved less. The authors conclude that teaching through visual aids improved students' achievement and recommend that policy and classroom practice prioritise visual aids alongside activity-based methods.
