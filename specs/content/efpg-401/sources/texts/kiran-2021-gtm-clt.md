## Evidence notes (original paraphrase)

Kiran reports an experimental study in a Government Girls Elementary School in Okara, Punjab, in which seventy-six Grade 7 pupils were taught English grammar either through the grammar-translation method or through the shallow-end approach of communicative language teaching. The article sets out the differences between the two approaches: grammar-translation teaches grammar deductively, emphasises accuracy, uses the mother tongue and keeps the teacher as authority, while communicative language teaching teaches grammar inductively, emphasises fluency, uses authentic materials and casts the teacher as facilitator. In the shallow-end approach, pupils are given examples that are not detached from their context and are asked to catch the underlying structure. The post-test results show that pupils taught through the shallow-end communicative approach achieved higher mean scores in English grammar than those taught through grammar-translation, at the knowledge, comprehension and application levels of Bloom's taxonomy. The study concludes that both approaches are effective, that CLT does not ignore grammar but repositions it as a communicative resource, and that the method should be tailored to local needs.
