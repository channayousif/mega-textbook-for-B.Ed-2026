## RRQ derivations

1. Guidance = information and direction, helping a person see options and choose a
   path (the head teacher's careers talk: accurate, future-aimed, missing the
   present). Counseling = a skilled relationship in which a trained listener helps a
   person work through a difficulty information cannot touch (the counselor asking
   what changed at home since October and listening without interrupting).

2. Teaching is aimed at learning; guidance at direction; counseling at
   understanding-of-self. Each is a discipline with its own ground: the teacher who
   counsels a pupil through a family crisis during the mathematics period has
   abandoned the mathematics and is counselling without training. The distinction is
   division of the work, not hierarchy.

3. What helps is being heard by someone steady, not the advice that person could
   have written on a slip; the pupil who talks twenty minutes and hears "what do you
   make of that?" twice does more work than one who receives ten excellent
   instructions. Advice is what a counselor sometimes ends with, and only after the
   understanding it stands on has been built.

4. (1) A named person pupils can reach; (2) a referral route every teacher knows by
   heart; (3) information about choices available before the choices close; (4)
   records that follow the pupil without labelling them.

5. Acceptable shape: "I have noticed, I am here, and there is someone whose job this
   is - come with me" plus the honest limit "I will only share it with the person
   whose job it is to help" (no absolute secrecy promise), listening more than
   speaking, and the handover written down.

6. Fairness (dilemma: the patron's child / the pupil you cannot help liking);
   confidentiality (dilemma: the disclosure that touches safety; never promised
   absolutely); boundaries (dilemma: warmth, the pupil who wants to keep talking);
   honest reporting (dilemma: the examiner's request, the school's average); pupil
   welfare first (orders the rest; dilemma: welfare against reputation, results,
   convenience).

7. The rules are the floor, not the work. In the marks case the rule (honest
   reporting) and the pressure arrive on the same table and the teacher must choose;
   the choice is the ethics. A teacher without an account of the duties decides by
   convenience, whatever the rules say - so "not breaking rules" understates the
   work.

8. (1) Whose interest: the raise serves the patron's and the school's, not the
   pupil's or the class's. (2) Which duty: fairness and honest reporting at once.
   (3) Standards: professional accountability, public trust (NPST context).
   (4) Do and record: hold the mark, write the reason, let the record carry the
   argument.

9. Any three of: practice with instant feedback (Unit 3 reinforcement / Unit 6
   attribution loop); access to the unseen (Unit 10 demonstration); creation and
   real audiences (Unit 5 elaboration); connection and research (Unit 3 analogy
   strategy).

10. Unit 3's strongest finding: pupils learn what trying looks like by watching what
    the teacher does with a wrong answer. The screen has no face, so the device has
    no equivalent of that modeling.
