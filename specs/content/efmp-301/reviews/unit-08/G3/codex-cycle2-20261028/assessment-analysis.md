Stems were read separately from the bounded answers section. The reasoning below was derived from the taught passages and then compared with the supplied guidance. No learner-facing material was changed.

MCQ 1: b, definition is design for learning. 2: b, time/safety/participation. 3: b, prevention avoids the problem. 4: b, a routine settles a recurring question. 5: a, greeting by name builds relationships. 6: c, prize chart is not one of the five parts. 7: b, rules assign responsibility. 8: b, replacing a taken lunch is imposed and logically connected. 9: c, proximity starts the ladder. 10: b, norms concern the room's response to a speaker. All ten match supplied guidance. Seven are b, two c, one a, none d. Items 3/4/7/10 largely recall taught definitions despite Understand labels; MCQ 5 repeats an explicitly taught move, so Apply is debatable.

RRQ 1: define design/running and identify the three services. 2: prevention anticipates problems, routines settle repeated questions, engagement makes work startable, consistency stabilizes responses; give classroom examples. 3: quiet copying can lack understanding, safety or participation. 4: teacher models good-try response, peers adopt it. 5: space/routines/relationships/norms/feedback; seat for sight, practise routines, greet by name are three moves. 6: established trust supports correction before conflict arises. 7: routines organize repeated work; rules establish responsibilities; bags routine versus respecting others' learning. 8: late arrival misses instructions naturally; lunch replacement is an imposed logical result. 9: proximity/private word/choice/consequence/parent meeting; private correction preserves dignity. These nine derivations agree with the supplied two-point guidance. RRQ 10 cannot be derived from Unit 8's taught content: day-one seating map in topic-02 helps learn names, whereas the question demands a once-a-term map of peer belonging and break-time support. The supplied response adds peer-group inclusion and tolerated/invisible categories. This is not the taught environment construct. Concept row 8-5 assigning RRQ-10 to 8.2 does not establish coverage. Thus RRQ spread is 3/3/3 plus one extraneous item, not approved 3/4/3.

ERQ 1: mid-lesson register and mood-led starts waste attention, prevention/routine/consistency; redesign entry routine and justify recovered time. ERQ 2: cover all five environmental parts with cheap moves, model respectful wrong-answer response, avoid public naming. ERQ 3: public naming may stop behaviour briefly but reinforces audience attention and anxiety; private staged responses protect participation and authority. ERQ 4: expect many low-level incidents to resolve at proximity/private word, fewer at higher stages, so practise prevention and early responses; treat distribution as a prediction to test, not established data. ERQ 5: integrate entry routine, responsibility rules, norm, ladder and environmental moves; explain limits such as resources and varying needs. Rubrics cover these derivations and each sums to 20 (20/20/20/20/20). ERQ 1/2/4 have topic anchors, 3/5 integrate. Analyze/Create demand is substantive. All five rubrics award four marks for being within a word count, but the ERQ stems and shared bank instructions specify no word count; topic tasks' 250-300 words do not establish a limit for these different questions. Author should supply explicit limits or remove that criterion.

Formative checks: topic 8.1 asks definition, four principles, silence versus learning, register interruption, consistency binding teacher; topic 8.2 asks five parts, norm modelling, trust before correction, three moves, repair of silent wrong work; topic 8.3 asks rules/routines, responsibility rules, consequence types, ladder steps, developmental reading of refusal.
