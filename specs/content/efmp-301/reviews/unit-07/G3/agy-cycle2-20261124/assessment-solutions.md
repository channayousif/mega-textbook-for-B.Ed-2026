## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. The learning-styles claim, at its strongest, holds that:
   - Candidate options: a) pupils report preferences; b) teaching matched to a diagnosed style improves learning; c) pupils differ in pace; d) multimodal teaching reaches more pupils.
   - Derivation: The core hypothesis distinguishing the learning-styles doctrine from ordinary common sense is the "matching hypothesis": that instruction tailored to an individual learner's diagnosed style yields superior learning outcomes.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

2. The research verdict on matched teaching is that it shows:
   - Candidate options: a) strong gains for matched pupils; b) little or no gain; c) gains only for visual learners; d) gains only in primary classes.
   - Derivation: Extensive methodological reviews (e.g., Pashler et al., 2008; Rohrer & Pashler, 2012) found virtually no empirical evidence supporting the matching hypothesis.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

3. What survives the styles verdict is:
   - Candidate options: a) the three-style chart; b) pupils differ, and multimodal teaching reaches more of them; c) matching by self-report; d) grouping pupils by style.
   - Derivation: The valid residue that requires no invalid diagnostic apparatus is that individuals possess distinct background knowledge and interests, and multimodal instructional presentation offers multiple entry points into content.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

4. A profile, as this unit uses the word, is:
   - Candidate options: a) a fixed input channel; b) a shape of present levels across domains; c) a self-reported preference; d) a single rank.
   - Derivation: In contrast to a single scalar ranking or an immutable modality channel, a profile represents multidimensional performance levels across different cognitive and practical domains at a given moment in time.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

5. The slow learner's label describes:
   - Candidate options: a) a ceiling on attainment; b) pace, not a ceiling; c) a disability category; d) low intelligence.
   - Derivation: Pedagogically and developmentally, slow learning describes the rate of acquisition and the need for smaller scaffolding steps, not an insurmountable barrier or intellectual ceiling.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

6. Which of these does NOT help the slow learner?
   - Candidate options: a) shorter steps to the same destination; b) time; c) more of the same worksheet faster; d) prior-knowledge checks.
   - Derivation: Increasing speed and volume on repetitive worksheets causes frustration and cognitive overload rather than conceptual mastery. Shorter steps, time, and prior-knowledge activation are supportive.
   - Independent solve: (c).
   - Key: (c). Match.

7. A gifted pupil's support should be mainly:
   - Candidate options: a) acceleration: the same work, sooner; b) enrichment: real problems, choice, honest demand; c) more worksheets at the same level; d) withdrawal from the interesting work.
   - Derivation: Enrichment provides depth, complex problem-solving, and authentic challenge, whereas busywork or premature acceleration fails to nurture genuine talent.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

8. The inclusion principle says the pupil with a disability should:
   - Candidate options: a) attend a separate school with better equipment; b) participate in and contribute to the life of the class as much as possible; c) join the class only for games; d) be taught at home until fully able.
   - Derivation: Inclusion mandates meaningful participation and shared community in general education classrooms to the maximal extent possible, removing barriers rather than segregating learners.
   - Independent solve: (b).
   - Key: (b). Match.

9. A colleague teaches every new idea in three modes - said, shown, done. The evidence supports this because:
   - Candidate options: a) it matches each pupil's diagnosed style; b) it is quicker than teaching in one mode; c) the course guide requires it; d) an idea with more than one route in reaches more pupils.
   - Derivation: Multimodal teaching provides dual/multiple coding and varied representations, facilitating comprehension across a diverse classroom without requiring style diagnostic tagging.
   - Independent solve: (d).
   - Key: (d). Match.

10. Inclusion teaches the classmates:
    - Candidate options: a) nothing; it is for the disabled pupil alone; b) that school is partly about providing opportunities for everyone, not just comparing skills; c) to lower their expectations; d) to compete harder.
    - Derivation: Inclusive settings socialize all students into empathy, mutual responsibility, and an understanding of equity beyond competitive comparison.
    - Independent solve: (b).
    - Key: (b). Match.
