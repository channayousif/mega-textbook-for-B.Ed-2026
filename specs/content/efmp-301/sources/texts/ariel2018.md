# Bound excerpt - ariel2018

Ariel, R., & Karpicke, J. D. (2018). Improving self-regulated learning with a retrieval
practice intervention. In *Proceedings of the 40th Annual Conference of the Cognitive
Science Society*. ERIC ED599268.
https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED599268.pdf

Open-access supplement (ERIC full-text authorized). Retrieved and read directly from the
ERIC repository on 2026-09-25 (14 pp; Purdue University experiments on repeated
retrieval practice). Bound as the carrying source for the retrieval-practice and spacing
evidence behind the study-strategy guidance. Excerpts below are quoted for non-commercial
educational use.

## Retrieval practice over restudying; spacing (verified; used by Unit 4)

On retrieval practice's power and students' misuse of it: "Repeated retrieval practice
is a powerful learning tool for promoting long-term retention, but students use this
tool ineffectively when regulating their learning."

On retrieval versus restudying: instructions in the experiments "emphasized the mnemonic
benefits of retrieval practice over a less effective strategy (restudying)".

On spacing: "other factors exists that also improve the effectiveness of retrieval
practice, including the spacing of retrieval attempts for items within study sessions
(Karpicke & Bauernschmidt, 2011; Karpicke & Roediger, 2007; Pyc & Rawson, 2009)".

On the finding: students given the direct instruction showed "more effective
self-regulated use of repeated retrieval practice and better retention of the
translations compared to a control group", and "spontaneously used repeated retrieval
practice 1 week later to learn new materials".
