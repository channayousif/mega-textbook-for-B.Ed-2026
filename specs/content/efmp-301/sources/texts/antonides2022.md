## Three-stage enactive-iconic-symbolic model (verified; used by Unit 3)

On the model and its origin: "Over half a century has passed since Bruner suggested his
three-stage enactive-iconic-symbolic model of instruction. In more recent research,
predominantly in educational psychology, Bruner's model has been reformulated into the
theory of instruction known as concreteness fading (CF)."

On what the three modes are and their order: "Research suggests one effective means for
introducing abstract symbols and ways of operating on them in ways that are meaningful to
students is the three-stage enactive-iconic-symbolic 'concreteness fading' (CF)
instructional model, originally proposed by Bruner (1966).... Within the CF model, a
concept is first represented using 'concrete' materials on which students may perform
sensory-motor actions, followed by 'iconic' representations which may include graphic or
pictorial forms, and lastly 'symbolic' representations such as words or letters for the
concept."
