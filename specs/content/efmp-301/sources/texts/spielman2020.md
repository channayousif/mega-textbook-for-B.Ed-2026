## Section 5.1 - Sensation versus perception (verified; used by Unit 4)

On the two stages: "When sensory information is detected by a sensory receptor,
sensation has occurred." And: "Perception refers to the way sensory information is
organized, interpreted, and consciously experienced." On attention's role: "Attention
plays a significant role in determining what is sensed versus what is perceived." On
inattentional blindness, the Simons and Chabris (1999) study: while viewers counted
basketball passes, "a person dressed in a black gorilla costume walks among the two
teams", yet "Nearly half of the people who watched the video didn't notice the gorilla
at all."

## Section 8.1 - How memory functions (verified; used by Unit 4)

On the three processes: "Encoding involves the input of information into the memory
system." "Storage is the retention of the encoded information." "The act of getting
information out of memory storage and back into conscious awareness is known as
retrieval." On effort: "It probably required a lot of work and attention on your part
in order to encode that information"; "Active rehearsal is a way of attending to
information to move it from short-term to long-term memory."

On sensory memory: "storage of brief sensory events, such as sights, sounds, and
tastes. It is very brief storage - up to a couple of seconds."

On short-term and working memory: "Short-term memory (STM) is a temporary storage
system that processes incoming sensory memory"; "Short-term memory is more accurately
described as a component of working memory." On capacity: the classic "magic number"
of "7 plus or minus 2" (Miller, 1956), with recent work indicating "working memory
capacity is 4 plus or minus 1" (Cowan, 2010).

On long-term memory: "Long-term memory (LTM) is the continuous storage of information"
with a storage capacity "believed to be unlimited."
