## The learning-styles evidence (verified; used by Unit 5)

On the claim and its reach: "the wide acceptance of the idea that instruction should be
tailored to a student's so called learning style.... The visual-verbal distinction is
only one simple example of the many proposed taxonomies; a recent review described 71
different schemes."

On the evidence: "as we and others have pointed out, a thoughtful review of the data
provides no support for style-based instruction."

On the design needed to support the claim: "only a small portion of these studies use
the only research design capable of supporting the idea that customized instruction
produces better learning than using the same kind of instruction for everyone" - that
design requires dividing learners by assessed style, randomly assigning each to matched
or unmatched instruction, and giving all the same test.
