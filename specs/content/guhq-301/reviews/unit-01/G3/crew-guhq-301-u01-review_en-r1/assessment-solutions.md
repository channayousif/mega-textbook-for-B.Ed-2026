## RRQs (independent answer, then comparison)

1. Memorising preserves the sounds; understanding knows what the words say and ask. A child can
   recite without knowing a word. 12:2 and 38:29 ask for both. Matches the model answer.
2. Any two of guidance (2:2), prayer (1:5), reflection (38:29, 47:24) and teaching (pupils ask).
   Matches.
3. *Ṣalāh*, *zakāh* or *ṣawm* gained exact worship meanings; or *kutiba* (writing) means "decreed"
   in 2:183. Matches.
4. Not opposites: Quranic Arabic is part of Classical Arabic, its central text; shared words and
   images get a new purpose. Matches.
5. In poetry the ruined campsite (*aṭlāl*) wakes longing and the passing of time; in the Qur'an
   ruined peoples are signs that ask for reflection and a change of life. Matches, and Yazar and
   Batur (2025) support the contrast.
6. ق and ک are separate Arabic sounds; merging them can turn *qulūb* (hearts, 2:10) into *kalb*
   (dog, 18:18). Matches.
7. Root: ك ت ب, writing. Pattern: *fāʿil*, the one who does it, giving *kātib*. Matches.
8. *ʿĀlim*: root ع ل م (knowing), pattern *fāʿil*, so "one who knows". Matches.
9. *yā* (O), *ayyuhā* (you), *alladhīna* (who), *āmanū* (they believed); root ء م ن; Urdu *īmān*.
   Matches.
10. A root gives a core idea, but *kutiba* moves from "writing" to "decreed"; the verse decides.
    Matches.
