# Contemporary Literacies

EFMP-406 is a Semester 3 Major: Professional course. It develops understanding of literacy beyond reading and writing, including critical, digital, media, visual and information literacies. Learners examine how literacy works in social and cultural settings and consider teaching for diverse classrooms.

## Course learning outcomes

From the course guide, by the end of the course learners should be able to:

1. Define and explain the concept and scope of contemporary literacy.
2. Differentiate between traditional and modern perspectives of literacy.
3. Analyze the role of literacy in social, cultural, and digital contexts.
4. Apply contemporary literacy teaching strategies in classroom settings.
5. Design literacy activities that promote critical thinking and learner engagement.

## Units

1. Concept and Evolution of Literacy
2. [Contemporary Forms of Literacy](./unit-02/)
3. Literacy, Language, and Society
4. Literacy in the Digital Era
5. Pedagogical Approaches to Contemporary Literacy
6. Issues, Challenges, and Future Directions

## Teaching strategies and practical work

The guide names group discussions, seminars, role-plays, simulations, case study analysis, visits to inclusive schools, reflection journals and portfolios. It also lists lecture, discussions, question-answer and brainstorming. Practical work includes group work, group assignments, individual assignments and presentations.

## Recommended resources

The guide lists Booth and Ainscow's *Index for Inclusion*, Mittler's *Working Towards Inclusive Education*, Loreman, Deppeler and Harvey's *Inclusive Education: Supporting Diversity in the Classroom*, UNESCO's *A Guide for Ensuring Inclusion and Equity in Education* (2017), and the Government of Pakistan's *National Education Policy Framework* (2018). Some printed years in the guide do not resolve to verified editions; the approved reading list records those limits. These inclusion readings do not supply literacy claims in Units 1–5.

Unit 2 uses Minott's critical literacy study (2011), Alcolea-Díaz, Reig and Mancinas-Chávez's media literacy analysis (2020), and accessible Jisc and UNESCO frameworks. The unit's further-reading sections provide links and explain their scope. Recommended works are cited, not reproduced.
