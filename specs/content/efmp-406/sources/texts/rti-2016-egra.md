# Bound excerpt - rti-2016-egra

RTI International. (2016). *Early Grade Reading Assessment (EGRA) toolkit* (2nd ed.). Washington, DC: United States Agency for International Development.
https://shared.rti.org/node/257

Open access. The full PDF (https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/ierc-v2-publicfiles/public/public/resources/EGRA%20Toolkit%20V2%202016.pdf, linked from the RTI page above) was retrieved on 2026-11-02 and the text of its first 40 pages extracted. The passages below are copied from that extraction.

## Section 1, pp. 5-6: types of assessment (used by Unit 5, U5-04)

"The continuum is broken into three broad categories: examinations, assessment surveys, and classroom assessments. Kanjee (2009) defines examinations as processes used for testing the qualifications of candidates (e.g., quarterly exams, promotion exams, and matriculation exams)."

"At the other end of the spectrum are classroom assessments, which are defined as measures used to obtain evidence on knowledge, skills, and attitudes of individual learners for the purpose of informing and improving teaching and learning (Kanjee, 2009)."

"By design, classroom assessments are intended to be inexpensive, to take less time, and to involve lower stakes (particularly when compared with examinations)."

"RTI developed a protocol for an individual oral assessment of students' foundational reading skills."

## Contents, pp. v-vi: instrument components (used by Unit 5, U5-04)

Listed components include Listening Comprehension; Letter Identification; Nonword Reading; Oral Reading Fluency with Comprehension; Phonological Awareness; Familiar Word Reading; and, as additional components, Dictation, Phoneme Segmentation, and Maze and Cloze.

## pp. 15-16: uses and limits (used by Unit 5, U5-04)

"\"the subtasks included in EGRA can be adapted for teachers to inform their instruction. As a formative assessment, teachers can either use EGRA in its entirety or select subtasks to monitor classroom progress, determine trends in performance, and adapt instruction to meet children's instructional needs\" (Dubeck & Gove, 2015, p. 2)."

"It is not intended to be a high-stakes accountability measure to determine student grade promotion or to evaluate individual teachers. EGRA is designed to complement, rather than replace, existing curriculum-based pencil-and-paper assessments."

"neither EGRA nor any other single instrument is capable of measuring all skills required for students to read with comprehension."

## How Unit 5 uses it

Topic 5.2 paraphrases the assessment continuum, the classroom-assessment purpose, EGRA's oral, one-child-at-a-time design and four of its subtasks, and its stated limits, to correct the "a written test is the only way to assess reading" misconception. The reading-record procedure in Topic 5.2 is general classroom practice, not an EGRA subtask, and the unit does not present it as one.
