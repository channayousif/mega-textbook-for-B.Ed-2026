# Bound excerpt - ashraf-2021-multilingual

Ashraf, M. A., Turner, D. A., & Laar, R. A. (2021). Multilingual language practices in education in Pakistan: The conflict between policy and practice. *SAGE Open*, 11(1). CC BY 4.0. https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211004140

Open-access research article under CC BY 4.0 license. Excerpted for academic analysis and curriculum alignment.

## Multilingual landscape and the hierarchy of languages in Pakistan (used by U3-03, U3-04)

From Section 1 & 2 (pages 1-4):
"Pakistan is a deeply multilingual federation where linguistic diversity reflects rich regional and ethnic identities. Over seventy languages are spoken across the country. In the formal educational and political system, however, a three-tier linguistic hierarchy operates. English occupies the apex as the language of elite power, bureaucracy, higher education, and international commerce. Urdu serves as the national language and unifying lingua franca across provinces. Regional and local mother tongues - including Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi, Balochi, Saraiki, and others - represent the lived languages and identity foundations of the majority of the population, yet their institutional status in schooling remains uneven and contested."

## Language, citizenship, and educational equity (used by U3-03)

From Section 4 (pages 5-8):
"Language policies in education are directly linked to citizenship, democratic participation, and social justice. When the language of schooling is completely detached from the language of the child's home and community, literacy acquisition becomes a formidable barrier rather than an empowering tool. Democratic citizenship presupposes that citizens can access legal rights, understand public policies, evaluate political debates, and voice their needs to state authorities. When educational systems restrict literacy development exclusively to dominant languages without pedagogical support, they inadvertently disenfranchise linguistic minorities and entrench socio-economic stratification."

## Policy-practice contradiction and classroom multilingualism (used by U3-04)

From Section 5 & 6 (pages 8-12):
"Our empirical investigation reveals a pronounced gap between official language-in-education policies and everyday classroom practices. While official curriculum documents often prescribe strict English-only or Urdu-only medium of instruction, teachers and students in Pakistani schools routinely deploy translanguaging - drawing flexibly upon their full linguistic repertoire (regional mother tongues, Urdu, and English) to explain difficult concepts, maintain learner engagement, and scaffold comprehension. Far from being a sign of pedagogical failure or cognitive confusion, multilingual classroom interaction is an adaptive, functional strategy that bridges unfamiliar academic registers with learners' prior linguistic knowledge. Educational policy must move away from deficit views of mother-tongue speakers and embrace additive multilingualism to foster equitable literacy development."
