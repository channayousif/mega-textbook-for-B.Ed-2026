## About the study (scope and limits)

Section "Data Collection": "Data for this study were collected from January to August 2017 through semi-structured interviews with 24 school teachers (n = 6 from each of the four provinces), two university teachers, and three policymakers from three different policymaking institutions. Data were also collected through unstructured observations in the institutions of all the participants and the study of national education policy documents."

The study is a small qualitative interview study. It reports what teachers and policymakers said and
did; it does not test teaching methods or measure pupils' reading, comprehension or dropout.

## Roles of English, Urdu and local languages (used by U3-04)

Introduction: "This study adopted discourse-ethnographic methods to examine how stakeholders including teachers and policymakers in Pakistan negotiate language choices by challenging local and national ideologies in educational settings where English is the dominant language, Urdu the national language, and local languages most accurately signify the speaker's identity."

Section "Language Pluralism and Language Ideologies in Pakistan": "Pakistan is a multilingual and multicultural country with speakers of 77 languages (Eberhard et al., 2020). While each province is linked to a specific ethnic group that has its own language and culture, English is the language of bureaucracy together with Urdu, the national language. The Urdu language is generally used for communication and as a lingua franca between different ethnic groups."

Same section: "Vernacular-medium schools include Urdu-medium, Sindhi-medium, and Pashto-medium."

Conclusion: "National identity is linked to Urdu, whereas individual identity is linked to a local language, global identity to English, and religious identity to Arabic."

## Policy and practice (used by U3-03, U3-04)

Section "Conflict in Language Policy, Planning, and Practice": "In the context of policy, the government's language policy is highlighted in the National Education Policy (2009), which states that the Ministry of Education aims to develop a comprehensive plan of action for implementing English language policy in the shortest possible time, in consultation with the provincial and regional education departments."

Same section: "in most offices, staff communicate in Urdu, or in local languages if both speakers belong to the same language group, but official documents are released in English."

Abstract: "Moreover, the power of national education policy to produce adequate results is limited by the regional discourses that policymakers ignore."

## Language practices in classrooms (used by U3-04)

Section "Language Practices in Teaching": "Outside the classroom, students mostly use local languages with their classmates, friends, and families. English is used in the classroom only during learning and memorizing words. It was difficult for teachers to teach primary school students science and math in English because students could not even recognize the English alphabet."

Same section: "However, public schools do not have such rules regarding language use at school, nor ask parents to practice any particular language at home. Nor do teachers have any rules regarding speaking a specific language at school. Most teachers revealed that they often communicate with their colleagues and students in local languages or in Urdu."

Same section: "Participants explained that local languages remain the dominant form of communication among people in rural areas, and students from rural areas face extra problems during language interactions at school as well as in the curriculum."

Discussion: "However, the teaching practices in all sectors of education were similar, transferring knowledge of English through national or local languages."

Conclusion: "teaching practices in school and classrooms should be measured carefully, before and after policymaking process, as teachers are important mediators of classroom-level language policy."

## What this source does not support

The article does not evaluate mother-tongue-based multilingual education, translanguaging, additive
or subtractive bilingualism, or effects on reading scores, anxiety or dropout. Topic 3.2 presents
those ideas as this textbook's explanation of terms used in language education, qualified as
positions to test, and cites Ashraf et al. (2021) only for the observations quoted above.
