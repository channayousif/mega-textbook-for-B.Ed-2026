## Evidence notes

1. Seminar Weeks 1-4 (pp. 8-14): Classroom observation is defined as a purposeful, systematic inquiry into teaching and learning. It serves to connect theoretical pedagogical knowledge with actual school realities. It emphasizes developmental learning rather than punitive evaluation.
2. Roles of participants (pp. 12-16): Defines the distinct roles of the university supervisor (academic oversight, standards verification, course alignment), the cooperating school mentor (daily contextual guidance, classroom modeling, supportive mentoring), and the student-teacher observer (respectful, non-intrusive factual recording and professional learning).
3. Professional ethics during practicum (pp. 17-21): Specifies core ethical requirements: obtaining administrative permission, maintaining confidentiality, protecting student anonymity, demonstrating cultural and professional respect, and avoiding classroom disruption.
4. Observation instruments and proformas (pp. 24-32): Introduces standard observation tools including structured checklists for classroom routines, descriptive rating scales, episodic anecdotal records, and personal reflective journals.
5. The observation cycle (pp. 33-38): Outlines the four sequential stages of clinical observation: pre-observation briefing to establish focus and build trust, classroom observation and data collection, post-observation reflection, and collaborative feedback debriefing.
6. Feedback and reflection (pp. 39-45): Details the principles of constructive, evidence-based feedback. Explains how student-teachers analyze observational findings to identify patterns and translate insights into personal targets for self-improvement.
