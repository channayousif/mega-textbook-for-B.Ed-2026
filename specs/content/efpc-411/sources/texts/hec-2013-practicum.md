Consulted: 2026-11-26; notes rewritten 2026-11-28 from the official PDF (44 pages; printed page numbers match PDF pages).

## Evidence notes

1. What the practicum asks of observation (pp. 9-10, 18, 30): The school placement starts with observation. Student Teachers initially conduct formal observations (p. 9), the seminar links pre-service programme content to classroom practice (p. 10), time spent observing varies with the situation (p. 18), and seminar assignments include presenting an analysis of one's own or a peer's teaching and conducting focused observations (pp. 10, 30). Peer coaching, and coaching by the Cooperating Teacher and the Practicum Supervisor, are provided for (pp. 10, 30). The handbook gives no formal definition of classroom observation.
2. Roles of the practicum triad (pp. 17-19, 36-38): The Student Teacher, the Cooperating Teacher and the College/University Practicum Supervisor form the triad. The role summaries are on pp. 18-19 (semester 3) and pp. 36-38 (semester 4). The Cooperating Teacher guides daily work and observes formally and informally, giving feedback. The Supervisor represents the institution, makes observation visits, confers with the Student Teacher before and after observations, holds three-way conferences and gives feedback. The Student Teacher takes part actively, behaves professionally as a member of school staff, reflects and documents professional growth.
3. Observation foci and school-based assignments (pp. 12-14, 23-26, 32-33): Weekly tasks direct observation to the classroom environment, classroom interactions, small-group and individual engagement, classroom management, movement, on-task and off-task behaviour, verbal behaviour, lesson pacing and questioning. School assignments include an inventory of school resources, discipline procedures, school policy, routines and community involvement.
4. Observation instruments and records (pp. 11, 23, 26, 31-33): The handbook names a Student Teaching Checklist, daily logs and reflection forms, reflective journals and selective verbatim recording of classroom talk (p. 33). Rating scales and anecdotal records are not among the instruments this handbook sets out.
5. Formal observation (pp. 19, 33-34, 38): Formal observations are prepared with a Formal Observation Cover sheet, a Pre-observation Guide and a Post-observation Reflection (pp. 33-34), and feedback follows from the Cooperating Teacher or Supervisor (pp. 19, 38). The handbook does not itself set out a numbered four-stage cycle; the four stages in the unit are a synthesis of these elements.
6. Feedback, reflection and growth (pp. 11, 18, 31, 34): Course outcomes ask Student Teachers to invite, accept and use feedback from the Cooperating Teacher, peers and Supervisor in a non-defensive manner (pp. 11, 31). Continued reflection and a developmental portfolio serve as evidence of professional growth (p. 18); a professional portfolio addresses the National Professional Standards for Teachers (p. 34). The handbook does not set out a feedback protocol or a method for setting personal targets.

## Limits

The handbook contains no guidance on research or observation ethics: it does not treat permission, consent, confidentiality, anonymity or privacy. It is not evidence for those points.
