# Bound source evidence: scoupe-2024

Source: Scoupe, R., Delnoij, L., & Beausaert, S. (2024). Reflective practice among pre-service teachers: The role of portfolios. *Educational Research*, 66(4), 433-453. https://doi.org/10.1080/00131881.2024.2412642
Registry: Crossref-verified open access (CC BY 4.0), consulted 2026-11-27.

## Evidence notes

1. Portfolio as a reflective tool (pp. 434-438): Empirically investigates how student-teacher portfolios serve as catalysts for critical reflection. Shows that unstructured dossier filing results in shallow descriptive reporting, whereas guided portfolio frameworks with structured reflection prompts significantly increase critical reflection and pedagogical self-regulation.
2. Levels of reflection in portfolio entries (pp. 439-444): Distinguishes three developmental tiers of reflection: (a) descriptive reporting (stating what occurred without rationale), (b) pedagogical explanation (linking classroom events to teaching strategies and pupil responses), and (c) critical/transformative reflection (evaluating assumptions, ethics, inclusivity, and formulating future instructional adjustments).
3. Linking reflection to goal setting (pp. 445-450): Demonstrates that student-teachers who regularly synthesize observation evidence into explicit action plans show measurably greater competence in classroom adaptation during field placement.
