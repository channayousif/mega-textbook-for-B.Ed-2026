# Bound source evidence: richards-lockhart-1994

Source: Richards, J. C., & Lockhart, C. (1994). *Reflective teaching in second language classrooms*. Cambridge University Press.
ISBN: 9780521451987 / OL15053472W.

Consulted: 2026-11-27, examined for reflective teaching frameworks, journal writing, and post-observation inquiry.

## Evidence notes

1. Reflective practice overview (pp. 1-8): Defines reflective teaching as a cyclical process in which teachers systematically collect data about their teaching, examine their beliefs and instructional decisions, and use these critical insights to evaluate and adapt future classroom practice. Emphasizes that unexamined experience leads to repetitive habit rather than professional growth.
2. Written reflection and journals (pp. 6-12): Explains the pedagogical function of structured reflective writing. Distinguishes purely descriptive records ("what happened") from analytical and evaluative reflection ("why it happened, how students reacted, and what alternatives existed").
3. Questioning assumptions about classroom dynamics (pp. 138-145): Provides structured prompts for analyzing classroom interactions, pupil participation rates, and management strategies. Highlights how teacher beliefs directly shape classroom climate and rules.
4. Action planning and pedagogical transformation (pp. 200-208): Outlines how teachers translate diagnostic insights from observation notes and reflections into concrete, targeted action plans for pedagogical refinement.
