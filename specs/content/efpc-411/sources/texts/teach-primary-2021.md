## Evidence notes

1. Observation protocol and observer conduct (p. 12): Before the lesson the observer introduces themself to the principal and teacher, explains the purpose, brings consent forms and reminds the teacher that the observation is confidential and not an evaluation, with the teacher's identity kept confidential. A teacher cannot be forced to be observed; if consent is still declined the observer leaves. During the lesson the observer sits towards the back without blocking pupils' view, does not interact or take part, avoids distraction and keeps a neutral manner. Afterwards the observer thanks the teacher, does not discuss scores and does not talk about the lesson in a joking or disrespectful way.
2. Note-taking (p. 13): Notes record what the teacher says, with specific behaviours, questions, instructions and actions, as descriptively as possible, so that every score can be justified with evidence. Techniques given are scripting (quotes), tallies, shorthand and anecdotes. The manual does not present "high-inference" and "low-inference" notes as two kinds of record.
3. What is observed (pp. 6-7; element pages from p. 23): Time on task, and teaching practices grouped as classroom culture, instruction and socioemotional skills (nine elements, 28 behaviours), including lesson facilitation, checks for understanding, feedback to students and critical thinking.
4. Rating (pp. 14-15): Observers give each behaviour a low, medium or high quality rating against written descriptions, then score each element from 1 to 5 on the evidence collected.
5. Threats to objectivity (pp. 15-16): Personal experiences, additional information about the teacher or pupils, comparison between observations, failure to separate elements, weighing of specific events or first impressions, and central tendency. The manual does not use the terms halo effect, leniency or severity.
6. Use of observation for feedback (p. 3): The foreword reports that in Punjab pedagogical leaders use an adapted version of the tool to give teachers tailored feedback. The manual sets out no procedure for aggregating tallies, analysing trends or holding a feedback conference; its element on feedback concerns the teacher's feedback to pupils.
