## Evidence notes (paraphrased; short phrases quoted)

1. Pp. 5-6 ("The unseen factor of production"): land, labour and capital produce nothing until they are directed by "an entrepreneurial mind"; entrepreneurship is "the unseen factor of production". The word comes from the French *entreprendre*, to do or undertake something. Cantillon (1730) used it for someone taking a financial risk in business; Mill (1848) for those who assume both the risk and the management of a business; today economists stress innovation, spotting opportunities and taking risks under uncertainty.
2. P. 6 ("Innovation and economic growth"): in a competitive economy entrepreneurs face constant pressure to find cheaper, better products; that pressure to raise productivity explains most of the rise in living standards.
3. Pp. 13-14: entrepreneurs commit effort, capital and other resources while facing deep uncertainty about future customers; Hayek's view of competition as an active, ongoing process in which entrepreneurs play a vital part.
4. P. 23: commonly named traits include creativity, alertness to opportunities, commitment, confidence, experience, the ability to multitask and willingness to take risks (citing Foss and Klein, 2010). Not all entrepreneurs have all these traits, nor in equal measure; there are brilliant innovators who are poor managers and inspiring managers who misread the market.
5. P. 34: experience may count for more than formal education.

Use in GENT-401 Unit 1: notes 1-3 support the meaning of entrepreneurship and its importance in economic development (Topic 1.1) and uncertainty in risk-taking (Topic 1.2); note 4 supports the characteristics, the "born or made" correction and the entrepreneurial versus managerial contrast; note 5 supports the "made, not born" correction.
