## 2. Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs 1-10)

All RRQ stems were solved independently and compared against the mark schemes in `unit-assessment.mdx`:

- **RRQ-01 (Understand)**: Contrast small business vs scalable start-up with Pakistani examples. Solved: Small business aims for local steady livelihood (e.g. general store in Thatta); start-up aims for rapid multi-market expansion with external financing (e.g. phone repair chain). Supplied mark scheme (4 marks) perfectly reflects this contrast.
- **RRQ-02 (Understand)**: Two differences between social enterprise and charity. Solved: Social enterprise earns commercial revenue and aims for financial self-sufficiency; charities depend on philanthropy/donations and cannot distribute profits. Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-03 (Understand)**: Refuting "small business is failed start-up". Solved: Small business is an intentional choice for community stability and sustainable livelihood, not an unsuccessful attempt at rapid scaling. Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-04 (Analyze)**: Classifying the teacher skills club. Solved: Primarily intrapreneurship (operates within host school with head teacher approval); also exhibits social enterprise characteristics (social goal with cost-recovery fees). Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-05 (Understand)**: Two barriers for women entrepreneurs in Pakistan. Solved: Sociocultural mobility constraints requiring familial consent; restricted access to formal banking/credit. Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-06 (Understand)**: Definition of opportunity + non-invention example. Solved: Point where real customer demand meets practical delivery feasibility; example is a ready-made school uniform shop in an underserved town. Matches model answer (3 marks).
- **RRQ-07 (Understand)**: Two sources of business opportunities. Solved: Solving everyday problems (e.g. shared student transport); customer feedback (e.g. custom alterations requested from a tailor). Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-08 (Analyze)**: Why "great idea = opportunity" is false. Solved: An idea must be validated against customer willingness to pay (demand) and delivery economics (feasibility). Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-09 (Understand)**: Brainstorming vs crowdsourcing. Solved: Brainstorming is uncritical generation within a small collaborative group; crowdsourcing solicits solutions from a broad external/online public. Matches model answer (4 marks).
- **RRQ-10 (Analyze)**: Most critical screening criterion in small town. Solved: Market demand, because without paying local customers, operational feasibility and margins cannot sustain the business. Matches model answer (4 marks).
