## RRQ derived answers

1. Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying a problem or opportunity others
   have not acted on, acting on it, and accepting the risk. Example: a retired
   teacher writing and selling low-cost Sindhi worksheet packs.
2. A small business owner follows an existing model (a fourth identical shop); an
   entrepreneur creates something new. They overlap when an owner brings a product
   or service to an area where nobody offered it before.
3. Jobs (two apprentices employed), community welfare (affordable desks for village
   schools), innovation (recycled wood used in a new way). Income or competition
   accepted with a correct explanation.
4. Competitive pressure forces entrepreneurs to find cheaper, better, more useful
   products; each improvement raises the value obtained from the same resources;
   accumulated over time this explains most of the long-run rise in living
   standards (Butler).
5. Alertness to opportunity (noticed the lack of safe transport); problem-solving
   and persistence (found another driver within a week). Creativity accepted.
6. Butler: no entrepreneur has all the characteristics in equal measure and
   experience can count for more than formal education. Saeed et al.: entrepreneurship
   education raised Pakistani students' intentions. OpenStax: anyone can learn to
   notice opportunities.
7. Creativity = an original idea; innovation = an idea put to use that adds value;
   invention = a truly new product, service or process. School examples as in the
   model answer.
8. Notice (pupils forget diaries); ask questions (why, which pupils); generate ideas
   (gate check, monitor, hook); test (one class, two weeks); improve (extend or try
   another idea).
9. Starting small spends little to test demand, so the loss is small and the learning
   is real; example: selling 20 rolls on two mornings.
10. Entrepreneurial thinking finds the opportunity; managerial thinking plans, keeps
    records and controls costs as the venture grows; only one kind risks disorder or
    stagnation; example: a roll seller expanding to three schools.
