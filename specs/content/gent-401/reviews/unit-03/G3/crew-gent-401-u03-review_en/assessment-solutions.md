## Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. **Q:** Define market research in one sentence, and explain why it is not the same as asking friends whether they like your idea. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Market research is the systematic collection and analysis of information about the target market and potential buyers to understand their needs and willingness to pay. It is not the same as asking friends because friends are a tiny, biased convenience sample who want to be encouraging rather than honest, and who are rarely paying customers.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark for definition naming data about potential customers; 1 mark for difference from friends; 1 mark for reason (small/biased sample). Fully aligns.
2. **Q:** Explain the difference between primary and secondary data, and give one example of each. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Primary data is firsthand information collected directly by the researcher for their specific purpose (e.g. surveying 40 Class 9 pupils about physics difficulty). Secondary data is pre-existing information collected by someone else for another purpose (e.g. government district school enrolment figures).
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark per definition; 1 mark for each fitting example (max 3 marks). Fully aligns.
3. **Q:** A trainee wants to sell mathematics practice sheets to Class 8 pupils. Describe two data-collection methods she could use, one primary and one secondary, and say what each would reveal. *(Apply, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Primary: A questionnaire distributed to Class 8 pupils at school, revealing which mathematical topics they find hardest and how much pocket money they would pay per sheet. Secondary: School examination results or education board reports, revealing overall pass rates and common area-wide weaknesses in mathematics.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark for primary method; 1 mark for what it reveals; 1 mark for secondary source; 1 mark for what it reveals. Fully aligns.
4. **Q:** Read this case: "Sadiqa asked her five classmates whether they would buy her handmade greeting cards. All five said yes. She ordered 200 cards." Analyse two weaknesses in her evidence. *(Analyze, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** (1) Severe sampling bias: 5 classmates form an unrepresentatively small sample whose social ties incline them to give polite, affirmative responses. (2) Lack of demand and pricing validation: Stating interest verbally does not test actual willingness to pay money or establish repeat purchase frequency, creating high financial risk for an inventory of 200 cards.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 2 marks per weakness with reasoning. Fully aligns.
5. **Q:** Analyse why a market with three small competitors may be a better opportunity than a market with none. *(Analyze, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** The presence of three small competitors provides empirical proof that a paying market exists and customers have validated the basic need. An empty market often signals that the need is non-viable, demand is too small, or previous ventures failed. Furthermore, small rivals leave clear opportunities for differentiation (e.g. local language support or better customer service).
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for competitors proving demand; 1 for room to differentiate; 1 for empty market risk; 1 for reason. Fully aligns.
6. **Q:** Explain, in three sentences, the difference between a business model and a business plan. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** A business model is an iterative, conceptual architecture showing how a venture creates, delivers, and captures value, designed to be tested and adapted as customer evidence arrives. A business plan is a formal, detailed written document (often 20 or more pages) detailing marketing, operations, management, and financial projections for external audiences like banks. The model is the underlying workable strategy, whereas the plan is the formal document detailing its execution.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for each definition; 1 for distinction. Fully aligns.
7. **Q:** A classmate says, "A value proposition is just a slogan." Explain two differences between a slogan and a value proposition. *(Understand, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** (1) Purpose: A slogan is a marketing phrase designed to be memorable and build brand awareness, whereas a value proposition is a testable operational statement of the concrete benefits, problems solved, and gains offered to customers. (2) Specificity: A slogan does not need to name the customer's specific job to be done or differentiate against rivals, whereas a value proposition must define the exact job and why the offering is superior to alternatives.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 2 marks per difference (1 for statement, 1 for reasoning/example). Fully aligns.
8. **Q:** A trainee in Hyderabad runs an evening mathematics club for Class 9 pupils. Fill in four blocks of the business-model canvas for that club. *(Apply, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Customer Segments: Class 9 pupils in government schools needing exam support, and their parents. Value Proposition: Structured evening problem-solving practice helping pupils pass board math without paying high home-tutor fees. Channels: School gate announcements and local parent WhatsApp groups. Revenue Streams: Monthly fee of 300 rupees per pupil plus sale of printed revision booklets.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark per sensible block. Fully aligns.
9. **Q:** Analyse why a venture with five revenue streams may serve its customers worse than one with two. *(Analyze, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Managing five revenue streams fragments the founder's time, operational focus, and scarce resources across multiple half-built mechanisms. As a result, none of the streams delivers high quality or reliable customer service. Focusing on one or two fitting streams allows the venture to refine its core offering, build customer loyalty, and establish operational stability.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for energy split; 1 for weak delivery risk; 1 for fit with customer job; 1 for reasoning/example. Fully aligns.
10. **Q:** Read this case: "A tuition centre charges low fees and gets many pupils, but the owner earns little." Analyse which model choice the centre made, and what evidence would tell the owner whether to change. *(Analyze, 5 marks)*
    - **Derived:** The centre chose sales volume over profit margin, competing on low price. The warning signal is high enrolment without net profit, indicating variable and fixed costs per pupil match or exceed tuition revenue. Evidence required: the exact unit cost per pupil (rent, electricity, teacher salaries, photocopying) compared against fees collected, alongside a competitor price and quality audit to evaluate whether a modest price increase would retain pupils while restoring profitability.
    - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for volume over margin; 1 for warning sign; 1 for cost-per-pupil evidence; 1 for rival comparison; 1 for reasoning. Fully aligns.

## Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **ERQ 1 (Evaluate, 12 marks):** Hina's research vs friends' approval.
   - Solved: Evaluates customer targeting (Class 9-10 government girls vs 3 friends), primary data (interviewing 40 pupils, 2 teachers) and secondary data (counting stationery shop inventory/prices), and triangulation. Reasoned judgement concludes empirical inquiry prevented fatal misallocation of capital on unwanted kits and correctly pivoted the venture to affordable practice sheets. Rubric criteria (Target customer, Primary/secondary data, Triangulation, Judgement) completely met.
2. **ERQ 2 (Analyze, 12 marks):** Researching customers and competitors for Shikarpur learning materials venture.
   - Solved: Analyses customer identification, direct/indirect competitors (bazaar bookshops vs free digital resources), and identifies customer willingness-to-pay validation as the single most critical research step before spending money. Rubric criteria (Customer research, Competitor analysis, Analysis of most important step, Judgement) completely met.
3. **ERQ 3 (Create, 12 marks):** Venture from survey data (60 surveyed, 42 struggle with numericals, 35 would pay Rs 30-50, 28 buy at bazaar).
   - Solved: Creates value proposition: "Weekly step-by-step physics numericals practice sheets in Sindhi and Urdu, helping Class 9 pupils pass board exams for 40 rupees a week." Proposes two revenue streams: (1) Direct sale of weekly printed sheets at Rs 40; (2) End-of-term bundled exam revision pack at Rs 120. Links directly to survey figures and Class 9 government school reality. Rubric criteria (Value proposition, Two revenue streams, Use of survey data, Fit to pupils) completely met.
4. **ERQ 4 (Evaluate, 12 marks):** "A business model is only a plan for making money."
   - Solved: Evaluates statement using the nine BMC blocks, demonstrating that making money (Revenue Streams and Cost Structure) represents only two blocks, while the other seven focus on creating and delivering customer value. Distinguishes model from business plan. Rubric criteria (Nine-block canvas, Model vs plan, Evaluation of claim, Consequence for venture) completely met.
5. **ERQ 5 (Analyze, 12 marks):** Bilal's science club (Imran's fee, Sajid's kit rental, Uncle's business plan).
   - Solved: Integrates Topic 3.1 and 3.2 concepts. Analyses Imran's monthly fee as a direct revenue stream depending on parental willingness to pay; Sajid's kit rental as a B2B model depending on bureaucratic school approval; and the Uncle's business plan as a premature formalisation before market validation. Concludes with an optimal combination: pilot with monthly parent fee and student worksheets, validate with a small customer sample, and document using a 1-page canvas before seeking external funding. Rubric criteria (Fee analysis, Kit analysis, Plan analysis, Judgement on combination) completely met.
