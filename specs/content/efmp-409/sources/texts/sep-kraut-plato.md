## Evidence notes

1. Theory of Forms and Ideas: Plato distinguishes between the changing realm of physical sensible particulars and the eternal, unchanging realm of Forms (Ideas), accessible only through pure reason and intellect. The Form of the Good is the highest object of knowledge. Supports U3-01 (metaphysics and epistemology of idealism).
2. Socratic dialogue and dialectic: True learning is not the pouring of information into an empty vessel, but the awakening of latent knowledge through rigorous questioning and dialectical examination. Supports U3-02 (Socratic method and dialectic).
3. Educational curriculum in *The Republic*: Plato outlines a sequential, state-regulated curriculum designed to cultivate justice in the individual soul and the city. Early education balances gymnastics (physical health and discipline) and music/poetry (moral harmony and cultural values). Higher education progresses to arithmetic, plane and solid geometry, astronomy, and harmony, culminating in dialectic philosophy (ages 30-35) and civic governance (ages 35-50) to produce wise guardians and philosopher-rulers. Supports U3-02 and U3-03.
4. Role of the teacher and aims of education: The teacher serves as a guide who turns the soul around towards the light of truth (Allegory of the Cave). Education aims at the harmonious development of the soul's three parts (appetite, spirit, reason) and the realization of justice. Supports U3-03.
