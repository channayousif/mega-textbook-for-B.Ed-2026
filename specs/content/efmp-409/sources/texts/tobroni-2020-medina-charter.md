## Evidence notes

1. Abstract: the article examines whether the Medina Charter obliges Muslims to establish an Islamic state and compares its human-rights spirit with Indonesia's constitution. It argues the Charter was a legal product of the Prophet's era, not a mandatory state model, and describes it as a symbol of peace, tolerance and moderation. Supports the reading of the Charter as a plural constitutional agreement rather than only a military treaty (U4-04).
