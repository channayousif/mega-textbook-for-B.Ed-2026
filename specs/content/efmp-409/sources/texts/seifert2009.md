## Evidence notes

1. Book and licence: *Educational Psychology*, Second Edition, by Kelvin Seifert and Rosemary Sutton, published by The Global Text Project, licensed CC BY 3.0. Supports the citation line (U2-03).
2. Chapter 2, "Behaviorism: changes in what students do": behaviorism is "a perspective on learning that focuses on changes in individuals' observable behaviors"; it is most useful for identifying relationships between a student's actions and their consequences. Supports the behaviourist perspective (U2-03).
3. Chapter 2, B. F. Skinner: animals and people repeat actions that are rewarded; the chapter describes operant conditioning and reinforcement. Supports the Skinner example (U2-03).
4. Chapter 2, "Constructivism: changes in how students think": constructivism is "a perspective on learning focused on how students actively create (or 'construct') knowledge out of experiences". Supports the constructivist perspective (U2-03).
5. Chapter 2, Piaget: learning is the interplay of assimilation and accommodation, and the mental representations are called schemas. Supports the assimilation, accommodation and schema terms (U2-03).
6. Chapter 2, Vygotsky: the difference between solo performance and assisted performance is the zone of proximal development. Supports the zone of proximal development (U2-03).
7. Chapter 6, "Motivation as self-efficacy": self-efficacy is "the belief that you are capable of" a task, and it shapes task choice and persistence; Bandura is cited (1977, 1986, 1997). Supports the self-efficacy claim (U2-03).
8. Chapter 6, Maslow: a hierarchy of motives and needs, from deficit needs to being needs and self-actualization, the "desire for fulfillment of one's potential". Supports the humanist use of Maslow (U2-03).
