## Evidence notes

1. Philosophy of *Khudi* (Selfhood/Individuality): Iqbal defines *Khudi* not as selfish arrogance, but as the active, conscious realization of the human self in relationship with God and society. Education must nurture, strengthen, and fortify the individuality of the learner rather than extinguishing it in passive conformity. Supports U3-14 (Iqbal as an educationist).
2. Creative action and dynamic struggle: Education is fundamentally dynamic. Life is a continuous creative struggle (*tag-o-dou*); passive fatalism is rejected. Verse from *Bang-e-Dra*: "Aml se zindagi banti hai jannat bhi jahannum bhi / Yeh khaki apni fitrat mein na noori hai na naari hai" (Action makes life either heaven or hell; this earthly creature by nature is neither of light nor of fire). Education must produce active, courageous, and purposeful doers. Supports U3-14 and U3-15.
3. Balance of Intellect (*Aql*) and Love (*Ishq*): Iqbal warns against two extremes: dry, destructive intellect detached from moral vision, and blind emotion without intellectual rigour. Modern scientific inquiry (*aql*) must be balanced with deep spiritual and ethical commitment (*ishq*). Supports U3-15 and U3-16.
4. The Teacher and the Curriculum: The teacher is a spiritual and intellectual guide (*murshid* or creative mentor) who awakens the dormant fire in pupils. The curriculum must integrate modern empirical sciences, mathematics, and technological skills with religious and ethical values, literature, and history to develop a complete, integrated personality. Supports U3-16.
5. Education of youth (*Shaheen*): Iqbal's poetic symbol of the Falcon (*Shaheen*) represents soaring ambition, self-reliance, moral dignity, and refusal to feed on the crumbs of others. Verse from *Bang-e-Dra*: "Sabaq phir parh sadaqat ka, adalat ka, shaja'at ka / Liya jaye ga tujh se kaam dunya ki imamat ka" (Learn again the lesson of truth, justice, and bravery; you will be called upon to lead the world). Supports U3-15 and U3-16.
