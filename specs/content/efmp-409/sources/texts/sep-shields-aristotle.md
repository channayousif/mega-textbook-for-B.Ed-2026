## Evidence notes

1. Realist metaphysics and epistemology: Aristotle rejects Plato's separate realm of Forms. Forms exist in concrete particulars (hylomorphism: compound of matter and form). Knowledge begins with sensory perception and observation, ascending through induction and demonstration to universal scientific principles. Supports U3-05 (realism).
2. Educational purpose and *eudaimonia*: Education aims at human flourishing (*eudaimonia*), achieved through virtuous activity in accordance with reason. Moral virtue requires habituation: good habits must be cultivated in practice before children can fully comprehend their rational justification. Supports U3-04 (Aristotle as an educationist).
3. Public education in the *Politics*: Aristotle argues that education must be one and the same for all citizens, publicly provided and regulated by the state, rather than left to private whim, because the preservation of a just community requires a common civic formation. Supports U3-04 and U3-06.
4. Realism in educational practice: Realist education emphasizes objective facts, direct observation of the physical world, empirical science, systematic categorization, and practical reasoning. The teacher presents reality clearly and guides pupils to discover the natural order. Supports U3-06.
