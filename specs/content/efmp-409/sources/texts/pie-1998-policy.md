# Bound source evidence: pie-1998-policy

Source: Government of Pakistan. (1998). *National Education Policy 1998-2010*. Ministry of Education, Islamabad. Official copy listed by the Pakistan Institute of Education.
URL: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1wgok27S1TekT8JJMFSutDE3MFLcSoZO2/view

Consulted: 2026-11-25. PDF downloaded from the PIE listing (176 pages, OCR text layer) and the introduction, the aims and objectives, the literacy targets and the implementation chapter read. Quotations are under 15 words.

## Evidence notes

1. Introduction, 1.4: the Government devised an Education Policy for 1998-2010 aiming at obliterating the rampant ills of the education system. Supports the policy's own framing and purpose (U5-07).
2. Introduction, 1.5: the prevailing illiteracy rate of 61 percent, especially among parents, is one of the stumbling blocks in the promotion of education. Supports the baseline (U5-07).
3. Introduction, 1.6: universalisation of primary education by the year 2010 is the cornerstone of the policy; 40,000 new formal primary schools, evening shifts in 20,000 schools and 20,000 mosque schools. Supports the cornerstone target and the school-building programme (U5-07).
4. Introduction, 1.7: the policy provides for an additional 15,000 middle schools and 7,000 secondary schools during the next five years, and the recruitment of 15,000 fresh teachers. Supports the secondary expansion target (U5-07).
5. Introduction, 1.8: the policy envisages enhancing enrolment in polytechnics from 12,000 at present to 62,000, and in vocational and commercial institutions, by the year 2002. Supports the technical and vocational target (U5-07).
6. Introduction, 1.13: the major innovative programmes include non-formal basic education community schools, mosque schools, evening shifts, an education card for needy students, and a monitoring and implementation cell. Supports the programme list (U5-07).
7. Aims and objectives, 2.9.1-2.9.7: the policy envisages Islamic order, basic education for all, private and NGO provision, removal of inequalities and wider access for females, provincial and local government roles, two school systems (academic and vocational-technical high schools of twelve years), and quality measures. Supports the objectives (U5-07).
8. Literacy and Non-Formal Education, 4.4.1: the current literacy rate of about 39 percent will be raised to 55 percent during the first five years of the policy and 70 percent by the year 2010. Supports the literacy targets (U5-08).
9. Literacy and Non-Formal Education, 4.5.8: the existing Non-Formal Basic Education community schools will be increased to 82,000 (including the existing 7,000) by the year 2002, to enrol 5.5 million out-of-school children. Supports the non-formal target (U5-07).
10. Implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation, 15.1.1: the education system has been the target of experimentation through a number of major reforms and policy packages during the last 50 years, and none produced encouraging results; poor implementation was the major reason. Supports the analysis of failure (U5-04, U5-08).
11. Implementation, 15.1.2: most development programmes and policy provisions could not be implemented since 1947, primarily due to lack of proper political commitment, lower funding and weak or non-existent implementing, monitoring and evaluation mechanisms. Supports the failure factors (U5-04).
12. Implementation, 15.1.3: the literacy rate in Pakistan, despite efforts over 50 years, is only 38.9 percent; in rural areas among females in some provinces it is as low as 27 percent; the primary participation rate is 74.8 percent with female participation at 63 percent. Supports the baseline evidence (U5-08).
13. Implementation, 15.1.2: except the 1959 and 1992 policies, there was no other policy in which an implementation mechanism was identified; infrastructure created for the 1992 policy was soon abolished. Supports the analysis of earlier policies (U5-06, U5-08).
14. Implementation, 15.3.1-15.3.7: the framework proposes a National Council for Educational Development chaired by the Prime Minister, a policy implementation committee, provincial councils, district education authorities, school management committees, and a strengthened EMIS supported by AEPAM. Supports the implementation machinery (U5-03).
