## Evidence notes

1. Abstract: the review analyses education policy and reform in Punjab since 2001, synthesizing policy documents, peer-reviewed research and government reports. Supports the scope of the source (U5-04).
2. Abstract: chronic policy inconsistency, driven by volatile political cycles, prevents long-term strategies from taking root. Supports the continuity factor (U5-04).
3. Abstract: weak monitoring and evaluation systems make it nearly impossible to track progress or make evidence-based adjustments. Supports the data and monitoring factor (U5-04, U5-08).
4. Abstract: deep-seated urban-rural disparities in funding and resources perpetuate cycles of inequality. Supports the finance and equity factor (U5-04, U5-08).
5. Abstract: systemic corruption and administrative inefficiency divert critical funds and erode public trust. Supports the governance factor (U5-04).
6. Abstract: a pervasive lack of meaningful stakeholder engagement in the policy process undermines local ownership and the sustainability of reforms. Supports the stakeholder factor (U5-04, U5-09).
7. Conclusion: policies tend to be very ambitious but prove limited once implemented, particularly regarding fairness and quality; the review calls for a shift away from top-down policies towards sustainable and participatory reform. Supports the analysis of the policy-practice gap and the argument for participatory implementation (U5-08, U5-09).
8. Conclusion: no policy, even the best one, can be implemented without trained and motivated teachers and principals. Supports the capacity factor (U5-04).
