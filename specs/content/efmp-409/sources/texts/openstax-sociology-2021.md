## Evidence notes

1. Book and licence: *Introduction to Sociology 3e*, authors Tonja R. Conerly, Kathleen Holmes and Asha Lal Tamang, published 3 June 2021, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Supports the citation line (U2-02).
2. Section 16.2 describes four perspectives on education. **Functionalism**: education serves intended (manifest) roles - socialization, cultural transmission, social control and placement - and hidden (latent) roles such as courtship, social networking and group work. Supports the functionalist view (U2-02).
3. Section 16.2, **conflict theory**: schools reinforce and perpetuate existing inequalities tied to class, race and gender, rewarding dominant cultural capital and tracking students into unequal positions. Supports the conflict view (U2-02).
4. Section 16.2, **feminist theory**: educational systems treat women unequally, and limited access to schooling restricts women's ability to attain equal rights and financial independence. Supports the fourth perspective named in the topic (U2-02).
5. Section 16.2, **symbolic interactionism**: classroom labeling and credentialism shape students' identities and trajectories, sometimes producing self-fulfilling prophecies. Supports the interactionist view (U2-02).
6. The section uses "symbolic interactionism"; the HEC guide and the unit use the shorter "interactionist". The unit notes the equivalence (U2-02).
