## Evidence notes

1. Natural goodness and naturalism: In *Emile, or On Education* (1762), Rousseau asserts that human beings are innately good and that vice and corruption arise from corrupt social institutions and artificial vanity (*amour-propre*). Education must follow the natural growth of the child. Supports U3-07 (naturalism).
2. Concept of negative education: In early childhood, education should be primarily "negative" - preserving the child's mind from error and the heart from vice, rather than inculcating adult verbal knowledge prematurely. Senses must be educated before reason. Supports U3-08.
3. Developmental stages in *Emile*: Rousseau divides child development into distinct phases: infancy (ages 0-5, physical care and motor freedom); childhood (ages 5-12, training of the senses, learning through bodily interaction with things and natural consequences); pre-adolescence (ages 12-15, emergence of intellectual curiosity and utility, learning a manual trade); and adolescence (ages 15-20, birth of social sentiments, moral reasoning, and spiritual awareness). Supports U3-08 and U3-09.
4. Naturalistic pedagogy: Rejection of corporal punishment and authoritarian moralising; discipline is taught by natural consequences. The teacher acts as an attentive facilitator who arranges an environment where the child learns through personal discovery. Supports U3-09.
