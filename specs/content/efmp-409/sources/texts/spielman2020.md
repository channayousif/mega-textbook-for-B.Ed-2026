## Evidence notes

1. Book and licence: *Psychology 2e* by Rose M. Spielman, William J. Jenkins and Marilyn D. Lovett, OpenStax, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Supports the citation line (U2-03).
2. Section 6.4, "Observational Learning (Modeling)": the section describes social learning theory, associated with Albert Bandura, and notes that his work departed from "pure behaviorism" by incorporating cognitive processes. Supports the social cognitivist perspective (U2-03).
3. Section 6.4: Bandura identified four processes required for successful modeling - attention, retention, reproduction and motivation. Supports the four processes (U2-03).
4. Section 6.4: seeing a model reinforced makes the observer "more motivated to copy them"; seeing a model punished reduces the motivation to imitate (vicarious reinforcement and vicarious punishment). Supports the vicarious reinforcement claim (U2-03).
5. Section 11.5, "Humanistic Approaches": the section covers Abraham Maslow and Carl Rogers, and frames humanism as the "third force". Supports the humanist perspective (U2-03).
6. Section 11.5, Maslow: a "hierarchy of needs" met in sequence, capped by "self-actualization", the fulfillment of one's fullest potential. Supports the hierarchy and self-actualization (U2-03).
7. Section 11.5, Rogers: the "self-concept" compares the "ideal self" with the "real self"; close alignment gives "congruence", a wide gap creates "incongruence"; parental "unconditional positive regard" fosters self-acceptance. Supports the Rogers material (U2-03).
