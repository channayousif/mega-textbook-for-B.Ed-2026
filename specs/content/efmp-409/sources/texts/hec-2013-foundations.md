# Bound source evidence: hec-2013-foundations

Source: Higher Education Commission. (2012). *Foundations of education: Course guide, B.Ed. (Hons) Elementary* (revised September 2013). HEC, Islamabad.
URL: https://www.hec.gov.pk/english/services/universities/RevisedCurricula/Documents/2011-2012/Education/FoundationsEd_Sept13.pdf

Consulted: 2026-11-25, read from the repository's bundled copy (`Scheme-and-Course-guides/extracted-text/course-guides-2025/FoundationsEd_Sept13.txt`). Line numbers refer to that file. Notes are paraphrases except where quotation marks are used.

## Evidence notes

1. Course rationale and Unit 1 overview (lines 928-955): the course relates education to philosophy and widens student teachers' understanding of education; the unit introduces the meaning and basic concepts of education. Supports the course introduction (U1-01) and the list of foundations (philosophical, sociological, psychological, ideological, historical) that the 2026 guide's course description also names.
2. Week 1 lecture notes, "Definition of education" (lines 1355-1385): quotes Kneller (1971, pp. 20-21): in its broad sense education is any act or experience with a formative effect on mind, character or physical ability; in its technical sense it is the process by which society, through schools, colleges and universities, deliberately transmits its cultural heritage. Quotes Pestalozzi: education is "a natural, harmonious and progressive development of man's innate powers". Supports the narrow and broad meanings and the Pestalozzi definition (U1-02).
3. "Education in Islam" (lines 1386-1400): seeking knowledge is a duty for every Muslim; the first revealed word was "Iqra"; cites Az-Zumar 39:9. Supports the Muslim view (U1-02).
4. "The nature of education" (lines 1480-1505): education is lifelong, systematic, the development of individual and society; Dewey's view of education as a continuous reconstruction of experience in a socially desirable way. Supports the Dewey summary (U1-02).
5. Week 2 Session 2, "Aims of education" (lines 1576-1700): good citizens, transferring values, education as social change agent, education for vocational preparation, education for individual development, Islamic aims of education (balance of religious and social life, brotherhood and tolerance), modern aims. Supports grouping aims into individual, social, vocational, moral and spiritual (U1-06).

## Unit 2 evidence notes (added 2026-11-25)

6. Unit 3 outline, "The sociological foundations of education" (lines 338-352): the unit explores how society and culture, social structure, history and economics influence schooling, and how education in return strengthens the societal culture and social structure; it names three perspectives, functionalist, conflict and interactionist. Supports the sociological foundation and its three perspectives (U2-02).
7. Unit 4 outline, "The psychological foundations of education" (lines 370-392): psychology bears on educational objectives, student characteristics, learning processes, teaching methods and evaluation procedures; it names four perspectives - behaviourist, constructivist, social cognitivist and humanist. Supports the psychological foundation and its four perspectives (U2-03).
8. Unit 5 outline, "The historical foundations of education" (lines 400-412): the unit unfolds the education system before the British invasion of the subcontinent, Darul Uloom Deoband, Darul Uloom Nadwat-ul-Ulma, the Mohammedan Anglo Oriental College, and Pakistan's education system in the light of its policies. Supports the five-stage historical trace (U2-04).
9. Unit 1 outline, "The ideological foundations of education" (lines 265-285): the unit asks student teachers to understand the Islamic ideological perspectives on education, the Islamic concept of peace, and the interaction of other religions with Islam in an Islamic state, including the education of religious minorities. Supports the Islamic foundation and the note on minorities (U2-07).
10. Unit 2 outline (lines 957-985): the unit explains how education is affected by philosophy, sociology, psychology, ideology and religion, and its learning objectives ask student teachers to differentiate among the philosophical, sociological, psychological, ideological and religious foundations. Supports the unit's comparative design and the philosophical-versus-ideological comparison (U2-01).

## Unit 4 evidence notes (added 2026-11-25)

11. Unit 4 outline (lines 1036-1055): the unit is titled "Islamic perspectives on education" and covers Surah Al-Alaq, the emphasis on education in the Qur'an and the Hadith, Meesaq-e-Madina, the Last Sermon and As-Suffa. Supports the unit's scope and the place of the first revelation (U4-01).
12. "Education in Islam" (lines 1384-1400): seeking knowledge is a sacred duty, obligatory for every Muslim, male and female; the first revealed word was "Iqra"; and three short sayings are recorded - "Seek knowledge from the cradle to the grave", "Seek knowledge even if it is far as China", and "Seeking of knowledge is a duty of every Muslim". Supports the Hadith emphasis and the scope of seeking knowledge (U4-03).
13. Unit 4 introduction (lines 1036-1041): the unit will explain the method of teaching of the Prophet in the Islamic way and discuss brotherhood in light of the Last Sermon. Supports the framing of the Last Sermon's principles for education (U4-06).
