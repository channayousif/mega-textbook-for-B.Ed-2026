## Section 9.1: Null and Alternative Hypotheses

https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/9-1-null-and-alternative-hypotheses

The null hypothesis is a statement of no difference; the alternative is contradictory and
"usually what the researcher is trying to prove". Decisions are "reject H0" or "do not reject
H0". H0 always carries an equality; Ha never does. Supports Topic 15.1 Steps 1 and 5 and the
misconception box.

## Section 9.3: Probability Distribution Needed for Hypothesis Testing

https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/9-3-probability-distribution-needed-for-hypothesis-testing

A z-test uses a simple random sample, a normally distributed population or a sufficiently
large sample, and a known population standard deviation. A t-test uses a simple random sample
from an approximately normal population and the sample standard deviation in place of the
population standard deviation. Supports Topic 15.2 and Topic 15.3 "When to use" lists.

## Section 9.4: Rare Events, the Sample, Decision and Conclusion

https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/9-4-rare-events-the-sample-decision-and-conclusion

The p-value is the probability that, if the null hypothesis is true, another randomly selected
sample would be as extreme or more extreme than the observed sample. Compare the p-value with
a preset alpha: reject H0 when alpha > p-value, otherwise do not reject. Supports Topic 15.1
Step 4 and Step 5.

https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/9-5-additional-information-and-full-hypothesis-test-examples
