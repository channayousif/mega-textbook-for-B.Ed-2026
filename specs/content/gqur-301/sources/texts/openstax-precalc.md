## Section 11.5: Counting Principles (verified 2026-10-21)

Section 11.5 builds permutations and combinations on the Multiplication
Principle. It states that "The number of permutations of n distinct objects
can always be found by n!", and counts r of n distinct objects in order as
P(n, r) = n! / (n - r)!, with worked examples: three officers elected from a
club of six members, P(6, 3) = 120; first, second and third place among 9
swimmers, 9 x 8 x 7 = 504; all nine swimmers lining up, 9! = 362,880. It
recalls that 0! is equal to 1, which makes P(n, n) = n!.

For selections it states: "When we are selecting objects and the order does
not matter, we are dealing with combinations." It obtains C(n, r) by dividing
P(n, r) by r!, the number of ways to order the chosen objects, giving
C(n, r) = n! / (r! (n - r)!); for example, choosing 2 of 5 side dishes gives
C(5, 2) = 10, and C(5, 3) = 10 because choosing r objects from n is the same
as not choosing n - r.

For permutations of non-distinct objects, it divides n! by the factorial of
each group of identical items, noting that "many of the 12! permutations we
counted are duplicates": 12 stickers with 4 identical stars and 3 identical
moons give 12! / (4! 3!) = 3,326,400 arrangements, and the letters of DISTINCT
give 8! / (2! 2!) = 10,080.
