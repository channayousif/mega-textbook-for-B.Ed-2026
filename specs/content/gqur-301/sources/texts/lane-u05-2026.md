## Chapter 3: Measures of Central Tendency (verified)

Chapter 3 defines the mean, median and mode. The mode is the most frequently
occurring value; for continuous data it is computed from a grouped frequency
distribution, with the interval of highest frequency (the modal class) and its
midpoint providing the estimate. A later section compares the measures: for
symmetric distributions they are equal (except for the mode in bimodal data),
while skewed distributions separate them, with the mean typically higher than
the median in a positive skew, though not always in bimodal data. It concludes
that no single measure is sufficient for a skewed distribution and that
several measures should be reported.

## What this supports in GQUR-301 Unit 5

- Topic 5.1: the definition of the mode as the most frequent value, and the
  modal class for grouped data.
- Topic 5.2: the comparison of mean, median and mode and the effect of skew,
  including its limits for bimodal data.
- Topic 5.3: choosing and reporting a measure based on the shape of the data,
  and reporting more than one measure when they differ.
