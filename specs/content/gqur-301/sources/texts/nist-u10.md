# Bound source notes: NIST, Unit 10

NIST/SEMATECH. (n.d.). What is a Probability Distribution? Section 1.3.6.1.
https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda361.htm
Retrieved 2026-10-21. Original paraphrase of the verified page, not a quotation.

The discrete definition assigns probabilities to listable values. The values
need not be integers. Probabilities are nonnegative and total one; therefore
no individual probability exceeds one. For the continuous definition, the
density is nonnegative and its total area is one. Interval probabilities are
areas beneath it. An exact point has zero probability. Density height can
exceed one. These support U10-01 and U10-03, including half-mark steps and
the contrast between probability bars and a density curve.
