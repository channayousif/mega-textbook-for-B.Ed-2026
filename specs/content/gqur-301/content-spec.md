**Why this course exists.** GQUR-301 is a General Education course in Semester 2 of
the B.Ed (4-Year) programme. It develops prospective teachers' understanding of
fundamental statistical concepts and their ability to analyze and interpret
quantitative information. Through theoretical concepts and practical exercises,
the course cultivates quantitative literacy and problem-solving skills for
professional practice and everyday decision-making.

## Course review plan

- **Course summary points:** statistics as a habit of reasoning about data, not
  just calculation; data types and sources before any computation; sampling as
  the bridge from population to evidence; central tendency and dispersion as two
  complementary pictures of a distribution; counting techniques as the foundation
  of probability; probability rules and distributions as models for uncertainty;
  bivariate analysis and correlation for relationships between variables;
  estimation and hypothesis testing for drawing conclusions from samples.
- **Practice-question mix**: `### MCQs` exactly 10 per unit (Remember to Apply);
  `### RRQs` exactly 10 per unit (Understand to Analyze); `### ERQs` exactly 5
  per unit (Analyze and above), including one data-interpretation and one
  real-Pakistan-data task per unit.
- **Practicum project ideas** (4):
  1. School enrolment data study - collect enrolment figures by class and gender
     at your practicum school, build a frequency table, draw a bar chart and a
     pie chart, and compute the mean and median class size.
  2. Classroom marksheet analysis - take a real class test (anonymised), compute
     mean, median, mode, range, and standard deviation, and write one paragraph
     explaining what the dispersion tells the teacher that the mean alone does
     not.
  3. Probability survey - ask 30 colleagues a yes/no question, record the
     responses, compute the empirical probability, and compare it with the
     expected probability under equal likelihood.
  4. Correlation observation - collect two numerical variables (e.g., hours of
     sleep and self-reported alertness score) from 10 colleagues, plot a scatter
     diagram, and describe the direction and strength of the relationship.
