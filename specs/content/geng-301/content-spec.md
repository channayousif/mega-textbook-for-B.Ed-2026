# GENG-301 - Expository Writing - Content Spec

Semester 2 (General Education), 3 (3-0) credit hours, 16 weeks. Source:
`Scheme-and-Course-guides/extracted-text/2nd 2026.txt` lines 3-180 (Faculty of Education, University of Sindh).

**Why this course exists.** GENG-301 is a General Education course in Semester 2 of the B.Ed
(4-Year) programme. Building on the prerequisite Functional English (GENG-300), it refines
students' abilities to produce clear, concise and coherent written texts in English. The course
develops expository writing skills across types, purposes and audiences, with attention to ethical
writing practices and critical thinking. It prepares student-teachers to write well-organized essays,
to substantiate viewpoints using credible sources, and to uphold academic integrity.

**Placement (owner decision):** inside the pre-service (B.Ed) textbook, in
`docs/semester-2/geng-301/unit-NN/` (content root `docs/`, default docs plugin,
`routeBasePath: `/`), grouped under Semester 2 alongside EFMP-304, EFMP-305 and GQUR-301.

**Bilingual:** English-only, per catalog entry (`bilingual: false`). No Urdu mirror is required,
and bilingual diagram/glossary parity rules are waived. For consistency with the corpus, a Urdu
translation track is noted but gated `draft` until reviewed.

## Course-wide items

- **Course outcomes (guide, verbatim):** by the end of this course, students will be able to:
  1. Understand the essentials of the writing process integrating pre-writing, drafting, editing
     and proof reading to produce well-structured essays.
  2. Demonstrate mastery of diverse expository types to address different purposes and audiences.
  3. Uphold ethical practices to maintain originality in expository writing.
- **Teaching strategies (guide):** lecture, discussions, question-answer, brain storming.
- **Assessment criteria (guide):** class test, mid term test, evaluation of assignments, class
  attendance, class participation/performance (initiative, responding, curiosity, inquiry).
- **Practical work (guide):** group work, group assignments, individual assignment, presentations.
- **Suggested practical activities (guide):** students build a writing portfolio of varied expository
  texts and present it at the end of the course.
- **Recommended resources:** cited by reference only, never reproduced (Constitution Art. III.5).
  The guide's own list includes Axelrod & Cooper, Graff & Birkenstein, Rosenwasser & Stephen,
  Williams & Bizup, Strunk & White, Faigley & Selzer, Zinsser, Bullock et al., Longknife &
  Sullivan, and Johnson-Sheehan & Paine.

## Unit 1: Introduction to Expository Writing

Weeks 1 to 3 (derived). Unit Spec (G1) for `docs/semester-2/geng-301/unit-01/`.

- **Key terms**: Expository writing, Narration, Description, Exposition, Argumentation, Paragraph,
  Topic sentence, Supporting details, Concluding sentence.
- **Topics**: understanding expository writing (definition, types, purpose, applications);
  characteristics of effective expository writing (clarity, coherence, organization); introduction to
