## Evidence notes (original paraphrase of the abstract)

- The article studies how the early twentieth-century nonviolent movement of the Pashtuns, the Khudai Khidmatgar (1929-1948), has been written about.
- It identifies three problems in that writing: stereotyping the Pashtuns as inherently violent, so that nonviolence looks like an aberration; denying the movement's indigenous roots by treating it as a provincial offshoot of Gandhianism; and valuing the principles of nonviolence above its concrete acts.
- The author argues that the movement was an example of radical nonviolent action that drew on Islamic principles and engaged with wider debates.

Use in GCCE-400 Unit 4: Topic 4.1 gives the movement's dates, describes it as non-violent resistance drawing on Islamic principles and local roots, and reports Bala's warning against the stereotype. The unit takes no position on the movement's politics.
