## Evidence notes for GCCE-400 Unit 5

Section 21.2 defines social movements as purposeful, organized groups striving toward a common social goal. It sets out the classical four-stage framework of social movements based on the sociological work of Blumer (1969) and Tilly (1978):

1. **Emergence (Preliminary stage):** People become aware of an issue, and leaders emerge. Discontent is felt but not yet systematically organised.
2. **Coalescence (Popular stage):** People join together, organise collective action, publicise the issue, and recruit supporters to raise wider public awareness.
3. **Bureaucratisation (Institutionalisation stage):** The movement establishes formal organisation, clear leadership structures, defined policies, and regular procedures to sustain long-term advocacy.
4. **Decline:** The movement moves past its peak. Decline can occur because the movement achieved its goal, became institutionalised into regular political life, failed to maintain public interest, fragmented, or faced repression.

Use in GCCE-400 Unit 5: Topic 5.3 uses this four-stage lifecycle framework (fig-U5-5) to teach how social action movements develop, organise, and bring about social change.

## Evidence notes for GCCE-400 Unit 7

### Chapter 11.1, Racial, Ethnic, and Minority Groups (U7-01)

- The chapter's learning objectives are to understand the difference between race and ethnicity, to define a majority (dominant) group, and to define a minority (subordinate) group.
- "The idea of race refers to superficial physical differences that a particular society considers significant, while ethnicity describes shared culture."
- "A human race is a grouping of humankind based on shared physical or social qualities that can vary from one society to another."
- "Ethnicity is based on shared culture - the practices, norms, values, and beliefs of a group that might include shared language, religion, and traditions, among other commonalities."
- On minority groups: "the term 'minority groups' describe groups that are subordinate, or that lack power in society regardless of skin color or country of origin." The chapter quotes Louis Wirth (1945): a minority group is singled out "because of their physical or cultural characteristics" for "differential and unequal treatment", and "who therefore regard themselves as objects of collective discrimination". The unit paraphrases this definition because the full quotation exceeds 15 words.
- "The term minority connotes discrimination, and in its sociological use, the term subordinate group can be used interchangeably with it."

Support limits: the chapter is a United States textbook. Its examples (elderly people as a minority group, Latinx identity, United States census categories) are American and are not used in the unit. The unit uses only the definitions of race, ethnicity and minority group, and the point that a minority group is defined by power and treatment rather than by size.

### Chapter 21.2, Social Movements (U7-03)

- "Social movements are purposeful, organized groups that strive to work toward a common social goal."
- The chapter sets out four stages: emergence, coalescence ("people join together and organize in order to publicize the issue and raise awareness"), institutionalization ("the movement no longer requires grassroots volunteerism: it is an established organization, typically with a paid staff"), and decline.
- The chapter distinguishes types of social movement and theoretical perspectives (resource mobilization, framing, new social movement theory). The unit uses only the definition and the four stages.

Support limits: the chapter's cases are largely North American. The unit uses the definition, the stages and the point that movements are organised and purposeful. It does not attribute any specific campaign, statistic or movement to the chapter.
