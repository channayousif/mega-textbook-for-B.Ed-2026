## Evidence notes (verified summary)

1. Civics teaching in the schools studied followed a transmission approach: teachers passed on textbook knowledge for rote memorisation for examinations.
2. The curriculum, textbook and classroom analysis found that schooling produced exclusionary and passive citizenship: pupils could recall facts but did not develop civic skills (such as problem-solving and decision-making) or civic values and dispositions.
3. Effective citizenship education needs a body of knowledge together with skills, values and dispositions, and participatory experience.
4. The teacher is central: teachers need to move from transmission towards collaborative and reflective classroom strategies.

Use in GCCE-400 Unit 1: point 3 supports the three parts of civics and the civics-citizenship relationship (Topics 1.1 and 1.2); points 1 and 2 support the claim that civics was often taught by rote and produced passive citizens.
