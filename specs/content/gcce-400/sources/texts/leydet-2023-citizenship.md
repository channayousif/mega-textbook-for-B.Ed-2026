# Bound source evidence: leydet-2023-citizenship

Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/citizenship/

Consulted: 2026-11-22. Entry by Dominique Leydet, first published 13 October 2006, substantive revision 5 September 2023.

## Evidence notes

- Section 1 names three dimensions of citizenship: citizenship "as legal status, defined by civil, political and social rights"; citizens "as political agents" taking part in political institutions; and citizenship "as membership in a political community that provides a distinct source of identity". Used in Topics 1.2 and 1.3.
- The republican model traces to Athenian democracy and Republican Rome and stresses civic self-rule and sharing in office; the entry states that active participation in deliberation and decision-making "ensures that individuals are citizens, not subjects". Used in Topic 1.1 (Athens) and Topic 1.2 (subject to participant; the four-word phrase is quoted).
- The liberal model traces to the Roman Empire: citizenship "meant being protected by the law rather than participating in its formulation or execution". Used in Topics 1.1 and 1.2 (paraphrased).
- The entry presents T. H. Marshall's "Citizenship and Social Class" as the main reference for the legal-status model and his thesis on the expansion of rights, including social rights. Topic 1.2 reports Marshall's civil, political and social sequence through this entry.
- The entry describes the civic virtue of public reasonableness: listening to others and stating one's own position with respect for fellow citizens' different experiences and identities. Used in Topic 1.3.

Support limits: the entry is philosophical and not about Pakistan. Pakistani examples in the unit come from the Constitution and other sources.
