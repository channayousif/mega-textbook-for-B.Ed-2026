## Evidence notes (original paraphrase)

The article examines Pakistan's national social studies curriculum and textbooks of its time. It describes a debate between a liberal-democratic and a more theocratic vision of citizenship education. From a content analysis of social studies textbooks, the author argues that they gave heavy emphasis to state ideology and little attention to democratic values such as participation and tolerance, and he calls for reform.

Use in GCCE-400 Unit 1: Topic 1.2 reports only the narrow claim that Ahmad argued the curriculum gave little space to democratic values such as participation and tolerance, presented as one researcher's argument that learners may test against their own school's current textbook. The unit does not repeat the article's wider claims about intolerance or sectarian violence, and it does not describe current textbooks on the article's authority.
