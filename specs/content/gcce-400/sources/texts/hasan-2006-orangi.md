## Evidence notes (original paraphrase; figures as reported in the article)

- Section II.e: Orangi is a Karachi town made up of katchi abadis, with a population of about 1.2 million in 13 union councils. The Orangi Pilot Project was set up in 1980 to overcome the constraints government faced in improving katchi abadis, by developing solutions that people could manage, finance and build. Participatory research found four problem areas (sanitation, employment, health, education), with sanitation the most important. In 1988 the project became four autonomous institutions, including the OPP Research and Training Institute.
- Section II.b: sewage in informal settlements flows into cesspools or the natural drainage system; the government's Katchi Abadi Improvement and Regularization Programme (since 1973) improved only about 1.5 per cent of katchi abadis a year, partly because it lacked proper community participation.
- Section II.f and the introduction: sanitation is divided into internal development (latrines in homes, lane sewers, neighbourhood collector sewers), which communities finance, manage and build with technical and managerial support, and external development (trunk sewers, treatment plants), ideally built by local government. Technical help consisted of plans, maps, estimates, tools, training and supervision. The institute does not collect money from communities; residents collect and use it themselves.
- Introduction: residents built and financed sewers in 5,479 lanes containing 98,527 houses; good relations with local government engineers allowed the project to lobby successfully for converting natural drains into box trunks. Projects in Orangi and 248 other locations in Pakistan show that organised communities can build internal development.
- Section II.f: more than 95,000 houses invested about Rs 90 million in neighbourhood sanitation, against an estimated Rs 630 million for the same work by local government. Infant mortality in communities that built sanitation fell from 128 per 1,000 in 1983 to 37 per 1,000 in 1993.
- Lane process (section on the sanitation programme): lane-level meetings to form an organisation; a lane manager elected, selected or nominated, who applied to the OPP for help; a survey and estimates by the OPP team; the lane manager and lane committee collected money and organised the work.
- The Youth Training Programme in surveying and mapping began in 1994; also in 1994 the Sindh Katchi Abadi Authority decided to follow the OPP methodology. In 2004, because of its drainage mapping, the OPP was invited by the executive district officer for works and services to join Karachi's focal group on nalas and drains.
- Section II.a: the Devolution Plan 2001 was facing "enormous teething problems" at the time of writing.
