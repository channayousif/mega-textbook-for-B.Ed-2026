# Bound source evidence: constitution-pakistan

Source: https://na.gov.pk/uploads/documents/1333523681_951.pdf (National Assembly edition as modified up to 28 February 2012); text checked in the accessible transcription with amendment notes at https://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/constitution/ (Part I, Part II Chapter 1, Part III Chapter 2).

Consulted: 2026-11-22.

## Checked provisions

- Article 4(1): to enjoy the protection of law and to be treated in accordance with law is the inalienable right of every citizen.
- Article 5(1): "Loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen." Article 5(2): obedience to the Constitution and law is the inviolable obligation of every citizen.
- Article 16: every citizen has the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law in the interest of public order.
- Article 17(1): every citizen has the right to form associations or unions, subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law.
- Article 19: freedom of speech and expression, and of the press, subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law on listed grounds.
- Article 25(1): "All citizens are equal before law and are entitled to equal protection of law."
- Article 25A: the State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children aged five to sixteen, in such manner as may be determined by law (inserted in 2010).
- Article 51(2): a person is entitled to vote if a citizen of Pakistan, not less than eighteen years of age, on the electoral roll and not declared of unsound mind by a competent court.

Currency: the unit cites the 2012 edition for these provisions, read in the transcription with its amendment notes on 2026-11-22. Effects of the Twenty-sixth (2024) and Twenty-seventh (2025) Amendments were not checked article by article; none of the articles above is known to be among those they changed, but a reviewer should confirm before stating any of them as current law.

## Provisions checked for GCCE-400 Unit 2

Consulted: 2026-11-22, current text with amendment notes in the transcription (Part I; Part II Chapter 1; Part III Chapters 2 and 3; Part IV Chapters 1-3; Part V Chapter 1; Part VII Chapter 1). Paraphrased unless quoted.

- Article 1(1): Pakistan "shall be a Federal Republic" known as the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. Article 1(2): its territories comprise the Provinces of Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, the Punjab and Sindh, the Islamabad Capital Territory, and such States and territories as are or may be included by accession or otherwise.
- Article 7: in Part II, "the State" means the Federal Government, Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament), a Provincial Government, a Provincial Assembly, and such local or other authorities as are by law empowered to impose any tax or cess.
- Article 50: Parliament consists of the President and two Houses, the National Assembly and the Senate.
- Article 51(1): the National Assembly has three hundred and thirty-six seats, including seats reserved for women and non-Muslims. Article 51(3) allocates 266 general and 60 women's seats among the provinces and the Federal Capital (total 326); the remaining ten are the seats reserved for non-Muslims.
- Article 59(1): the Senate consists of ninety-six members.
- Article 70(1)-(3): a Bill on a Federal Legislative List matter may originate in either House; if passed, it goes to the other House; if passed there without amendment it is presented to the President; if rejected or not passed within ninety days, it may at the request of the originating House be considered in a joint sitting and, if passed by a majority of members present and voting, presented to the President.
- Article 73(1): a Money Bill shall originate in the National Assembly.
- Article 75(1): the President shall, within ten days, assent to a Bill or, for a Bill other than a Money Bill, return it to Parliament for reconsideration.
- Article 90(1): the executive authority of the Federation is exercised by the Federal Government, consisting of the Prime Minister and the Federal Ministers, acting through the Prime Minister, who is the chief executive. Article 91(3)-(4): the National Assembly elects the Prime Minister by a majority of its total membership (with run-off provisions).
- Article 101(1): the Governor of each Province is appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister (Eighteenth Amendment, 2010).
- Article 106(1): the Provincial Assembly of Sindh has 130 general seats, 29 seats for women and 9 for non-Muslims, total 168.
- Article 129(1): the executive authority of the Province is exercised in the name of the Governor by the Provincial Government, the Chief Minister and Provincial Ministers, acting through the Chief Minister. Article 130(3): the Provincial Assembly elects one of its members as Chief Minister.
- Article 140A(1): each Province shall, by law, establish a local government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to elected local representatives. Article 140A(2): local government elections are held by the Election Commission of Pakistan.
- Article 142: Parliament has exclusive power over the Federal Legislative List; Parliament and a Provincial Assembly both have power over criminal law, criminal procedure and evidence; a Provincial Assembly, and not Parliament, has power over any matter not in the Federal Legislative List; Parliament legislates for areas not in any Province. The amendment notes show that the Eighteenth Amendment (2010) replaced the earlier clauses that referred to the Concurrent Legislative List.
- Article 175(1): there shall be a Federal Constitutional Court, a Supreme Court, a High Court for each Province and for the Islamabad Capital Territory, and other courts established by law; the Federal Constitutional Court words were inserted by the Twenty-seventh Amendment Act, 2025 (with effect from 13 November 2025).
- Articles 16, 17, 19 and 25A: as recorded above for Unit 1.

Currency: unlike the Unit 1 note, every article listed in this section was read in its current form, including notes for the Twenty-sixth (2024) and Twenty-seventh (2025) Amendments where shown.
