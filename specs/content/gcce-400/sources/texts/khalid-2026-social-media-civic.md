## Evidence notes

- The study surveyed 384 students (58.3% male, aged 18-29) from public and private universities in Pakistan, using validated scales for social media usage, digital literacy, civic engagement and social cohesion.
- Social media usage significantly predicted civic engagement (beta = 0.341, p < 0.001), and digital literacy partially mediated the link. Topic 6.1 uses the finding that digital literacy partly explains the link; Topic 6.3 uses digital literacy as the bridge between access and use.
- Digital literacy strongly predicted responsible online behaviour (beta = 0.428, p < 0.001). Topic 6.2 uses this only as background; the unit does not report coefficients.
- The authors describe a dual effect of social media: it enhanced connectivity while also contributing to echo chambers and polarisation. Topic 6.1 uses the dual effect to frame the opportunities and risks.

Support limits: the study concerns university students, not school pupils or the general population. The unit reports only the abstract-level findings and does not claim causality for Pakistan as a whole. The full text was not read on this host.
