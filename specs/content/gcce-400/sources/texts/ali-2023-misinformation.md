## Evidence notes

- The authors ran a randomised experiment in urban Pakistan to test educational interventions against misinformation among people with low digital literacy.
- General video-based messages about fake news had no significant effect on identifying fake news. Topic 6.2 uses this to show that a simple warning is not enough.
- Adding personalised feedback based on a participant's past engagement with fake news improved fake-news identification by 0.14 standard deviations. Topic 6.2 uses this to show that specific, repeated help works better.
- The authors found negative but insignificant effects on identifying true news, driven by female respondents, and conclude that interventions "can enable information discernment" but depend on being tailored to the population. Topic 6.2 does not report the negative result; a reviewer may wish to note it.

Support limits: the experiment was conducted in urban Pakistan with low-digital-literacy participants, so the findings are not a national estimate. The unit reports the abstract-level findings only and does not claim that any one lesson will work everywhere. The published version's page range is a single article number (103108).
