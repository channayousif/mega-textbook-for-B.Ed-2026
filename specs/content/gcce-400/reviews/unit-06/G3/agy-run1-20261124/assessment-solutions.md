## Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. **Q:** Define digital citizenship in one sentence, and name three forms of digital civic engagement. *(Understand, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Digital citizenship is the ability to participate in digital society safely, ethically, and constructively. Three forms: e-petitions, citizen portals, and online campaigns (or community groups, online volunteering).
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark for definition; 3 marks for three valid forms (1 each). Fully aligns.
2. **Q:** In three sentences, explain two opportunities and two risks of digital platforms for civic participation. *(Understand, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Opportunities: fast/wide reach and low cost. Risks: rapid misinformation spread and exclusion of citizens without connectivity.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 2 marks for two opportunities; 2 marks for two risks. Fully aligns.
3. **Q:** A classmate says, "Posting about a problem online is not real civic engagement." Analyse this claim in three or four sentences, using one Pakistani example. *(Analyze, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** The claim has a grain of truth because liking or sharing can be shallow and does not replace physical action. However, it is incorrect to dismiss online action: filing a complaint on the Pakistan Citizen's Portal creates a mandatory official audit trail, and video evidence of a collapsed wall can compel municipal response. Online action is real civic engagement, often serving as the initial catalyst.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for grain of truth; 1 for why wrong; 1 for example; 1 for balanced conclusion. Fully aligns.
4. **Q:** Explain in three sentences how Article 19 and Article 17 of the Constitution of Pakistan connect to digital civic engagement. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Article 19 protects freedom of speech and expression, which extends online to posting views and campaigning. Article 17 protects freedom of association, enabling online community groups and civic coalitions. Both rights operate subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark per article; 1 mark for linking to digital platforms. Fully aligns.
5. **Q:** In three sentences, explain why sharing a false post is an ethical act for which the sharer is responsible. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Sharing publishes content to one's network and attaches personal credibility to it. If the post is false, the sharer actively accelerates its reach and potential harm. Publication creates accountability for both author and disseminator.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for sharing as publication; 1 for consequence; 1 for responsibility. Fully aligns.
6. **Q:** List the five checks you should make before sharing a post about a public issue, and explain in one sentence what the last check asks. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived checks:** 1. Check the source; 2. Check the date; 3. Check the evidence; 4. Consider the harm; 5. Decide (share with note or pause/correct).
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** Line 131 states: "Model: Check the source, check the date, check the evidence, consider the harm, then decide. The last check asks what the post could do to a person or community before you share it. Marks (3): 2 for all five checks (1 for three or four, 2 for all five); 1 for the harm check explained."
   - **Discrepancy noted (Advisory finding):** The question asks what the *last* check asks. In the five-check sequence (Topic 6.2 and fig-U6-3), the fifth and final check is "Decide" (deciding whether to share with a short note or pause and correct it). The model answer and mark scheme instead explain the *fourth* check ("consider the harm"). Authors should align the wording or mark scheme.
7. **Q:** A WhatsApp message says a government school in your district is closed this week. It has no source, the photo is from another year, and the education office says no closure is planned. Analyse, in three or four sentences, what the five checks show and what you should do. *(Analyze, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Source fails (anonymous); date fails (outdated photo); evidence fails (denied by education office); harm check warns of unnecessary school absence and panic. Decision: pause, do not share, and post the official clarification.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for each check applied; 1 for decision. Fully aligns.
8. **Q:** Define the digital divide in one sentence and name its four kinds. *(Understand, 4 marks)*
   - **Derived:** The digital divide is the gap between individuals who can access and use digital tools and internet effectively and those who cannot. Four kinds: access, usage, socioeconomic, geographic.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for definition; 3 for four kinds. Fully aligns.
9. **Q:** According to Shair et al. (2022), what are the leading determinants of the digital divide in Pakistan, and what other factors did they find? *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Leading determinants: ICT infrastructure and access to technology. Other factors: socioeconomic, demographic, regional, and supply-side factors.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for infrastructure; 1 for access; 1 for other factors. Fully aligns.
10. **Q:** Analyse, in three or four sentences, how the digital divide changes who can take part in civic life. *(Analyze, 4 marks)*
    - **Derived:** Because modern civic interaction increasingly runs on digital platforms, disconnected citizens cannot sign e-petitions, track complaints, or access public schemes. The divide aligns with income, gender, age, and rural location, thereby exacerbating existing political and social inequalities.
    - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for one effect; 1 for two effects; 1 for structured pattern; 1 for conclusion about inequality. Fully aligns.
