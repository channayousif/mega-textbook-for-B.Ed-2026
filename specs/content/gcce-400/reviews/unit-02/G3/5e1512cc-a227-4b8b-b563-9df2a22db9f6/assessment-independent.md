### Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

#### MCQ 1
- **Prompt:** Which set lists the four elements of a state used in this unit?
- **Options:**
  - A) Population, territory, government, sovereignty
  - B) Population, religion, language, government
  - C) Territory, army, parliament, currency
  - D) Government, constitution, elections, courts
- **Independent Derivation:** By standard political science definition and the Montevideo Convention (1933) / civics curriculum, a state requires a permanent population, a defined territory, an organised government, and sovereignty (the final internal and external authority). Religion and language (B) are cultural features, while army, parliament, currency (C) and constitution, elections, courts (D) are institutional instruments of government, not foundational elements of the state itself.
- **Derived Key:** A
- **Supplied Key:** A (Matches)
- **Bloom Level:** Remember. No ambiguity.

#### MCQ 2
- **Prompt:** After a general election, a new Prime Minister takes office. What has changed?
- **Options:**
  - A) The state, because its government is new
  - B) The government, while the state stays the same
  - C) Both the state and its territory
  - D) Neither, because the President has not changed
- **Independent Derivation:** The state is the enduring political entity defined by its population, territory, government, and sovereignty. The government is merely the temporary administrative mechanism through which the state exercises authority. An election changes the executive/government, leaving the state intact.
- **Derived Key:** B
- **Supplied Key:** B (Matches)
- **Bloom Level:** Understand. Distinguishes state from government.

#### MCQ 3
- **Prompt:** Under Article 142, on which matters can a Provincial Assembly make laws?
- **Options:**
  - A) Only matters in the Federal Legislative List
  - B) Only matters in the Concurrent List
  - C) Matters not in the Federal Legislative List, and criminal law shared with Parliament
  - D) Only matters the President sends to it
- **Independent Derivation:** Under Article 142 of the 1973 Constitution (as amended by the 18th Amendment in 2010), Parliament has exclusive power to make laws on matters in the Federal Legislative List. The Concurrent Legislative List was omitted. Provincial Assemblies have exclusive power to make laws on residual matters (matters not enumerated in the Federal Legislative List). Furthermore, both Parliament and Provincial Assemblies have power to make laws regarding criminal law, criminal procedure, and evidence under Article 142(c).
- **Derived Key:** C
- **Supplied Key:** C (Matches)
- **Bloom Level:** Remember. Tests precise constitutional allocation.

#### MCQ 4

- **Independent Derivation:** Under Article 70(3) of the Constitution of Pakistan, if a bill transmitted to the second House is not passed within 90 days, or is rejected, it may be considered in a joint sitting of Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament) summoned by the President on the request of the House in which the bill originated.

- **Supplied Key:** C (Matches)

#### MCQ 10
- **Prompt:** In a ward, Candidate A wins by 30 votes, and 900 registered voters did not vote. Which conclusion is best supported?
- **Options:**
  - A) The non-voters could not have changed the result
  - B) The result was decided by one voter
  - C) A small share of the non-voters could have changed the result
  - D) Turnout does not matter in local elections
- **Independent Derivation:** If Candidate A leads by 30 votes, a net swing of only 31 votes (from the 900 non-voters voting for Candidate B, which is 31 / 900 ≈ 3.4% of non-voters) would have reversed the election outcome. Therefore, a small fraction of non-voters held decisive power over the outcome.
- **Derived Key:** C
- **Supplied Key:** C (Matches)
- **Bloom Level:** Apply / Analyze. Counteracts the "one vote makes no difference" misconception with simple arithmetic logic.

### Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

1. **RRQ 1 (Understand):** Four elements of a state with Pakistani examples.
   - *Derived answer:* Population (approx. 240 million citizens of Pakistan); Territory (four provinces of Sindh, Punjab, Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, plus Islamabad Capital Territory under Article 1); Government (federal, provincial, and local government institutions); Sovereignty (supreme legal authority of Pakistan to make internal laws and conduct external foreign relations without foreign dictate).
   - *Key match:* 4 marks. Exactly matches model answer (1 mark per element with fitting Pakistani example).

2. **RRQ 2 (Analyze):** Why Sindh is a province and not a state.
   - *Derived answer:* Sindh possesses a permanent population, a defined territorial boundary, and an organised government (Chief Minister, Provincial Assembly, High Court). However, it lacks sovereignty: it operates under the overarching Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, cannot conduct independent foreign policy or sign international treaties, and cannot override federal legislative supremacy where mandated. Because it lacks sovereignty, it is a subnational federating unit (province), not an independent state.
   - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 mark for the 3 elements it possesses; 1 mark for the missing sovereignty element; 1 mark for the logical conclusion).

3. **RRQ 3 (Understand):** Three tiers of government and primary law-making body of each.
   - *Derived answer:*
     - Federal tier: Majlis-e-Shoora / Parliament (comprising the President, National Assembly, and Senate).
     - Provincial tier: Provincial Assembly (e.g. Provincial Assembly of Sindh).
     - Local tier: Elected local councils established under Article 140A (such as Metropolitan Corporations, Municipal Committees, and Union Councils in Sindh).
   - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 mark per tier with its designated legislative/council body).

4. **RRQ 4 (Understand):** Four steps in tracing a federal bill becoming an Act of Parliament.
   - *Derived answer:*
     - Step 1: Introduction and passage in the originating House (National Assembly or Senate; Money Bills solely in National Assembly).
     - Step 2: Transmission to and passage by the second House without amendments.
     - Step 3: Dispute resolution mechanism: if rejected or unpassed within 90 days, referral to a joint sitting of both Houses.
     - Step 4: Submission to the President for assent within ten days, or return for reconsideration (except Money Bills).
   - *Key match:* 4 marks (1 mark for each step in chronological order).

5. **RRQ 5 (Analyze):** Institutional matching for three rural problems (closed school, broken street light, land dispute in court).
   - *Derived answer:*
     - Closed government school: Provincial tier (executive department: District Education Office), because school education is a devolved provincial subject under Article 142 following the 18th Amendment.
     - Broken street light: Local tier (Union Council / Municipal Committee), because municipal infrastructure and street lighting are designated local government functions under the Sindh Local Government Act 2013.
     - Land dispute in court: Judicial branch (Civil Court / District Judiciary), whose constitutional role is adjudicating disputes and interpreting property rights according to law.
   - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 mark per problem with correct tier/branch and justification).

6. **RRQ 6 (Understand):** Civil society definition and refutation of the "NGOs only" misconception.
   - *Derived answer:* Civil society is the arena of voluntary, collective citizen association that operates outside the family, the market, and the state. Equating civil society solely with registered NGOs is incorrect because registration is a formal administrative process, not the defining criterion. Any voluntary, non-state, non-profit citizen initiative - including unregistered mohalla committees, mothers' study circles, trade unions, literary societies, and press clubs - constitutes civil society.
   - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 for definition; 1 for clarifying NGOs as one form; 1 for other examples or non-state/non-profit/voluntary features).

7. **RRQ 7 (Understand):** Two-way relationship between democracy and civil society with Pakistani examples.
   - *Derived answer:*
     - Civil society strengthens democracy by acting as a civic watchdog over government power and amplifying public needs between elections (e.g. the Hyderabad mothers' group advocating for a school teacher, or FAFEN monitoring electoral transparency).
     - Democracy protects civil society by establishing constitutional fundamental rights that legally safeguard free association, speech, and peaceful assembly (e.g. Article 17 allowing teachers and trade workers to form associations without state suppression).
   - *Key match:* 4 marks (1 mark per direction [2], 1 mark per concrete Pakistani example [2]).

8. **RRQ 8 (Analyze):** Batool et al. (2019) on Pakistan's developing civil society and why teachers should care.
   - *Derived answer:* Batool et al. (2019) explain that Pakistani civil society was historically hindered by the inherited post-colonial administrative structure that concentrated power in bureaucratic and military institutions, leaving civic groups with constrained operational space. Although civil society has expanded significantly in recent decades, it remains developing due to capacity constraints and uneven democratic culture. Teachers must care because classroom pedagogy and school environments are where future citizens cultivate the participatory habits, critical dialogue, tolerance, and democratic skills necessary to sustain robust civic institutions.
   - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 for historical cause; 1 for current developing state; 1 for educational/pedagogical link).

9. **RRQ 9 (Understand):** Three forms of political participation other than voting and requirements of each.
   - *Derived answer:*
     - Contacting an elected representative (e.g. meeting an MPA): Requires identifying the correct representative, articulating a public grievance clearly, and presenting evidence.
     - Submitting a petition or formal complaint: Requires gathering empirical documentation, collecting community signatures, and systematically following up with administrative authorities.
     - Engaging in peaceful protest/assembly (under Article 16): Requires peaceful collective organisation within the law to highlight urgent social grievances.
   - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 mark per form with what it demands of a citizen).

10. **RRQ 10 (Analyze):** 2024 election turnout paradox and its implication for democratic representation.
    - *Derived answer:* Both statistics are mathematically valid because while absolute voter turnout reached a historic high of 60.6 million votes in 2024 (up from 54.8 million in 2018), total registered voters surged at an even faster rate to 128.6 million (an increase of 22.6 million). Consequently, the percentage turnout declined from 52.1% to 47.6%. In terms of democratic representation, this indicates that more than half of registered citizens (over 52%) did not participate, meaning governing assemblies were elected by a minority of the registered electorate, potentially diluting the voice and accountability of disengaged populations.
    - *Key match:* 3 marks (1 for voter registration surge; 1 for percentage drop calculation; 1 for representative implication).
