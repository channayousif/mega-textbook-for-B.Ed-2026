## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

### MCQ 1

- **Independent Solution:** Option B ("The study of how a community or country is run and of its people's rights, duties and part in it"). Civics derives from *civis* and encompasses governance, rights, duties, and participation. Option A is politics; C is Pakistan Studies; D is Economics.

### MCQ 2
- **Prompt:** In ancient Athens, who could take part in the citizens' assembly?
- **Independent Solution:** Option B ("Free adult men only"). Athenian direct democracy excluded women, enslaved persons, and resident foreigners (metics).
- **Supplied Key:** B.
- **Verdict:** Pass.

### MCQ 3
- **Prompt:** Pakistan's first general elections held nationally on universal adult franchise took place in:
- **Independent Solution:** Option C ("1970"). 1946 was restricted franchise; 1951-1954 were provincial adult franchise elections (Sindh in 1953); 1970 was the first national one-person-one-vote election.
- **Supplied Key:** C.
- **Verdict:** Pass.

### MCQ 4
- **Prompt:** Neighbours in Thatta meet a school management committee to ask for a second teacher, and speak about the problem at the meeting. Which form of civic engagement is this mainly?
- **Independent Solution:** Option C ("Public debate"). Speaking at an institutional meeting to address a public problem is public debate/advocacy, not voting, protest, or unpaid manual volunteering.
- **Supplied Key:** C.
- **Verdict:** Pass.

### MCQ 5
- **Prompt:** Which of these is one of the three dimensions of citizenship described in this unit?
- **Independent Solution:** Option A ("Membership in a political community that gives a shared identity"). Leydet's three dimensions are legal status, political agency/rights and duties in action, and membership/identity. Options B, C, D are not dimensions.
- **Supplied Key:** A.
- **Verdict:** Pass.

### MCQ 6
- **Prompt:** Article 5 of the Constitution of Pakistan says that the basic duty of every citizen is:
- **Independent Solution:** Option C ("Loyalty to the State"). Article 5(1) states: "Loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen."
- **Supplied Key:** C.
- **Verdict:** Pass.

### MCQ 7
- **Prompt:** A resident of British India before 1947 who obeyed the ruler and had no say in government is best described by which role?

### MCQ 8

### MCQ 9

## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

- **RRQ 1 (Understand):** Definition of civics + 3 parts with examples.
  - Derived solution: Study of how society is run and the role of its members. 3 parts: Knowledge (e.g. structure of local government), Skills (e.g. writing a complaint, dialogue), Values/Dispositions (e.g. fairness, respect, public care). Matches model and 3-mark rubric.
- **RRQ 2 (Understand):** Civics vs Politics + Pakistani example.
  - Derived solution: Politics focuses on competition for political power and elections; civics is broader, encompassing legal structures, community problem-solving, and everyday civic life. Example: clearing a blocked mohalla drain is civics, not party politics. Matches 3-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 3 (Understand):** Franchise expansion 1946 to 1970.
  - Derived solution: 1946 limited property/tax franchise; 1951-1954 provincial adult franchise elections; 1970 first national general elections on universal adult franchise. Matches 3-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 4 (Understand):** Identity card as evidence vs whole of citizenship.
  - Derived solution: Citizenship exists by legal status (e.g. birth) before documentation; CNIC is proof that facilitates rights (e.g. voting); citizenship also requires active participation, civic virtues, and sense of belonging. Matches 3-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 5 (Understand):** Two rights and one duty from Constitution + connection.
  - Derived solution: Rights: Art. 25 (equality before law), Art. 16 (peaceful assembly). Duty: Art. 5 (loyalty to State and obedience to Constitution/law). Rights depend on all citizens observing mutual duties under law. Matches 4-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 6 (Understand):** Changing role from subject to participant.
  - Derived solution: Subject obeys without voice (pre-1947); voter selects representatives (1970); participant actively engages in ongoing community and institutional governance. Matches 4-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 7 (Analyze):** Civics vs citizenship in union council lesson.
  - Derived solution: Learning functions is civics (knowledge); drafting and delivering petition is citizenship (action/agency). Civics prepares informed action; civic experience deepens civic knowledge. Matches 4-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 8 (Understand):** Active vs participatory citizenship + Pakistani examples.
  - Derived solution: Active is individual lawful civic action (e.g. voting, flood volunteering); participatory is organised collective institution-building (e.g. establishing school water management committee). Matches 3-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 9 (Analyze):** WhatsApp blood donor youth group.
  - Derived solution: Digital tool usage = digital citizenship; collective transport organisation = participatory citizenship; both represent active citizenship. Overlap reflects hybrid digital-physical civic action. Matches 4-mark scheme.
- **RRQ 10 (Analyze):** One pillar of modern society and consequences of weakness.
  - Derived solution: Law (Articles 4 & 5) ensures predictable, equal rules; weakness destroys institutional trust and leads to informal dispute escalation. Matches 3-mark scheme.
