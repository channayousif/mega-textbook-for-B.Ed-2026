## Course Description

This course introduces students to sociology as the systematic study of society, social relationships, and social institutions. It examines how social structures, cultures, and interactions shape individual behaviour and collective life. The course covers classical and contemporary sociological theories, socialization, culture, social stratification, institutions, and major social issues at local and global levels. Emphasis is placed on developing sociological imagination and critical thinking skills.

Two consequences for authoring, both load-bearing:

1. **The course is explicitly for trainee teachers**, so every example must ground in a Pakistani/Sindh classroom or community context. Abstract sociological concepts land best when tied to the lived experience of a B.Ed student who will teach in a government or private school.
2. **Six units cover a full introductory sociology syllabus** in 2 credit hours. The prose must be efficient and concept-dense without being jargon-heavy. HSC register throughout.

## Course review plan

Seeds an eventual `docs/semester-2/gsos-301/course-review.mdx` (Spec 008 FR-016). Not parsed by any gate.

- **Course summary points**:
  - Sociology is the systematic study of society, relationships, and institutions, not common sense or personal opinion.
  - The three major perspectives (functionalism, conflict theory, symbolic interactionism) offer competing explanations of social life.
  - Culture and socialization shape who we become; they are not fixed at birth.
  - Social structures (status, roles, groups, institutions) organize collective life and constrain individual action.
  - Stratification by class, caste, and gender produces persistent inequality; it is a social fact, not a natural one.
  - Social institutions (family, education, religion, economy, politics, healthcare) meet society's core needs.
  - Social change is constant; modernization, globalization, and technology drive it, but so do inequality, crime, and poverty.
- **Practicum project ideas**:
  - *Sociological observation* - spend one school day recording three examples of social interaction (cooperation, competition, or conflict) and analyse them using one of the three perspectives.
  - *Family institution mapping* - interview one family about how the institution of education has shaped their children's life chances; write a one-page analysis.
