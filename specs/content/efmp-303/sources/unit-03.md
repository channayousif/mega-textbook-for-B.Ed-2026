## Inspectable passages and claim boundaries

Page numbers below are printed numbers, not a PDF viewer's page count. All summaries are paraphrases.

- **Vision 2025, p. 3 (PDF page 7).** Distinguishes the aspiration from programmes implemented through five-year and annual plans. Topic 3.1 definition and worked example; the Vision is not itself a costed five-year plan.

- **Vision 2025, p. 33 (PDF page 37), Education.** Reports education spending as barely 2% of GDP and proposes 4% by 2018; reports literacy of 57%, rural literacy 50%, low enrolment and gender gaps. Figure U3-2 and Topics 3.1/3.3. These are historical statements in the 2014 publication, with no claim that all baseline observations were measured in 2014 or that targets were achieved. GDP is not the plan-budget denominator.

- **Vision 2025, pp. 33–34 (PDF pages 37–38).** Describes education development as province-led with federal partnership/support. Topics 3.1/3.2 and RRQ 7. Does not establish exclusive provincial control of every education function or a school-financing formula.

- **Vision 2025, p. 46 (PDF page 50), Productivity Growth.** Links skills development and labour-force quality to intended productivity growth. Topic 3.1 and RRQ 10 explain a proposed mechanism, not a measured causal return.

- **Vision 2025, p. 101 (PDF page 105), Goals 1 and 3.** Lists literacy, primary participation/completion and gender parity targets. Figure U3-2 and the provided exercise. Targets are not outcomes.

- **NEP 2009, p. 16 (PDF page 26), paragraphs 62–66, especially 64.** Describes divided jurisdictions, shared federal/provincial responsibilities, and federal coordination. Corrects exclusive-federal pre-2010 claims in Topics 3.1/3.2, teacher notes and RRQ 7. Paragraph numbering differs from some online copies; the November UNESCO-hosted copy is used here.

- **Coombs 1970, pp. 14–15.** Defines planning through systematic analysis addressing learners' and society's needs; includes implementation, adaptation and feedback. Topic 3.2's teaching synthesis and six-stage teaching cycle. The six labels are a teaching organisation, not a quotation or an asserted Coombs taxonomy.

- **Coombs 1970, p. 55.** Distinguishes the system-wide view from attention to internal processes and subsystems; connects objectives and priorities to evaluation. Scale distinction and needs/goals teaching. School-level examples simplify the broader subsystem meaning of micro planning.

## Teaching-example provenance

The classroom hooks, classification scenarios, six-stage attendance walkthrough and budgets are
hypothetical teaching examples. The new worked school example's Rs 40,000 ceiling is invented for
arithmetic and resource-choice practice, not a reported allocation. The four planning frameworks
are simplified authoring aids; Coombs is cited for broad planning principles only.
