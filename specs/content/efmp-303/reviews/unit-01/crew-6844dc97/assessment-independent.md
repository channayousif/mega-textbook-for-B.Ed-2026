## 1. Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

### MCQ 1
- **Prompt**: Which of the following best describes educational policy?
- **Options**:
  - A) A step-by-step plan for building schools
  - B) A statement of what the education system aims to achieve
  - C) A list of teacher salaries
  - D) A record of examination results
- **Independent Solution**: Policy sets direction and objectives (the "what" and "why"), whereas planning operationalizes them (the "how"). Therefore B is the correct definition.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 2
- **Prompt**: What was the literacy rate in the territory that became Pakistan at independence?
- **Options**:
  - A) About 50 per cent
  - B) About 35 per cent
  - C) About 15 per cent
  - D) About 5 per cent
- **Independent Solution**: In 1947, historical estimates consistently place overall literacy in the areas constituting Pakistan at approximately 15% (with female literacy under 5%).
- **Derived Answer**: **C**

### MCQ 3
- **Prompt**: What is the main difference between educational policy and educational planning?
- **Options**:
  - A) Policy is written by teachers; planning is written by government
  - B) Policy sets out what is to be done; planning sets out how, when and by whom
  - C) Policy is about schools; planning is about universities
  - D) Policy and planning are the same thing
- **Independent Solution**: Policy articulates intentions, goals, and standards; planning determines the implementation mechanism, timelines, budgets, and personnel allocation.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 4
- **Prompt**: How does society shape education, as described in this unit?
- **Options**:
  - A) Society has no influence on education
  - B) Through language norms, gender expectations and economic conditions that affect who goes to school
  - C) Only through government funding
  - D) Only through examination systems
- **Independent Solution**: Societal forces (cultural values, patriarchal restrictions, poverty, regional language usage) establish barriers and priorities that influence access and curriculum.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 5
- **Prompt**: A new policy says all primary schools must teach a second language. Which is a planning question that must be answered?
- **Options**:
  - A) What colour should the school building be
  - B) How many additional language teachers must be recruited and trained
  - C) What the school's motto should say
  - D) Who designed the policy document
- **Independent Solution**: Teacher supply, training pipelines, and materials distribution are standard planning considerations required to execute a language policy.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 6
- **Prompt**: Which milestone introduced the grade structure (primary, middle, secondary, higher secondary) that still shapes Pakistan's schools?
- **Options**:
  - A) The 1947 Education Conference
  - B) The 1959 Sharif Commission
  - C) The 2009 National Education Policy
  - D) The 1992 Education Policy
- **Independent Solution**: The Commission on National Education (Sharif Commission, 1959) established the 5-3-2-2 tier structure (Grades 1-5 primary, 6-8 middle, 9-10 secondary, 11-12 higher secondary).
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 7
- **Prompt**: A father says, "My daughter will not benefit from school because no educated woman from our village has found work." Using the education-society-development triangle, what is the father questioning?
- **Options**:
  - A) The cost of school uniforms
  - B) Whether education actually delivers the development benefits it promises
  - C) Whether the school building is safe
  - D) Whether the teacher is qualified
- **Independent Solution**: The parent questions the economic payoff of education in the development axis (human capital returns), demonstrating how weak economic opportunities undermine educational demand.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 8
- **Prompt**: Which of the following was a feature of colonial education that Pakistan inherited?
- **Options**:
  - A) A system designed for mass literacy
  - B) Urdu as the medium of all education
  - C) An examination system modelled on the British system
  - D) Free and compulsory education for all
- **Independent Solution**: British colonial administration installed centralised external matriculation/intermediate examinations focused on memorisation and screening, which persisted post-independence.
- **Derived Answer**: **C**

### MCQ 9
- **Prompt**: The timeline of milestones shows that many policies name the same problems. What does this pattern suggest?
- **Options**:
  - A) Policies are always successful
  - B) Naming a problem in a policy does not guarantee it is solved
  - C) Education policy is not important
  - D) Problems disappear on their own
- **Independent Solution**: The recurrent declaration of targets (universal primary education, eradicating illiteracy) across successive policies without achieving them highlights the implementation gap.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

### MCQ 10
- **Prompt**: Which milestone nationalised private schools and colleges, expanding government control over education?
- **Options**:
  - A) 1970 National Education Policy
  - B) 1972 Education Policy
  - C) 1998 National Education Policy
  - D) 2017 National Education Policy
- **Independent Solution**: The 1972-1980 Education Policy promulgated by Z.A. Bhutto nationalised private schools and colleges in 1972.
- **Derived Answer**: **B**

## 2. Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

- **RRQ 1**: Distinction between policy ("what/why" - goals, intent, direction) and planning ("how/when/by whom" - activities, resources, sequencing, budgeting). Model answer accurately reflects this 2-point distinction.
- **RRQ 2**: Milestone identification (e.g. 1947 Conference, 1959 Sharif Commission, 1972 Policy) with clear significance. Mark scheme provides 1 mark per valid milestone with correct year.
- **RRQ 3**: Bidirectional education-society relationship: society sets expectations (e.g., gender roles influencing enrollment), education transforms society (e.g., female literacy improving health and reducing infant mortality).
- **RRQ 4**: 5-3-2-2 grade structure recommended by 1959 Commission, creating distinct organizational divisions that persist in provincial education departments today.
- **RRQ 5**: Implementation failure factors: resource deficits, political instability, lack of institutional accountability, or absence of realistic cost estimates.
- **RRQ 6**: Colonial focus on administrative clerk production vs 1947 Conference emphasis on universal mass education, ideological framing, and socio-economic citizenship.
- **RRQ 7**: Three planning requirements for universal primary completion: teacher staffing/training, physical classroom infrastructure/distribution, and non-salary/textbook budget allocation.
- **RRQ 8**: Colonial legacies: centralized external high-stakes examinations and English as the gatekeeper language for civil administration and higher education.
- **RRQ 9**: Contrast between 1972 (socialist focus, nationalization, state expansion) and 1979 (Islamization, private sector reopening, madrasah integration).
- **RRQ 10**: Triangle concept: education develops skills for economic growth, economic growth funds schools, societal norms shape access to both.

All 10 RRQs have precise, objective mark allocations and unambiguous criteria.

## 3. Extended-Response Questions (ERQs)

- **ERQ 1** (Analyze): Examines policy failure without planning (e.g. declaring single national curriculum or computer education without teachers or electricity). Analytic rubric 0-4 evaluates specific Pakistani example, structural contrast, and synthesis.
- **ERQ 2** (Evaluate): Evaluates the myth of complete systemic redesign post-1947. Successfully contrasts continuity (bureaucracy, examination boards, elite schools) with reforms (curriculum orientation, national language discourse). Rubric evaluates balanced historical evidence.
- **ERQ 3** (Analyze): Rural Sindh girls' schooling through the triangle framework. Evaluates two distinct social barriers (poverty, distance/mobility, cultural modesty norms) and educational policy interventions (female teachers, local boundary walls, stipends).
- **ERQ 4** (Analyze): Policy proliferation vs implementation inertia pattern. Evaluates governance discontinuity, symbolic policy adoption, and uncosted mandates.
- **ERQ 5** (Create/Evaluate): Detailed case evaluation of a chosen milestone (e.g., 1959 Commission or 1972 nationalization) with critical appraisal of intended vs unintended consequences.

All 5 ERQs demand Bloom level Analyze or higher. Rubrics are differentiated (0-3 or 0-4 marks per sub-dimension) and clearly set out standards for pre-service teacher candidates.
