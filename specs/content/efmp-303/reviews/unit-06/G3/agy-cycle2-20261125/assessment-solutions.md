## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

### Item 1
- **Stem**: Which country achieved near-universal primary enrolment through community schools and female teacher recruitment?
- **Options**: A) Sri Lanka, B) Bangladesh, C) Nepal, D) India
- **Cognitive level**: Remember
- **Independent solve**: Topic 6.1 highlights Bangladesh's specific policy interventions (community schools, female teachers, school feeding) that achieved near-universal primary enrolment despite lower per-capita GDP.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 2
- **Stem**: What does SDG4 aim to achieve by 2030?
- **Options**: A) Universal military service, B) Inclusive and equitable quality education for all, C) Free university education only, D) A single global curriculum
- **Cognitive level**: Remember
- **Independent solve**: Goal 4 is officially defined as ensuring inclusive and equitable quality education and promoting lifelong learning opportunities for all.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 3
- **Stem**: What is the primary function of an Education Management Information System (EMIS)?
- **Options**: A) To replace teachers with computers, B) To collect, store and report education data for planning, C) To write education policies, D) To train teachers in ICT skills
- **Cognitive level**: Understand
- **Independent solve**: An EMIS is an institutional data infrastructure system designed for systematic collection, integration, storage, retrieval, and reporting of educational statistics to inform planning and management decisions.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 4
- **Stem**: Why does the topic describe Sri Lanka's education system as efficient?
- **Options**: A) It spends the most money per pupil in South Asia, B) It achieves strong outcomes through efficient teacher deployment and free education despite modest spending, C) It has the smallest population, D) It does not collect education data
- **Cognitive level**: Understand
- **Independent solve**: Topic 6.1 notes Sri Lanka achieves high adult literacy (>92%) and universal enrolment with modest GDP expenditure (~2.1%) due to efficient deployment, free education, and health/education synergy.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 5
- **Stem**: A district's EMIS shows that girls' enrolment drops by 30 per cent after class 5. Using data-driven planning, what is the most appropriate first step?
- **Options**: A) Ignore the data because enrolment is a provincial matter, B) Investigate the specific transition point and plan a targeted intervention, C) Build a new university in the district, D) Reduce the number of male teachers
- **Cognitive level**: Apply
- **Independent solve**: Data-driven planning requires diagnosing the specific local bottleneck (distance to middle school, sanitation, security, transition costs) before formulating a targeted intervention.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 6
- **Stem**: A teacher receives a new curriculum policy and realises it does not work for her multi-grade classroom. She adjusts the grouping strategy while keeping the learning objectives. Which role from Topic 6.3 is she demonstrating?
- **Options**: A) Interpreter, B) Adapter, C) Feedback provider, D) Policy writer
- **Cognitive level**: Apply
- **Independent solve**: Topic 6.3 distinguishes three teacher roles: Interpreter (understanding intent), Adapter (modifying implementation parameters for classroom reality), and Feedback provider (reporting back). Modifying grouping strategy is Adaptation.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 7
- **Stem**: Which innovation in policy design means setting shorter planning cycles and adjusting based on evidence?
- **Options**: A) Top-down design, B) Iterative planning, C) Political planning, D) Static planning
- **Cognitive level**: Apply
- **Independent solve**: Iterative policy design replaces rigid multi-year static plans with agile feedback cycles, regular reviews, and course corrections.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 8
- **Stem**: A developing country succeeds with a school feeding programme. A Pakistani planner wants to copy it exactly. What principle from Topic 6.1 should guide the planner?
- **Options**: A) Copy exactly; what works there will work here, B) Adapt, not transplant; policies must fit local conditions, C) Wait until the policy has worked for 50 years, D) Reject all international ideas
- **Cognitive level**: Apply
- **Independent solve**: Policy transfer literature and Topic 6.1 emphasize "adapt, not transplant", recognising contextual variables (governance, infrastructure, culture, budget).
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 9
- **Stem**: Which of the following best describes the relationship between technology and planning, as discussed in Topic 6.2?
- **Options**: A) Technology replaces the need for planning, B) Technology supports planning but does not replace human decisions, C) Technology is irrelevant to planning, D) Technology makes planning slower
- **Cognitive level**: Apply
- **Independent solve**: Technology is a cognitive and diagnostic tool that processes information; values, policy priorities, and trade-offs remain human professional judgments.
- **Derived key**: B
- **Supplied key**: B (Match)

### Item 10
- **Stem**: Strategic planning for equity requires disaggregated data. What does "disaggregated" mean in this context?
- **Options**: A) Data broken down by district, gender or school type rather than national totals only, B) Data that has been discarded, C) Data collected only from urban schools, D) Data that has not been checked for errors
- **Cognitive level**: Apply
- **Independent solve**: Disaggregation refers to decomposing macro statistics into demographic, geographical, and institutional sub-categories to reveal disparities hidden by aggregate means.
- **Derived key**: A
- **Supplied key**: A (Match)
