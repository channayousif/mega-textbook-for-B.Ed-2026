## Excerpts

1. Abstract: "Kolb's (1984) experiential learning cycle is perhaps the most scholarly influential and cited model regarding experiential learning theory." The review found that "critical reflection acts as a mediator of meaningful learning".
2. Section 2, quoting Kolb (1984, p. 30): learners need "concrete experience abilities (CE), reflective observation abilities (RO), abstract conceptualization abilities (AC) and active experimentation (AE) abilities"; they "must be able to use these theories to make decisions and solve problems (AE)."
3. Section 2, quoting Kolb (2015, p. 51): the process is "a four-stage cycle" in which the learner "'touches all the bases' - experiencing (CE), reflecting (RO), thinking (AC), and acting (AE) - in a recursive process".
4. Section 2: Kolb found the dimensions of the theory "in the works of prominent twentieth-century scholars Kurt Lewin, John Dewey, and Jean Piaget".

All four excerpts support Topic 3.7 (U3-13): the four stages of Kolb's cycle (Figure fig-U3-13), its roots, and the central role of reflection.
