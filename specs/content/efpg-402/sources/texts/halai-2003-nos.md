## Excerpts

1. Pakistan's science curriculum for classes one to eight addressed the nature of science "by including, 'scientific literacy' as one of the aims of teaching science" (citing Government of Pakistan, 2000). Teacher education programmes, by contrast, focus "more on teaching content knowledge and methods of teaching".
2. "There is research evidence, albeit limited, that science teachers in Pakistan also have an inadequate understanding of NOS". "Not knowing about science, the science teachers continue to teach science as a collection of facts." The author calls this a "vicious cycle" that breeds students who become teachers and repeat it.
3. Schwab (1964) is reported as complaining that science is taught as a "rhetoric of conclusions" rather than enquiry.
4. An abbreviated list of nature-of-science ideas for school science (from an NSTA position paper, 2000): "Scientific knowledge is simultaneously reliable and tentative"; no "single universal step-by-step scientific method captures the complexity of doing science", but "shared values and perspectives characterize a scientific approach"; "Creativity is a vital, yet personal ingredient"; theories and laws "are terms with very specific meanings"; contributions to science have been made "by people the world over"; questions, observations and conclusions are influenced by existing knowledge and the researcher's context; "old ideas are replaced or supplemented by newer ones" as new evidence arrives.
5. A study of three elementary teachers in Karachi found that hands-on activities helped, "but there is a need for more overt teaching of NOS and explicit discussion of the concepts of NOS, as they relate to each practical activity".
