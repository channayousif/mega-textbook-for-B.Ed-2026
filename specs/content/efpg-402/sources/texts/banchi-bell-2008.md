## Excerpts

1. p. 26: "Teachers sometimes believe that in order for students to be engaged in inquiry-oriented activities they need to be designing scientific investigations from scratch and carrying them out on their own. This simply isn't true." "Most students, regardless of age, need extensive practice to develop their inquiry abilities".
2. p. 26: "a four-level continuum - confirmation, structured, guided, open"; it "focuses on how much information (e.g., guiding question, procedure, and expected results) is provided to students and how much guidance you will provide as the teacher".
3. p. 26-27: "At the first level, confirmation inquiry, students are provided with the question and procedure (method), and the results are known in advance." "At the next level, structured inquiry, the question and procedure are still provided by the teacher; however, students generate an explanation supported by the evidence they have collected."
4. p. 27: "At the third level, guided inquiry, the teacher provides students with only the research question, and students design the procedure". "Just because students are designing their own procedures does not mean that the teacher's role is passive. To the contrary, students need guidance as to whether their investigation plans make sense."
5. p. 27: "At the fourth and highest level of inquiry, open inquiry, students have the purest opportunities to act like scientists, deriving questions, designing and carrying out investigations, and communicating their results." It is appropriate only "when they have demonstrated that they can successfully design and carry out investigations when provided with the question."

## Unit 3 use

All five excerpts support Topic 3.6 (U3-11): the four levels of inquiry, Figure fig-U3-11, and the point that the teacher is never passive.
