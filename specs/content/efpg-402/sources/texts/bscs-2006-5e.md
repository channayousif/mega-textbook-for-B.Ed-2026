## Excerpts

1. Introduction (p. 1): the BSCS 5E model "consists of the following phases: engagement, exploration, explanation, elaboration, and evaluation."
2. John Dewey (p. 5): "John Dewey began his career as a science teacher." In *How We Think* the traits of reflective thinking include "(1) defining the problem, (2) noting conditions associated with the problem, (3) formulating a hypothesis for solving the problem, (4) elaborating the value of various solutions, and (5) testing the ideas to see which provide the best solution for the problem." The report adds: "doing hands-on activities in science is not enough. Those experiences also must be minds on."
3. Table 3, Dewey's instructional model (p. 6): phases "Sensing Perplexing Situations", "Clarifying the Problem", "Formulating a Tentative Hypothesis", "Testing the Hypothesis", "Revising Rigorous Tests" and "Acting on the Solution".
4. Table 4, Heiss, Obourn and Hoffman learning cycle (p. 6): in the first phase "Students observe demonstrations to raise questions, propose a hypothesis to answer questions, and plan for testing."
5. Atkin-Karplus learning cycle (p. 7): Atkin and Karplus "collaborated on a model of guided discovery" in science for young children.
6. Engagement (p. 8-9): activities "make connections to past experiences and expose students' misconceptions". "Asking a question, defining a problem, showing a discrepant event, and acting out a problematic situation are all ways to engage the students".
7. Exploration (p. 9): "This phase should be concrete and hands on." "The teacher's role in the exploration phase is that of facilitator or coach."
8. Explanation (p. 9): "First, the teacher asks the students to give their explanations. Second, the teacher introduces scientific or technological explanations in a direct, explicit, and formal manner."
9. Elaboration (p. 10), quoting Champagne (1987): "During the group discussion, students present and defend their approaches to the instructional task." The report adds that group discussions let students "receive feedback from others who are very close to their own level of understanding."
10. Evaluation (p. 10): students "evaluate their understanding" and "receive feedback on the adequacy of their explanations."
11. Laboratory experiences (p. 16-17), quoting the National Research Council (2006): laboratory experiences let students "interact directly with the material world (or with data drawn from the material world), using tools, data collection techniques, models, and theories of science." Goals listed: "Enhancing mastery of subject matter; Developing scientific reasoning; Understanding the complexity and ambiguity of empirical work; Developing practical skills; Understanding the nature of science; Cultivating interest in science and interest in learning science; Developing teamwork abilities".
12. Integrated instructional units (p. 17): they "interweave laboratory experiences with other types of science learning activities, including lectures, reading, and discussion." "Research shows that typical laboratories suffer from fragmentation of goals and approaches." Table 9 (p. 18): for mastery of subject matter, a typical laboratory experience "Is no better or worse than other modes of instruction", while integrated units increase mastery.
13. Direct instruction and discovery learning (p. 18): "At its extreme, direct instruction relies on lecturing and rote memorization. At the other end of the continuum is discovery learning or full inquiry." "In reality, most teaching strategies are somewhere in the middle of the continuum."
14. Klahr studies (p. 18-21): Chen and Klahr (1999) had "children aged seven to 10 years old design and evaluate experiments"; "with explicit training (i.e., direct instruction), children were able to learn and transfer the basic strategy for designing unconfounded experiments". In Klahr and Nigam's discovery condition "there is no teacher intervention beyond the suggestion of a learning objective: no guiding questions and no feedback". The 5E model "incorporate[s] direct instruction in one phase in an integrated instructional model."
