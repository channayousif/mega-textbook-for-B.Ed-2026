Full citation: Rowe, M. B. (1974). Wait-time and rewards as instructional variables, their influence on language, logic, and fate control: Part one - Wait-time. *Journal of Research in Science Teaching, 11*(2), 81-94. https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.3660110202
Verified open access: ERIC EJ098336 / OpenAlex W2057396653, confirmed 2026-11-27.

## Key Excerpts on Wait-Time 1 and Wait-Time 2

### Definition and Baseline Duration
"Wait-time may be defined as the length of time a teacher pauses after asking a question before calling on a student, repeating, or rephrasing the question (Wait-time 1), and the pause after a student response before the teacher speaks or another student speaks (Wait-time 2). In baseline studies of science classrooms, the mean duration of Wait-time 1 was approximately one second, and Wait-time 2 was often less than one second." (p. 82)

### Consequences of Increasing Wait-Time to 3-5 Seconds
"When teachers lengthened the mean wait-time to three seconds or longer, a series of interrelated changes in student verbal behavior occurred:
1. The length of student responses increased threefold.
2. The number of unsolicited but appropriate student responses increased.
3. Failures to respond decreased.
4. Confidence, as reflected in fewer inflected responses (tentative questioning tones), increased.
5. The incidence of speculative responses increased.
6. Student-to-student interactions increased; classroom discourse shifted from a hub-and-spoke teacher monopoly to peer exchange.
7. The frequency of student questions increased.
8. Students previously identified by teachers as 'slow' or non-participating increased their verbal contributions." (pp. 83-85)
