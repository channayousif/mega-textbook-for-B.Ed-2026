# Bound source evidence: kilpatrick-1918

Source: Kilpatrick, W. H. (1918). The project method. *Teachers College Record, 19*(4), 319-335. Reprinted as a booklet by Teachers College, Columbia University (eleventh impression, 1929). Public domain.
URL: https://education-uk.org/documents/kilpatrick1918/index.html (transcription of the booklet); scan at https://archive.org/details/projectmethodus00kilpgoog

Consulted: 2026-11-27. The transcription was read in full; the site's note describes Kilpatrick as "a pupil of John Dewey". Page numbers are the booklet's.

## Excerpts

1. p. 4: the unifying idea is "wholehearted purposeful activity proceeding in a social environment, or more briefly, in the unit element of such activity, the hearty purposeful act." "It is to this purposeful act with the emphasis on the word purpose that I myself apply the term 'project'."
2. p. 16: four types of project, the third being "where the purpose is to straighten out some intellectual difficulty, to solve some problem"; "the project method logically includes the problem method as a special case."
3. p. 17: "For type 1 the following steps have been suggested: purposing, planning, executing, and judging. It is in accord with the general theory here advocated that the child as far as possible take each step himself. Total failure, however, may hurt more than assistance." "The teacher must steer the child through these narrows".

## Unit 3 use

All three excerpts support Topic 3.4 (U3-07): the definition of a project, its steps and the teacher's steering role.
