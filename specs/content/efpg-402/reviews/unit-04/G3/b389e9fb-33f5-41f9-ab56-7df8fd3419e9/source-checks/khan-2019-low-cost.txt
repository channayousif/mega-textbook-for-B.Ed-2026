- Authors: Saifullah Khan (Northern University Nowshera), Rehmat Ali Farooq, Nilsa Fleury (York College).
- The article is diamond open access; the full text was read in full for this review.

Abstract (verbatim, PDF p. 33):
"The equipment's of science teaching in the most of secondary school is not available in
Pakistan. The main purpose of this research is to find the effectiveness of low cost materials
on diverse achievers in the physics instructing at secondary level. The nature of the study was
experimental. ... The group which has been instructed with low cost teaching aids showed
successful result scores in the posttest and their achievement level further gets improved."

Method (PDF p. 40): pre-test/post-test equivalent-group design; 40 Grade X pupils of Government
High School No.2 Nowshera Cantt; six/seven weeks; retention test after two months.

Results (PDF p. 43):
- Table 3, low achievers post-test: experimental mean 52.63 (SD 12.06) vs control 36.72 (SD 2.14),
  t = 4.31 > table value 1.725. "students in the experimental group learned better than the
  students of control group."
- Table 4, high achievers post-test: experimental mean 76.77 (SD 4.02) vs control 53 (SD 5.70),
  t = 10.22 > table value 1.746.

Conclusions (PDF p. 44):
a) "Using of low cost materials is found to be more fruitful for teaching science especially
    physics contrary to conventional/traditional teaching."
b) "low achiever students of the experimental group showed brilliant performance over the control
    group as they were instructed with the help of low cost materials and took keen in interest in
    the learning process."
c) "The use of low cost materials also made an impact in the achievement of high achiever
    students ..."

Retention: the study administered a retention test two months after the post-test (PDF p. 40).
The results section reproduced above reports post-test comparisons; the literature review states
that improvisation "ensures a longer retention of the information gained" and "increases the
retention margin" (PDF p. 35). No retention-result table appears in the extracted text.

Verdict against the unit:
- topic-03.mdx:44 "significantly higher conceptual comprehension, greater curiosity, and superior
  retention" is supported in substance by (a), (b) and (c) and by the retention test, but
  "superior retention" is a literature-review/general claim rather than a reported retention
  result of this experiment. Advisory only.
- RRQ 6 and ERQ 5 use the source accurately.
