## RRQs (independent solution, compared with the model answers)

1. Two manipulative skills: reading a meniscus at eye level without parallax, and safe handling of
   hot glassware or a burner with clamps and wire gauze; neither is built by reading. Matches the
   model answer and the 3-mark scheme.
2. Two low-cost alternatives: mobile science kit boxes carried to ordinary desks, and a teacher
   demonstration station with a spill tray and water container, optionally plus outdoor inquiry.
   Matches the model answer and the 3-mark scheme.
3. Dual outward-opening exits: a second exit at the opposite end gives redundancy when fire or a
   spill blocks the first, and outward swing prevents the crowd crushing against the doorframe.
   Matches the 3-mark scheme.
4. Master controls in a locked rear room: the teacher cannot cut gas or power in the first seconds
   of a flash fire, shock or leak, and unlocking a room adds fatal delay. Matches the 3-mark scheme.
5. Alphabetical storage ignores chemical reactivity, so incompatible families sit side by side (the
   unit's example is acetic acid beside ammonium nitrate); a break or vapour leak can fire or poison.
   Matches the 3-mark scheme.
6. Copper sulfate is a persistent heavy-metal toxin that poisons aquatic life and sewage bacteria;
   collect it in a labelled carboy, precipitate it as an insoluble carbonate or hydroxide, filter and
   store the sludge for certified disposal. Matches the 3-mark scheme.
7. Permanent register: durable multi-year assets (microscopes, balances, retort stands). Consumable
   register: items exhausted in experiments (acid, filter paper, indicators). Matches the 3-mark
   scheme.
8. Unlabelled bottles risk poisoning, violent reactions from mixing unknowns, and audit failure;
   isolate, have an experienced chemist characterise, and dispose through certified hazardous waste.
   Matches the 3-mark scheme.
9. Cool water dissipates residual heat, halts deeper tissue destruction and numbs nerve endings;
   grease or butter traps heat and contaminates the wound. Matches the 3-mark scheme.
10. Passive desk supervision leaves unsafe handling uncorrected and loses formative questioning and
    technique coaching. Matches the 3-mark scheme.

  memorisation with empirical inquiry and the cognitive, psychomotor and affective gains. Four-level
  analytic rubric, 10 marks. Satisfies the blueprint.
- ERQ 2 (Analyze): five observed hazards (single blocked inward door, 0.8 m aisles, incompatible
  chemicals over the sink, distant locked first aid, no spectacles), risk mechanisms and a ranked
  corrective plan. This is the blueprint's required Analyze item. Four-level analytic rubric.
- ERQ 3 (Evaluate): a chemical storage and waste-disposal policy manual with compatibility groups,
  storage specifications and neutralisation/heavy-metal SOPs. Four-level analytic rubric.
- ERQ 4 (Create): an inventory control and GHS labelling system with permanent, consumable and
  breakage registers, annual audit and condemnation. Four-level analytic rubric.
- ERQ 5 (Create): an annual laboratory management framework spanning pre-lab, live supervision and
  post-lab accountability plus a weekly maintenance schedule. Four-level analytic rubric.
