## Evidence notes

1. Unit 1, Week 1 (pp. 31-34): Classroom assessment is defined as a continuous, collaborative process of gathering, analysing and interpreting evidence about student learning. It is broader than testing and includes informal observations, classroom dialogue, questioning, pupil work samples and teacher-made tests.
2. Unit 1, Week 1 (pp. 31-35): The guide contrasts assessment with evaluation and testing. A test is a specific instrument or systematic procedure for measuring a sample of behaviour (typically yielding a numerical score). Measurement is the quantitative description of student performance (assigning numbers according to rules). Assessment encompasses all methods used to collect information on learning. Evaluation is the qualitative judgment of value, worth or adequacy based on that information, often leading to decisions such as grading, certification or curricular revision.
3. Unit 1, Week 1 (pp. 31-36): Purpose and importance of assessment in Pakistani education: assessment serves to inform instruction, identify learning needs, provide feedback to learners, guide curricular decisions, and support accountability. Effective assessment is embedded in the teaching-learning process rather than standing apart as an isolated terminal event.
4. Unit 1, Week 2 (pp. 37-46): Formative versus summative assessment: Formative assessment (assessment for learning) occurs during the instructional process. Its primary purpose is to identify gaps and guide teacher adaptations and student self-correction while learning is still underway. Its stakes are low, and it emphasises constructive, actionable feedback rather than marks. Summative assessment (assessment of learning) occurs at the end of a unit, term or course. Its purpose is to evaluate and report cumulative mastery, assign grades, or certify achievement, carrying higher stakes for the student.
5. Unit 1, Week 2 & Unit 2 (pp. 47-55): Diagnostic and placement assessment: Diagnostic assessment identifies specific learning difficulties, persistent errors or misconceptions that impede progress, informing remedial support. Placement assessment determines the prerequisite entry knowledge, skills or track suitable for a learner prior to beginning instruction.
