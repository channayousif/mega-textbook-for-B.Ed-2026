## RRQs - derived answers

1. **Marking versus grading:** Marking decides marks earned against a scoring guide (diagnostic, granular). Grading categorises marks into shared symbols (compact, communicable). Example needing marks: diagnosing specific errors for lesson planning. Example needing grades: promotion decisions. Matches mark scheme (3 marks).
2. **Two identical percentages:** Percentages are paper-specific measurements. A score of 68 percent on a challenging paper demonstrates higher achievement than 68 percent on a simple recall paper. Cohort and test difficulty differ. Matches mark scheme (2 marks).
3. **Bare report card consequences:** For the pupil: lack of a comparator leaves standing unclear; absence of feedback provides no actionable next steps. For the parent: unable to identify specific strengths/weaknesses or know how to support learning at home. Matches mark scheme (3 marks).
4. **Measures of centre:** Mean is arithmetic average (sum / count). Median is middle value in ordered distribution. Mode is most frequent value. Matches mark scheme (3 marks).
5. **Mean misleading high-scoring parents:** (1) Hides spread: parent cannot tell if pupil is part of a cluster or an outlier. (2) Hides distribution shape: skewness or bimodality is flattened into an unrepresentative centre. Matches mark scheme (2 marks).
6. **Standard deviation interpretation:** SD = 3 indicates tight clustering near 60 (homogeneous readiness). SD = 12 indicates wide dispersion (heterogeneous readiness requiring differentiated instruction and multiple entry points). Matches mark scheme (3 marks).
7. **Difficulty index formula and meaning:** p = correct / attempted. A value of 0.90 indicates 90 percent answered correctly (easy item for that group). Matches mark scheme (2 marks).
8. **p = 0.95 and D = 0.05:** An item answered correctly by nearly everyone cannot discriminate between high and low scorers. It adds zero reliability or separation to a rank order while consuming testing time. Matches mark scheme (3 marks).
9. **Three groups and remainder:** Rank by total score; partition into upper (top third), middle (middle third), and lower (bottom third). If not evenly divisible by three, assign remainders equally to top and bottom groups, leaving the middle group slightly smaller. Matches mark scheme (3 marks).
10. **Negative discrimination causes:** Possible causes include miskeyed answer / incorrect scoring guide, or ambiguous wording that misleads high achievers into an alternate interpretation while low achievers guess the keyed option. Check scoring guide first because it is simplest to verify and fix. Matches mark scheme (4 marks).
