## Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. **Q:** Define an instructional objective in one sentence, and explain why an observable verb is essential. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** An instructional objective is an explicit statement of what learners will be able to do as a result of instruction, stated in observable and measurable terms. An observable action verb is essential because internal mental states cannot be directly seen, so a teacher requires observable pupil performance to determine whether the objective was achieved.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark for correct definition; 1 mark for stating the verb must be observable; 1 mark for explaining why with an example. Fully aligns.
2. **Q:** Explain the difference between the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains, and give one classroom example of each. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** The cognitive domain focuses on intellectual outcomes and knowledge processing (e.g. explaining the water cycle); the affective domain focuses on attitudes, values, and emotional disposition (e.g. demonstrating active listening and respect in peer critique); the psychomotor domain focuses on physical, manipulative, or motor skills (e.g. properly focusing a microscope or titrating a solution).
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark for each domain correctly described with a fitting example (max 3-4 marks). Fully aligns.
3. **Q:** A teacher's objective says "pupils will appreciate the importance of trees", her lesson has pupils copy a list of uses of trees, and her test asks pupils to design a poster campaigning to save trees. Analyse where the alignment breaks and what the pupil's score can and cannot support. *(Analyze, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Alignment breaks twice: first between the vague affective objective ("appreciate") and passive cognitive copying activity; second between passive copying and the high-level creative synthesis demanded by the test item ("design a poster"). A pupil's score can support inferences about graphic poster design skills, but cannot support inferences regarding environmental appreciation or deep conceptual understanding of ecological conservation.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for objective-activity mismatch; 1 for activity-item mismatch; 1 for score inference analysis. Fully aligns.
4. **Q:** Explain the difference between content validity and construct validity, and give one example of a threat to each in a Pakistani classroom. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Content validity examines whether a test adequately samples the domain of knowledge and skills taught in the curriculum. Construct validity examines whether the test accurately measures the theoretical trait or psychological construct it purports to measure. A threat to content validity is omitting 4 out of 5 syllabus chapters from an end-of-term test. A threat to construct validity is writing a science examination in dense, archaic English such that it measures English reading comprehension rather than scientific reasoning.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark per correct definition; 1 mark for fitting examples of threats. Fully aligns.
5. **Q:** Explain test-retest reliability and inter-rater reliability, and state one practical way a teacher can strengthen each. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Test-retest reliability measures the consistency of scores when the same test is administered to the same students at two different points in time; a teacher strengthens it by writing clear, unambiguous prompts and standardizing administration conditions. Inter-rater reliability measures the agreement between two or more independent markers scoring the same work; a teacher strengthens it by developing and applying an analytic scoring rubric with explicit criteria and performance descriptors.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 mark per definition; 1 mark for practical strengthening steps. Fully aligns.
6. **Q:** A Class 7 mathematics test has 30 items, of which 18 test only one of the five units taught. Identify the validity problem, explain its likely effect on the meaning of the scores, and state one step that would strengthen the test. *(Analyze, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** The validity problem is poor content validity caused by severe domain under-representation and disproportionate weighting. The effect is that total scores will artificially inflate performance for students who mastered only that single unit, distorting diagnostic and summative inferences about overall course mastery. To strengthen the test, the teacher should construct a Table of Specifications (test blueprint) distributing marks proportionally across all five units.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for identifying weak content validity; 1 for score distortion effect; 1 for Table of Specifications / blueprint step. Fully aligns.
7. **Q:** Define practicality in assessment and name three resources a teacher should check before choosing a test format. *(Understand, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Practicality refers to the feasibility of administering, scoring, and interpreting an assessment given realistic institutional and operational constraints. Three resources to check: available teacher and pupil time (for testing and marking), staffing/invigilation capacity, and material/budget resources (such as printing facilities, paper, apparatus, or electricity).
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for definition; 2 marks for three concrete resources described. Fully aligns.
8. **Q:** A Class 6 science item asks pupils to "explain how a refrigerator keeps food cold" in a school where no pupil has seen a refrigerator. Identify the source of bias, explain which pupils are disadvantaged, and rewrite the item so that it measures the same skill. *(Analyze, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** The source of bias is socioeconomic and experiential/cultural bias. Disadvantaged pupils are rural or underprivileged students who lack exposure to domestic refrigeration technology, causing an irrelevant barrier to testing their grasp of heat transfer/cooling principles. A fair rewrite assessing the same scientific concept of evaporative cooling: "Explain how storing water in an earthen clay pot (matka) keeps the water cool during hot summer days."
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for identifying bias; 1 for naming disadvantaged group; 1 for rewritten item preserving concept. Fully aligns.
9. **Q:** Explain why giving every pupil exactly the same paper under exactly the same conditions is not always fair, and give one example of a legitimate allowance. *(Analyze, 3 marks)*
   - **Derived:** Fairness does not mean identical treatment; treating all pupils identically ignores construct-irrelevant barriers (e.g. sensory, linguistic, or physical motor challenges) that prevent students from demonstrating actual competency. A legitimate allowance is providing enlarged font text to a visually impaired student or granting additional time to a student with written motor limitations, removing the extraneous barrier without altering the underlying skill assessed.
   - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for fairness principle; 1 for barrier illustration; 1 for legitimate allowance. Fully aligns.
10. **Q:** A head teacher sets one two-hour written examination for every subject in Class 5, with no coursework and no practical tasks. Analyse one practical problem and one fairness problem with this decision. *(Analyze, 3 marks)*
    - **Derived:** Practical problem: Enormous marking backlog and invigilation fatigue for solitary teachers in large multi-grade classes, consuming instructional time. Fairness problem: Disadvantages 10-year-old primary pupils whose physical handwriting stamina, reading speed, or test anxiety impede demonstration of subject understanding, completely excluding practical, oral, and continuous learning contributions.
    - **Model answer & mark scheme:** 1 for practical problem; 1 for fairness problem naming affected pupils; 1 for detailed explanation. Fully aligns.
