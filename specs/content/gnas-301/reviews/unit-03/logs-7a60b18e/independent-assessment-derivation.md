3. Stem: "In the air pollution chain, 'school closures on hazardous days' acts at the: (Apply)"

   - Key: 3. c (Match)

4. Stem: "Every 10 decibels of increase represents: (Remember)"

   - Key: 4. a (Match)

## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

1. Temperature inversion and smog:
   - Derivation: A warm atmospheric layer sits above cool surface air, preventing vertical convection and turbulent mixing. Emissions (particulates, vehicle and industrial smoke) are physically trapped near the ground.
   - Model answer match: 2-sentence explanation verified.

2. "Wind will clean the air" is a weak control:
   - Derivation: 1) Wind merely transports pollutants downwind to another community without reducing the mass of emissions; 2) During seasonal calm periods or inversions, wind is absent and cannot disperse pollutants.
   - Model answer match: 2 reasons verified.

3. Four noise health impacts (only one hearing loss):
   - Derivation: Disabling hearing loss (auditory), elevated blood pressure/cardiovascular stress, sleep disturbance, impaired cognitive performance/reading comprehension in children.
   - Model answer match: 4 impacts verified.

4. Noise control for generator outside classroom:
   - Derivation: Source: acoustic enclosure, vibration damping or silencer; Pathway: sound-absorbing barrier or increasing distance; Receptor: rescheduling reading lessons to generator-off periods or moving students to a shielded room.
   - Model answer match: 1 control at each link, 3 marks total.

5. Distinguish hazard from incident with school example:
   - Derivation: Hazard is the potential source of harm (e.g. bare electrical wire on a wall); incident is an event where harm actually occurred or nearly occurred (e.g. child touched wire and suffered electric shock or near miss).
   - Model answer match: Verified.

6. Five hazard classes with flood-camp example:
   - Derivation: Physical (extreme heat or sun exposure), mechanical (slippery muddy ground causing slips/falls), biological (contaminated standing water / mosquitoes), chemical (chlorine bleaching agent fumes or diesel spill), psychological (acute displacement stress, exhaustion or grief).
   - Model answer match: Verified.

7. Why psychological harm belongs on hazard register:
   - Derivation: Psychological stressors cause measurable somatic and physiological injury (cardiovascular disease, clinical depression, chronic exhaustion) and arise from environmental conditions just like physical or biological agents.
   - Model answer match: Verified.

8. Define risk and its two axes:
   - Derivation: Risk is the probability and seriousness of harm resulting from hazard exposure. Two axes: Likelihood (frequency/chance) and Severity (consequence/impact).
   - Model answer match: Verified.

9. Why PPE sits at bottom of hierarchy:
   - Derivation: PPE does not remove the hazard from the environment; it protects only the individual user, depends completely on correct fit and human compliance, and offers zero protection if removed or damaged.
   - Model answer match: Verified.

10. Cracked overhead sign vs splintered shelf in locked store:
    - Derivation: Overhead sign in crowded entrance has high likelihood and high severity (falls on students), demanding immediate action. Splintered shelf in locked store has low likelihood and low severity. Immediate control: cordon off entrance area and redirect pupils until repair is completed.
    - Model answer match: Verified.
