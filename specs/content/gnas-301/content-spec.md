## Course Description

The guide describes the course as building a comprehensive understanding of the interactions
between humans and their environment, with particular focus on environmental challenges,
climate change, and their implications for human health and sustainability
(`1st 2026.txt:152-163`). It runs from the structure and functioning of ecosystems and natural
resource management, through pollution (air, water, soil), waste management, occupational
health and safety, and risk assessment, to toxicology, environmental laws, international
agreements and the Sustainable Development Goals, at global, regional and local levels
including Pakistan. Its stated aim for future educators is environmental awareness,
responsibility, and informed decision-making: the ability to analyze environmental problems,
apply public health tools, and propose effective solutions.

Two consequences for authoring, both load-bearing:

1. **This is a science course taught to future teachers, not to environmental engineers.**
   Every technical concept (effluent treatment, bioaccumulation, risk assessment) must land in
   HSC-register prose with a Pakistan-grounded example, and wherever possible the worked
   example is one a trainee teacher can repeat in a school or neighbourhood: reading an air
   quality index, auditing a school's waste, spotting hazards in a school building.
2. **The course has two halves with different characters.** Weeks 1-6 (Units 1-3) are
   environmental science: ecosystems, resources, pollution, hazards. Weeks 8-16 (Units 4-6)
   move through occupational safety management and toxicology to global governance and climate.
   Unit 4 is the most list-like stretch of the guide (Week 10 alone carries eight sub-topics);
   the authoring risk there is a glossary recital, and the countermeasure is to hold every
   sub-topic to the same worked-example discipline as the rest of the course.

## Standards & frameworks anchors

The guide names these frameworks directly (SDGs at 12.2; multilateral environmental agreements
at 12.1; environmental laws at 12.3), and the subject matter engages the rest:

- **UN Sustainable Development Goals (2015)** - Unit 5's governance spine, especially SDG 3
  (health), 6 (water and sanitation), 7 (energy), 11 (cities), 12 (responsible consumption),
  13 (climate action) and 15 (life on land); the course review returns to them.
- **Multilateral environmental agreements** - Unit 5's 12.1: the UNFCCC and Paris Agreement
  (2015), the Stockholm Convention on POPs (2001), the Basel Convention (1989), the Rotterdam
  Convention (1998), the Minamata Convention on mercury (2013) and the Montreal Protocol
  (1987) as named anchors for the timeline figure.
- **Pakistan's environmental law framework** - Unit 5's 12.3 and Unit 4's statutes: the Pakistan
  Environmental Protection Act 1997 and the National Environmental Quality Standards (NEQS)
  it enables; provincial environmental protection acts post-2010 devolution; the National
  Climate Change Policy (2012, updated) and Pakistan's Nationally Determined Contributions.
- **WHO global air quality guidelines (2021)** - Unit 3's air-pollution yardstick and Unit 6's
  smog material.
- **ILO occupational safety and health framework** - Unit 4: Convention No. 155 (occupational
  safety and health), Convention No. 161 (occupational health services), and ISO 45001:2018
  as the management-system standard.
- **No framework is invented where the guide is silent.** The guide does not name a teacher-
  professionalism standard for this course; none is imported from other courses.

## Course review plan

Seeds an eventual `docs/semester-1/gnas-301/course-review.mdx` (Spec 008 FR-016; contract:
`specs/008-rich-unit-pedagogy/contracts/end-of-course-review.md`). Not parsed by any gate.
Authoring the file itself is follow-up work outside this feature.

- **Course summary points** (the through-lines the review should recap):
  - Environmental science studies the human-environment interaction at every scale, and the
    local scale (a school, a street, a canal in Sindh) is where a teacher can act (Unit 1).
  - Water and waste are managed systems, not facts of nature: pollution has sources, pathways
    and remedies, and so does every bin and drain (Unit 2).
  - Air and noise pollution and every workplace hazard follow the same logic: identify the
    hazard, assess the risk, control it, monitor (Units 3 and 4).
  - Safety is a managed property of organisations - plans, duties, statutes and tools - not
    a matter of individual carefulness (Unit 4).
  - A toxin's story runs from emission through absorption, biotransformation and sometimes
    bioaccumulation to effect, and risk management is the response at every step (Units 5-6).
  - Climate change is the course's integrator: the greenhouse effect, smog, mitigation and
    adaptation all reappear in the SDGs and the agreements that close the course (Units 5-6).
- **Practice-question mix**: `### MCQs` ~18 (all six units, Remember to Apply); `### RRQs` ~12
  (Understand to Analyze, >= 1 per unit); `### ERQs` ~6 (Analyze to Evaluate/Create, each
  integrating two or more units).
- **Practicum project ideas** (3-6 briefs a trainee carries into placement):
  - *School environment walk* - a structured hazard-and-resource audit of the placement school
    (building, water point, waste corner, nearest road); bring back the annotated map.
  - *Waste segregation trial* - design and run a two-week segregation scheme for one classroom
    block; bring back the before/after tallies and what had to change.
  - *Air and noise diary* - one week of daily AQI and noise observations at fixed school-day
    times; bring back the readings and a one-page pattern analysis.
  - *Safety induction checklist* - write and trial a ten-point safety induction for the school
    laboratory or craft room; bring back the checklist and a colleague's feedback.
  - *Climate action page* - a one-page, classroom-ready brief on one climate impact already
    visible in the school's district and two feasible school-level responses.

  asking the learner to take one local issue and trace it to its regional and global
  connections. Weighting: the course-specific 20/80 above, not the Art. III.7 default.

**Depth budget**: 12 sub-topics; 6 topics; 90-115 reading-min

**Prerequisite knowledge**: Units 1-5 (the whole course: ecosystems, pollution, hazards, risk
management, atmospheric agents and the fate pathway). This is the integrative closing unit and
may freely recall earlier material.

**Common misconceptions**: "the greenhouse effect is itself a problem" (it is natural and keeps
Earth warm; the enhanced greenhouse effect is the problem); "smog is just fog"; "natural
substances are always safe and synthetic ones always dangerous"; "anything can be poisonous or
nothing is - it depends only on the dose" (dose matters, but some agents have no safe
threshold, and the same substance can be essential at one dose and toxic at another);
"mitigation and adaptation are alternatives" (both are needed).

**Mapped readings**: haines-frumkin (verified real, print: title-level support only),
holland-oxford (flagged, title-level only). The G2 floor of two verifiable open-access
bindings applies (IPCC and WHO sources are natural candidates).

**Worked-examples plan** (one Pakistan-grounded example per sub-topic): introduction and
classes - "the dose makes the poison" tested against lead (no safe level) and salt (essential
at low dose, harmful high) (U6-01, U6-02); poisons - a natural one (snake venom or arsenic in
Sindh's groundwater) beside an anthropogenic one (pesticides on a cotton field) (U6-03);
effects - acute (pesticide poisoning at spraying time) vs chronic (arsenic in drinking water
over years) from the same district (U6-04); special effects - the carcinogen and teratogen
ideas held at mechanism level with the Minamata mercury story as the bioaccumulation bridge
(U6-05); pollutant biology and essentiality - iron, fluoride and arsenic as the same
essentiality-toxicity ladder at different doses (U6-06, U6-07); absorption routes - a farm
worker taking in the same pesticide by skin, breath and hand-to-mouth, and why the route
changes the dose (U6-08); bioaccumulation - the plankton-to-fish-to-family ladder in a Sindh
lake (U6-09); greenhouse and warming - the blanket mechanism, the main gases and Pakistan's
own emissions profile in brief (U6-10); smog - a Lahore winter episode as sulfurous-plus-
photochemical smog read through its causes and controls (U6-11); climate change - the 2015
Karachi heatwave and the 2022 floods classified by impact type and answered with mitigation
(tree plantation, energy) and adaptation (heat plans, flood-proof schools) (U6-12).

**International best-practice notes**: teach dose-response before any substance catalogue,
and hold the "no safe level" cases (lead, carcinogens) against the "essential substance"
cases (iron, fluoride) so neither becomes a slogan; keep the greenhouse mechanism physical and
short (shortwave in, longwave out, some trapped) before any politics; use IPCC assessment
language for climate findings (observed, attributed, projected) rather than newspaper
certainty; end the course on agency - what a teacher and a school can actually do - not on
despair.
