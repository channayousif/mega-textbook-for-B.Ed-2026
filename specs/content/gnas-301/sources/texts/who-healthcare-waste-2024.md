World Health Organization. (2024). *Health-care waste* (fact sheet, 24 October 2024).
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/health-care-waste

Open access (WHO publisher page). Verified by direct fetch from who.int on 2026-09-23; the

## Key facts (verified, excerpt)

- About 85% of waste from health-care activities is general, non-hazardous waste comparable
  to household waste; the remaining 15% is hazardous material that may be infectious,
  toxic, carcinogen, flammable, corrosive, reactive, explosive or radioactive.
- Roughly 16 billion injections are given worldwide each year, and not all needles and
  syringes are safely discarded afterward.
- Waste categories: infectious, pathological, sharps, chemical, pharmaceutical/cytotoxic,
  radioactive, and non-hazardous/general waste.
- WHO recommends waste minimisation and segregation, and alternatives to incineration where
  feasible (autoclaving, microwaving, steam treatment, chemical treatment).
- In 2021, only 61% of hospitals had basic health-care waste services.

## Further passages (verified by direct fetch 2026-11-03, verbatim)

Bound after the 2026-11-02 G3 report (20261102T212207Z-g3-desk-bc87d518) found the
handling rules in Topic 2.3 unsupported by the key facts alone.

- Key facts: "Open burning and low-temperature incineration of health care wastes can, under
  some circumstances, result in the emission of dioxins, furans and particulate matter."
- Sharps-related: "Additional hazards occur from scavenging at waste disposal sites and
  during the handling and manual sorting of hazardous waste from health-care facilities.
  [...] The waste handlers are at immediate risk of needle-stick injuries and exposure to
  toxic or infectious materials."
- Waste management, reasons for failure: "Many countries either do not have appropriate
  regulations, or do not monitor and enforce them."
- The way forward, key elements: "promoting practices that reduce the volume of wastes
  generated and ensure proposer waste segregation"; "where feasible, favouring the safe and
  environmentally sound treatment of hazardous health care wastes (e.g. by autoclaving,
  microwaving, steam treatment integrated with internal mixing, and chemical treatment) over
  medical waste incineration"; "selecting safe and environmentally friendly management
  options, to protect people from hazards when collecting, handling, storing, transporting,
  treating or disposing of waste."

## What this supports in GNAS-301 Unit 2

Topic 2.3 (U2-05) cites this source for the 85/15 general-versus-hazardous split of
health-care waste, the risk-waste categories (sharps, infectious, pathological,
pharmaceutical, chemical), the WHO key elements (minimise, segregate, handle, store and
transport safely, treat before disposal), the preference for non-incineration treatment where
feasible, and the immediate risk to waste handlers and manual sorters. It does **not**
support container colours, sharps-box specifications, licensing of handlers or industrial
manifests; Topic 2.3 now refers those details to provincial law and the facility's own waste
plan rather than stating them, and no longer ranks who is most at risk. The unit does not
rely on this source for any figure it does not state here.
