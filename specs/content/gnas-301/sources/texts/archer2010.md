## Abstract (verified, excerpt)

Pakistan is highly dependent on water resources originating in the mountain sources of the
upper Indus for irrigated agriculture which is the mainstay of its economy. Hence any change
in available resources through climate change or socio-economic factors could have a serious
impact on food security and the environment. In terms of both ratio of withdrawals to runoff
and per-capita water availability, Pakistan's water resources are already highly stressed
and will become increasingly so with projected population changes. Potential changes to
supply through declining reservoir storage, the impact of waterlogging and salinity or
over-abstraction of groundwater, or reallocations for environmental remediation of the Indus
Delta or to meet domestic demands, will reduce water availability for irrigation. ...
