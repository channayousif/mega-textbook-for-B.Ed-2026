# Bound excerpt - pakepa-neqs-2000

Pakistan Environmental Protection Agency. (2000). *National Environmental Quality Standards
for municipal and liquid industrial effluents* (S.R.O. 549(I)/2000, Annex-I). The Gazette
of Pakistan, Extraordinary, 10 August 2000.
https://mocc.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/National%20Environmental%20Quality%20Standards%20for%20Municipal%20and%20Liquid%20Industrioal%20Effluents.pdf

Open access (government gazette PDF on the Ministry of Climate Change site). Verified by
direct fetch on 2026-11-03. Bound after the 2026-11-02 G3 report
(20261102T212207Z-g3-desk-bc87d518, SOURCE-01) found the NEQS yardstick sentence in
Topic 2.2 without a primary legal passage.

## Passages (verified, excerpt)

- Preamble: "S.R.O. 549 (I)/2000. In exercise of the powers conferred under clause (c) of
  sub-section (1) of section of 6 of the Pakistan environmental Protection Act. 1997 [...]
  the Pakistan Environmental Protection Agency [...] is pleased to direct that the following
  further amendments shall be made in its Notification No. S.R.O. 742(I)/93".
- Annex-I heading: "NATIONAL ENVIRONMENTAL QUALITY STANDARDS FOR MUNICIPAL AND LIQUID
  INDUSTRIAL EFFLUENTS (mg/l, UNLESS OTHERWISE DEFINED)".
- Annex-I columns: revised standards "Into Inland Waters", "Into Sewage Treatment" and "Into
  Sea", one row per parameter (32 parameters, including BOD, COD, TSS, TDS and the metals).
- Row 18: "Chromium (trivalent and hexavalent)": 1.0 in each column.
- Note 1: "Dilution of liquid effluents to bring them to the NEQS limiting values is not
  permissible through fresh water mixing with the effluent before discharging into the
  environment."

## What this supports in GNAS-301 Unit 2

Topic 2.2 (U2-03) cites this source for the NEQS as the discharge yardstick, the structure
of the limits (maximum concentration per parameter in mg/L, separate columns by receiving
water), the chromium limit of 1.0 mg/L and the ban on diluting effluent with fresh water.
The unit does not state whether a province has since notified its own standards; that is
outside this excerpt.
