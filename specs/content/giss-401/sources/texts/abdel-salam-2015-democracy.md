## U5-04

From Section "The Principle of Shura in Governance" (pp. 112-114):

"Shura (mutual consultation) is an essential constitutional imperative in Islamic political ethics, explicitly rooted in Surah Ash-Shura (42:38) and Surah Ali 'Imran (3:159). Far from being an optional administrative courtesy, consultation binds political authorities to seek deliberation with the community and qualified scholars (Ahl al-Hall wal-'Aqd). Shura prevents executive autocracy and ensures that public policies reflect collective wisdom and the broader public interest (maslahah)."

## U5-05

From Section "Rule of Law, Justice, and Accountability" (pp. 115-117):

"In the Islamic system of governance, ultimate sovereignty (Hakimiyyah) belongs to Allah, which in constitutional terms establishes the supremacy of objective moral and legal standards above any human ruler. Executive authority is an amanah (trust) subject to institutional accountability (muhasabah). No leader or state official possesses immunity from the law; all individuals are subject to identical judicial standards of justice (adl), ensuring the institutional independence of courts and equal protection under the law."

## U5-06

From Section "Citizenship, Pluralism, and Minority Rights" (pp. 120-122):

"Islamic constitutional traditions provide established legal frameworks for religious pluralism and the protection of minority communities. Grounded in the Quranic declaration that there is no compulsion in religion (2:256) and the historical precedent of the Charter of Madinah (Mithaq al-Madinah), non-Muslim citizens are recognized as full civic partners with protected status (dhimmah). They are guaranteed complete freedom of religious conscience, internal administrative and judicial autonomy in family law, inviolability of places of worship, and equal protection of life and property."
