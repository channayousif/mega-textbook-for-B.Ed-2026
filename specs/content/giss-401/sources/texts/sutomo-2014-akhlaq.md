## Key arguments and text extracts

Sutomo examines the conceptual and pedagogical integration of modern character education frameworks (such as Lickona's moral knowing, moral feeling, and moral action) with Islamic traditions of *akhlaq* (virtue ethics).

### Core principles utilized in Unit 3:

1. **Definition of Akhlaq**:
   The article defines *akhlaq* (plural of *khuluq*) as a deeply rooted state of the soul or internal disposition from which deeds proceed spontaneously and smoothly, without necessitating prior mental deliberation or forced exertion. It transcends mere external conformity to rules.

2. **Integration of Knowing, Feeling, and Action**:
   Character education cannot succeed as purely cognitive rule-instruction. Sutomo argues that *akhlaq* education requires a tripartite synthesis:
   - *Moral knowing* (*ilm*): Understanding moral principles, discernment of right and wrong, and ethical reasoning.
   - *Moral feeling* (*hal* / *niyyah*): Empathy, love of goodness, reverent awe before God (taqwa), and emotional commitment to moral values.
   - *Moral action* (*amal*): Habitual, voluntary performance of virtues in real social settings.

3. **Habituation and Role Modelling**:
   The development of virtuous character depends upon continuous habituation (*istiqamah*) and positive living examples (*qudwah hasanah*). In educational institutions, teachers serve as living role models whose personal integrity, justice, patience, and honesty are internalised by learners through observation and communal interaction.

4. **Relevance to Misconception**:
   Supports correcting the misconception that "good character comes only from knowing rules". Cognitive knowledge without emotional conviction and habituated action fails to develop stable moral behaviour.
