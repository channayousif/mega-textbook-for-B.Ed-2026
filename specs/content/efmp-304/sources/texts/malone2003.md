## Scope note (verified)

The paper names three standing problems in deciding whether a text contains an argument: what
counts as evidence that a text argues at all, how to supply implicit premises, and why real
discourse teaches this better than textbook examples. The opening problem is the one this unit
faces: "because" and "since" are often causal, and deciding whether a given passage argues is
genuinely hard (p. 2). The paper treats the identification of arguments as something that
indicator words alone cannot settle.

## What this supports in EFMP-304 Unit 2

- **U2-01 (Argument distinguished from explanation):** the claim that indicator words are
  evidence about structure, never proof of it. The paper treats the identification problem as
  one that word-level cues alone cannot resolve, grounding the unit's "reliable test" (the
  dispute test) in the published literature on argument identification.
- **U2-06 (Identifying premises):** the discussion of implicit premises and what a reader
  supplies to make an argument complete. The paper addresses exactly this gap-filling as one of
  the three standing problems.
- **U2-08 (What is not an argument):** the indeterminacy of real discourse, and why a passage
  may resist classification as either argument or non-argument. The paper names this
  indeterminacy as one of the standing problems a reader faces.
