## Abstract (verified)

This classroom-based research introduces the Toulmin model in the teaching of argumentative
writing in a Turkish EFL university classroom and examines its effectiveness. Sixteen freshmen's
argumentative writing samples were collected before and after instruction. During instruction,
a variety of one-hour activities incorporating the Toulmin model were integrated into the
regular 10-week teaching curriculum, including explicit instruction of the Toulmin model,
awareness-raising about its elements with selected reading passages, class debate, and
identification of Toulmin elements in argumentative papers. Results showed that students'
argumentative papers improved after instruction, presenting more enriching and complex
structures, exhibiting important elements in argumentation such as opposing views and rebuttals,
which were almost non-existent in argumentative papers before the instruction. Students also
felt the instruction had empowered them to write argumentative papers in the future.

## What this supports in EFMP-304 Unit 2

- **U2-02 (The classical form of an argument):** the claim that explicit instruction in a
  structured model of argument, with practice labelling its elements, leads learners to produce
  more complete arguments including opposing views and rebuttals that were previously
  absent. The study was carried out with sixteen students in a Turkish English-as-a-foreign-
  language classroom, so it is evidence about explicit structural teaching, not about Pakistani
  secondary schools in particular.
- **U2-04 (Issue questions and information questions):** the observation that novice
  argumentative writers initially produce one-sided arguments and that explicit structural
  teaching changes this pattern. This supports the unit's claim that recognising an argument's
  structure is a learnable skill, not an innate one.
