## Summary (verified from abstract and metadata)

This article reviews the literature on teaching critical thinking skills in higher education. The
authors synthesise findings from multiple studies on instructional strategies that develop critical
thinking, including experiential and reflective approaches. The review finds that active,
experience-based learning strategies are associated with stronger critical-thinking outcomes than
passive transmission approaches, and that reflection is a recurring mechanism across effective
programmes.

## What this supports in EFMP-304 Unit 5

- U5-01: supports the claim that experiential learning (experience plus reflection) is more
  effective than experience alone for developing professional capability.
- U5-03: supports the theoretical strand that reflection is a recurring mechanism across
  experiential-learning traditions.

## Scope note

This is a literature review of higher-education studies, many of them from the United States. It
supports the *mechanism* (reflection as a recurring element in effective professional education)
but does not directly evidence Pakistani teacher education.
