## Abstract (verified)

This study examines how teacher candidates' attitudes toward critical thinking relate to and
predict their critical thinking standards. Using a correlation survey model, the research
involved education faculty students at a Turkish state university during the 2023-2024 academic
year. Findings indicated that both attitude and standard scores were at a very high level, with a
moderately significant relationship between them. The attitudes were found to significantly
predict the standards, accounting for 45% of the variance in critical thinking standards.

## What this supports in EFMP-304 Unit 1

The unit's Topic 1.2 teaches that critical thinking is judged against a set of recognised
standards (clarity, accuracy, precision, relevance, depth, breadth, logic, fairness). This
source grounds the claim that "critical thinking standards" is a measured, empirical construct in
teacher-education research - not merely a textbook list - and that trainees' attitudes toward
critical thinking predict how well they apply those standards. It does **not** provide the
standards themselves (those are taught from the course guide and the bassham2010 spine); it
supports the pedagogical claim that standards are real, research-backed, and attitude-linked.
