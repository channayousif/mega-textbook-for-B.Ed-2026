affecting critical thinking with international higher education students. *Active Learning in
Higher Education*, 25(3), 537-550. https://doi.org/10.1177/14697874231168341

## Abstract (verified)

The study examined whether international university students benefited from understanding
psychological and sociological factors - such as belief and confirmation biases, framing, social
pressure to conform, and poor risk assessment - that can impede critical thinking. Workshops based
on active learning principles were delivered to international students across disciplines at the
University of Sheffield, UK, followed by interviews analysed via thematic analysis. Findings
showed improved understanding of questioning and analysing, using multiple perspectives and
argument building, plus new awareness of confirmation bias, evidence use, framing, independent
thinking, and culture. Students from educational traditions favouring certainty and authority
displayed greater tolerance of ambiguity. Evidence also emerged of growth in critical dispositions
related to self-awareness, respect for others' views, and societal awareness.

## What this supports in EFMP-304 Unit 1

The unit's Topic 1.3 teaches that critical thinking is stopped by barriers from the self, the
group, the wish and unexamined assumptions. This source grounds those barriers in empirical
research: it names confirmation bias, social pressure to conform, framing and poor risk assessment
as factors that impede critical thinking, and shows that making learners aware of these factors
develops their critical dispositions. It supports the unit's claim that the barriers are ordinary
cognitive defaults (not character flaws) and that naming them helps learners think more
critically.
