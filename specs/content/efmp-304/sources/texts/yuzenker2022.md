## Scope note (title-and-abstract level)

The paper addresses how analysts identify the structure of arguments whose premises are linked
(premises that must work together) versus convergent (premises that work independently). The
title and journal placement make it a methodological paper about argument analysis. The unit's
claim that the paper argues for describing structure before evaluating strength is a title-level
inference from its subject matter, not a quoted abstract claim.

## What this supports in EFMP-304

- **U2-05 (Types of argument):** the claim that classifying an argument's type should precede
  judging its strength, and that conflating the two means describing your own reaction rather
  than the argument. This is the ordering the unit adopts: name the type, name what it must
  supply, then check whether this instance supplies it. The paper is cited as methodological
  support for this ordering, not as an empirical finding about classroom practice.
- **U3-04 (Deductive validity and inductive strength):** the claim that an argument's structure
  should be described before it is evaluated, and that judging strength before settling structure
  means describing the reader's reaction rather than the argument. This is the basis for the
  unit's insistence on classifying an argument as deductive or inductive before applying a test.
