Pedro, J. (2005). Reflection in teacher education: exploring pre-service teachers' meanings of
reflective practice. *Reflective Practice, 6*(1), 49-63.

## Summary (verified from abstract and metadata)

This article explores how pre-service teachers understand and experience reflective practice during
their teacher-education programmes. Pedro finds that trainees often begin with descriptive writing
and move toward more analytical reflection over time, and that structured prompts and mentoring
support the transition. The study highlights the gap between what trainees *call* reflection and
what reflective practice actually requires in terms of evidence-based analysis.

## What this supports in EFMP-304 Unit 6

- U6-01: supports the distinction between a diary and a reflective journal - trainees often
  confound the two, and structured support is needed to move from one to the other.
- U6-04: supports the claim that descriptive writing and reflective writing are different, and
  that the move from one to the other is a developmental progression supported by prompts.

## Scope note

The study is from pre-service teacher education. It supports the developmental claim (trainees move
from descriptive to analytical over time) but is not specific to Pakistani contexts.
