## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. **Question**: The three steps of the experiential-learning mechanism (Boud et al.) are:
   - **Derived answer**: B (Returning to the experience, attending to feelings, re-evaluating)
   - **Reasoning**: Boud, Keogh, and Walker (1985/2013) define the three stages of reflection on experience specifically as returning to experience, attending to feelings, and re-evaluating experience.
   - **Supplied key**: B. Agreement: Yes.

2. **Question**: "Experience alone does not produce learning" means that:
   - **Derived answer**: B (A reflective process is required to turn experience into learning)
   - **Reasoning**: Experience without examination leads merely to routine repetition; a cognitive/reflective process is necessary to extract actionable learning.
   - **Supplied key**: B. Agreement: Yes.

3. **Question**: A teacher has taught for twelve years and says, "I have seen it all. Nothing changes." This teacher is most likely on:
   - **Derived answer**: B (The bypass route (repetition without reflection))
   - **Reasoning**: The bypass route describes the default pattern where years of service represent one year repeated multiple times without reflective re-evaluation.
   - **Supplied key**: B. Agreement: Yes.

4. **Question**: The stage of the reflective cycle that produces a finding to carry to the next cycle is:
   - **Derived answer**: C (Re-examination)
   - **Reasoning**: In the six-stage cycle (Description, Analysis, Alternatives, Decision, Trial, Re-examination), re-examination evaluates the trial and extracts the finding carried by the return arrow.
   - **Supplied key**: C. Agreement: Yes.

5. **Question**: A journal entry describes a problem, analyses it, chooses an alternative, tries it, and concludes "it worked". What is missing?
   - **Derived answer**: B (A finding that can be carried to the next entry)
   - **Reasoning**: Simply asserting "it worked" omits explicit re-examination criteria and the distilled finding to be carried forward to close the loop.
   - **Supplied key**: B. Agreement: Yes.

6. **Question**: The theoretical strand that asks "what assumptions and power relations shape my practice?" is:
   - **Derived answer**: C (Critical reflection)
   - **Reasoning**: Critical reflection, derived from Brookfield and Freire, specifically interrogates power structures, hegemonic assumptions, and systemic dynamics.
   - **Supplied key**: C. Agreement: Yes.

7. **Question**: Gibbs' six-stage model is best suited for:
   - **Derived answer**: B (Producing structured, technique-focused reflection with clear action points)
   - **Reasoning**: Gibbs' structured cyclical stages provide clear prompts for concrete incidents leading to tangible action plans.
   - **Supplied key**: B. Agreement: Yes.

8. **Question**: A teacher wants to understand how she adjusts her explanation mid-lesson when she sees confusion. The most relevant thinker is:
   - **Derived answer**: C (Schön)
   - **Reasoning**: Donald Schön explicitly formulated "reflection-in-action" to describe real-time problem-setting and tacit reframing during active practice.
   - **Supplied key**: C. Agreement: Yes.

9. **Question**: Brookfield's four lenses include all of the following EXCEPT:
   - **Derived answer**: D (The statistical evidence lens)
   - **Reasoning**: Brookfield's four lenses are: autobiographical, students' eyes, colleagues' perceptions, and theoretical literature. Statistical evidence is not one of them.
   - **Supplied key**: D. Agreement: Yes.

10. **Question**: A teacher-training programme teaches only Gibbs' model and presents it as "the complete framework for teacher reflection." The main limitation of this approach is that it:
    - **Derived answer**: A (Leaves out the critical strand that examines assumptions and power)
    - **Reasoning**: Gibbs operates primarily on individual and technical classroom practice; exclusive use misses the critical questioning of systemic assumptions and power relations.
    - **Supplied key**: A. Agreement: Yes.

**Summary**: 10/10 derived keys match supplied keys (100% agreement). Distractors are distinct, plausible, and test genuine conceptual understanding.
