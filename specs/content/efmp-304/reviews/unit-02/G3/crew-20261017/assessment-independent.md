## RRQs

1. Argument offers support for accepting a conclusion; explanation accounts for an accepted event. Causal uses of because show why indicators alone fail.
2. Explanation answers why an event happened rather than whether a conclusion should be accepted. A locked gate attributed to the caretaker's absence supplies an explanation, not a failed attempt at proof.
3. P1: adjacent desks enable copying. P2: copying undermines marks as planning evidence. C: separate desks during tests. Implicit bridge: teachers should reduce copying to preserve useful marks.
4. Same speaker and subject, different audiences and decisions: head can approve spending, colleague can cooperate, parents need consequences and information. Each needs different selected reasons and wording.
5. Work requires a criterion such as attainment, attendance or equity and a comparison. A factual evidence search cannot select the evaluative criterion; it can help once the outcome is specified. This largely repeats Topic 2.2 and may be Understand rather than Analyze.
6. Evidence: observations/counts. Analogy: relevant resemblance transfers a judgement. Authority: competent testimony. Policy: feasibility, worthwhile consequences and comparison with alternatives. The taxonomy is overlapping, not exhaustive.
7. As written this is one statistical report, not an argument with a stated conclusion. If a recommendation for this school is intended, it is evidence-based policy reasoning requiring comparable conditions and a causal link beyond coincident improvement. Do not invent the omitted conclusion without flagging it.
8. Premise: because, since, given that. Conclusion: therefore, thus, so. Without cues identify what the author wants accepted and which statements support it. Listing examples is Remember; the last part demonstrates understanding rather than necessarily Apply.
9. Restore the route if its withdrawal causes avoidable absence and restoration is a reasonable remedy. A duty to support attendance alone does not connect this route to Hira's missed days; the two-sentence prompt is underdetermined. Disagreement could concern the cause, costs, authority or remedy as well as duty.
10. (a) description of fans, (b) causal explanation if decline is accepted, (c) evaluative assertion without reasons, (d) argument for earlier syllabus completion. Classification alone on familiar exemplars does not necessarily require Analyze.

## ERQs

1. Explained event: late syllabus completion attributed to teacher leave. Conclusion: run Saturday revision classes (and seek cooperation). Support: slipped results, lost teaching/revision time, connection of missed revision to lower scores. Bridge: Saturday revision can recover lost opportunity effectively enough to justify burdens. No obligation to treat the explanation as isolated from its supporting role in the wider argument.
2. Speaker: district office; audience: school heads; subject: daily reading period. Issue: whether this intervention should be introduced. A generic performance claim does not establish effects of fifteen minutes at this time, comparison with displaced activities or the relevant pupils. A head has an administratively actionable directive but inadequate evidence of pedagogical benefit. Distinguish compliance authority from evidential adequacy.
3. Notebook inspection: mechanism-based evidence and policy. Previous pass rates: comparative evidence with confounding. Doctor comparison: analogy with limits because instruction can be collective. Room/period availability: feasibility and policy, missing relative benefit. Tentative ranking: direct instructional mechanism ahead of uncontrolled association or loose analogy; feasibility alone cannot establish benefit. A fixed total ranking is underdetermined by these brief sketches. Accept multiple reasoned rankings.
4. Sentence 1: announcement/directive, also reconstructible as the practical conclusion. Sentence 2: lateness premise supported by sentence 4's count. Sentence 3: duty assertion, also normative support. Sentence 4: evidence/description supporting sentence 2. Sentence 5: request/instruction. Implicit bridge: moving the register will reduce late sign-ins sufficiently to justify the move. The duty claim is not defended but can still function as a premise. Multiple roles and an announcement-only reading are defensible with context.
5. Requires an actual learner-selected document, unavailable here. A successful response must quote/attach it, extract supported conclusions, reconstruct cautious assumptions, allow mixed types, list unsupported assertions and evaluate the need for justification. A defensible no-argument document is valid. This is mainly Analyze/Evaluate; producing a written analysis alone does not demonstrate Create. It does not ask for a new intervention, model or original argument design.
