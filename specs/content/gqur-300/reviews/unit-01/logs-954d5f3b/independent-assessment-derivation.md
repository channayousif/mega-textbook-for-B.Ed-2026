## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

1. Prompt: Define quantitative reasoning in one sentence and give one school example.
   - Independent Derivation: Using numbers, data, and logic in context to inform a practical decision; e.g. dividing 240 pupils into groups of 15 to plan a reading schedule.
   - Model Comparison: Definition emphasizing reasoning and decision purpose (1 mark); valid school example (1 mark). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

2. Prompt: Explain why a teacher's attitude towards numbers matters for pupils.
   - Independent Derivation: Pupils absorb and model the teacher's confidence or anxiety with mathematics; a teacher who reasons aloud demonstrates that numbers are accessible tools for decisions.
   - Model Comparison: Role modelling concept (1 mark); practical consequence for pupils (1 mark). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

3. Prompt: Correct the colleague's claim "QR means hard calculations".
   - Independent Derivation: The core intellectual work in QR is determining what to compute and interpreting what the result means for an action; arithmetic calculation is merely a mechanical tool within that judgement.
   - Model Comparison: Focus on decision/judgement (1 mark); contrasting example or clear conceptual correction (1 mark). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

4. Prompt: Difference between an estimate and an approximation, with an example of each.
   - Independent Derivation: An estimate is a purposeful, methodological guess (e.g. estimating 800 attendees by counting a section and multiplying); an approximation is a convenient rounded substitute value (e.g. using 400 in place of 398).
   - Model Comparison: Clear distinction with valid examples (1 mark each). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

5. Prompt: 348 pupils x 2 worksheets = 696 worksheets. Estimate packets of 10 needed and name strategy.
   - Independent Derivation: Round 348 to 350; 350 x 2 = 700 worksheets; 700 / 10 = 70 packets. Strategy: rounding to compatible numbers.
   - Model Comparison: 70 packets derived (1 mark); rounding / compatible numbers strategy named (1 mark). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

6. Prompt: Exact vs estimate appropriateness for (a) ordering exam papers; (b) van carrying all pupils in one trip.
   - Independent Derivation: (a) Exact count with a deliberate safety margin (buffer) so no pupil lacks a paper; (b) Exact head count required because vehicle safety capacity is a rigid physical/legal ceiling, though an initial estimate indicates if a second vehicle is needed.
   - Model Comparison: Appropriate stance and reasoning for (a) (2 marks) and (b) (2 marks). Total: 4 marks. Consistent.

7. Prompt: Why "about 800 people" was sufficient for the water contractor, but enrolment returns require exact counts.
   - Independent Derivation: The water contractor needs an operational order of magnitude to ensure sufficient drinking supply where slight excess is acceptable and exactness is impossible; enrolment returns are statutory records dictating school budgets, teacher allocations, and legal compliance.
   - Model Comparison: Contrast between operational buffer purpose and statutory reporting purpose (1 mark each). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

8. Prompt: Name the four logical-reasoning moves, and give an example of two.
   - Independent Derivation: Spotting patterns, if-then reasoning, deduction, elimination. Examples: If-then: if tank holds 500L and use is 100L/day, it lasts 5 days. Elimination: book not in cupboard, not on desk, not borrowed, therefore lost.
   - Model Comparison: Naming 4 moves (1 mark); valid examples for two (2 marks). Total: 3 marks. Consistent.

9. Prompt: Water tank 500L, 100L/day. State if-then conclusion and one breaking condition.
   - Independent Derivation: Conclusion: If the tank holds 500 litres and daily use is 100 litres, a full tank lasts five days. Breaking condition: tank is not full initially, pipe leaks, or sports day doubles water consumption.
   - Model Comparison: Conclusion (1 mark); breaking condition (1 mark). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

10. Prompt: "90% attendance Monday and Tuesday, so same pupils absent both days." Identify flaw.
    - Independent Derivation: The percentage indicates aggregate headcount (10% absent each day), not individual identity; 10% on Monday could be entirely different pupils from the 10% on Tuesday.
    - Model Comparison: Naming the flaw (aggregate vs individual) (1 mark); explaining the disconnect (1 mark). Total: 2 marks. Consistent.

## Extended-Response Questions (ERQs)

1. Prompt: Headteacher games period (63 pupils, ground fits 80). Write short note checking claim, naming ignored quantity, and recommending action.
   - Independent Analysis:
     - Arithmetic check: 63 < 80 is numerically valid for raw area capacity.
     - Ignored quantities: Teacher-to-pupil supervision ratio (1 teacher cannot safely manage 63 active children across two age cohorts), equipment availability (balls, nets), physical space per active game vs static standing room.
     - Recommendation: Do not combine without additional supervision staff and designated activity zones; or split into staggered periods.
   - Rubric Match: Total 8 marks (arithmetic check 2, omitted quantities 3, justified recommendation 3). Consistent.

2. Prompt: PTA 25,000 Rs budget, 512 pupils, 100 prizes at 250 Rs. Estimate fit, identify ignored quantity, and recommend.
   - Independent Analysis:
     - Arithmetic fit: 100 x 250 = 25,000 Rs fits the exact monetary total.
     - Ignored quantities: Packaging/wrapping costs, tax/delivery fees, and equity ratio (only 100 out of 512 pupils, less than 20%, receive a prize, potentially discouraging the majority).
     - Recommendation: Allocate a small contingency buffer for packaging (e.g. 90 prizes at 250 Rs + 2,500 Rs contingency) or reconsider prize distribution tiers.
   - Rubric Match: Total 8 marks (budget estimate 2, ignored quantities 3, recommendation 3). Consistent.

3. Prompt: Workbook failure drop (20 -> 10). Analyse claim, name reasoning move, identify 2 other changing quantities, state required evidence.
   - Independent Analysis:
     - Reasoning move: False causal attribution / assuming post hoc ergo propter hoc correlation implies causation.
     - Confounding variables: Cohort entry ability differences, exam difficulty changes, teacher experience/pedagogical shifts, pupil attendance rates.
     - Required evidence: Comparative cohort baseline data, item-level difficulty analysis, or a control group comparison.
   - Rubric Match: Total 8 marks (reasoning move 2, two changing quantities 3, required evidence 3). Consistent.

4. Prompt: Sports day crowd estimate (40 x 20 = 800). Reconstruct as four-step strategy, name estimation strategy, evaluate look-back.
   - Independent Analysis:
     - Four steps: (1) Understand: estimate total crowd size from visible sections; (2) Plan: count one sample section and multiply by total sections; (3) Carry out: 40 x 20 = 800 people; (4) Look back: verify reasonableness against overall ground capacity and contractor requirements.
     - Estimation strategy: Grouping (sample and scale).
     - Look-back evaluation: 800 was appropriately conservative for water provisioning, ensuring surplus rather than shortage.
   - Rubric Match: Total 8 marks (four steps 3, estimation strategy named 2, look-back evaluation 3). Consistent.

5. Prompt: Evaluate timetable puzzle / school seating decision.
   - Independent Analysis:
     - Analytical decomposition: Breaking problem into core components (space, time, human constraints).
     - Estimation vs exactness: Differentiating where approximations are safe versus where exact figures are mandatory.
     - Look-back verification: Validating the solution against real-world operational constraints.
     - Constructive alternative: Proposing a robust, defensible adjustment.
   - Rubric Match: Total 10 marks (analytic breakdown 3, estimate vs exact evaluation 3, look-back check 2, alternative recommendation 2). Consistent.
