## Record summary

The article traces how adult numeracy evolved from the quantitative-literacy tradition,
drawing lessons from international assessments of adult skills. It examines what the
large-scale assessments actually measure - the tasks, the definitions of competence, and
the frameworks behind them - and argues that what counts as literate or numerate depends
on the tasks asked. Cross-comparing headline numbers without reading their construction
misleads, because each assessment's framework shapes its results.
