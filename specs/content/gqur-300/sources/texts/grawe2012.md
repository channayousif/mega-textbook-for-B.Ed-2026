## Record summary

The article opens from Steen's declaration in the NCED's 2001 *Mathematics and Democracy*
that "The world of the twenty-first century is a world awash in numbers", and the call for
reform so students would be ready for the pervasive demand for quantitative literacy (QL)
across citizenship, education, work, personal finance and health, and culture. QL rests on
mathematical skill but differs from traditional mathematics: mathematics is deliberately
abstract, whereas QL pushes toward the concrete setting of a problem. A late-2001 forum
hosted by the NCED, the MAA and the Mathematical Sciences Education Board concluded that
most graduates of higher education lacked sufficient QL training, that faculty across the
disciplines needed professional development to build QL into their courses, that QL was
absent from assessment efforts, and that education policy leaders were largely unaware of
QL's growing importance. The article surveys the resources and organizations (AAC&U, PKAL,
SIGMAA-QL, the National Numeracy Network) that grew to close these gaps.
