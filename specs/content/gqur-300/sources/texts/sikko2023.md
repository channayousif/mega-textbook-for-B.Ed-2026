## Record summary

The author examines the cluster of overlapping terms linking mathematics and literacy -
"mathematical literacy, quantitative literacy, numeracy, matheracy, disciplinary literacy,
and content-area literacy" - noting their meanings are imprecise and sometimes used
interchangeably. Analysing mathematical literacy, numeracy and quantitative literacy
reveals "the importance of quantitative practices and elementary mathematics in society",
including "how mathematics is necessary for everyday life but also how it can be used to
change the world". The author concludes that "Having so many competing terms and approaches
has a negative effect on the academic discourse" and on communication outside academia.

## What this supports

GQUR-300 Unit 1 (topic-01.mdx, U1-01): that the family terms for mathematical literacy are
overlapping and imprecise, and that what they all describe is mathematics used in everyday
life and society rather than mathematics for its own sake. Supported at record level.
