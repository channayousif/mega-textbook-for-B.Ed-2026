## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. Prompt: Under Article 50, the Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament) consists of:
   - Independent solution: C (The President, the National Assembly and the Senate).
   - Analysis: Article 50 explicitly states: "There shall be a Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament) of Pakistan consisting of the President and two Houses to be known respectively as the National Assembly and the Senate." Common error is omitting the President (option D).
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Remember.

2. Prompt: Sindh and Punjab have very different numbers of National Assembly seats but the same number of Senate members. This is because:
   - Independent solution: A (Assembly seats follow population, and each Province has equal Senate membership).
   - Analysis: National Assembly seats are allocated based on population using the last census under Article 51(5), while the Senate affords equal representation to each federating province (23 each) under Article 59(1).
   - Key: A. Match. Bloom: Understand.

3. Prompt: A government notice says, "The President has been pleased to appoint Ms Y as a Federal Minister." Who actually made the choice?
   - Independent solution: B (The Prime Minister, whose advice the President follows).
   - Analysis: Article 92(1) mandates that the President appoints Federal Ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister. Under Article 48(1), presidential action on advice is binding after reconsideration. The notice is expressed in the President's name (Article 99(1)), but the substantive choice is the Prime Minister's.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Apply.

4. Prompt: The Prime Minister is elected by:
   - Independent solution: D (The National Assembly, by a majority of its total membership).
   - Analysis: Under Article 91(4), the Prime Minister is elected by the votes of the majority of the total membership of the National Assembly. Option B describes the presidential electoral college under Article 41(3).
   - Key: D. Match. Bloom: Remember.

5. Prompt: The Federal Government and a Provincial Government have a legal dispute with each other. Which court has original jurisdiction?
   - Independent solution: A (The Federal Constitutional Court).
   - Analysis: Following the Twenty-seventh Amendment (2025), original and exclusive jurisdiction over intergovernmental disputes was transferred from the Supreme Court (former Article 184) to the Federal Constitutional Court under Article 175E(1).
   - Key: A. Match. Bloom: Apply.

6. Prompt: Under Article 239, a Constitution Amendment Bill needs in each House:
   - Independent solution: D (Two-thirds of the total membership).
   - Analysis: Article 239(1) and (2) strictly specify "by the votes of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House". It is not a simple majority or two-thirds of those present and voting.
   - Key: D. Match. Bloom: Remember.

7. Prompt: Using the full seat numbers, an Amendment Bill gets 240 votes in the National Assembly and 60 in the Senate. What happens?
   - Independent solution: B (It fails, because the Senate needed 64 votes).
   - Analysis: National Assembly total membership is 336 (two-thirds is 224, so 240 passes). Senate total membership is 96 (two-thirds is 64). 60 votes in the Senate is below the constitutional threshold of 64. Article 239 provides no joint sitting mechanism.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Apply.

8. Prompt: A Bill would alter the limits of a Province. Besides passing in both Houses, it needs:
   - Independent solution: C (Two-thirds of the total membership of that Province's Assembly).
   - Analysis: Article 239(4) provides that an amendment altering the limits of a Province shall not be presented to the President for assent unless passed by that Provincial Assembly by not less than two-thirds of its total membership.
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Understand.

9. Prompt: The Eighteenth Amendment removed which part of the legislative lists?
   - Independent solution: B (The Concurrent Legislative List).
   - Analysis: The Eighteenth Amendment omitted the Concurrent Legislative List in the Fourth Schedule, giving residual legislative power to the provinces under Article 142(c).
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Remember.

10. Prompt: A pupil asks what the Twenty-fifth Amendment did to the Federally Administered Tribal Areas. The best short reply is that it:
    - Independent solution: C (Merged them into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa).
    - Analysis: The Twenty-fifth Amendment (2018) merged FATA into KP (Article 246(d)), repealed Article 247, and eliminated separate direct federal executive administration.
    - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Understand.

## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

1. Prompt: Differences between National Assembly and Senate election and federal importance (3 sentences).
   - Independent solution: National Assembly members are elected directly by adult voters on the basis of population, giving proportional weight to people. Senators are elected indirectly by Provincial Assemblies, with each province receiving an equal delegation of 23 members. In a federation, this ensures that heavily populated provinces cannot dominate legislation while smaller provinces retain an equal constitutional veto in one chamber.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer. Structured 4-mark rubric is objective. Bloom: Understand.

2. Prompt: Meaning of "in the name of the President" in Articles 90(1) and 99(1) (2-3 sentences).
   - Independent solution: The phrase indicates that executive orders and appointments are formally promulgated under the title of the Head of State to represent the unity of the Republic. It does not signify personal decision-making power, as the substantive executive authority is exercised by the Federal Government through the Prime Minister.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 3-mark rubric. Bloom: Understand.

3. Prompt: Comparison of President and Prime Minister on election and real executive power with articles (4 sentences).
   - Independent solution: The President is elected by an electoral college comprising both Houses of Parliament and all four Provincial Assemblies (Article 41(3)), whereas the Prime Minister is elected exclusively by the National Assembly (Article 91(4)). Substantive executive power is exercised by the Prime Minister as chief executive of the Federation (Article 90(1)), while the President acts on the binding advice of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (Article 48(1)).
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 4-mark rubric. Bloom: Analyze.

4. Prompt: Court identification and rationale for three legal scenarios: (a) Sukkur theft trial, (b) official refusing lawful duty with no other remedy, (c) Federal-Balochistan legal dispute.
   - Independent solution: (a) Subordinate court (district judiciary), as everyday criminal trials fall under ordinary local jurisdiction under Article 175(1). (b) High Court of Sindh, which exercises writ jurisdiction under Article 199 to compel public officials where no other adequate legal remedy exists. (c) Federal Constitutional Court, which possesses exclusive original jurisdiction under Article 175E(1) to adjudicate disputes between governments.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 3-mark rubric. Bloom: Apply.

5. Prompt: Two rules separating judges from executive/legislature and one open question/weakness (4 sentences).
   - Independent solution: Judges are protected through a specialized Judicial Commission for nominations (Article 175A) and can be removed only by the Supreme Judicial Council on grounds of incapacity or misconduct (Article 209). Furthermore, Parliament is barred from discussing judicial conduct in the discharge of duties (Article 68). An ongoing open question is the institutional balance of judicial appointments, which has been repeatedly contested and altered between judges and political representatives across the 18th, 19th, 26th, and 27th Amendments.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 4-mark rubric. Bloom: Analyze.

6. Prompt: Two differences between ordinary Bill and Constitution Amendment Bill.
   - Independent solution: An ordinary Bill requires a simple majority of members present and voting (Article 70), whereas an Amendment Bill requires a supermajority of two-thirds of the total membership of each House (Article 239). Additionally, disagreement on an ordinary Bill can be resolved in a joint sitting, whereas Article 239 provides no joint sitting mechanism, causing the Bill to fail if either House rejects it.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 2-mark rubric. Bloom: Understand.

7. Prompt: Vote calculation: 225 in NA, 65 in Senate (70 present).
   - Independent solution: In the National Assembly, 225 exceeds the required two-thirds threshold of 224 (two-thirds of 336 total seats). In the Senate, 65 exceeds the required threshold of 64 (two-thirds of 96 total seats), regardless of only 70 members being present in the chamber. Because both Houses satisfied the two-thirds total membership requirement independently, the Bill reaches the President for assent.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 3-mark rubric. Bloom: Apply.

8. Prompt: Background of Nineteenth Amendment and changes to judicial appointments (3 sentences).
   - Independent solution: The Nineteenth Amendment was enacted after the Supreme Court heard challenges to Article 175A in *Nadeem Ahmed v. Federation of Pakistan* (2010) and referred the matter back to Parliament with recommendations. The amendment increased the number of senior Supreme Court judges on the Judicial Commission from two to four. It also required the Parliamentary Committee to record formal reasons if it rejected a nomination by a three-fourths majority.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 3-mark rubric. Bloom: Understand.

9. Prompt: 25th Amendment and Article 239(4), plus change in governance (3-4 sentences).
   - Independent solution: The Twenty-fifth Amendment altered the territorial boundaries of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa by merging FATA into it, thereby triggering Article 239(4), which required passage by two-thirds of the KP Assembly (duly achieved in May 2018 with 92 votes for). By repealing Article 247, the amendment eliminated direct executive governance by the federal government and political agents. Consequently, the merged areas came under regular provincial legislation, high court jurisdiction, and provincial assembly representation.
   - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 4-mark rubric. Bloom: Analyze.

10. Prompt: Correcting "18th Amendment only renamed a province" (4 sentences).
    - Independent solution: Renaming NWFP to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa was merely section 3 of a comprehensive 102-section constitutional overhaul. The amendment abolished the Concurrent Legislative List under Article 142, established an inviolable revenue floor in NFC awards (Article 160(3A)), and granted equal joint ownership of natural resources to provinces (Article 172(3)). It also removed presidential unilateral dissolution under Article 58(2)(b) and added the fundamental right to education (Article 25A). For federalism, the abolition of the Concurrent List was most transformative because it devolved primary legislative responsibility over education, health, and social welfare to the provinces.
    - Evaluation: Matches model answer and 5-mark rubric. Bloom: Analyze.
