## Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

1. Prompt: Under Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act 1947, which legal document served as the adapted interim constitution of Pakistan at independence?
   - Independent solution: B (The Government of India Act 1935).
   - Analysis: Section 8 provided that each dominion would be governed in accordance with the Government of India Act 1935, as adapted by the Governor-General via the Pakistan (Provisional Constitution) Order 1947.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Remember.

2. Prompt: What core constitutional doctrine regarding state sovereignty was established by the Objectives Resolution on 12 March 1949?
   - Independent solution: C (Sovereignty belongs to Allah; state authority through the people is a sacred trust).
   - Analysis: The Resolution articulates that sovereignty over the universe belongs to Allah Almighty alone, and the authority delegated to the State through its people is a sacred trust.
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Understand.

3. Prompt: Why did the Federal Court reverse the Sindh Chief Court decision in the Tamizuddin Khan case in 1955?
   - Independent solution: B (It held that the law granting the requested writ jurisdiction lacked the Governor-General's assent).
   - Analysis: Chief Justice Muhammad Munir held that Section 223A of the Government of India Act 1935 had not received the Governor-General's assent, rendering the Chief Court without writ jurisdiction.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Understand.

4. Prompt: Under the Constitution of 1956, how was legislative representation divided between East Pakistan and West Pakistan in the National Assembly?
   - Independent solution: B (Under the principle of parity, assigning exactly 150 general seats to each province).
   - Analysis: The 1956 unicameral National Assembly comprised 300 general seats divided equally (150 East, 150 West), plus 10 reserved seats for women.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Remember.

5. Prompt: A secondary civics teacher asks students why the adapted Government of India Act 1935 had to be amended by the Pakistan (Provisional Constitution) Order 1947. Which explanation is historically accurate?
   - Independent solution: A (To eliminate the discretionary powers of the British Crown and Governor-General so that the new government operated under cabinet responsibility).
   - Analysis: The colonial discretionary vetoes had to be removed to establish executive accountability to the cabinet and domestic constituent authority.
   - Key: A. Match. Bloom: Apply.

6. Prompt: What electoral mechanism was established under the Constitution of 1962 to elect the President and the National Assembly?
   - Independent solution: B (An indirect Electoral College consisting of 80,000 Basic Democrats).
   - Analysis: Under the Basic Democracies Order 1959 and 1962 Constitution, 80,000 union council members (40,000 per wing) formed the exclusive indirect electoral college.
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Remember.

7. Prompt: How did the Constitution of 1962 restrict the financial authority of the National Assembly over state revenues?
   - Independent solution: C (The Assembly could vote only on new expenditures, leaving recurring and charged expenditures beyond parliamentary reduction).
   - Analysis: The Assembly had voting power only over new expenditure; recurring expenditures charged on the central fund could not be rejected or reduced.
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Understand.

8. Prompt: When the Constitution of 1962 was first promulgated on 1 March 1962, how did it treat citizens' fundamental rights?
   - Independent solution: B (It omitted enforceable fundamental rights, providing only advisory Principles of Law-Making that courts could not enforce).
   - Analysis: The original text included only non-justiciable Principles of Law-Making (Article 6). Enforceable Part II fundamental rights were restored only by the First Amendment Act 1963 (enacted January 1964).
   - Key: B. Match. Bloom: Understand.

9. Prompt: An educational historian observes that the 1962 presidential system lacked democratic feedback loops. Which institutional feature best illustrates this structural defect?
   - Independent solution: C (The cabinet answering to the President and voters choosing national representatives through local electors).
   - Analysis: Ministers were personal presidential appointees not responsible to parliament, while voters could only vote for local ward intermediaries rather than national legislators.
   - Key: C. Match. Bloom: Apply.

10. Prompt: A trainee teacher asks why Field Marshal Ayub Khan's transfer of power to General Yahya Khan on 25 March 1969 was unconstitutional. What provision of the 1962 Constitution did this act violate?
    - Independent solution: A (The succession rule directed the Speaker of the National Assembly to act as President when that office became vacant).
    - Analysis: Article 16 mandated that in the event of resignation or vacancy, the Speaker of the National Assembly (Abdul Jabbar Khan) assumed the acting presidency.
    - Key: A. Match. Bloom: Apply.

## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

1. Prompt: Legal function of Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act 1947.
   - Independent solution: Section 8 provided the transitional constitutional basis by directing that Pakistan would be governed under the Government of India Act 1935 as adapted, until a new constitution was framed. This preserved administrative, judicial, and fiscal continuity and prevented a legal vacuum at independence.
   - Evaluation of model answer: Covers statutory identity (s. 8), the adapted 1935 Act, and preservation of legal continuity. 3-mark scheme is clear and objective. Bloom: Understand.

2. Prompt: Objectives Resolution's description of divine sovereignty and human authority.
   - Independent solution: Absolute sovereignty belongs to Allah Almighty alone; the authority exercised by the State of Pakistan is a sacred trust delegated to the people, who exercise it through freely chosen representatives within divine limits.
   - Evaluation of model answer: Clear 3-part allocation (sovereignty of Allah, delegated sacred trust, democratic representation). Bloom: Understand.

3. Prompt: Two major political dilemmas delaying drafting between 1947 and 1956.
   - Independent solution: 1) The federal dilemma of representation between the populous East Bengal and the geographically larger West Pakistan. 2) The state language controversy regarding Urdu and Bengali. (Also the role of Islam in state legislation).
   - Evaluation of model answer: Identifies representation/parity and language/Islamic character. 3 marks evenly split. Bloom: Understand.

4. Prompt: Principle of parity in 1956 Constitution and its political objective.
   - Independent solution: Parity allocated 150 general seats each to East and West Pakistan in a 300-seat unicameral National Assembly. Its political objective was to secure West Pakistani agreement by neutralizing the Bengali demographic majority, in exchange for Bengali agreement to federal unity.
   - Evaluation of model answer: Accurately defines the 150/150 allocation and political purpose. Bloom: Understand.

5. Prompt: How One Unit scheme altered West Pakistan.
   - Independent solution: In October 1955, the provinces and territories of West Pakistan (Punjab, Sindh, NWFP, Balochistan, princely states) were amalgamated into a single province administered from Lahore. It created a single western counterpart to East Pakistan for parity, but deeply alienated smaller provinces whose autonomy was extinguished.
   - Evaluation of model answer: 1.5 marks for administrative merger, 1.5 marks for parity context and regional alienation. Bloom: Understand.

6. Prompt: Two significant changes introduced by the Constitution (First Amendment) Act 1963.
   - Independent solution: 1) Re-established enforceable fundamental rights in Part II and restored judicial writ jurisdiction. 2) Renamed the state from "Republic of Pakistan" back to "Islamic Republic of Pakistan".
   - Evaluation of model answer: Directly corresponds to historical legislation. Bloom: Understand.

7. Prompt: Contrast election of head of state in 1956 vs 1962.
   - Independent solution: Under 1956, the President was elected by an electoral college comprising members of the National Assembly and both Provincial Assemblies. Under 1962, the President was elected by 80,000 Basic Democrats acting as an indirect Electoral College.
   - Evaluation of model answer: Sharp contrast between parliamentary assembly electors and local ward intermediaries. Bloom: Apply.

8. Prompt: Two structural reasons why 1962 Constitution was not parliamentary.
   - Independent solution: 1) Abolished the office of Prime Minister; executive authority was unified in an independent President. 2) Ministers were chosen by and responsible solely to the President, barred from sitting in the legislature, rather than being collectively responsible to parliament.
   - Evaluation of model answer: Clear institutional differentiation. Bloom: Apply.

9. Prompt: Two reasons Basic Democracies alienated East Pakistan.
   - Independent solution: 1) Replaced universal adult franchise with 40,000 easily manipulated local electors, neutralizing East Pakistan's popular numerical majority. 2) Consolidated executive centralism and parity, entrenching economic disparities in revenue and development allocation.
   - Evaluation of model answer: Combines electoral disenfranchisement with regional economic disparity. Bloom: Analyze.

10. Prompt: How Tamizuddin Khan (1955) ruling impacted executive-legislative balance.
    - Independent solution: The Federal Court held that Section 223A lacked the Governor-General's assent, nullifying the Chief Court's writ jurisdiction and denying the Assembly a legal remedy against dissolution. This drastically shifted power to the executive in the immediate crisis, establishing judicial deference to executive action. (Distinguished from the subsequent necessity Reference).
    - Evaluation of model answer: Distinctly highlights the assent issue, jurisdictional ruling, and executive empowerment. Bloom: Analyze.
