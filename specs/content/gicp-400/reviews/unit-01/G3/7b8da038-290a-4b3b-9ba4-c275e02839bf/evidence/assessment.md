## Restricted-Response Questions (RRQs)

1. Prompt: Three parts of definition of ideology, and why "Sukkur is on the River Indus" is not ideological.
   - Independent response: An ideology is (1) shared by a collective group, (2) normative (describing how society should be rather than merely what is), and (3) action-guiding (directing collective effort). "Sukkur is on the River Indus" is an empirical geographic statement describing an existing fact; it contains no normative vision for society and inspires no political or social action.
   - Comparison with model answer: Model answer matches exactly. Allocation of 3 marks (1 for three parts, 2 for explanation of non-ideological nature) is fair and objective.

2. Prompt: Three jobs of a constitution with one article for each.
   - Independent response: (1) Establishing institutions of government (e.g., Article 50 creates Parliament; Article 175 establishes the judiciary); (2) Limiting State power (e.g., Article 8(2) voids laws taking away Fundamental Rights); (3) Protecting citizens' rights (e.g., Article 25 establishes equality before law; Article 25A guarantees free, compulsory education).
   - Comparison with model answer: 3 marks (1 for each job with fitting article). Guidance matches.

3. Prompt: Article 239 stability and significance for ordinary citizens.
   - Independent response: Article 239 requires a supermajority of at least two-thirds of the total membership of both the National Assembly and the Senate to amend constitutional provisions. This prevents a simple, temporary parliamentary majority from altering basic legal structures or revoking citizen protections at will, providing citizens with enduring stability, predictability, and protected rights.
   - Comparison with model answer: 3 marks (1 for two-thirds rule, 1 for ordinary law comparison, 1 for citizen impact). Guidance matches.

4. Prompt: Trace "religious freedom" from value through Preamble to article.
   - Independent response: Value: Individuals and communities have an inherent right to profess and observe their religious convictions. Principle: The Preamble proclaims that adequate provision shall be made for minorities freely to profess and practise their religions, and guarantees freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship. Article: Article 20 makes the freedom to profess, practise, and propagate religion an enforceable Fundamental Right.
   - Comparison with model answer: 3 marks (1 per link). Guidance matches.

5. Prompt: Article 25 vs Article 33 legal force and citizen consequence.
   - Independent response: Article 25 is an enforceable Fundamental Right (Part II, Chapter 1); under Article 8, any state action or enacted law inconsistent with it is void to the extent of inconsistency, allowing citizens to challenge violations in the superior courts. Article 33 is a Principle of Policy (Part II, Chapter 2); under Article 30(2), the validity of an action or law cannot be questioned in court solely because it diverges from a Principle of Policy. Thus, citizens have a justiciable legal remedy for equality violations under Article 25, whereas compliance with Article 33 must be pursued politically through legislative scrutiny and public advocacy.
   - Comparison with model answer: 4 marks (1 each for chapter placement, 1 for citing Art. 8 / Art. 30(2), 1 for citizen consequence). Guidance matches.

6. Prompt: Two-Nation Theory: what it claimed and what it did not claim.
   - Independent response: It claimed that the Muslims and Hindus of South Asia constituted two distinct nations based on distinct religious, social, and cultural frameworks, and that Muslims therefore possessed the right to self-government in a sovereign homeland. It did not claim that Hindus and Muslims could not peacefully coexist as neighbours, nor did it demand the exclusion of non-Muslims from equal citizenship in the resulting state.
   - Comparison with model answer: 3 marks (1 for two nations, 1 for self-government, 1 for what was not claimed). Guidance matches.

7. Prompt: Contributions of Sir Syed, Iqbal, and Jinnah.
   - Independent response: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan pioneered the political concept of distinct Muslim identity in modern South Asia following 1857 and the Hindi-Urdu controversy. Allama Muhammad Iqbal provided the philosophical foundation and proposed a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim state at Allahabad in 1930. Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah organized the Muslim League into a mass movement and articulated the demand into the political reality of the 1940 Lahore Resolution.
   - Comparison with model answer: 3 marks (1 for each leader). Guidance matches.

8. Prompt: Four features of the Islamic concept of state with references.
   - Independent response: (1) Sovereignty of Allah (Preamble); (2) Authority as a sacred trust exercised by chosen representatives (Preamble; Qur'an 4:58); (3) Justice and rule of law (Qur'an 4:58); (4) Consultation (shura) in public administration (Qur'an 42:38; 3:159). Additional valid features: human equality (Qur'an 49:13); freedom of conscience (Qur'an 2:256).
   - Comparison with model answer: 4 marks (1 per feature with valid textual support). Guidance matches.

9. Prompt: Iqbal's Islamic state vs theocracy.
   - Independent response: A theocracy is characterized by rule by an ecclesiastical or priestly hierarchy claiming infallible divine authority without democratic accountability to the populace. Iqbal explicitly rejects an earthly representative screening their personal will behind a claim of divine infallibility; he describes the state as a human endeavour to realize the spiritual principles of equality, solidarity, and freedom in institutional life, viewing a representative republican assembly as consistent with the spirit of Islam. He uses the term "theocracy" only in the metaphorical sense of a community devoted to spiritual ideals.
   - Comparison with model answer: 4 marks (1 for theocracy definition, 1 for Iqbal's rejection, 1 for republican account, 1 for special usage). Guidance matches.

10. Prompt: Qureshi vs Jalal on Pakistan's creation, and Hassan's conclusion.
    - Independent response: Qureshi interprets the creation of Pakistan primarily through the prism of Muslim communal consciousness and the Two-Nation Theory as an ideological imperative. Jalal argues that religion was not the sole or inevitable determinant, focusing on strategic negotiations, constitutional deadlocks, and political manoeuvring by the Muslim League and Congress. Hassan concludes that while the Two-Nation Theory provided a powerful rallying framework, the establishment of Pakistan was the outcome of an accumulated sequence of political events and choices rather than a single predetermined cause.
    - Comparison with model answer: 4 marks (1 for Qureshi, 1 for Jalal, 1 for contrast point, 1 for Hassan's synthesis). Guidance matches.
