## RRQs (independent answer, then compared with the model and scheme)

1. Three parts: shared; about how things should be; guides action. "Sukkur is on the River Indus" describes a fact, not how things should be, and guides no action. Scheme awards 1 for the three parts and 2 for the two explanation points. Aligned.
2. Three jobs: sets up institutions (Article 50 or 175); limits power (Article 8(2)); protects rights (Article 25 or 25A). Scheme awards 1 per job with a fitting article. Aligned.
3. Article 239 needs two-thirds of the total membership of each House, harder than an ordinary majority, so citizens can rely on lasting rights and institutions. Scheme awards 1 for the rule, 1 for the comparison, 1 for the citizen consequence. Aligned.
4. Value: religious freedom. Principle: Preamble "to profess and practise their religions". Article: Article 20. Scheme awards 1 per link. Aligned.
5. Article 25 is a Fundamental Right enforced through Article 8; Article 33 is a Principle of Policy and Article 30(2) bars a challenge on that ground alone. Consequence: a legal remedy under Article 25, political pressure under Article 33. Scheme awards 2 for placing the articles, 1 for Articles 8 or 30(2), 1 for the consequence. Aligned.
6. Claimed: two nations and a right to self-government. Did not claim: that people of different faiths cannot live side by side. Scheme awards 1 for two nations, 1 for self-government, 1 for the negative. Aligned.
7. Sir Syed: pioneer after 1857 and the Hindi-Urdu controversy. Iqbal: philosophical basis and the 1930 consolidated north-western state. Jinnah: political demand at Lahore 1940. Scheme awards 1 per leader. Aligned.
8. Four features: sovereignty of Allah (Preamble); authority as a trust (Preamble); justice (Qur'an 4:58); consultation (Qur'an 42:38). Equality (49:13) and freedom of faith (2:256) also fit. Scheme awards 1 per feature with support. Aligned.
9. Theocracy: rule by a religious class without consent. Iqbal rejects a ruler claiming infallibility; his state realises equality, solidarity and freedom; he uses "theocracy" for a state serving ideal principles. Scheme awards 1 per element. Aligned.
10. Qureshi: the Two-Nation Theory as the reason. Jalal: religion one reason among several. Hassan: the theory mattered, but Pakistan came from a series of events and decisions. Scheme awards 1 for Qureshi, 1 for Jalal, 1 for the difference, 1 for Hassan's conclusion. Aligned.

## ERQs (independent reasoning, then compared with the rubric)

1. The trainee is right that a constitution is a legal document and wrong that it holds no values. The Preamble states democracy, freedom, equality, tolerance and social justice; Articles 25 and 25A carry values into binding rights. Rubric's four criteria match the task.
2. The headteacher is right that ideals matter and wrong that articles are only technical. Ideology does not bind; a constitution does. Article 25A matters to a pupil's schooling. Rubric rewards a clear judgement and a real article.
3. The pupil's reading confuses political union with neighbourly coexistence. Jinnah's 1940 speech asks for peace with neighbours; the 1947 speech promises equal citizenship; Articles 20, 25 and 36 protect minorities. Rubric requires two speeches and two articles and an explanation of the error.
4. The textbook is right that Islam is central and wrong that nothing else needs saying. The three commitments are self-government, Islamic principles and equal citizenship; the Preamble and Articles 2, 2A, 20, 25, 36 and 227 carry them; the tension between the 1947 speech and the Objectives Resolution is real. Rubric rewards a fair judgement.
5. One principle traced from a founding statement through the Preamble to two articles, with legal force analysed. Equal citizenship runs from the 1947 speech through the Preamble to Articles 25 and 36; Article 25 is a Fundamental Right, Article 36 a Principle of Policy, so the carry is unequal. Rubric rewards a reasoned judgement with a gap.

All five ERQs demand Analyze or Evaluate and carry a four-level analytic rubric. No rubric
