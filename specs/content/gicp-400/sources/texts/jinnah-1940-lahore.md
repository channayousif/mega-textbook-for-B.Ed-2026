## Evidence notes (original paraphrase)

Jinnah argues that Muslims form a nation, drawing on differences in religion, social life, literature and historical memory. He rejects permanent subordination to a majority in one political system. He also expresses a wish for peace with neighbours. The unit reads the political argument alongside his later citizenship speech; peaceful interstate relations alone would not establish domestic equal citizenship.
