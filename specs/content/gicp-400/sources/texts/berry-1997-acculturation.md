## Evidence notes (verified summary)

Berry sets out a framework for how individuals and groups adapt when cultures live in contact. He names four acculturation strategies seen from the side of non-dominant groups: assimilation, integration, separation and marginalisation.

Key points supported by the text:
1. **Assimilation**: individuals do not wish to keep their cultural identity and seek daily interaction with other cultures.
2. **Integration**: there is interest both in keeping one's original culture and in daily interaction with other groups; some cultural integrity is kept while taking part in the larger social network.
3. **Forced versus chosen**: when the dominant group enforces a strategy or constrains choice, other terms apply; forced assimilation is likened to a "pressure cooker" rather than a melting pot.
4. **Conditions for integration**: integration can only be freely chosen and pursued when the dominant society is open and inclusive towards cultural diversity.
5. **Outcomes**: the integrationist strategy appears to be a consistent predictor of more positive outcomes than assimilation, separation or marginalisation.

## Scope limits

Berry writes about immigrants and other non-dominant groups adapting within a society of settlement. The unit uses his definitions of assimilation and integration as concepts and applies them to a multilingual federation; that application is the unit's, not Berry's. He does not discuss Pakistan or its Constitution.
