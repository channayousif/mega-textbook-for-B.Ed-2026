# Individual Differences and Special Needs - assessment and review

## Unit summary

The learning-styles claim fails at its matched-teaching half: preferences are real
but matching shows little gain, and the self-report measures liking, not processing.
What survives is free: pupils differ, and multimodal teaching reaches more of them.
The right account of difference is the profile - a present level per domain, sampled
poorly by any single rank.

The slow learner needs pace, not a ceiling: shorter steps to the same destination,
time, prior-knowledge checks, visible progress. The gifted learner needs real
challenge, choice and honest demand, with the group life every pupil needs. The
pupil with a disability needs a specific barrier removed and deliberate inclusion in
every activity - and the class needs that pupil, because inclusion teaches what
school is for. Disabilities are ambiguous, the pupil is never the label, and
inclusion is a direction whose first step is always visible.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The learning-styles claim, at its strongest, holds that: *(Remember)*
   a) pupils report preferences
   b) teaching matched to a diagnosed style improves learning
   c) pupils differ in pace
   d) multimodal teaching reaches more pupils

2. The research verdict on matched teaching is that it shows: *(Understand)*
   a) strong gains for matched pupils
   b) little or no gain
   c) gains only for visual learners
   d) gains only in primary classes

3. What survives the styles verdict is: *(Understand)*
   a) the three-style chart
   b) pupils differ, and multimodal teaching reaches more of them
   c) matching by self-report
   d) grouping pupils by style

4. A profile, as this unit uses the word, is: *(Understand)*
   a) a fixed input channel
   b) a shape of present levels across domains
   c) a self-reported preference
   d) a single rank

5. The slow learner's label describes: *(Remember)*
   a) a ceiling on attainment
   b) pace, not a ceiling
   c) a disability category
   d) low intelligence

6. Which of these does NOT help the slow learner? *(Apply)*
   a) shorter steps to the same destination
   b) time
   c) more of the same worksheet faster
   d) prior-knowledge checks

7. A gifted pupil's support should be mainly: *(Understand)*
   a) acceleration: the same work, sooner
   b) enrichment: real problems, choice, honest demand
   c) more worksheets at the same level
   d) withdrawal from the interesting work

8. The inclusion principle says the pupil with a disability should: *(Remember)*
   a) attend a separate school with better equipment
   b) participate in and contribute to the life of the class as much as possible
   c) join the class only for games
   d) be taught at home until fully able

9. "Disabilities are ambiguous" means: *(Understand)*
   a) disabilities do not exist
   b) categories overlook the person, and a pupil's needs are hard to sort into types
   c) teachers should not name any barrier
   d) every disability looks the same

10. Inclusion teaches the classmates: *(Apply)*
    a) nothing; it is for the disabled pupil alone
    b) that school is partly about providing opportunities for everyone, not just
       comparing skills
    c) to lower their expectations
    d) to compete harder

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. State the styles claim and the evidence verdict, fairly, in two sentences.
   *(Understand)*
2. Name the two things that survive the verdict, and say why neither needs a chart.
   *(Understand)*
3. Distinguish a style claim from a profile reading with one example of each.
   *(Understand)*
4. Define the slow learner in one sentence and name what the label must never be
   confused with. *(Understand)*
5. Name two things that help the slow learner and two that do not. *(Understand)*
6. Explain three recognition signs of giftedness that look past the marks. *(Understand)*
7. Explain why "more worksheets" fails the gifted pupil, and what replaces it.
   *(Understand)*
8. State the inclusion principle in one sentence, in your own words. *(Understand)*
9. The school's job with a disability is to remove the barrier, not manage the
   category. Explain the difference with an example. *(Apply)*
10. In three sentences, answer the second school's "we are not equipped" argument.
    *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The staff meeting's charts.** The school sorted pupils into styles and planned
   three-lesson versions for two months. Analyse what the initiative got right, got
   wrong and cost, and write the three-sentence memo you would have given instead.
   *(Analyze)*

2. **The lopsided profile.** A Class 6 pupil is two years ahead in language and two
   behind in number. Read the pupil through the profile idea, design the teaching
   that fits each domain at its own level, and say what the single-rank report card
   hides. *(Analyze)*

3. **The two schools.** One moved a bench; one sent the pupil home. Analyse what each
   school assumed a classroom is for, what each gained and lost, and answer the
   second school's resources argument at its strongest. *(Analyze)*

4. **The first month.** You are the new head teacher of the school with two steps at
   every entrance, a three-passes-behind reader and an early finisher. Rank the first
   month's steps by cost, justify the ranking, and name the step you would take first
   and why. *(Analyze)*

5. **One lesson, three learners.** Design one lesson - any class, any subject - that
   genuinely serves the slow learner, the gifted learner and the pupil with a
   disability at once. Name what each needs in this unit's terms, the mechanism that
   serves all three, and the one honest limit of the design. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - the claim's load-bearing half is the matched-teaching gain.
2. **b** - matching studies show little or no gain.
3. **b** - the free residue: difference and multimodality.
4. **b** - a shape of present levels across domains, not a channel or a rank.
5. **b** - pace, never a ceiling.
6. **c** - more-faster of the same is the classic harm.
7. **b** - enrichment; acceleration only moves the boredom forward.
8. **b** - participate in and contribute to the life of the class.
9. **b** - categories overlook the person; needs defy neat sorting.
10. **b** - school is partly about opportunities for everyone, not just comparison.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. The claim: fixed styles, matched teaching, better learning; the verdict: matching
   shows little or no gain (2).
2. Pupils differ; multimodal teaching reaches more pupils (1); both were true before
   the theory and need no chart, only a look at the room and one extra mode (1).
3. A style is a claimed input channel ("teach him through his hands"); a profile is a
   present level in a domain ("his number work is two years behind his language")
   (2).
4. A pupil who needs more time and more passes to reach the same place (1); never
   confused with a ceiling, a disability category or low intelligence (1).
5. Helps: shorter steps same destination, time, prior-knowledge checks, visible
   progress (any two, 1); harms: more-faster worksheets, public comparison,
   withdrawal of the interesting work (any two, 1).
6. Any three of: unusually quick independent learning; advanced language; motivation
   on challenge; high self-imposed standards; the early finisher; arguing the
   textbook into a corner (1 for three).
7. More worksheets feed the boredom, not the need (1); real problems, choice and
   honest demand replace it (1).
8. The pupil with a disability participates in and contributes to the life of the
   class as much as possible (1).
9. Managing the category acts on the label (the "disabled child"); removing the
   barrier acts on the specific obstacle - a step, a font size, a hearing distance
   (1 for the distinction, 1 for any apt example).
10. Any three-sentence answer that names: barriers are specific and cheap to start
    removing; the class loses what the pupil would have taught it; "not equipped"
    describes the first step as the whole journey (2).
