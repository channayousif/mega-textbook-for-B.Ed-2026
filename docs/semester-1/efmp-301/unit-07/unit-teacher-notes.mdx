# Individual Differences and Special Needs - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Week 12** to Chapter 7: learning
styles and abilities; slow learners and gifted learners; inclusive education. Two
topics, one session each.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This unit runs on two paired cases and one walk.

**Run the styles staff meeting as the opener.** Most trainees have sat in that
meeting or its cousin. Let them describe it before the evidence is named; the verdict
then lands on their own experience rather than on a straw man.

**Pair the two schools.** The bench and the "not equipped" refusal are the unit's
moral centre, and the counter-case must be made at its strongest before it is
answered. A room that has never felt the resources argument has never taught in a
Sindh school.

**Set the walk before the second session.** The practical work (below) asks trainees
to walk their practicum school as a wheelchair user would. The walk does more for the
inclusion material than any lecture, because it turns "the school is not equipped"
from a verdict into a list.

verdict is one finding; inclusion is the guide's own CLO 6 outcome and the unit's
centre of gravity.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"The styles theory is harmless."** Meet it with the narrowing harm: the
"kinaesthetic learner" who never gets the board, the reading, the listening. A wrong
explanation of a real difference always ends by narrowing the pupil.

**"Slow means weak."** The pace-not-ceiling distinction, with the visible-progress
remedy: the slow learner who can see October beating September has been taught; the
one who cannot has been ranked.

**"Gifted pupils will be fine on their own."** Unit 5's material returns here: the
boredom-to-behaviour path. The early finisher is the standing counter-example in
every trainee's own schooling.

**"Inclusion is charity."** The two schools, read side by side, do the work: the
classmates in the first school learned what school is for. Do not state this; ask the
room what the classmates in each school learned, and let them find the asymmetry.

**"We are not equipped."** The honest reply is not denial but the walk: a list of
specific barriers, each with a cost, cheapest first. The argument dissolves into a
plan.

## Practical work

The practical work for this chapter (the unit's instantiation of the course guide's
course-generic group-assignment slot): **each group
walks one school as a wheelchair user would - entrances, rooms, toilets, shelves,
seats - and produces the three cheapest changes that would open the most of the
school.**

Set the walk before the second session; run the reporting in it. Each group presents
its three changes with costs; the class ranks all proposals into a single school
inclusion plan. Fifteen minutes of reporting; the plan is the session's artefact and
the seed of ERQ 4.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the two topics' `Check your understanding` blocks
(ten items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The `unit-assessment`
page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded
section.

ERQ 5 is the capstone and integrates both topics; schedule it after the practical
work so the lesson design has a real school behind it. ERQ 3 rewards the two-schools
analysis the second session trains, and works well as the week's written homework.
