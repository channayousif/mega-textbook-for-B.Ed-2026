# Individual Differences and Special Needs

Unit 1 said learners differ. This one-week unit asks what a teacher should do about
it - and, just as important, what a teacher should *not* believe about it. The
learning-styles claim is tested against the evidence. Slow learning, giftedness and
disability are separated from each other and from the labels the staff room uses. And
inclusion is treated as the guide's own CLO 6 outcome: the classroom that every pupil
belongs to, not the charity the school extends.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- State the **learning-styles claim** and what the evidence actually supports.
- Explain **abilities as a profile** rather than a point, and what profiles mean for
  teaching.
- Recognise and support **slow learners**: what helps, what does not, and what the
  difference is from disability.
- Recognise and support **gifted learners** as an educational need.
- Explain **inclusive education**: what it means, why it matters, and what a Sindh
  school's first steps are.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-6 (the learner-difference idea; intelligence and giftedness from Unit 5;
motivation from Unit 6). This unit turns those ideas toward the pupils a teacher
actually meets.

## How to use this unit

- **Hold the styles claim honestly.** The unit asks for the same two-handed skill as
  Unit 5: what the staff room believes, and what the research found, both stated
  fairly.
- **Keep one real class in mind.** The unit assessment asks for a mixed-ability class
  analysed into a differentiation plan; the notes you keep now are the raw material.
- **Refuse the labels.** Every topic here replaces a staff-room word ("weak",
  "lazy", "special") with a reading that has a next step.
- After the two topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank
  with answers and marking guidance.
