# Assessment and Evaluation - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Assessment is evidence gathered for a decision. Its purposes: diagnose (before,
feeding where to start), form (during, feeding tomorrow's teaching), sum (after,
feeding mastery, readiness and grades) and certify (feeding selection beyond the
classroom). Teacher-made and standardised instruments trade fit for comparability in
opposite directions, and the choice follows the decision.

Validity belongs to the interpretation of a score, not the test: an interpretation
is valid when rivals are ruled out or the mixture is named. Reliability is
consistency - across days, items and markers - and the two properties are
independent, each with its own repair. Bias is the group-level version of the
mixture problem. Difficulty answers neither quality question.

Formative and summative evaluation differ by purpose, not timing: the elicit-
interpret-act cycle at the speed of days versus the after-instruction certification
of mastery. The same quiz can be either, the class reads the difference within a
month, and the rule is to name the decision first and let it choose the timing.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Assessment, as this unit defines it, is: *(Remember)*
   a) a test at the end of a unit
   b) the gathering of evidence about learning so that somebody can decide
      something
   c) the allocation of marks
   d) whatever the board sends

2. The four purposes of assessment are: *(Remember)*
   a) test, quiz, exam, project
   b) diagnose, form, sum, certify
   c) marks, grades, ranks, reports
   d) written, oral, practical, observed

3. The April fractions check, given before instruction, is: *(Apply)*
   a) formative
   b) diagnostic
   c) summative
   d) certification

4. Formative assessment's defining feature is that it: *(Understand)*
   a) is short
   b) changes teaching while learning is still under way
   c) carries no marks
   d) happens every week

5. A standardised test standardises: *(Remember)*
   a) the pupils
   b) the items, administration and scoring
   c) the teaching
   d) the interpretations

6. Validity belongs to: *(Understand)*
   a) the test itself
   b) the interpretation and use of the results, for a given group
   c) the publisher
   d) the difficulty level

7. Reliability is: *(Remember)*
   a) the same thing as validity
   b) the consistency of the measurement
   c) the fairness of the content
   d) the difficulty of the items

8. A test that consistently measures the wrong mixture is: *(Apply)*
   a) valid and reliable
   b) reliable and invalid
   c) valid and unreliable
   d) neither

9. The same quiz became formative in one room and summative in the next because
   of: *(Apply)*
   a) the items
   b) the timing
   c) the purpose its results fed
   d) the subject

10. The practical rule for choosing assessment timing is: *(Understand)*
    a) formative on Tuesdays, summative in December
    b) name the decision first, and let the decision choose the timing
    c) always summative, for safety
    d) always formative, for kindness

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The fourth decision.** The Class 7 year had assessments for three decisions;
   the fourth - which pupils need which support next term - sat unaddressed. Argue
   what instrument should feed it, when it should run, and what the three existing
   assessments cannot tell it. *(Analyze)*

2. **The 40 per cent.** Evaluate the geography test: what the score measures, what
   interpretation it can and cannot support, whether it is reliable, and where the
   bias sits. Repair it in two moves - one for validity, one for reliability - and
   name each repair's cost. *(Analyze)*

3. **The two corridors.** The same quiz fed planning in one room and the record
   book in the other. Analyse what changed in each class and why, using the purpose
   distinction and the motivation finding, and say what the second teacher should
   have done with the marks instead. *(Analyze)*

4. **The difficult test.** A colleague defends a hard test: "it sorts them out".
   Evaluate the defence: what difficulty does and does not tell you about a test,
   and what the two quality questions would ask instead. Use the target diagram. *(Analyze)*

5. **The fortnight's cycle.** Design a complete formative cycle for one real
   topic: the goal as a DO, the eliciting instruments with days, the
   interpretations against the goal, the teaching moves for three possible
   findings, and the summative item that closes the fortnight. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - evidence gathered for a decision; the decision organises it.
2. **b** - diagnose, form, sum, certify.
3. **b** - before instruction, feeding where to start: diagnostic.
4. **b** - the purpose is to change teaching while learning is live.
5. **b** - items, administration conditions and scoring rules.
6. **b** - validity is the adequacy of interpretations and uses, for a given group.
7. **b** - consistency of measurement.
8. **b** - consistent (reliable) but on the wrong thing (invalid).
9. **c** - the purpose the results fed, not the instrument or the day.
10. **b** - name the decision first; the decision chooses the timing.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. Evidence gathered so somebody can decide something (1); the decision organises
   it because an assessment without a decision is paperwork (1).
2. Diagnostic feeds where to start (before instruction); formative feeds the next
   teaching move (during) - the difference is the decision, not the calendar alone
   (2).
3. Standardised fixes items, administration and scoring (1); teacher-made trades
   comparability for exact fit to this class's question (1).
4. Validity is the adequacy and appropriateness of the interpretations and uses of
   results, for a given group (1); the staff room's claim was invalid because a
   rival explanation - English reading - fit the evidence as well (1).
5. Would scores be similar on another day? With different items? With a different
   marker? (1 for the three questions; 1 for "consistency" named).
6. Reliability is consistency, validity is fit of interpretation; a test can
   measure the wrong mixture consistently - the geography test twice (2).
7. Elicit, interpret, act (1); act makes it formative - without a changed decision
   the first two are theatre (1).
8. The purpose the results fed: planning book versus record book; the class reads
   the difference as information versus threat (2).
9. Motivation and confidence - the type of assessment and the feedback given both
   influence them (1).
10. The December test feeds grades, not re-teaching; its evidence arrives when the
    teaching it would shape is over - the decision chose the timing, and the
    fractions gap needed April (2).
