# Assessment and Evaluation - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Assessment is evidence gathered for a decision. Its purposes: diagnose (before,
feeding where to start), form (during, feeding tomorrow's teaching), sum (after,
feeding mastery, readiness and grades) and certify (feeding selection beyond the
classroom). Teacher-made and standardised instruments trade fit for comparability in
opposite directions, and the choice follows the decision.

Validity belongs to the interpretation of a score, not the test: an interpretation
is valid when rivals are ruled out or the mixture is named. Reliability is
consistency - across days, items and markers - and the two properties are
independent, each with its own repair. Bias is the group-level version of the
mixture problem. Difficulty answers neither quality question.

Formative use feeds ongoing or future learning; summative use reports mastery,
readiness and grades. Evidence gathered for one can serve the other if its uses
are justified. It cannot change past lessons, but can guide future support.
Assessment and feedback can influence motivation and confidence, without a fixed
response or timescale.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Assessment, as this unit defines it, is: *(Remember)*
   a) a test at the end of a unit
   b) the gathering of evidence about learning so that somebody can decide
      something
   c) the allocation of marks
   d) whatever the board sends

2. The four purposes of assessment are: *(Remember)*
   a) diagnose, form, sum, certify
   b) test, quiz, exam, project
   c) marks, grades, ranks, reports
   d) written, oral, practical, observed

3. Before a new science unit, a teacher checks which prerequisite ideas pupils
   already know to choose the starting lesson. This use is: *(Apply)*
   a) formative
   b) diagnostic
   c) summative
   d) certification

4. Formative assessment's defining feature is that it: *(Understand)*
   a) is short
   b) carries no marks
   c) changes teaching while learning is still under way
   d) happens every week

5. A standardised test standardises: *(Remember)*
   a) the pupils
   b) the interpretations
   c) the teaching
   d) the items, administration and scoring

6. Validity belongs to: *(Understand)*
   a) the interpretation and use of the results, for a given group
   b) the test itself
   c) the publisher
   d) the difficulty level

7. Reliability is: *(Remember)*
   a) the same thing as validity
   b) the fairness of the content
   c) the consistency of the measurement
   d) the difficulty of the items

8. A board examination used to award a qualification primarily serves which
   assessment purpose? *(Understand)*
   a) diagnosing prior knowledge
   b) shaping tomorrow's lesson
   c) recording a practice attempt
   d) certifying attainment beyond the classroom

9. After a unit test is graded, a teacher groups pupils by their errors and
   plans extra lessons. This additional use of the results is: *(Apply)*
   a) certification only
   b) ranking only
   c) formative
   d) proof of reliability

10. The practical rule for choosing assessment timing is: *(Understand)*
    a) formative on Tuesdays, summative in December
    b) name the decision first, and let the decision choose the timing
    c) always summative, for safety
    d) always formative, for kindness

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define assessment in one sentence, and say why the decision organises it.
   *(Understand)*
2. Explain the diagnostic-formative difference by decision, not timing. *(Understand)*
3. Explain what a standardised test fixes, and what a teacher-made test trades for
   its freedom. *(Understand)*
4. Define validity, and say why the geography staff room's claim was invalid.
   *(Understand)*
5. Explain reliability using the three consistency questions. *(Understand)*
6. Explain why a test can be reliable and invalid, with an example. *(Understand)*
7. Describe the formative cycle's three steps, and say which one makes it
   formative. *(Understand)*
8. A teacher grades a completed unit test, then groups pupils by their errors for
   next week's lessons. Analyse the two uses of the evidence and explain why the
   second use can still help learning. *(Analyze)*
9. Explain what assessment influences besides achievement, on the research summary.
   *(Understand)*
10. Before a reading unit, a teacher must decide which pupils need decoding
    support. Choose an instrument and its timing, name the assessment purpose,
    and explain why a total mark from last term may be insufficient. *(Apply)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Write **250-300 words for each ERQ**. Use the evidence given; label hypothetical
outcomes and state what cannot yet be concluded.

1. **The support decision.** The Class 7 year includes the April check, Tuesday
   quizzes, December unit test and March board examination. Decide what additional
   evidence would identify support needed next term. Argue when to gather it and
   how each of the four assessments could help but might leave gaps. *(Analyze)*

2. **The 40 per cent.** Evaluate the geography test: what the score measures, what
   interpretation it can and cannot support, what is unknown about reliability from
   one score, and where bias may sit. Propose
   a validity repair and a reliability check with a repair if inconsistency is
   found; name their costs. *(Analyze)*

3. **The two corridors.** Analyse the uses of the same quiz in the two rooms.
   Using the motivation and feedback account, compare possible pupil responses
   without assuming a fixed reaction or timescale. Propose how the second teacher
   could retain a justified grade and also use the errors to help learning. *(Analyze)*

4. **The difficult test.** A colleague defends a hard test: "it sorts them out".
   Evaluate the defence: what difficulty does and does not tell you about a test,
   and what the two quality questions would ask instead. Use the target diagram. *(Analyze)*

5. **The fortnight's cycle.** Design a complete formative cycle for one real
   topic: the goal as a DO, the eliciting instruments with days, the
   interpretations against the goal, the teaching moves for three possible
   findings, and the summative item that closes the fortnight. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - evidence gathered for a decision.
2. **a** - diagnose, form, sum, certify.
3. **b** - diagnostic: prerequisite evidence chooses the starting point.
4. **c** - formative use changes teaching while learning is under way.
5. **d** - items, administration and scoring.
6. **a** - validity concerns interpretations and uses for a given group.
7. **c** - reliability is consistency of measurement.
8. **d** - the qualification decision is certification.
9. **c** - the additional use shapes future learning.
10. **b** - name the decision first; let it choose the timing.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. Evidence gathered so somebody can decide something (1); the decision organises
   it because an assessment without a decision is paperwork (1).
2. Diagnostic feeds where to start (before instruction); formative feeds the next
   teaching move (during) - the difference is the decision, not the calendar alone
   (2).
3. Standardised fixes items, administration and scoring (1); teacher-made trades
   comparability for exact fit to this class's question (1).
4. Validity is the adequacy and appropriateness of the interpretations and uses of
   results, for a given group (1); the staff room's claim was invalid because a
   rival explanation - English reading - fit the evidence as well (1).
5. Would scores be similar on another day? With different items? With a different
   marker? (1 for the three questions; 1 for "consistency" named).
6. Reliability is consistency, validity is fit of interpretation; a test can
   measure the wrong mixture consistently - a hypothetical language-heavy test
   producing similar scores repeatedly
   (1 for the distinction; 1 for a consistent but irrelevant mixture). The opening
   geography score alone does not establish reliability.
7. Elicit, interpret, act (1); act makes it formative - without a changed decision
   the first two are theatre (1).
8. Reporting completed-unit mastery or grades is summative (1). Grouping by errors
   to plan future lessons is formative, even though the evidence came from the same
   test; those future lessons remain open to change (1).
9. Motivation and confidence - the type of assessment and the feedback given both
   influence them (1).
10. A short decoding task before instruction, used diagnostically to allocate
    support (1). Last term's total may hide which decoding errors persist and may
    be out of date; targeted current evidence is needed (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20. For every content criterion, award the full maximum
for an accurate explanation supported by case evidence or a justified proposal;
award half for a relevant claim with incomplete evidence or reasoning; award zero
for an absent or incorrect response. Use intermediate whole marks for mixed work.
For the 4 writing marks, award 2 for clear organisation and 2 for accurate terms;
award 1 in either part when partly achieved, 0 when absent. The requested length
is 250-300 words; give length feedback separately, without deducting content marks.

**Model outlines for the content criteria**

- **ERQ 1:** Use the April check as a baseline, Tuesday errors as evidence during
  learning, December results as mastery evidence and March results for broader
  attainment. None guarantees a current skill-level support diagnosis. Before next
  term, use targeted tasks and work samples to identify specific needs; justify
  relevance and timing. Full credit needs uses and limits of all four, not dismissal
  of every existing score.
- **ERQ 2:** The pupil recognises tools but struggles with English prompts, so weak
  geography is not warranted. Reduce irrelevant language and check tool use, at a
  cost in preparation and comparability. Reliability is unknown from one score.
  Compare independent markings or equivalent tasks under comparable conditions;
  if inconsistent, clarify criteria or items and recheck, at a cost in staff time.
  Investigate whether other Sindhi-medium pupils face the same irrelevant barrier.
  Full credit on the 8-mark criterion requires uncertainty, a check and a conditional
  repair as well as a distinct validity repair.
- **ERQ 3:** Separate lesson planning from grading. Useful feedback may encourage
  revision; unexplained ranking may discourage some pupils, but reactions need
  evidence and have no fixed timescale. Keep a justified grade, discuss errors and
  plan follow-up teaching. Full credit needs both legitimate uses and qualified
  reasoning about motivation, not a prediction that all grading produces threat.
- **ERQ 4:** A hard test may distinguish attainment when tasks match the goals. A
  low pass rate alone establishes neither valid interpretations nor consistent
  measurement. Check content and irrelevant demands, then occasions/items/markers;
  use the target diagram to distinguish those questions.
- **ERQ 5:** Example goal: add unlike fractions. Elicit on day 1, compare errors to
  the goal and revisit with equivalent tasks on day 4. For missing equivalence,
  model fraction strips; for denominator errors, practise common multiples; for
  secure work, advance to word problems. Close with an independent addition task
  on day 10 to report mastery. Full credit needs three findings tied to different
  moves, dated evidence checks and a summative decision justified by the goal.
