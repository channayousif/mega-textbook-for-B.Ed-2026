# Assessment and Evaluation - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Week 14** to Chapter 9: types
and purposes of assessment, psychological tests and measurement, and formative and
summative evaluation. Three topics, one week - and one meta-opportunity no other
unit has: the course itself is the running example.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This unit's best case study is the trainees' own transcript.

**Open with the four assessments.** The April check, Tuesday quiz, December unit
test and March board examination illustrate the four purposes. Place the trainees'
assessments against these purposes, allowing an instrument to serve more than one.

**Use the course as the worked example.** The per-topic `Check your understanding`
blocks are formative cycles; the unit banks are summative instruments; this unit's
own bank is the demonstration of everything it teaches. Say so, in the third session,
and the whole course clicks into place.

**Run the double-marking.** The practical work's marking-twice exercise teaches
reliability faster than any definition: the trainee who disagrees with their own
first marking has met inconsistency in the one marker they trusted.

## Sequencing across the week

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| First session | Topic 9.1 - the decision-first discipline, the four purposes | the name-the-decision activity |
| Second session | Topic 9.2 - standardisation, validity, reliability, bias | the same-quiz-twice activity |
| Third session | Topic 9.3 - the cycle, purpose not timing, the two corridors | the design-the-cycle activity |

If a session is lost, compress Topic 9.1's table work rather than Topic 9.2's
definitions. Validity and reliability are the unit's transferable vocabulary; the
four purposes can be re-derived from them.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Assessment means marks at the end."** The four purposes, placed against the
trainees' own schooling, do the work. Ask how many of the tests they listed fed a
decision while learning was still live.

**"A reliable test is a valid test."** The geography case is the standing
counter-example: a consistently low score could still reflect irrelevant language.
The opening supplies no repeat scores, so reliability must remain unestablished. Draw the target diagram; the
independence lands visually before it lands verbally.

**"A difficult test is a good test."** Ask what difficulty indicates - and keep
asking until the room admits it indicates difficulty. The two quality questions
then arrive as the replacement.

**"Formative assessment is just small tests."** The two-corridors case is the
reply: the same quiz, two uses. Reactions to grades and feedback may differ;
the illustration establishes no fixed response or one-month timescale. The
instrument is not the purpose.

## Practical work

The guide lists general practical categories, including individual and group
assignments, rather than a Chapter 9 double-marking task. This unit's authored
implementation is: **each
trainee takes one recent test from their practicum, marks it twice on different days
without looking back, and brings both markings to the second session.**

In pairs, the differences are diagnosed: unclear rubric, ambiguous wording, marker
mood - each a reliability repair. Each pair then names one interpretation the scores
could not support (the validity question), and repairs it. Allow preparation and marking on two different days before the second session,
then twenty minutes for pair discussion. These tests practise the reasoning used in
ERQ 2; they supply no repeat evidence for its separate geography case.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the three topics' `Check your understanding`
blocks (fifteen items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The
`unit-assessment` page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in
its final bounded section - and is itself the unit's worked example.

ERQ 5 is the capstone and integrates all three topics; schedule it after the
practical work so the cycle has a real topic behind it. ERQ 3 rewards the
two-corridors analysis the third session trains, and works well as the week's
written homework.
