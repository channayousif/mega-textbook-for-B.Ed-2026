# Assessment and Evaluation

The trainees taking this course have been assessed their whole lives, mostly without
anyone asking what the assessments were for. This one-week unit asks. Assessment is
not the end of teaching; it is part of its machinery - a way of gathering evidence
about learning so that somebody can decide something. Who decides what, on which
evidence, and when, turns out to be the whole subject.

The unit's spine is a distinction the trainees already know without knowing they know
it: the difference between the test that ranks them in December and the five-minute
exit question that changed Wednesday's lesson.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Name the **types of assessment** and the decisions each feeds: diagnostic,
  formative, summative, teacher-made, standardised.
- Explain **psychological tests and measurement**: what a standardised test
  standardises, and what teacher-made tests trade for their freedom.
- Define **validity and reliability** precisely, and evaluate a described test for
  each with reasons.
- Distinguish **formative and summative evaluation** by purpose, not timing, and
  design a formative cycle for a named topic.

## How to use this unit

- **Test every claim on an assessment you have sat.** The trainee's own history of
  tests is the unit's richest data set.
- **Keep one topic in mind.** The unit assessment asks you to design a formative
  cycle and evaluate a test for validity and reliability; a real topic makes both
  concrete.
- **Notice the course practising what it teaches.** The per-topic checks in this
  course are formative cycles; the unit banks are summative instruments. The unit
  will ask you to see that.
- After the three topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank
  with answers and marking guidance.
