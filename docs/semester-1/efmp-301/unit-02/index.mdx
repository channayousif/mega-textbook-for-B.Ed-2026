# Human Growth and Development

Unit 1 ended with a promise: psychology gives a teacher better questions than the staff room
supplies. This unit collects the first set of those questions. A Class 2 pupil who cannot
compose a sentence, a Class 7 pupil who cannot sit still after lunch, a Class 5 pupil who
cries at a wrong answer: each of these is usually read as a behaviour problem or an ability
fact. Development offers a third reading - a description of where the pupil is on a long
road that every human travels, in an order that is largely fixed and at a speed that is
almost entirely individual.

That third reading changes what a teacher does next. It replaces "why is this pupil not like
the others?" with "where is this pupil, and what is the next step from there?" This unit
works through that shift in four topics.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Define **development** and distinguish it from growth, maturation and learning.
- State the main **principles** of development - continuous yet stage-like, orderly in
  sequence, uneven in rate - and use them to read a real classroom observation.
- Explain how **heredity and environment** work together as joint drivers rather than
  competitors.
- Describe **physical development** through childhood and adolescence, and say what each
  phase does and does not mean for seating, safety, sport and school work.
- Describe the **cognitive, emotional and social** domains of development and give a
  classroom sign of each.
- Read a pupil's work or behaviour against a **developmental stage** description, and
  justify a teaching choice as matched to that stage.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 (the three-part scope; evidence over proverbs). No biology beyond HSC is assumed.
What you do bring, again, is your own schooling: you have lived through every stage this
unit describes, and several topics will ask you to remember it.

## How to use this unit

- **Work through the topics in order.** Topic 2.3 uses the principles from 2.1, and Topic
  2.4 assembles everything into the stage reading a teacher actually uses.
- **Keep a named pupil in mind.** Each topic asks you to apply its idea to one real pupil
  you have taught or been. The unit assessment will ask you to do this in writing, so the
  notes you keep now are worth having.
- **Do not skip the activities.** The drawing comparison in 2.1 and the video task in 2.3
  are where the principles stop being words.
- After the four topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank with
  answers and marking guidance.
