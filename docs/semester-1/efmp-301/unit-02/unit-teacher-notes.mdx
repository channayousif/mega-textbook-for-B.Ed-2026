# Human Growth and Development - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Weeks 3 and 4** to Chapter 2, with
Week 3 on principles of development and physical development, and Week 4 on the
cognitive, emotional and social domains plus stages and their educational implications.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This unit rewards three of them disproportionately.

**Demonstrate, do not narrate.** The two-glasses demonstration is the unit's spine. Pour
the water in front of the trainees, ask for the vote, and only then show the clips or
tell them the ages. A narrated conservation task persuades nobody; a performed one
surprises everyone, including trainees who have taught Class 2 for years.

**Use the case study as a running thread.** Allah Dino opens Topic 2.1, returns in the
assessment, and can be invoked in every topic in between. Trainees hold a unit together
far better around one pupil they have argued about than around a syllabus of terms.

**Protect the drawing comparison.** The Class 1 versus Class 5 person-drawing task in
Topic 2.1 produces genuine surprise. Collect the drawings anonymously; the argument
about what they show is the sequence and rate principles being built by the class.

## Sequencing across the two weeks

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| Week 3, first session | Topic 2.1 - engines, principles, heredity and environment | the sort-the-changes activity in full |
| Week 3, second session | Topic 2.2 - physical development through adolescence | the read-the-room advice activity |
| Week 4, first session | Topic 2.3 - the three domains, Piaget descriptively | the two-glasses demonstration and the three-readings activity |
| Week 4, second session | Topic 2.4 - stages and matched teaching | the match-or-mismatch activity; expect it to overrun |

If a session is lost, compress Topic 2.2's table work rather than Topic 2.3's
demonstration. The stage idea without the domains behind it collapses into labels,
which is exactly the misconception the unit exists to remove.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Development means growing bigger."** Handle it with the engines table early. Ask what
grew when a pupil learned the birds of the schoolyard; the answer ("nothing you can
measure") breaks the equation immediately.

**"Piaget's stages are deadlines."** The most damaging one, because it turns a reading
tool into a judgement. Ask the trainees what follows from "the pupil has not reached
concrete operations" - and keep asking until someone says "so I teach with materials".
The stage vocabulary is only worth having if its last step is a teaching decision.

**"Small means slow."** The Nawabshah situation is built for this. Ask which of the two
staff-room explanations changes anything the school can do; the room usually supplies
the answer itself.

**"Hereditary means hopeless."** The two-sisters example is the counter: the same
family, the same genes for height, and a vocabulary difference made entirely of evening
stories. The point to land is the teacher's six hours.

## Language and access

The register of this unit is concrete by design - engines, roads, maps, pages. Keep the
Urdu equivalents active (نشوونما for development is banked in the terminology list) and
let trainees argue in Sindhi where the argument runs better, as in Unit 1. The written
summative tasks remain in the language of assessment.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter: **each trainee brings one observation
of a real pupil - from their own schooling, their practicum, or their family - that
changed when the pupil got older, and one that changed when the pupil was taught.**

In pairs, they sort their two observations into the engines, then swap and challenge
each other's sorting. Collect the disputed cases on the board; they are almost always
the mixtures, which is the point of the closing note in Topic 2.1's concept figure.

Allow twenty-five minutes. It works best at the end of Week 3, after Topic 2.2 and
before the domains.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the four topics' `Check your understanding` blocks
(twenty items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The `unit-assessment`
page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded
section.

ERQ 5 is the unit's capstone and deliberately integrates all four topics. Warn trainees
in advance that it will ask for one week's plan for four pupils; the notes they keep in
the "keep a named pupil in mind" thread through the unit are the raw material for it.
