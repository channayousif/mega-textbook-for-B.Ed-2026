# Mental Health and Well-being in Schools - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Mental health, in WHO's framing, is a state of well-being enabling coping,
realising abilities and contributing - more than the absence of illness, and a basic
human right. Well-being is a balance of demands against supports, and the school is
one of the supports: its routines, belonging, feedback and safety are well-being
machinery, and social-emotional learning works across income levels.

The observable signs - withdrawal, unexplained falls in marks, out-of-scale
irritability, clustered absence, and talk of hopelessness - are read as patterns,
never as diagnoses, and the talk is always taken as real. Self-harm talk and a
sudden calm in distress demand same-day escalation.

The teacher's role has three parts: notice (the private map), respond within the
classroom (the course's own moves as well-being machinery), and refer beyond it
(the words written, dated, handed over the same day). The boundary protects the
pupil twice over; what ends at the handover is the responsibility for treatment,
never the relationship.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. WHO's definition of mental health is: *(Remember)*
   a) the absence of mental illness
   b) a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with the stresses
      of life
   c) a feeling of happiness
   d) a diagnosis made by a doctor

2. "Mental health is more than the absence of illness" means: *(Understand)*
   a) illness does not exist
   b) a pupil without a diagnosis can still be unwell, and a diagnosed pupil can
      function well with supports
   c) diagnoses are always wrong
   d) only feelings matter

3. Well-being, in this unit's image, is: *(Remember)*
   a) a feeling of calm
   b) a balance of demands against supports
   c) the absence of demands
   d) high marks

4. The two Class 8 pupils show that: *(Apply)*
   a) marks measure well-being
   b) the staff room reads well-being backwards: the brilliant pupil's pan is
      loaded on one side only
   c) middling marks mean middling well-being
   d) boys are always steadier

5. Which of these is NOT a sign as this unit lists them? *(Remember)*
   a) withdrawal from friends and activities
   b) a fall in marks with no teaching explanation
   c) enjoying break time
   d) talk of hopelessness

6. The rule for reading signs is: *(Understand)*
   a) any single bad day is a sign
   b) read the pattern, not the day: the change that persists
   c) only marks count
   d) wait a month before noticing

7. The two same-day escalation signs are: *(Remember)*
   a) absence and irritability
   b) talk of self-harm, and a sudden calm in visible distress
   c) falling marks and withdrawal
   d) crying and anger

8. The teacher's role in well-being has: *(Remember)*
   a) one part: teach well and leave it there
   b) two parts: notice and treat
   c) three parts: notice, respond within the classroom, refer beyond it
   d) unlimited scope

9. Which classroom response is inside the teaching role? *(Apply)*
   a) diagnosing the pupil's condition
   b) promising to keep a disclosure secret
   c) keeping the routine unbroken and greeting by name
   d) counselling the pupil weekly

10. "Referring is giving up on the pupil" is: *(Understand)*
    a) true; the teacher should carry it alone
    b) false: the referral is the care delivered by the right hands, and the
       relationship continues after it
    c) true; only therapists help
    d) false; referrals are only for emergencies

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain WHO's definition of mental health and what it is more than.
   *(Understand)*
2. Explain the demands-and-supports balance with the two Class 8 pupils. *(Apply)*
3. Why is "who is ill?" the wrong school question? Give the right one.
   *(Understand)*
4. Explain how one station of the school day can be both a demand and a support,
   with an example. *(Understand)*
5. Describe any four signs a teacher can notice. *(Understand)*
6. Explain the rule for reading signs, and why a single bad Monday is not one.
   *(Understand)*
7. Describe the teacher's role in three parts, and say where the third one ends.
   *(Understand)*
8. Explain any four classroom responses and what each supports. *(Understand)*
9. Describe any three triggers for the same-day referral. *(Understand)*
10. Explain why the boundary protects the pupil twice over. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The brilliant pupil.** The high marks and constant dread have been visible
   since September. Analyse her well-being balance, name the signs with their
   pattern, and describe the response within the teacher's role - what you would
   do this week, and what you would NOT do. *(Analyze)*

2. **The walk of the timetable.** Take one real school day and walk it station by
   station: each station's demand and its current support, including the honest
   "none". Choose the one station whose flip would protect the most pupils, and
   design the flip - specific, cheap, inside this term's power. *(Create)*

3. **The four-minute walk.** The tutor's pupil disclosed something she could not
   carry alone. Write what she did next as a plan: what she wrote down and why
   the words matter, who she walked it to, what she said to the pupil before she
   went, and what she did NOT do. *(Analyze)*

4. **The confidence.** A pupil says "please don't tell anyone" and then discloses
   something that must be escalated. Analyse the position: what the pupil is really
   asking for, what the teacher can and cannot promise, and how the teacher gets
   the walk made without betraying the relationship. *(Analyze)*

5. **The well-being audit.** Design a one-page well-being audit for your school:
   the stations, the demands, the supports, the pupils you would watch and how,
   and the referral route with its designated person by role. Justify each element
   from this unit, and name the audit's honest limit. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - a state of well-being enabling coping; the definition is a functioning,
   not a diagnosis.
2. **b** - the undiagnosed can be unwell; the diagnosed can function with
   supports.
3. **b** - a balance of demands and supports.
4. **b** - the staff room has the balance backwards.
5. **c** - enjoying break is a support, not a sign.
6. **b** - the pattern, not the day.
7. **b** - self-harm talk, and the sudden calm that can mean a decision made.
8. **c** - notice, respond, refer.
9. **c** - the routine and the greeting: protective, and inside the role.
10. **b** - the referral is the care; the relationship continues.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. A state of mental well-being enabling people to cope with the stresses of life,
   realise abilities, learn, work, contribute (1); more than the absence of
   illness (1).
2. Her pan: exam demands, comparison, midnight revision, no visible supports; his:
   work, play, sleep, steady balance (1); the staff room reads the marks as
   well-being and misses hers (1).
3. The wrong question looks for illness; the right one asks who is coping and who
   is not (2).
4. Any station with both sides named: assembly (public comparison / predictable
   start); break (social test / the peer group that includes); last period (spent
   attention / one-demand rule) (2).
5. Any four of: withdrawal; unexplained fall in marks; out-of-scale irritability;
   clustered absence; changes in self-care; talk of hopelessness (2).
6. Read the pattern, not the day - the change that persists (1); a single bad
   Monday is within ordinary life, and treating it as a sign cheapens the noticing
   (1).
7. Notice (the private map), respond within the classroom, refer beyond it (1); the
   third ends at the handover - the responsibility for treatment, never the
   relationship (1).
8. Any four of: greet by name (belonging); keep the routine (predictability);
   reduce the demand you control (load); name feelings calmly (the workbench
   returned); keep included by role (inclusion) (2).
9. Any three of: talk of self-harm; sudden calm in distress; a pattern deepening
   despite supports; anything the teacher cannot keep safe (2).
10. It protects against a teacher counselling beyond training (harm no kindness
    repairs), and against a confidence that leaves the pupil carrying it alone
    (2).
