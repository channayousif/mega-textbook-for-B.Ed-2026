# Mental Health and Well-being in Schools

The course has treated the pupil's mind as a learning machine. This one-week unit
treats it as a person's life. Mental health is not a topic beside the others; it is
the condition the others run on - a frightened pupil cannot risk (Unit 8's
environment), an anxious one has no workbench (Unit 4), and a pupil whose
attributions have closed (Unit 6's protect loop) sometimes needs more than a
teaching move.

The unit holds two things at once: the school's genuine power to protect
well-being, and the honest limits of a teacher's role. Both halves matter. The
school that thinks it is powerless abandons pupils it could help; the teacher who
thinks they are a therapist harms pupils they meant to help.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Define **mental health and well-being** in WHO's framing, as more than the
  absence of illness.
- Name the **common difficulties** and their signs in school, and distinguish the
  teacher's response from the referral.
- Read the **school day as demands and supports**, and name the school-level
  supports that protect well-being.
- Describe the **teacher's role and its limits**: notice, respond within the
  classroom, refer beyond it - never diagnose or counsel beyond training.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-10 (the whole course; especially Unit 2's emotional domain, Unit 6's
emotions and anxiety, and Unit 8's positive learning environment). This unit reads
the whole classroom through the well-being lens.

## How to use this unit

- **Handle the material with the seriousness it asks for.** This unit names
  hopelessness and despair; treat the naming as professional knowledge, not gossip.
- **Keep one pupil in mind** - anonymised - whose school year worried you. The unit
  assessment asks for exactly that pupil, read through this unit.
- **Learn the referral line by heart.** The boundary between the teacher's response
  and the referral is the unit's most consequential sentence.
- After the two topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank
  with answers and marking guidance.
