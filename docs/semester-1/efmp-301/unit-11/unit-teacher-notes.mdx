# Mental Health and Well-being in Schools - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The guide places Chapter 11 in **Week 16**, shared with
Chapter 12 and the course review - the combined closing week. Two topics, one
session each, with Chapter 12 taking the third session.

## SENSITIVITY RULES (read before teaching)

This unit names hopelessness, self-harm and disclosure. Before teaching it:

1. **Announce the content at the start of the session.** Trainees may have lived
   experience; the announcement costs nothing and gives them the choice of seat,
   of silence, of a word with you afterwards.
2. **No personal disclosure required.** The activities use anonymised pupils from
   practicum or memory - never "tell us about yourself". If a trainee discloses
   in class, follow the unit's own referral discipline: listen, do not promise
   secrecy, escalate to the institution's designated person.
3. **Teach the signs as professional knowledge, not as gossip or drama.** The
   naming is precise because precision is protective; vagueness and horror-stories
   are both failures of the same discipline.
4. **Know your institution's referral route before the session.** The unit's
   referral line should land on a real named route in the trainees' own college -
   teach it with the real name.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration
and case study analysis. This unit runs on two paired cases and one walk.

**Open with the two Class 8 pupils.** The brilliant pupil with dread and the
steady pupil with middling marks are the unit in miniature: every definition, sign
and move lands on them. Ask which pupil the staff room worries about, and why the
answer is wrong.

**Run the tutor's four minutes as a rehearsal.** The walk to the head teacher
with the sentence written down is the unit's professional centre. Rehearse it
literally: what the paper says, what the pupil is told, what the head teacher
hears. The rehearsal is the demonstration the guide asks for.

**Set the walk of the timetable before the second session** (the practical work,
below), so the station audit runs on the trainees' own data.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Mental health means mental illness."** The WHO definition, read slowly, does
the work: a state of functioning, not a diagnosis. The two-pupils case grounds
it.

**"It is not a school's business."** The course's own history is the reply: the
frightened pupil who cannot risk (Unit 8), the anxious one with no workbench
(Unit 4) - well-being has been the condition every unit assumed. What is not the
school's business is diagnosis and therapy, and that boundary is the unit's
content, not its exclusion.

**"Referring is giving up."** Meet it with the twice-over protection and the
continuation: the greeting, the routine and the quiet question matter MORE after a
referral than before it.

**"I can handle it myself."** Usually said warmly. Ask what happens to the pupil
if the teacher is wrong, or absent, or simply not a therapist - the boundary
protects the pupil from the teacher's best intentions.

## Language and access

The unit's key terms are banked where banked (ذہنی صحت، فلاح و بہبود، اضطراب،
دباؤ). The demands-and-supports vocabulary and the three-part role (notice,
respond, refer) belong on the board in both languages for both sessions. The
referral line should be learnable verbatim.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is a group assignment: **each
group walks one school's day, station by station, and produces the one-page
well-being audit: stations, demands, supports, the watch-list method, and the
referral route with its designated person by role.**

Set the walk before the second session; run the reporting in it. The audits are
pooled into a single template the cohort takes away - the artefact behind ERQ 5,
and the most portable page this course produces.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the two topics' `Check your understanding`
blocks (ten items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The
`unit-assessment` page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in
its final bounded section.

ERQ 5 is the capstone and integrates both topics; schedule it after the practical
work so the audit has a real school behind it. ERQ 4 (the confidence) rewards the
boundary analysis the second session rehearses, and works well as the week's
written homework - with the sensitivity rules in mind when returning it.
