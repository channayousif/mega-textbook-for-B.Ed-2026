# Motivation and Emotion - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Motivation is the wants or needs that direct behaviour toward a goal. Intrinsic
motivation runs on personal satisfaction, extrinsic on what others give, and paying
for what a pupil already owns can eat it - the overjustification effect. Maslow's
hierarchy orders the deficit needs before growth: a triage list for what might be in
the way. Herzberg separates motivators from hygiene: removing dissatisfaction and
creating satisfaction are two different jobs. Self-determination theory names
autonomy, competence and relatedness as the three needs that feed intrinsic
motivation.

Achievement motivation sorts pupils by goals - mastery, performance, and the
destructive failure-avoidance. Between outcome and attempt sits the attribution, on
locus, stability and controllability: changeable explanations feed the approach loop,
fixed ones feed the protect loop, and structured experience moves them, not argument.
Emotions run through all of it: anxiety narrows the workbench, interest widens and
holds, curiosity opens gaps - each with a teacher move that costs attention, not
money.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Motivation is best defined as: *(Remember)*
   a) the energy a pupil has on a given day
   b) the wants or needs that direct behaviour toward a goal
   c) the willingness to obey
   d) the marks a pupil aims at

2. The overjustification effect is that: *(Understand)*
   a) rewards always increase effort
   b) intrinsic motivation is diminished when extrinsic motivation is given
   c) pupils justify their failures
   d) extrinsic rewards stop working after two weeks

3. Maslow's hierarchy, in order from the base, is: *(Remember)*
   a) safety, physiological, esteem, belonging, self-actualization
   b) physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualization
   c) belonging, physiological, safety, self-actualization, esteem
   d) esteem, belonging, safety, physiological, self-actualization

4. In Herzberg's two-factor theory, hygiene factors: *(Understand)*
   a) motivate when present
   b) do not motivate when present, but their absence demotivates
   c) are the same as motivators
   d) apply only to schools

5. The Class 9 project ran past the bell. In SDT terms, it fed: *(Apply)*
   a) pay, conditions and policy
   b) autonomy, competence and relatedness
   c) esteem only
   d) physiological needs first

6. The pupil who leaves the hardest question unattempted is most likely holding: *(Apply)*
   a) a mastery goal
   b) a failure-avoidant goal
   c) a performance-approach goal
   d) no goal at all

7. "I am not a maths person" is an attribution that is: *(Apply)*
   a) internal, stable, uncontrollable
   b) external, unstable, controllable
   c) internal, unstable, controllable
   d) external, stable, uncontrollable

8. The attribution loop's stages, in order, are: *(Remember)*
   a) attempt, outcome, explanation, expectation
   b) outcome, explanation, expectation, attempt
   c) explanation, expectation, attempt, outcome
   d) expectation, outcome, explanation, attempt

9. A little anxiety sharpens focus; a lot: *(Understand)*
   a) improves memory
   b) narrows the workbench until only the fear fits
   c) widens interest
   d) has no effect on learning

10. The teacher move for curiosity is: *(Understand)*
    a) answer every question immediately
    b) let questions live, and let gaps stay visible until findable
    c) forbid off-topic questions
    d) reward every question with a sticker

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain intrinsic versus extrinsic motivation with one example of each, in two
   sentences. *(Understand)*
2. Explain the overjustification effect, using the prize chart, in three sentences.
   *(Apply)*
3. Explain why Maslow's hierarchy is a triage list rather than a ladder with a lock,
   in two sentences. *(Understand)*
4. Distinguish motivators from hygiene factors with one school example of each.
   *(Understand)*
5. Describe SDT's three needs and one classroom translation of each. *(Understand)*
6. Distinguish mastery goals from failure-avoidant goals by what each does after
   failure. *(Understand)*
7. Place "I did not sleep last night" on the three attribution dimensions, and say
   which loop it feeds. *(Apply)*
8. Explain why arguing with Zubair about his attribution fails, and name the better
   lever. *(Analyze)*
9. Explain what anxiety does to the workbench and one move that returns some of it.
   *(Understand)*
10. Distinguish situational from personal interest, and name the bridge between them.
    *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The prize chart's two months.** The chart raised marks for two weeks, then
   stopped, and the story-writer stopped too. Analyse what the chart supplied,
   starved and re-told, and design what you would do in January instead, each element
   justified by a named theory. *(Analyze)*

2. **Three theories, one school.** A school has a broken water cooler, a Class 7 that
   will not try, and one teacher whose class always works. Read the school through
   Maslow, Herzberg and self-determination theory, and argue which reading should
   drive the first change, conceding what each theory's limit says it cannot do. *(Analyze)*

3. **Zubair's December.** Trace Zubair's path from the failed test to the abandoned
   section through the attribution loop, name where a teacher could have intervened
   in October, and design the structured experience - not the argument - that would
   have moved the explanation. *(Analyze)*

4. **The attribution profile.** For one week you have recorded what pupils say after
   wrong answers. Explain what the profile tells you that the marks do not, how you
   would use it to plan feedback, and what the profile cannot tell you. *(Analyze)*

5. **One plan, three needs.** Design one fortnight's plan for the class that will not
   try, in which autonomy, competence and relatedness are each fed by a named element,
   the emotions of the room are answered by a named move, and the first change you
   would make is defended against the alternatives. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - the definition puts the cause inside wants and needs, not character.
2. **b** - payment re-tells the pupil what the activity is for, and the free motive
   withdraws.
3. **b** - physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualization.
4. **b** - hygiene prevents dissatisfaction when present; it does not create
   satisfaction.
5. **b** - choice, doable work, teams: the three needs.
6. **b** - unattempted questions cannot be wrong: failure avoidance.
7. **a** - inside me, fixed, beyond my control.
8. **b** - outcome, explanation, expectation, attempt.
9. **b** - the workbench narrows until only the fear fits.
10. **b** - curiosity lives on visible, findable gaps.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. Intrinsic: for the satisfaction it brings; extrinsic: to receive something from
   others (1); any apt example of each (1).
2. The chart paid for what some pupils already did for free (1); the payment re-told
   them what the writing was for, and the stories, now work, stopped (2).
3. The order is a checklist of what might be in the way (1); the strict order does not
   always hold - people fast for belief and create in despair (1).
4. Motivators drive satisfaction (achievement, recognition, the work itself);
   hygiene's absence demotivates (pay, conditions, policy) (1 for the distinction);
   any apt school example of each (1).
5. Autonomy: choice inside a frame; competence: visible progress; relatedness:
   deliberate connection (1 for all three; 1 for any apt translation).
6. The mastery pupil reads failure as information and tries differently (1); the
   failure-avoidant pupil reads it as verdict and avoids the courtroom (1).
7. Internal (it is about him), unstable (a night's sleep changes), controllable
   (partly) (1); it feeds the approach loop - the cause can change (1).
8. Argument is another courtroom appearance; the lever is structured experience -
   tasks where effort visibly pays, feedback naming the controllable cause (2).
9. Anxiety narrows working memory until only the fear fits (1); familiarity with the
   format, known steps, or calm naming returns some of it (1).
10. Situational interest is brief and sparked by the situation; personal interest is
    durable and owned by the pupil (1); the bridge: catch with the situational, keep
    by returning to what caught and letting the pupil go deeper (1).
