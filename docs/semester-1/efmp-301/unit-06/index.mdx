# Motivation and Emotion

Every teacher has met the class that will not try. The staff room explains it with
laziness, background, or the weather. This unit replaces those explanations with
theories that can be checked: Maslow's hierarchy, Herzberg's two factors,
self-determination theory, the attribution loop, and the emotions that sit under all
of them.

The unit's spine is a single question: **why does a pupil bother?** Not "is the pupil
bothered?" - the theories here all assume pupils want things, and differ only about
what the wanting runs on.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Define **motivation** and distinguish its **intrinsic and extrinsic** forms,
  including the overjustification risk.
- Explain **Maslow's hierarchy of needs** and read a classroom through its levels.
- Explain **Herzberg's two-factor theory** and its careful application to schools.
- Explain **self-determination theory**: autonomy, competence and relatedness.
- Describe **achievement motivation** - approach and protect - and the **attribution
  loop** that follows each outcome.
- Explain the **role of emotions** in learning, and name the teacher moves that
  answer each.

## How to use this unit

- **Work through the topics in order.** The theories come first; the attribution loop
  and the emotions assume them.
- **Keep one class in mind.** The unit assessment asks you to analyse one described
  unmotivated class through the theories into an action plan; the notes you keep on a
  real class are the raw material.
- **Watch the overjustification effect in yourself.** The unit's most uncomfortable
  idea is that rewards can eat the motivation they replace. Test it against your own
  paid and unpaid work.
- After the two topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank with
  answers and marking guidance.
