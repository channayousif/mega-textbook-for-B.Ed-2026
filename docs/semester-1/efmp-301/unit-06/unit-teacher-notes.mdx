# Motivation and Emotion - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Week 11** to Chapter 6: theories
of motivation (Maslow, Herzberg, Self-Determination Theory), achievement motivation,
and the role of emotions in learning. Two topics, one session each.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This unit runs on one running case and one week of listening.

**Run the prize chart as the spine.** The chart that worked for two weeks and then
stopped is the unit's most honest case: every theory in it has something to say, no
theory fully explains it, and the trainees have all seen one. Return to it in every
session; by the end, the room should be able to read it three ways.

**Collect real attributions before teaching the loop.** The practical work (below)
asks trainees to record what pupils say after failure. Set the recording task BEFORE
the second session, so the loop is taught into their own data rather than examples
from a book.

**Watch the overjustification land.** It is the unit's most resisted finding, because
most trainees were raised on prize charts. Do not soften it: state the effect, state
the scalpel rule (reward where no intrinsic motive exists yet), and let the room
argue.

## Sequencing across the week

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| First session | Topic 6.1 - the two forms, overjustification, Maslow, Herzberg, SDT | the three-theories-one-school activity |
| Second session | Topic 6.2 - goals, the attribution loop, emotions | the change-the-attribution activity, using the week's collected sentences |

If a session is lost, cut Herzberg's detail before the attribution loop. The loop is
the unit's transferable core; Herzberg is a diagnostic distinction.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Punishment motivates."** Back it into Unit 3's operant material: punishment
suppresses, and the price is avoidance. The pupil working to avoid a scolding is
running on negative reinforcement, and the debt compounds.

**"Rewards are always good."** The overjustification effect is the answer, but let
the room find the scalpel rule themselves: where is a reward safe, and where is it
buying what the pupil already owns?

**"Maslow's ladder is a lock."** The strict order fails in real people (fasting for
belief). Present the hierarchy as triage - what might be in the way - and the
misreading dissolves.

**"He has a bad attitude."** The unit's signature reframe: attitude is an attribution
that has not been listened to. The recorded sentences from the practical work do more
to convert this than any argument.

## Language and access

The unit's key terms are banked where banked (محرک، باطنی محرک، خارجی محرک، اضطراب،
دباؤ). The attribution dimensions (locus, stability, controllability) are worth
keeping on the board in both languages for the whole week; the loop diagram is the
unit's one visual the trainees must be able to redraw from memory.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is an individual assignment: **each
trainee records, verbatim, four or five sentences pupils say after wrong answers or
failed tests during one week of their practicum, and brings the list to the second
session.**

In pairs, the lists are placed on the three attribution dimensions and sorted into
approach-loop and protect-loop explanations. Each pair nominates the sentence they
found hardest to move, and the room designs the structured experience - not the
argument - that would move it over a term. Twenty minutes; the lists are the
session's data.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the two topics' `Check your understanding` blocks
(ten items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The `unit-assessment`
page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded
section.

ERQ 5 is the capstone and integrates both topics; schedule it after the practical
work so the plan has real data behind it. ERQ 3 (Zubair) rewards the loop-tracing
skill the second session trains; it works well as the week's written homework.
