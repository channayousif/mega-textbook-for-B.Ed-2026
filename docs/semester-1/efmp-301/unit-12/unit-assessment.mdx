# Guidance and Counseling - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Guidance is information and direction; counseling is a skilled relationship aimed
at understanding; teaching is a third discipline aimed at learning. The teacher's
part is the first line: the noticing, the listening that helps, and the referral
that carries, with the words written down. Confidentiality is the method and has
exactly safety's limit.

Professional ethics is the set of duties that make the role trustworthy: fairness,
confidentiality with safety's limit, boundaries, honest reporting, and pupil
welfare first - the duty that orders the rest, standing on Pakistan's
professional-standards context. The decision path - whose interest, which duty,
what the standards say, what I will do and record - beats a slogan because it
names what pressure arrives dressed as.

Applying educational psychology means reading one real thing with all of it: the
six clusters of the course held together. Technology's affordances are real -
practice, access, creation, connection - and its costs are equally real: the load
moves, comparison travels, the device is not a model, equity depends on design.
Technology amplifies the teaching; the augmentation is chosen by a teacher who
knows what each tool is for.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Guidance differs from counseling in that guidance: *(Remember)*
   a) is for younger pupils
   b) is information and direction, while counseling is a skilled relationship
   c) needs no training
   d) is what the class teacher does

2. A Class 7 pupil's marks have fallen since his father lost his job. The teacher
   explains the school's fee concession, where to apply, and whom to ask. In this
   unit's terms, what she gave him is: *(Apply)*
   a) guidance: information and direction at a moment of choice
   b) counseling, because the cause is painful
   c) a referral to the counselor
   d) teaching, because it happened in the classroom

3. Counseling's method is: *(Understand)*
   a) giving excellent advice
   b) the relationship: being heard by someone steady
   c) solving the pupil's problem quickly
   d) writing a report

4. "Applying educational psychology", as this unit closes the course, means: *(Understand)*
   a) revising every unit's definitions before the examination
   b) memorising the six clusters of the course
   c) leaving the theory behind now that practice has begun
   d) reading one real classroom thing with all of the course at once

5. The teacher's first-line response to a disclosure is: *(Apply)*
   a) counsel the pupil yourself
   b) promise secrecy, then decide
   c) listen, do not promise what you cannot keep, refer with the words written
   d) send the pupil to the counselor without a word

6. Which is NOT one of the five duties of this unit? *(Remember)*
   a) high examination results
   b) fairness
   c) confidentiality
   d) honest reporting

7. "Pupil welfare first" means: *(Understand)*
   a) the pupil is always right
   b) when duties conflict, the pupil's welfare outranks reputation, results and
      convenience
   c) the school's reputation is the pupil's welfare
   d) welfare outranks safety

8. The first box of the ethics decision path asks: *(Remember)*
   a) what will I record?
   b) which duty is broken?
   c) what do the standards say?
   d) whose interest is served by each option?

9. A quiz app marks a shy pupil's answers instantly, and she attempts the hard
   questions privately all evening, with no one watching her fail. In the course's
   terms, the app's power here is: *(Apply)*
   a) access to the unseen
   b) creation and real audiences
   c) practice with instant feedback, without public failure
   d) connection and research

10. "Technology will replace the teacher" fails against the course because the
    device cannot: *(Understand)*
    a) display content
    b) mark quizzes
    c) read the room, hold the ladder's dignity, or notice the third Monday
    d) show videos

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Distinguish guidance from counseling in two sentences, with the Larkana
   example. *(Understand)*
2. The examiner's request, the school's average and the easy number all pull the
   same way. Analyse what each is dressed as, what each breaks, and why the
   decision path's first box answers all three. *(Analyze)*
3. Explain what "the relationship is the method" means, and what advice has to do
   with it. *(Understand)*
4. Describe the four minimum parts of a school's guidance programme. *(Understand)*
5. Name any three costs of technology this unit names, each with what a teacher
   watches for. *(Understand)*
6. Describe the five ethical duties, with a dilemma for any two of them. *(Understand)*
7. Explain why "ethics means not breaking rules" is a misconception, using the
   marks case. *(Understand)*
8. Run the marks case through the decision path in four lines. *(Apply)*
9. Name any three affordances of technology, each with the unit it comes from.
   *(Understand)*
10. Explain why the device is not a model, on Unit 3's finding. *(Apply)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The corridor version.** Your school has no counselor: guidance happens in
   corridors, untrained and unrecorded. Design the minimum programme you could
   build this term with the staff you have - the named person, the route, the
   information point, the record - each justified from this unit, and the
   first-built part defended. *(Create)*

2. **The record that defends.** The examiner's request has been refused; the mark
   stands. Write the record: what was asked, by whom, refused why, on which duty
   and which standard - and what the record changes if the pressure returns. Name
   honestly what it does not protect the teacher from. *(Analyze)*

3. **The teacher-augmented lesson.** Design one lesson in which the technology
   does what it is good at and the teacher does what only a teacher can, each half
   named with its unit. The quiet girl must be served by both halves. Close with
   the honest limit. *(Create)*

4. **The episode through the course.** Take one real classroom episode with a
   difficulty in it. Read it through the course's six branches - one sentence per
   branch - then argue which lens should drive the first response, and what would
   make you change. *(Analyze)*

5. **The course capstone.** One full classroom case - a described class with a
   described difficulty - analysed through the whole course and answered with a
   justified plan: what you would do first, on which evidence, with which supports
   beside it, and what you would watch to know whether it is working. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - information and direction versus a skilled relationship.
2. **a** - guidance: information and direction, delivered at the moment of choice.
3. **b** - the relationship is the method; advice is what it sometimes ends with.
4. **d** - reading one real thing with all of it: the course held together, not
   revised apart.
5. **c** - listen, promise only what you can keep, refer with the words written.
6. **a** - results are an outcome, not a duty.
7. **b** - the ordering duty: welfare outranks the rest when they conflict.
8. **d** - whose interest is served: naming what pressure arrives dressed as.
9. **c** - practice with instant feedback, without the public risk: Unit 3's
   reinforcement, privately delivered.
10. **c** - the load-bearing work is made of what the device cannot do.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. Guidance: information and direction (the careers talk); counseling: a skilled
   relationship aimed at understanding (the counselor's listening) (2).
2. The request is dressed as reputation, the average as convenience, the easy
   number as kindness (1); each breaks fairness and honest reporting at once,
   because what is written stops being what happened - and the first box, whose
   interest does each option serve?, names interests that are never the pupil's,
   which settles all three (1).
3. What helps is being heard by someone steady; advice is sometimes the ending,
   only after the understanding it stands on has been built (2).
4. A named person pupils can reach; a referral route every teacher knows;
   information before choices close; records that follow without labelling (2).
5. Any three of: the load moves (watch the English instructions consuming the
   same workbench on screen); the comparison culture travels (watch the public
   leaderboard); the device is not a model (watch what pupils learn about trying
   from a screen with no face); equity is not automatic (watch the four pupils
   around one device) (2).
6. Fairness, confidentiality, boundaries, honest reporting, pupil welfare first
   (1 for all five); any two apt dilemmas (1).
7. The rules are the floor, not the work: the examiner's request puts rule and
   pressure on the same table, and the choice - not the rulebook - is the ethics
   (2).
8. Whose interest: the patron's and school's, not the pupil's; which duty:
   fairness and honest reporting; standards: professional accountability; do and
   record: hold the mark, write the reason (2).
9. Any three of: practice with instant feedback (Unit 3's reinforcement, Unit 6's
   loop); access to the unseen (Unit 10's demonstration); creation and audiences
   (Unit 5's elaboration); connection and research (Unit 4's analogy) (2).
10. Pupils learn what trying looks like by watching what the teacher does with a
    wrong answer - the screen has no face, so the modelling cannot happen (2).
