# Topic 12.3 - Educational psychology in practice: application and technology

## A real classroom situation

A tablet project arrives in a rural Sindh school: one device for every four pupils,
loaded with lessons, videos and quizzes. For a term, the school runs on it. Some
things change: the videos show processes the blackboard never could; the quiz app
marks instantly; the attendance of the computer periods is perfect.

Other things do not change. The pupil who could not read the English instructions
still cannot read them off a screen. The pupil who protected his reputation by not
trying now protects it by "helping" others navigate. And the moment a teacher sits
beside the quiet girl and asks her one question about what she is watching - that
moment, the project's own evaluation later finds, is what moved her learning.

## Explanation

### Application of educational psychology: the course whole

The guide's "Application of educational psychology" bullet is this section, and the
honest way to apply a course is to read one real thing with all of it. Take the
tablet project's quiet girl.

**Units 1-2** ask where she is: the three-part scope says the question is not "is
she weak" but which of the learner, the process or the situation is at work; the
developmental reading says compare her work to stage descriptions, never to the
class average. **Unit 3** asks what learning is happening: the videos worked
because they carried the enactive-to-iconic route the blackboard skipped, and the
quiz app's instant marking is operant conditioning with the delay removed.
**Unit 4** explains why the English instructions failed her - the reading load
consumed the workbench - and why the teacher's one question worked: it aimed the
beam and retrieved. **Units 5-8** read the room: her profile, her motivation, the
environment the devices changed and the one they could not. **Units 9-10** would
design the evidence: what would show the project worked, gathered before the term
ended. **Units 11-12** ask what the quiet was made of, and where the teacher's
role ends.

That is the application. Educational psychology is not a package of techniques; it
is a set of questions that hold together - and a teacher who holds them together
sees more of the room than any single lens allows.

### Technology and learning: what it changes, and what it cannot

The guide's practical-work list names hands-on computer exercises and
internet-research tasks, and the tablet project is their classroom-scale version.
Technology's genuine affordances, seen through the course:

**Practice with instant feedback** - the quiz app is Unit 3's reinforcement with
the delay removed, and Unit 6's attribution loop receiving its outcome at once.
**Access to the unseen** - the video shows the process (Unit 10's demonstration)
that sixty pupils could not all see at one kettle. **Creation and real audiences**
- the typed, printed, displayed product is Unit 5's elaboration with a reason.
**Connection and research** - the internet-research task is Unit 3's analogy
strategy at the speed of a search box.

And its costs, equally seen. **The load moves, not disappears**: English
instructions on a screen consume the same workbench as English instructions on
paper. **The comparison culture travels**: the quiz app that ranks publicly is
Unit 6's protect loop with a leaderboard. **The device is not a model**: Unit 3's
strongest finding - pupils learn what trying looks like by watching what the
teacher does with a wrong answer - has no tablet equivalent, because the screen
has no face. **Equity is not automatic**: one device for four pupils is a
cooperation design or a hierarchy, depending entirely on the teacher.

The conclusion the project's own evaluation reached is the course's conclusion:
**technology amplifies the teaching; it does not replace it.** The videos, the
marking, the perfect attendance - all of it moved the quiet girl less than one
teacher, one question, one moment of aimed attention. The teacher who knows what
the device is doing (reinforcement, demonstration, load, audience) uses it
deliberately; the teacher who does not is used by it.

> **The misconception to clear now.** "Technology will replace the teacher." Run
> the replacement claim against the course: can the device read the room's
> attribution profile (Unit 6)? Hold the ladder's dignity (Unit 8)? Notice the
> third Monday running (Unit 11)? Decide whose interest the easy number serves
> (Unit 12)? The device can deliver, mark and display - the load-bearing work of
> the profession is made of exactly what it cannot do. The future this course
> prepares you for is not teacher-less; it is teacher-augmented, and the
> augmentation is chosen by a teacher who knows what each tool is for.

## Activity: The episode through the course

**In pairs, about 25 minutes.**

Choose one real classroom episode you have witnessed or taught - one with a
difficulty in it, not a showcase.

1. Read it through the course's six branches (the figure): one sentence per
   branch, each naming what that cluster sees.
2. Mark which lens you reached for FIRST, and say why - the preference is data
   about you, not a fault in you.
3. Then the course's question: what would you do first, on which lens's evidence,
   and what would make you change?

## Check your understanding

1. Name the course's six branch-clusters with a headline idea for each.

4. Name any two costs of technology, seen through the course. *(Understand)*
5. Why does the device fail as a model, on Unit 3's finding? *(Apply)*

## Summary

Applying educational psychology means reading one real thing with all of it: the
learner and development, the theories, the mind's work, the room, the evidence,
and the profession - six clusters of questions that hold together. Technology's
affordances are real - practice with instant feedback, access to the unseen,
creation and audiences, connection and research - and its costs are equally real:
the load moves rather than disappears, comparison travels, the device is not a
model, and equity depends on the design. The tablet project's own evaluation
reached the course's conclusion: technology amplifies the teaching and does not
replace it, and the augmentation is chosen by a teacher who knows what each tool
is for.

## Try this at your practicum school

Take one technology the school already has - a phone, a projector, a lab of old
computers - and list, honestly, what it currently amplifies and what it could
amplify with one deliberate teaching decision. The list is the guide's hands-on
exercise, done at classroom scale.

## Summative task

Write **250-300 words** on the teacher-augmented lesson.

Design one lesson in which the technology does what it is good at (name the
affordance and the unit behind it) and the teacher does what only a teacher can
(name the work and the unit behind it). The quiet girl must be served by both
halves. Close with the honest limit: what this lesson still cannot do, and who it
still will not reach.

**Mini-rubric**

| Criterion | What a strong answer shows |
|---|---|

| The quiet girl is served by both halves | Her reading load and her silence are both answered. *(Create)* |

| Plain, accurate writing | Terms used correctly; no padding; within the word count. |
