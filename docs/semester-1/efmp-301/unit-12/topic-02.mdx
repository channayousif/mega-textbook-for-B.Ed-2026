# Topic 12.2 - Professional ethics

## A real classroom situation

An examiner pressures a teacher to raise a pupil's marks - the pupil is the child
of a school patron, and the school's reputation, the examiner says, is at stake.
The teacher has a career, a family, and a class that watched her mark the scripts
fairly. Whatever she does next will be watched too.

The rules will not decide this one, because the rules are what the examiner is
asking her to break. What decides it is the teacher's account of what a teacher
is - and the quality of that account is what this topic builds.

## Explanation

### The teacher's ethical duties

Professional ethics is not a list of prohibitions; it is the set of duties that
make the role trustworthy, and each duty earns its place by protecting someone the
pupil cannot protect alone. Five carry a Sindh classroom's weight.

**Fairness**: every pupil the same standard, the same attention, the same chance.
Its dilemma is always personal - the patron's child, the colleague's nephew, the
pupil you cannot help liking. The line: marks and opportunities reflect the work,
never the relationship.

**Confidentiality**: what a pupil tells you stays - with exactly one limit, which
Unit 11 drew and this topic repeats because it is the most tested line in the
school: safety outranks secrecy. Never promise confidentiality absolutely; promise
instead what you can keep - "I will only share it with the person whose job it is
to help."

**Boundaries**: the relationship serves the pupil, and it is a professional
relationship. Its dilemma is warmth: the pupil who wants to keep talking, the
teacher who feels needed. The line is the referral of Topic 12.1 - the teacher who
becomes a pupil's daily confidant has built a friendship the pupil did not ask for
and a clinic the teacher cannot staff.

**Honest reporting**: records, reports and marks tell the truth. Its dilemma is
pressure - the examiner's request, the school's average, the number that would
make everyone's term easier. The line: what is written is what happened.

**Pupil welfare first**: the duty that orders all others. When duties conflict -
welfare against reputation, against results, against convenience - the pupil's
welfare outranks them. This is the duty the marks case turns on, and it is the
duty that makes the refusal defensible: the teacher who holds the fair mark is not
being stubborn; she is being what the word professional means.

Pakistan's own context gives these duties a standing beyond the school gate: the
National Professional Standards for Teachers, which frame teacher professionalism
as a matter of public accountability, not private virtue (Khizar, Anwar & Malik,
2019). The teacher's ethics are the state's promise to its pupils, kept one
classroom at a time.

### The ethics decision path

When a dilemma is real - two duties pulling, both costly - a path beats a slogan:

1. **Whose interest does each option serve?** Name it plainly: the pupil's, the
   school's, the examiner's, yours. The naming is half the work, because pressure
   always arrives dressed as someone else's interest.
2. **Which duty does each option honour or break?** Name the duty from the table -
   fairness, confidentiality, boundaries, reporting, welfare.
3. **What do the standards say?** The professional standards, the school's policy,
   the law - named, not assumed. The teacher who can cite the standard is harder
   to pressure than the teacher who can only object.
4. **What will I do, and what will I record?** The decision and its reason,
   written down - the same discipline as Unit 11's referral note, and for the same
   reason: what is written is what happened, and what is written can be defended.

The marks case runs the path in ninety seconds. Whose interest does the raise
serve? The patron's and the school's - not the pupil's, and not the class's. Which
duty does it break? Fairness and honest reporting at once. What do the standards
say? Professional accountability, public trust. What will I do, and record? Hold
the mark, write the reason, and let the record carry the argument.

> **The misconception to clear now.** "Ethics means not breaking rules." The rules
> are the floor, not the work. The examiner's request puts the rule and the
> pressure on the same table and asks the teacher to choose - and the choice is
> the ethics. A teacher who has never faced a duty pulling against another duty
> has not yet met the part of the profession that cannot be delegated, and the
> teacher who faces one without an account of the duties will decide it by
> convenience, whatever the rules say.

## Activity: The dilemma round

**In groups of four, about 20 minutes.**

Each member brings one real ethical dilemma from practicum or schooling - the kind
that had no comfortable answer. One is chosen. The group runs the decision path on
it aloud, box by box, with one member recording each answer. Then the group states
its decision and its cost - every real dilemma has one.

Rotate to a second dilemma if time allows. The point is the path, not the verdicts.

## Check your understanding

1. Name the five duties, with the dilemma each can raise. *(Remember)*
2. Explain why ethics is dilemmas rather than rules, using the marks case.
   *(Understand)*
3. Which duty orders the others, and what does it outrank? *(Remember)*
4. Name the four steps of the decision path in order. *(Remember)*
5. Run the marks case through the path in four lines. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Professional ethics is the set of duties that make the role trustworthy: fairness,
confidentiality with safety's limit, boundaries, honest reporting, and pupil
welfare first - the duty that orders the rest. Pakistan's professional standards
give the duties a public, accountable standing. Real dilemmas put two duties on the
same table, and the decision path - whose interest, which duty, what the standards
say, what I will do and record - beats a slogan because it names what pressure
arrives dressed as. What is written is what happened; the record carries the
argument the teacher should not have to make alone.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask two teachers for a moment when a duty pulled against another duty - no names
needed, the shape of the thing only. Run each privately through the decision path.
You are practising the path, not judging the teachers.

## Summative task

Write **250-300 words** on the record that defends.

The examiner's request has been refused; the mark stands. Write the record: the
note the teacher writes that evening - what was asked, by whom, what was refused
and why, on which duty and which standard - and what that record changes about the
conversation if the pressure returns. Close with what the record does NOT protect
the teacher from, honestly named.

**Mini-rubric**

| Criterion | What a strong answer shows |
|---|---|
| The record is concrete | What was asked, by whom, refused why - in the note's own words. |
| The duties and standards are named | Not "it felt wrong" but "it broke fairness and honest reporting". *(Analyze)* |
| The return conversation is anticipated | What changes the second time the pressure comes. |
| Plain, accurate writing | Terms used correctly; no padding; within the word count. |
