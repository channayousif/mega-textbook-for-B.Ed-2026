# Guidance and Counseling - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The guide places Chapter 12 in **Week 16**, shared with
Chapter 11 and the course review - the combined closing week. Unit 11 takes the
first two sessions; this unit takes the third, and the guide's "Final revision /
assessment" slot follows it. Three topics in one session is not possible - run
Topic 12.1 and 12.2 in the third session, and fold Topic 12.3 into the revision
slot, where it belongs anyway.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration
and case study analysis. This unit's cases are the course's own.

**Open with the Larkana pupil.** Three adults, three well-meant responses, only
one that began where the pupil was - the case does the three-way distinction
before any definition arrives. Ask which response each trainee would have given in
October; the honest answers are the topic's work.

**Run the dilemma round as the ethics session's core.** Real dilemmas, no
comfortable answers, the decision path run aloud - the path is the deliverable,
not the verdicts. Protect the time for it; the table can be read, the path cannot.

**Close the course on agency.** Topic 12.3's tablet project and the quiet girl
return the whole course to one question - what would you have done, and why - and
the answer the evaluation found (the teacher's one aimed question) is the course's
thesis in miniature. End there. Do not end on a summary slide.

## Sequencing across the week

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| Third session (Ch 12) | Topic 12.1 - guidance, counseling, the first line; Topic 12.2 - the duties and the path | the dilemma round |
| Revision slot | Topic 12.3 - the course whole, technology | the episode-through-the-course activity |

If time is lost, cut Topic 12.1's programme detail before Topic 12.2's decision
path. The path is the unit's most portable instrument; the programme table can be
re-derived from the distinction.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Counseling means giving advice."** The careers talk is the standing
counter-example: advice without the relationship. Ask what the counselor actually
did in the Larkana case - asked, and listened without interrupting.

**"Ethics means not breaking rules."** The marks case puts rule and pressure on
the same table. The choice, not the rulebook, is the ethics - and a teacher
without an account of the duties decides by convenience.

**"Technology will replace the teacher."** Run the replacement claim against the
course's own units: the attribution profile, the ladder's dignity, the third
Monday. The device can deliver, mark and display; the load-bearing work is made
of what it cannot do.

## Language and access

The unit's key terms are banked where banked (رہنمائی و مشاورت، پیشہ ورانہ
اخلاقیات، ضابطہ اخلاق، اخلاقی مخمصہ). The three-way distinction (teaching,
guidance, counseling) and the four-step path belong on the board in both languages
for both sessions.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is a case study analysis and
presentations: **each group takes one real ethical dilemma, runs the decision path
on it aloud, and presents the decision with its cost - every real dilemma has
one.**

Run the presentations in the third session, after the path is taught. The
presented dilemmas (anonymised) are collected as the cohort's own ethics casebook
- the artefact behind ERQ 2, and the material the revision slot can return to.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the three topics' `Check your understanding`
blocks (fifteen items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The
`unit-assessment` page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in
its final bounded section.

ERQ 5 is the course capstone - the whole course in one case - and deserves the
revision slot's best hour. Schedule it after Topic 12.3 so the six-branch reading
is fresh. ERQ 4 (the episode through the course) is its rehearsal, and works well
as the week's written homework.
