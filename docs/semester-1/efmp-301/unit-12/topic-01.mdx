# Topic 12.1 - Guidance and counseling

## A real classroom situation

A Class 9 pupil in Larkana is failing mathematics and has stopped speaking in
class. Three adults respond. The class teacher tells him to work harder and sets
extra sums - teaching, offered kindly, aimed at the wrong thing. The head teacher
gives him a talk about careers and the importance of mathematics - guidance,
offered sincerely, aimed at his future and missing his present. The counselor, when
he finally reaches one, asks what has changed at home since October, and listens to
the answer without interrupting it - counseling, and the only response of the three
that began where the pupil actually was.

All three adults meant well. The differences between their responses are the
differences this topic exists to teach.

## Explanation

### What guidance and counseling are

**Guidance** is information and direction: helping a person see the options and
choose a path. A guidance programme answers questions like which stream to choose,
which subjects lead where, how to apply, what the examination expects. Its tool is
accurate information, delivered at the moment a choice is actually open; its
failure mode is the careers talk delivered to a pupil whose question was never
about careers.

**Counseling** is a skilled relationship: a trained listener helping a person work
through a difficulty that information alone cannot touch. Its tools are the
listening that does not interrupt, the questions that open rather than close, and
the confidentiality that makes the opening safe. <Glossary term="Guidance and Counseling" /> as a school
service is what the failing, silent pupil needed in October - not because his
mathematics was weak (the teacher's ground) and not because his future needed
mapping (the head teacher's ground), but because something had changed and he had
nowhere to put it.

**Teaching** is a third thing - aimed at learning, not at direction or
understanding-of-self - and the three roles are held by different hands precisely
because each is a discipline. The teacher who counsels a pupil through a family
crisis during the mathematics period has abandoned the mathematics and is
counselling without training at the same time (Unit 11's boundary). The
distinction is not hierarchy; it is division of the work.

Two features of counseling matter to every teacher, trained or not.
**Confidentiality** is what makes the relationship work - and it has exactly the
limit Unit 11 drew: it ends where safety begins, and the counselor, too, escalates.
**The relationship is the method**: what helps is being heard by someone steady,
not the advice the steady person could have written on a slip.

### The guidance programme and the teacher as first line

A school's guidance programme, at minimum: a named person pupils can reach; a
referral route every teacher knows by heart (Unit 11's line, now with a
destination); information about choices available before the choices close; and
records that follow the pupil without labelling them. A school without these four
still has guidance happening - it is just happening in corridors, untrained and
unrecorded.

The teacher's part is the **first line**: the noticing of Unit 11's map, the
listening that helps, and the referral that carries. The first-line listening has
rules, and they are learnable: listen more than you speak; do not promise secrecy
you cannot keep; do not solve what you cannot carry; and hand over with the words
written down. The strongest first-line move in the repertoire is often the
smallest: "I have noticed, I am here, and there is someone whose job this is -
come with me."

> **The misconception to clear now.** "Counseling means giving advice." If advice
> were the method, the head teacher's careers talk would have been counseling. The
> method is the relationship: the pupil who talks for twenty minutes and hears
> "what do you make of that?" twice has done more work than the pupil who receives
> ten excellent instructions. Advice is what a counselor sometimes ends with - and
> only after the understanding it stands on has been built.

## Activity: Three responses, one pupil

**In small groups, about 20 minutes.**

1. Re-read the Larkana pupil's three responses. For each, name the role, the aim,
   and what it missed.
2. Write the first-line response: the actual sentences the class teacher could
   have said in the mathematics period - no more than three - that would have
   opened the door without opening a counseling session.
3. Then write the referral sentence: what the teacher says when the door opens and
   what is behind it exceeds the role.

## Check your understanding

1. Distinguish guidance from counseling in two sentences, with the Larkana
   example. *(Understand)*
2. Why is teaching a third thing rather than a lesser version of the other two?
   *(Understand)*
3. Name the two features of counseling that matter to every teacher. *(Remember)*
4. Name the four minimum parts of a school's guidance programme. *(Remember)*
5. Write, in one sentence, the strongest first-line move and why it is the
   smallest. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Guidance is information and direction; counseling is a skilled relationship aimed
at understanding; teaching is a third discipline aimed at learning. The three are
held by different hands because each is a discipline, and the teacher's part is the
first line: the noticing, the listening that helps, and the referral that carries -
with the words written down. Confidentiality is counseling's method and has exactly
Unit 11's limit. A school's guidance programme needs a named person, a known route,
information before choices close, and records that follow without labelling; a
school without them has guidance happening anyway, untrained and unrecorded.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find out - by asking, not by guessing - who the designated person is that a
referral would go to, and what the route is. If the answer is "there is none",
that is the finding, and it belongs in the well-being audit of Unit 11.

## Summative task

Write **250-300 words** on the corridor version.

Your school has no counselor and no named person: guidance is happening in
corridors, untrained and unrecorded. Design the minimum programme you could build
this term with the staff you have: the named person, the route, the information
point, the record - each named concretely, each justified from this unit, and the
one part you would build first defended against the second.
