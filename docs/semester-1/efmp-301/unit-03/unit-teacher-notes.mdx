# Learning Theories - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Weeks 5, 6 and 7** to Chapter 3:
Part 1 (Week 5) on behaviourist theories (Pavlov, Skinner), Part 2 (Week 6) on cognitive
theories (Piaget, Bruner) and social learning theory (Bandura), Part 3 (Week 7) on
constructivist approaches with application activities and review.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This unit is where the case study method earns its keep.

**Run the cases as cases.** The corridor scene, the star chart, the two poems, Miss
Sadia's "good try" and Babar's ninety seconds are not illustrations to be mentioned;
they are the unit's evidence. Give each its four minutes of argument before any theory
is named, and the theory will arrive as an answer to a question the room already holds.

**Demonstrate the conditioning, do not describe it.** The two-glasses demonstration
belonged to Unit 2; this unit's equivalent is the consequence audit. Trainees who have
listed eight real consequences and named what each trains will never again say
"negative reinforcement" loosely.

**Let the lenses argue.** The four-lens activity is the unit's capstone and the guide's
own "Application activities & review" slot. Protect the disagreement. A room that
reaches consensus on Amjad in ninety seconds has not used the lenses; it has used the
first one named.

## Sequencing across the three weeks

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| Week 5, first session | Topic 3.1 - behaviourism, both conditionings | the consequence audit in full |
| Week 5, second session | Topic 3.1 - reinforcement and punishment precisely | the star-chart redesign |
| Week 6, first session | Topic 3.2 - the cognitive turn, Piaget and Bruner | the two-lessons activity |
| Week 6, second session | Topic 3.3 - Bandura, models, vicarious reinforcement | the map-the-models activity |
| Week 7 | Topic 3.4 - constructivism, ZPD, scaffolding, the four lenses | the four-lens activity; expect it to overrun |

If a session is lost, compress Topic 3.2's table work rather than Topic 3.4's lenses.
The unit's whole point is the choice, and a trainee who has met the families but never
chosen among them has memorised a menu.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Negative reinforcement is punishment."** The most durable error in the unit. The
quadrant figure is the cure: make them place the scolding-that-stops and the
scolding-for-calling-out in different quadrants, out loud, until the room flinches at
misuse.

**"Conditioning is for dogs."** Meet it with the corridor scene and the medicine smell
- both from their own schools. The honest concession (it explains only the automatic
part) opens the door to Topic 3.2 rather than closing the argument.

**"Piaget's stages mean you cannot teach it early."** This returns from Unit 2 wearing a
new coat. The reply is Bruner's: the stages describe readiness for the symbolic form,
and the enactive-to-iconic-to-symbolic route is how you teach anything at any age.

**"Constructivism means hands off."** The Babar case is the counter: ninety seconds of
deliberate, planned, removed help. Ask what Mr Qasim had to know to place those ninety
seconds; the answer (the zone, the missing step, the withdrawal plan) is the work the
theory demands.

**"One theory is right."** Usually behaviourism, among trainees trained on
reward-and-punish schooling. Do not argue; run the four lenses. The lens exercise
converts more trainees than any lecture, because it makes the choice theirs.

## Language and access

The unit's key terms are banked in the terminology list (طرزِ عمل پسندی، ادراکیت،
تعمیریت، تقویت). Keep the Urdu equivalents active in discussion, as in Units 1-2. The
consequence quadrant is the one place where English terms are worth insisting on in
discussion, because the misconception lives in the English words themselves.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is a case-study analysis: **each
trainee brings one real classroom episode - a behaviour that appeared or changed in a
class they know - and the group reads it through all four lenses.**

Run it in Week 7 after the four-lens activity. Each trainee writes their episode in
five lines on a slip; slips are shuffled and redistributed, so no one analyses their
own case. Groups of four produce the four readings, choose a first response, and
present the choice with its switch condition. Twenty-five minutes plus presentations.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the four topics' `Check your understanding` blocks
(twenty items across the unit). The `unit-assessment` page carries the graded bank with
worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded section.

ERQ 5 is the unit's capstone and integrates all four topics by design. It is the same
pupil as the Week 7 practical work, so trainees who engaged there arrive with their
evidence already gathered.
