# Learning Theories

Unit 2 asked where a pupil is. This unit asks a different question: what happened when
they learned? Every teacher acts on an answer, usually without noticing. The teacher who
gives a star for neat work is acting on one theory of learning. The teacher who lets
pupils test which objects sink is acting on another. The teacher whose class learns how
to treat a wrong answer by watching how she treats the first one is acting on a third.

This unit makes those theories visible, names them, and puts them to work. It is the
theory core of the course: the ideas here return in Unit 4 (cognitive processes), Unit 6
(motivation) and every unit after.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain the **behaviourist** view of learning as change in observable behaviour, and
  describe Pavlov's classical conditioning and Skinner's operant conditioning.
- Use **reinforcement and punishment** precisely, including why negative reinforcement is
  not punishment, and read a classroom consequence for what it makes more or less likely.
- Explain the **cognitive turn**: learning as change in thinking, through Piaget's
  schemas and Bruner's representation and spiral curriculum.
- Describe **Bandura's social learning theory**: learning by observation, the conditions
  that strengthen it, and what the observational-learning research showed.
- Explain **constructivism** - the learner builds understanding - including Vygotsky's
  zone of proximal development and scaffolding.
- **Choose and combine** theories in real teaching: read one classroom episode through
  all four lenses and justify a teaching decision.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-2 (the three-part scope; development, especially the cognitive domain that
Topic 3.2 deepens). No laboratory psychology is assumed; every theory is introduced
through a classroom.

## How to use this unit

- **Work through the topics in order.** Each theory family answers the one before it:
  behaviourism is answered by the cognitive turn, both are widened by social learning,
  and constructivism assembles the whole.
- **Keep one episode in mind.** In Topic 3.1 a pupil refuses work; by Topic 3.4 you will
  have read that same episode four ways. The unit assessment asks for exactly that.
- **Watch your own reactions.** The theories in this unit describe teachers as much as
  pupils. Notice which lens you reach for first - that preference is itself data.
- After the four topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank with
  answers and marking guidance.
