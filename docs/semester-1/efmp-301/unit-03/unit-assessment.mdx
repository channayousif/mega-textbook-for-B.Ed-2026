# Learning Theories - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Behaviourism defines learning as change in observable behaviour, through two mechanisms:
classical conditioning, learning by association, which explains what pupils cannot help;
and operant conditioning, learning from consequences, where reinforcement (positive or
negative) makes behaviour more frequent and punishment makes it less. The discipline is
reading consequences for what they actually train.

The cognitive turn defines learning as change in thinking. Piaget gives it structure -
schemas adjusted by assimilation and accommodation, with productive confusion as the
signature of real rebuilding. Bruner gives it a route - enactive before iconic before
symbolic, guided discovery, and the spiral that returns to each idea stronger.

Bandura shows that much learning arrives by observation: attention, retention,
reproduction and motivation, with reinforcement often vicarious. Models are chosen by
importance, similarity and warmth, and the model's outcome, not the model's words, is
what observers read.

Constructivism holds that understanding is built, not delivered - psychologically by the
individual, socially with assistance. Vygotsky's zone of proximal development locates
the teachable gap; Bruner's scaffolding names the support that must come down. The four
families are lenses, not rivals, and the professional act is the evidenced choice.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Behaviourism defines learning as: *(Remember)*
   a) a change in what the learner can explain
   b) a change in observable behaviour
   c) the construction of understanding with assistance
   d) the observation of a model's outcome

2. The strongest models in a classroom are perceived as: *(Understand)*
   a) the loudest pupils in the room
   b) the best dressed
   c) the oldest in the school
   d) important, similar to the learner, or warmly related to the learner

3. Negative reinforcement is: *(Understand)*
   a) punishment by another name
   b) removing something disliked, which strengthens the behaviour it follows
   c) adding something disliked, which weakens the behaviour it follows
   d) the absence of any consequence

4. A star chart raises completed work, and copying also rises. The most precise reading
   is: *(Apply)*
   a) the chart has failed
   b) the chart is reinforcing completion, and copying is the cheapest route to it
   c) the pupils are cheating and must be punished
   d) stars have become a conditioned stimulus for copying

5. A pupil calls every four-legged animal "billi". Meeting a dog and separating it from
   cats is: *(Understand)*
   a) assimilation
   b) accommodation
   c) extinction
   d) vicarious reinforcement

6. Bruner's three modes of representation, in the order teaching should follow,
   are: *(Remember)*
   a) symbolic, iconic, enactive
   b) enactive, symbolic, iconic
   c) enactive, iconic, symbolic
   d) iconic, enactive, symbolic

7. Rashid recites a poem perfectly and cannot say what it is about. The most accurate
   account is: *(Apply)*
   a) he has not been reinforced enough
   b) the words met no schema that carried the poem's meaning
   c) his zone of proximal development is too small
   d) he lacks the enactive mode of representation

8. A pupil watches a classmate praised for politeness and becomes more polite. This
   is: *(Understand)*
   a) classical conditioning
   b) negative reinforcement
   c) vicarious reinforcement
   d) accommodation

9. Babar finishes four sums with one hint and two alone by Friday. The hint, and its
   removal, illustrate: *(Apply)*
   a) the zone of proximal development and scaffolding
   b) extinction and generalisation
   c) positive punishment
   d) the spiral curriculum

10. "Constructivism means the teacher stops teaching." The corrected version is: *(Understand)*
    a) the teacher teaches less and tests more
    b) the teacher designs the site, supplies the schemas and removes the scaffold on
       schedule
    c) the teacher becomes a peer among learners
    d) the teacher waits for each pupil's zone to widen on its own

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define behaviourism in one sentence and name the two mechanisms it offers, with a
   classroom example of each. *(Understand)*
2. Label the corridor scene's four conditioning elements in two lines: footsteps,
   scolding, falling silent, and the repeated pairing. *(Apply)*
3. The teacher's response to the first wrong answer of the year is among the most
   consequential acts in a classroom. Explain why, using at least two of the four
   observational-learning steps. *(Apply)*
4. Give the reinforcement-punishment distinction precisely, with one classroom instance
   of negative reinforcement that is not punishment. *(Understand)*
5. Explain assimilation and accommodation with one example of your own, in two
   sentences. *(Understand)*
6. Name Bruner's three modes and place one lesson you know into their order, or name
   the missing mode. *(Apply)*
7. Explain why Miss Sadia's "good try" spread to pupils she never spoke to, using
   vicarious reinforcement. *(Apply)*
8. List two things that make a model strong, and say why a teacher cannot decline the
   office. *(Understand)*
9. Define the zone of proximal development with Babar's three task levels, in three
   lines. *(Understand)*
10. Give the four-lens reading of Amjad: one sentence per lens, each naming its
    response. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The star chart, precisely.** Miss Farzana's chart raised completed work and copying
   together. Analyse what the chart actually reinforced, redesign it so the trained
   behaviour is what was intended, and name the risk your redesign carries. *(Analyze)*

2. **Two poems.** Rashid recited perfectly and understood nothing; Sana stumbled and
   understood. Using the cognitive turn, explain the difference, and argue what each
   pupil's teacher should do next. *(Analyze)*

3. **The first wrong answer.** Using social learning theory, explain why a teacher's
   response to the first wrong answer of the year is among the most consequential acts
   in a classroom, and what a teacher should deliberately make observable instead. *(Analyze)*

4. **Ninety seconds.** Babar's breakthrough came not from a better explanation but from
   the right help at the right distance. Analyse the episode through social
   constructivism: where the zone was, what the scaffold was, and why its removal was
   the point. *(Analyze)*

5. **Four lenses, one pupil.** Amjad completes no written work, watches demonstrations
   attentively and explains them orally. Read him through all four theory families,
   choose the lens you would act on first, justify it with evidence, and name what
   would make you switch. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - the behaviourist focus is observable behaviour, by deliberate decision.
2. **d** - importance, similarity and warmth are what make a model strong; the
   teacher is all three by default.
3. **b** - negative reinforcement removes something disliked and strengthens the
   behaviour; it is one of the two strengtheners.
4. **b** - the chart is precise: it reinforces completion, and the cheapest route to
   completion is copying.
5. **b** - the schema itself changed: accommodation.
6. **c** - do it, see it, then the sign for it.
7. **b** - the recitation was trained (operant) but the words met no meaning-carrying
   schema.
8. **c** - the reinforcement was watched, not felt: vicarious.
9. **a** - the hint is the scaffold inside the zone; its removal is the design.
10. **b** - constructivism demands more deliberate teaching, not less: site, materials,
    scaffold, and its removal.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. Learning as change in observable behaviour (1); classical conditioning (association)
   and operant conditioning (consequences), with any apt example of each (1).
2. Footsteps: neutral stimulus turned conditioned stimulus (1); scolding:
   unconditioned stimulus; falling silent: response; the pairing: the repeated
   bell-before-food arrangement (1 for the remaining three correctly placed).
3. Every pupil is watching what happens to the first wrong answer (attention) and
   retains whether the error was worked through or mocked (retention) (1); what they
   saw shapes whether they risk answering wrongly out loud (reproduction and
   motivation) - the response is a demonstration by the room's strongest model, not
   one exchange with one pupil (1).
4. Reinforcement makes behaviour more frequent (positive: adds wanted; negative: removes
   disliked); punishment makes it less (1 for the full distinction); any apt negative
   reinforcement example, e.g. seatwork finished and the scolding stops (1).
5. Assimilation: new experience filed into an existing schema; accommodation: the
   schema rebuilt to fit what would not go in (1); any accurate original example (1).
6. Enactive, iconic, symbolic (1); a lesson placed in that order, or the missing mode
   named with its place (1).
7. The reinforcement was vicarious: pupils watched the outcome of answering wrongly
   (help, not shame) and adjusted without being addressed (2).
8. Any two of: importance, similarity, warm relationship (1); the teacher is present
   daily and central, so is a model in every interaction whether or not they accept it
   (1).
9. The zone is the gap between independent and assisted performance (1); Babar's three
   levels: sums alone (behind the zone), sums with the hint (in it), the whole
   worksheet not even with help (beyond it) (1).
10. Behaviourist: Amjad's copying earns the marks without the work - reward what only
    his own writing can earn (1); cognitive: the worksheet assumes a writing fluency
    his oral answers show he has not built - find and supply the missing step (1);
    social learning: he has watched copying pass and trying fail - change what trying
    is seen to earn; constructivist: written work sits beyond even assisted reach - a
    nearer task plus a scaffold (1 for the remaining two).
