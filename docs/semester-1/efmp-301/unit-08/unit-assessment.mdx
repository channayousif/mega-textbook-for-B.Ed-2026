# Classroom Management - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Classroom management is the design and running of the room so learning has somewhere
to happen - serving learning time, safety and participation, not silence. Its
principles: prevention (the problems that do not happen), routine (recurring
questions decided once), engagement (work that grips) and consistency (the same
response every time, binding the teacher too).

The positive learning environment is designed, not felt: physical space that shows
who the room belongs to, knowable routines, relationships built before they are
needed, participation norms taught by the teacher's first answers and defended by the
room, and feedback that names the next step. The first week carries the year's
highest leverage.

Discipline holds standards through few, responsibility-focused rules and natural or
logical consequences - never arbitrary ones. Individual behaviour meets a ladder -
proximity, private word, choice, consequence, parent meeting - that keeps dignity at
every step, gives the choice before the consequence, and reads behaviour
developmentally before morally while the standard still holds.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Classroom management, as this unit defines it, is: *(Remember)*
   a) the control of pupils' behaviour
   b) the design and running of the room so learning has somewhere to happen
   c) the maintenance of silence
   d) the enforcement of school rules

2. The three things management serves are: *(Remember)*
   a) silence, order and obedience
   b) learning time, safety and participation
   c) marks, discipline and results
   d) the teacher, the syllabus and the timetable

3. "The easiest management problems to solve are ones that do not happen in the first
   place" is the principle of: *(Understand)*
   a) routine
   b) prevention
   c) engagement
   d) consistency

4. A routine is best described as: *(Understand)*
   a) a rule with a punishment attached
   b) a decided answer to a recurring question
   c) a mood of the teacher
   d) a school policy

5. A silent class copying without understanding is: *(Apply)*
   a) well managed
   b) controlled and badly managed
   c) the ideal
   d) impossible to improve

6. Which of these is NOT one of the five parts of the positive learning
   environment? *(Remember)*
   a) physical space
   b) routines
   c) the prize chart
   d) feedback

7. Rules, as this unit presents them, are: *(Understand)*
   a) procedures for efficiency
   b) standards of behaviour for which pupils take responsibility
   c) the teacher's preferences
   d) the school's circulars

8. A pupil who takes a lunch replaces it. The consequence is: *(Apply)*
   a) natural
   b) logical
   c) arbitrary
   d) punitive

9. The ladder's first step is: *(Remember)*
   a) the private word
   b) the consequence
   c) proximity
   d) the parent meeting

10. A consequence invented mid-anger is: *(Understand)*
    a) a logical consequence
    b) a natural consequence
    c) a punishment with a costume on
    d) the choice step done properly

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define classroom management in one sentence, naming what it serves. *(Understand)*
2. Explain the four principles with a classroom instance of each. *(Understand)*
3. Explain why silence is not the goal, using the three things management serves.
   *(Understand)*
4. Explain how the Sukkur room's "good try" norm was built and how it outlived its
   author. *(Understand)*
5. Describe the five parts of the positive learning environment and one first-week
   move for any three. *(Understand)*
6. Explain why the relationship must exist before it is needed, with the difficult
   pupil in mind. *(Understand)*
7. Distinguish rules from routines with one example of each. *(Understand)*
8. Distinguish natural from logical consequences with one example of each.
   *(Understand)*
9. Describe the ladder's five steps and the principle any one of them serves. *(Understand)*
10. The pupil refuses work. Give the developmental reading, the ladder response, and
    the support offered beside the standard, in three lines. *(Apply)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The second teacher's class.** Using the principles, analyse the second Class 5
   room's first ten minutes: which principles are starved, what the "difficulty" is
   made of, and what the first week of a redesign would decide once. Defend the one
   change that recovers the most learning time. *(Analyze)*

2. **The first week of a difficult class.** You inherit the second room in a new
   year, reputations arriving before pupils. Design the first week: the decisions
   made once, the moves for each of the five parts, the first norm and how you build
   it, and one thing you would deliberately NOT do. *(Create)*

3. **The naming teacher.** Analyse naming a pupil to the room: what it achieves, what
   it costs, and on what schedule the costs arrive (reinforcement, anxiety). Design
   the same week with the ladder, and say what it does for authority that naming
   undoes. *(Analyze)*

4. **The ladder borrowed.** A teacher borrows the ladder for a week and keeps a tally
   of where behaviours actually resolved. Predict the distribution with reasons, and
   say what the prediction implies about where a new teacher's preparation is best
   spent. *(Analyze)*

5. **One room, one plan.** Design a first-week management plan for a real class you
   know: the 8:05, the five rules in the pupils' words, the first norm, the ladder
   pinned inside the desk, and the first-week move for each part of the environment.
   Justify each element by its principle, and name the plan's honest limit. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - design and running for learning, not control for its own sake.
2. **b** - learning time, safety, participation.
3. **b** - prevention: the problems that do not happen.
4. **b** - a decided answer to a recurring question.
5. **b** - controlled and badly managed: the purpose has left the room.
6. **c** - the prize chart is a tool, not one of the five parts.
7. **b** - standards for which pupils take responsibility.
8. **b** - imposed by others but logically connected: logical.
9. **c** - proximity: the smallest intervention that works.
10. **c** - a punishment with a costume on.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. The design and running of the room so learning has somewhere to happen (1);
   serving learning time, safety and participation (1).
2. Prevention, routine, engagement, consistency (1 for all four); any apt instances
   (1).
3. Silence is a sound, not a state of learning; a class can be silent and starve all
   three services (2).
4. Built by the teacher's first answers to wrong answers (the "good try" pattern)
   (1); outlived its author because the room adopted it as its own norm - pupils say
   it to pupils (1).
5. Physical space, routines, relationships, participation norms, feedback (1 for
   all five); any three apt first-week moves (1).
6. The relationship is the channel the difficult pupil will eventually need; built
   in calm, it exists to be used in storm (1); built during the storm it is another
   confrontation (1).
7. Routines are efficiency answers (bags, register); rules are responsibility
   standards (respect for others' learning) (1 for the distinction; 1 for the
   examples).
8. Natural: happens on its own (late arrival misses instructions); logical: imposed
   by others but obviously connected (the lunch replaced) (2).
9. Proximity, private word, choice, consequence, parent meeting (1); any one
   principle accurately (1).
10. Developmental: outside the zone, or the protect loop (1); ladder: the choice,
    not the ultimatum, after the private word (1); support: find the missing step,
    offer it beside the standard (1).
