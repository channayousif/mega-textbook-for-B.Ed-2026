# Classroom Management

<Figure id="fig-U8-7" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efmp-301/unit-08/fig-U8-7.webp" alt="A teacher greets pupils by name at the door of a Sindh government school classroom while inside the class has already settled to work: bags stowed, pairs comparing answers, a pupil handing out books, desks facing the board and pupils' work on the wall. A well-managed room is busy, not silent." width={1376} height={768} />

Unit 3's reinforcement and Unit 6's motivation both ended at the same door: the room
where sixty pupils and one teacher spend their day. This one-week unit opens it.

The staff room treats management as control - of pupils, of noise, of behaviour. The
psychology of the classroom treats it as design: the arranging of space, routines,
rules and responses so that learning has somewhere to happen. The difference is not
wordplay. A controlled class is quiet; a well-managed class is working, and the two
only look alike from the corridor.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Define **classroom management** and state its principles: prevention, routine,
  engagement, consistency.
- Describe the parts of a **positive learning environment** and the first-week moves
  that build each.
- Manage **discipline**: rules that are few and responsibility-focused, and
  consequences that are natural or logical rather than arbitrary.
- Respond to **individual behaviour** through an escalation ladder that keeps dignity
  at every step, reading behaviour developmentally before morally (Unit 2's habit).

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-7 (the learning situation from Unit 1; reinforcement from Unit 3; motivation
from Unit 6; individual differences from Unit 7). Management is where those ideas
become the daily running of a room.

## How to use this unit

- **Work through the topics in order.** The principles come first, the environment
  applies them, and the discipline topic assumes both.
- **Keep one room in mind.** The unit assessment asks for a first-week management
  plan for a described class; the notes you keep on a real room are the raw material.
- **Watch the dignity rule.** Every strategy in this unit has a version that shames
  and a version that does not. The unit teaches the second.
- After the three topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank
  with answers and marking guidance.
