# Classroom Management - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Week 13** to Chapter 8:
principles of classroom management, creating positive learning environments, and
managing discipline and behavior. Three topics, one week - run the third at a
squeeze if you must, but never skip it.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This unit's best demonstration is the room itself.

**Run the two rooms as the spine.** The decided 8:05 and the mood-led morning are
the unit in miniature: every principle, every part and every ladder step can be
found in their first ten minutes. Return to them in all three sessions.

**Borrow the ladder for real.** The practical work (below) has trainees use the
ladder in their practicum for one week and keep a tally. The tally's distribution -
most behaviours resolving at proximity and the private word - converts more
certainty about "strictness" than any argument.

**Model the norms you teach.** The sessions on participation norms and dignity land
only if the trainer's own room does what the unit says: wrong answers from trainees
met with "good try - how did you get that?", no naming, no sarcasm. The room is the
reading.

## Sequencing across the week

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| First session | Topic 8.1 - the definition, the principles | the two-rooms ten-minute analysis |
| Second session | Topic 8.2 - the five parts, the first week | the room audit |
| Third session | Topic 8.3 - rules, consequences, the ladder | the place-it-on-the-ladder activity, with the private word drafted aloud |

If a session is lost, compress Topic 8.2's table work rather than Topic 8.3's
ladder. The ladder is the unit's most portable artefact and the one trainees will
actually carry into a classroom.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"A well-managed class is silent."** Meet it with the copying class: silent,
orderly, learning nothing. Then ask what the three services would say about that
room.

**"Prevention is soft; discipline is strong."** The prevention-first finding is
empirical, not sentimental: the problems that do not happen are the easiest to
solve. Ask the room where the staff room's energy goes - to the problems that did
happen.

**"Naming works."** It does, briefly. Ask what it trains (Unit 3), what it costs the
quiet pupils (Unit 6's anxiety), and what it does to the pupil's next wrong answer.
The naming teacher is usually defending authority; ask what the ladder does for
authority instead.

**"Consequences must hurt to teach."** The natural-logical distinction is the reply:
the connection teaches, not the pain. The lunch replaced connects; the mathematics
test missed for talking in Urdu teaches only that power exists.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is a group assignment: **each
trainee borrows the escalation ladder for one week in their practicum - proximity,
private word, choice, consequence, parent meeting, each tried before the next - and
keeps a tally of where behaviours actually resolved.**

Run the reporting in the third session. The tallies are pooled; the room predicts
the distribution BEFORE the tallies are read out, and the gap between prediction and
evidence is the session's lesson. Twenty minutes; the pooled tally is the artefact
behind ERQ 4.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the three topics' `Check your understanding`
blocks (fifteen items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The
`unit-assessment` page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in
its final bounded section.

ERQ 5 is the capstone and integrates all three topics; schedule it after the
practical work so the plan has a real room behind it. ERQ 3 rewards the naming
analysis the third session trains, and works well as the week's written homework.
