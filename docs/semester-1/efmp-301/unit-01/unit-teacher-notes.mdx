# Introduction to Educational Psychology - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Weeks 1 and 2** to this chapter, with the
second week ending in discussion and a short assessment.

## Teaching strategies

The course guide specifies interactive lecture with Pakistani classroom examples, and small-group
discussion and case analysis. Both suit this unit, but the order matters.

**Lead with the case, not the definition.** Every topic opens with a classroom situation for a
reason. Trainees who meet "educational psychology is the scientific study of..." first will
memorise it and learn nothing. Trainees who have spent four minutes arguing about why Ayesha can
halve a roti but not a fraction will absorb the definition as an answer to a question they now
have.

**Use their own schooling as data.** This is the one topic in the programme where every trainee
has twelve years of first-hand evidence. Ask what their own teachers assumed about how learning
works, and what those assumptions cost them. It is usually a livelier discussion than the reading.

**Protect the disagreements.** The Topic 1.1 sorting activity is designed to produce arguments
about which part of the scope a question belongs to. Do not resolve them. The point is that the
three overlap and that a teacher must hold all three; a tidy set of correct assignments would destroy the lesson.

## Sequencing across the two weeks

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1, first session | Topic 1.1 - what the field is, its scope, its methods | the sorting activity in full; it anchors the rest |
| Week 1, second session | Topic 1.2 - the two-way relationship, applied character | the three-teachers role play |
| Week 2, first session | Topic 1.3 - learner differences | the three-pupils activity; expect this to overrun |
| Week 2, second session | Topic 1.4 - planning, management, assessment, reflection | rebuilding Miss Farah's lesson, then the short assessment |

If a session is lost, compress Topic 1.2's role play rather than Topic 1.3's activity. Trainees
can recover the two-way relationship from reading; they rarely recover the distinction between
Sana, Kiran and Hina without arguing it through aloud.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"This is just common sense."** Do not argue it down. Write two contradictory proverbs on the
board - "practice makes perfect" and "you either have a gift for it or you do not" - and ask
which one common sense endorses. The point lands better than any defence of the discipline.

**"Researchers discover, teachers apply."** This survives most of the reading. It is best broken
by Mrs Naz in Topic 1.2: she implemented faithfully and it failed. Ask what she should have done
instead, and trainees will invent the feedback loop themselves.

**"Ability explains it."** The most stubborn one, because it is how most trainees were described
at school. Expect resistance when you suggest that "weak" is a prediction rather than a
description. The three-pupils activity is the lever: once a group has proposed three different
next steps for three pupils on the same staff-room list, the single label is visibly useless.

**"Reflection means writing how the lesson felt."** Address this directly in Topic 1.4 by asking
the class to make one of their own reflections falsifiable. Most first attempts still contain no
prediction; the second attempt usually does.

## Language and access

Several trainees will be more fluent in Sindhi than in English, which makes Topic 1.3 unusually
delicate: you are teaching about language cost to a group experiencing it. Let the discussion
work in whichever language carries the argument, and ask for the written summative in the
language of assessment. Naming this openly, once, tends to improve participation for the rest of
the unit.

## Practical work

The course guide's practical component for this chapter is a discussion task. The version used
here: **each trainee brings one remembered lesson - their own, from school - in which they
genuinely learnt something, and one in which they did not.**

In pairs, they work out what differed. Collect the factors on the board and sort them into the
three parts of the scope from Topic 1.1. Two things reliably emerge: the list is dominated by
the learning situation rather than by the learner, and almost nobody names ability. That is the
unit's argument, produced by the class rather than asserted by you.

Allow twenty-five minutes. It works best at the end of Week 1, after Topic 1.1 and before Topic
1.3.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in each topic's `Check your understanding` block; across the four
topics that is twenty items, comfortably above the five-to-eight floor the pipeline expects for a
unit. The `unit-assessment` page carries the graded bank, with worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded section.

The guide's "short assessment" at the end of Week 2 is best served by the Topic 1.4 summative
task, which requires trainees to draw on at least two topics and so functions as a unit-level
check rather than a topic-level one.
