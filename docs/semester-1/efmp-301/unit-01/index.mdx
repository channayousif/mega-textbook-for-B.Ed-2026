# Introduction to Educational Psychology

Every teacher already has a theory of how pupils learn. It may never have been written down,
and it may be wrong, but it is there, and it decides what you do when a class goes quiet or a
bright pupil stops trying. Educational psychology is the effort to test those theories instead
of trusting them. This unit works through what the subject is, where it came from, and what it
actually gives you on a Monday morning, in four short topics.

Each topic is a complete learning cycle: it opens with a real classroom situation, explains the
ideas, gives you an activity, checks your understanding, and ends with something to try on your
practicum.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Define **educational psychology** and explain what makes it a distinct field rather than
  psychology in general or teaching experience written down.
- Describe its **scope** - the learner, the learning process and the learning situation - and
  place a given classroom question inside one of the three.
- Explain why it is called a **scientific** study, and name the main ways it gathers evidence.
- Explain the **two-way relationship** between psychology and education, including what school
  classrooms give back to psychology.
- Describe the main ways learners in one class **differ**, and explain what each difference does
  and does not tell you about a pupil.
- Explain how psychological ideas shape **planning, classroom management, assessment and a
  teacher's own reflection**, and use them to analyse a described classroom episode.

## Prerequisite knowledge

None beyond HSC-level general study skills. This is the opening unit of the course; no
psychology is assumed. What you do bring is years as a school pupil yourself, and several
topics start from that.

## How to use this unit

- **Work through the topics in order.** Topic 1.3 uses the scope idea from 1.1, and Topic 1.4
  assumes you have met learner differences in 1.3.
- **Do the activity and the "Check your understanding" questions before moving on.** They are
  not graded; they show you which ideas are still shaky while it is cheap to fix that.
- **Keep your Topic 1.4 reflection note.** Later units on development, learning theories and
  motivation each send you back to it.
- After the four topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank with answers
  and marking guidance, so you can test yourself before a class test.
