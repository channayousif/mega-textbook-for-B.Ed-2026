# Teaching-Learning Process - assessment and review

## Unit summary

Teaching methods are tools known by their jobs: lecture covers ground, demonstration
shows process, discussion makes thinking audible, question-answer carries guided
practice, group work rehearses ideas socially, case study demands judgement. None is
best; each is best for something. The professional choice is one decision in three
parts - purpose, constraints, method, in that order - with a check that the method
still serves the purpose.

Instructional design is planning backward: the outcome first (a behaviour that can
be observed, with conditions and a criterion, in Mager's tradition), the evidence
second, the activity third, and a forward check that changes the activity, never
the outcome. Teacher effectiveness is the same discipline made habitual: a
repertoire of observable practices - planning backward, the formative cycle,
attention and load, the three needs and dignity, the developmental reading - each
nameable, watchable and growable.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The guide's strategy list includes all of these EXCEPT: *(Remember)*
   a) lectures
   b) interactive discussions
   c) memorisation drills
   d) case study analysis

2. A method is best known by: *(Understand)*
   a) its popularity
   b) the job it does
   c) its modernity
   d) how quiet it keeps the room

3. What a demonstration gives that a lecture cannot is: *(Understand)*
   a) speed of coverage
   b) the process seen as an event, and the why-question it earns
   c) easier marking
   d) syllabus coverage

4. Group work demands, beyond rearranged seating: *(Understand)*
   a) nothing; the seating is the method
   b) a task that needs the group, roles, and monitoring
   c) a loud room
   d) a prize chart

5. The choosing frame's parts, in order, are: *(Remember)*
   a) method, purpose, constraints
   b) purpose, constraints, method
   c) constraints, method, purpose
   d) activity, evidence, outcome

6. "If you don't know where you're going, you could end up someplace else" opens
   the case for: *(Understand)*
   a) flexibility in teaching
   b) planning backward from outcomes
   c) lecturing clearly
   d) strict discipline

7. In Mager's formulation, a good objective specifies: *(Remember)*
   a) a topic to be covered
   b) an observable behaviour, with conditions and a criterion
   c) the teacher's activities
   d) the examination's date

8. "The pupil will understand fractions" fails because it: *(Apply)*
   a) is too short
   b) names no behaviour that can be observed
   c) mentions diagrams
   d) has no topic

9. The forward check can change: *(Understand)*
   a) the outcome
   b) the activity, never the outcome
   c) nothing; the plan is fixed
   d) the constraints

10. Teacher effectiveness, on this unit's account, is: *(Understand)*
    a) charisma
    b) a gift one is born with
    c) a repertoire of observable, growable practices
    d) years of service

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain what any four methods are best at, in four lines. *(Understand)*
2. Explain what a demonstration costs that a lecture does not. *(Understand)*
3. State the choosing frame's three parts and why method comes last. *(Understand)*
4. Explain the water cycle's three purposes and the method each one called for.
   *(Apply)*
5. Describe the backward order and what each step produces. *(Understand)*
6. Give one objective that passes Mager's test and one that fails, and name the
   difference. *(Understand)*
7. Explain why the evidence comes before the activity, using validity. *(Understand)*
8. Explain the forward check and what it may change. *(Understand)*
9. A colleague says "lecturing is bad teaching." Give the tool reading, and name the
   demand the two-hour lecture fails to pay. *(Analyze)*
10. Explain what "growable" means for a practice, with the formative cycle as your
    model. *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The first lesson.** Choose a real topic and defend the method you would teach
   first: the purpose it serves, the constraints it works within, and what it
   deliberately leaves for the second lesson. Name the method you rejected and the
   purpose difference that justifies the rejection. *(Analyze)*

2. **The outcome sentence.** Write the outcome sentence for one real lesson -
   behaviour, conditions, criterion - then the evidence that would show it met, then
   the activity chosen to produce that evidence. Run the forward check explicitly. *(Create)*

3. **Two teachers.** Analyse the more effective of your two remembered teachers
   against the practice inventory: which practices were present, what each looked
   like, and which single practice carried the most weight - with evidence, not
   affection. *(Analyze)*

4. **The script and the choice.** A colleague follows the textbook's method sequence
   for every lesson, whatever the class. Evaluate the practice: what the choosing
   frame says about why methods vary, when following the sequence is nevertheless
   the design working, and what the script-follower and the improviser both miss.
   *(Analyze)*

5. **One lesson, fully designed.** Design one complete lesson for a named Sindh
   class (name the class and subject): the outcome sentence, the evidence, the
   activity with its minutes, the method choice justified by the frame, and the
   formative check built into the close. Name the plan's honest limit. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **c** - the guide names lectures, discussions, question-answer, demonstration,
   case study analysis.
2. **b** - a tool is known by its job.
3. **b** - the process as event, and the curiosity it earns.
4. **b** - task, roles, monitoring.
5. **b** - purpose, constraints, method.
6. **b** - the case for planning backward.
7. **b** - observable behaviour, conditions, criterion.
8. **b** - no observable behaviour is named.
9. **b** - the activity changes, never the outcome.
10. **c** - a repertoire: observable and growable.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Award marks for the substance below, not for matching wording.

1. Any four of: lecture - covers ground fast, shared framework; demonstration -
   shows process; discussion - thinking audible; question-answer - guided practice;
   group work - social rehearsal; case study - judgement on real material (2).
2. Materials, visibility for sixty pupils, and time - the lecture needs only a
   voice and a board (2).
3. Purpose, constraints, method (1); method comes last because it is chosen to fit
   the purpose inside the constraints, not the reverse (1).
4. Know the cycle (lecture's map), see the cycle (demonstration), test the cycle
   (investigation) - purpose naming the method (2).
5. Outcome - what pupils will DO; evidence - what would show it; activity - chosen
   to produce the evidence (2).
6. Pass: "adds unlike fractions using a diagram, five of six sums" (behaviour,
   condition, criterion); fail: "understands fractions" (no behaviour) (2).
7. The assessment designed before the activity tests the outcome rather than
   whatever the activity happens to ask - validity at the planning stage (2).
8. Does this activity produce that evidence for that outcome? If not, the activity
   changes (2).
9. Tool reading: a lecture covers ground no other method covers as fast - the
   defect is the two-hour lecture, not the method (1); the demand it fails to pay:
   attention tires and the workbench holds only so much, so the lecture earns its
   length by pausing, recapping and being short (1).
10. A practice can be watched, named, tried and checked against evidence - the
    cycle applied to one's own teaching; a gift cannot (2).
