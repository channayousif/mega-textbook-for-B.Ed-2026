# Teaching-Learning Process

The course has spent nine units on the learner: how pupils develop, learn, think,
differ, are motivated, belong, and are assessed. This one-week unit turns the lens
around and asks about the teacher's side of the desk - the methods, the design of
instruction, and the question every trainee is quietly asking: what makes a teacher
effective, and can it be learned?

The guide's own teaching-strategy list - lectures, interactive discussions,
question-answer, demonstration, case study analysis - is this unit's material, and
the unit's answer to the quiet question is yes: effectiveness is a set of observable
practices, and every one of them can be named, watched and practised.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Name the main **teaching methods** and match each to the purpose it serves, using
  the guide's own strategy list.
- Explain **choosing strategies**: purpose, constraints and method as one decision.
- Describe **instructional design** as planning backward: outcome first, evidence
  second, activity third.
- Explain **teacher effectiveness** as observable, growable practice rather than
  charisma.

## How to use this unit

- **Teach one topic twice.** The unit assessment asks for one topic taught by three
  methods; the notes you keep on a real topic are the raw material.
- **Use your own teachers.** The effectiveness topic asks you to compare two
  remembered teachers against the research; choose them now.
- **Notice the course's own methods.** This course has used lecture, discussion,
  demonstration and case study on you for ten units - the unit will ask you to see
  that.
- After the two topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank
  with answers and marking guidance.
