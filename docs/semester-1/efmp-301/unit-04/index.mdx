# Cognitive Processes in Learning

Unit 3 asked what learning is. This unit asks what the learner's mind is doing while it
happens. The question is practical, not philosophical: a lesson that demands what
attention cannot hold, or teaches in an order memory cannot keep, fails for reasons no
amount of effort fixes. And a lesson that works *with* the mind's systems - one thing at
a time, retrieval practised, steps made visible - succeeds for reasons that look like
talent and are not.

Three topics: the gateway (attention and perception), the workbench (memory and the
information-processing model), and the workshop (thinking, problem-solving and
reasoning).

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain **attention** as the gateway to learning and describe what captures, divides
  and exhausts it in a real classroom.
- Explain **perception** as active interpretation, and what that means for how pupils
  read a lesson's materials.
- Describe the **information-processing model**: sensory memory, working memory and
  long-term memory, and the jobs of encoding, storage and retrieval.
- Explain why **forgetting** happens - decay, interference and retrieval failure - and
  what the evidence says about study strategies that work.
- Describe **thinking and concept formation**, **problem-solving** strategies and their
  obstacles, and **reasoning** in its inductive and deductive forms.
- Design and justify classroom activities that work **with** attention and memory
  rather than against them.

## How to use this unit

- **Work through the topics in order.** Topic 4.2 assumes the gateway is understood;
  Topic 4.3 assumes the workbench.
- **Test the claims on yourself.** Every topic's demonstrations work on the reader: try
  the span task, the gorilla video, the anagram before you read its solution. First-hand
  failure is the fastest teacher of these limits.
- **Keep one lesson in mind.** The unit assessment asks you to redesign a study or
  revision plan against the memory model, so the notes you keep on your own study habits
  are the raw material.
- After the three topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank with
  answers and marking guidance.
