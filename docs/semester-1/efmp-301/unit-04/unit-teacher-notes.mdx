# Cognitive Processes in Learning - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Weeks 8 and 9** to Chapter 4:
Week 8 on attention and perception, and memory and information processing; Week 9 on
thinking, problem-solving and reasoning, ending in the guide's class-activities slot.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. This is the demonstration unit of the course: every claim can be
made to fail in front of the room.

**Run the failures on the trainees.** The span test, the gorilla video, the
unrelated-versus-story word lists: each one lands harder as a personal failure than as
a described experiment. A trainee who could not hold eight words has been *shown* the
workbench's size; no lecture does that.

**Name the strategies aloud, always.** The problem-solving topic's whole method is
naming: "we are working backwards". Model it in every session of the fortnight, not
just this unit's sessions, so the naming becomes the class's habit rather than the
unit's vocabulary.

**Use their own study lives as the case study.** The October chapter is their chapter.
The strategy audit (ERQ 4) works only if the habits audited are real; set it up by
asking, in session, for the three strategies each trainee actually used for the last
test, before any evidence is discussed.

## Sequencing across the two weeks

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| Week 8, first session | Topic 4.1 - attention and perception | the attention audit activity |
| Week 8, second session | Topic 4.2 - the memory systems, forgetting | the span test and strategy audit |
| Week 9, first session | Topic 4.3 - concepts, strategies, reasoning | the name-the-move problems |
| Week 9, second session | The class-activities slot: designing with the unit | the ERQ 5 lesson design, started in class |

If a session is lost, compress Topic 4.1's table work rather than Topic 4.2's
demonstrations. The memory model is the unit's spine; attention is its doorway, and a
doorway can be summarised in ten minutes if the demonstrations have already shown it.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"He cannot concentrate."** The trait reading of attention. Meet it with the
design reading: ask what the demand was, when it ran, and what competed. The
timetable case (Miss Hina) usually converts the room.

**"Memory is a recorder."** The October chapter is the cure, but only if the trainees
recognise their own strategy in it. Ask for a show of hands on re-reading before
analysing why it fails.

**"Problem-solving is a gift."** The Bilal case is built for this. Ask the room to
solve the rickshaw problem first, name their own moves, and then meet Bilal's answer
as a repertoire difference, not an ability difference.

**"Induction and deduction are jargon."** Keep both anchored in the staff room: "this
teacher always marks late" is induction; "if the test is Friday..." is deduction. The
jargon dissolves when the examples are theirs.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is the class-activities slot: **each
trainee designs one class activity that works with attention and memory, teaches it
for ten minutes in the session, and names the rule each part follows.**

Run it in Week 9's second session. Keep the materials ordinary - a text passage, a
set of sums, a map - so the design, not the props, is the work. The room's job during
each ten minutes is to spot the rule being followed or broken: every observer holds
the four design rules and reports one sighting.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the three topics' `Check your understanding` blocks
(fifteen items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The `unit-assessment`
page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded
section.

ERQ 5 integrates the whole unit and is best set after the practical work, when every
trainee has taught one design and been observed against the rules. ERQ 4 is the
self-study capstone; schedule it before the evidence is taught and mark it after, so
the audit is honest and the learning is visible.
