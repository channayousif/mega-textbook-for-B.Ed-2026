# Intelligence and Creativity - teacher notes

For the teacher educator. The course guide allots **Week 10** to Chapter 5 - a single
week for theories of intelligence, multiple intelligences, measurement, creativity and
giftedness. The unit is built to that scale: two topics, one session each, with the
talent-show activity bridging them.

## Teaching strategies

The guide names lectures, interactive discussion, question-answer, demonstration and
case study analysis. In one week, choose ruthlessly.

**Run the two-pupils case first.** Salman and Kiran open the unit because the staff
room's two words are the theory the trainees already hold. Let the room argue about
the labels before any theory is named; the axis then arrives as a map of positions
the room has already taken.

**Hold the evidence honestly, and make the trainees do it too.** This unit's
signature skill is holding a theory's appeal and its evidence caution at once. The
talent-show activity is designed to force the distinction: the show demonstrates
pupil difference abundantly, and demonstrates the eight-intelligences theory not at
all. Do not resolve the tension for the room; the CLO 4 outcome is the holding.

**Mark something with the fifth box.** The originality box is the unit's most
portable idea. Have trainees mark the two village essays with and without it in the
session, so the marking plan's silence becomes visible rather than described.

## Sequencing across the week

| Session | Focus | Leave time for |
|---|---|---|
| First session | Topic 5.1 - the axis, the caution, the measurement facts | the two-pupils argument and the score table |
| Second session | Topic 5.2 - creativity's parts, giftedness as need | the talent show and the fifth-box marking |

If a session is lost, cut the measurement detail before the evidence caution. The
mean and the deviation are facts a trainee can look up; the habit of asking what a
score does not show is the unit's transfer.

## Misconceptions to expect, and how to handle them

**"Multiple intelligences is proven."** Usually held warmly, by trainees who have
seen pupils saved by the wider lens. Do not argue it down: state the criticism's
content (the eight do not separate in test data), state the appeal's content
(abilities are wider than schools sample), and hold both on the board. The room's
discomfort at holding both is the learning.

**"Learning styles and multiple intelligences are the same thing."** The commonest
confusion in the unit. Untangle with the two questions: does the theory claim
teaching should match a diagnosed style (styles - weak evidence), or that abilities
are multiple (intelligences - disputed but different)? The residue either way is
multimodal teaching.

**"IQ measures how intelligent you are."** Meet it with the sampling point: the
score summarises the sampled tasks, and Kiran's wire is not in the sample. The
history (early misuse) lands the humility better than the statistics do.

**"Gifted pupils will be fine on their own."** The early finisher is the counter,
and the research summary behind him: boredom, then behaviour problems. Ask what
"more worksheets" actually trains, and let the room design the alternative.

## Practical work

The guide's practical component for this chapter is a case study analysis: **each
trainee brings one pupil (anonymised, five lines) whose ability the school's
examinations undersample, and the group designs the fifth box - an originality
criterion - for that pupil's subject.**

Run it in the second session after the fifth-box marking. Groups of four produce the
criterion, its weight, and one sample task that could earn full marks under it.
Fifteen minutes plus presentations. The criteria are collected and become the
session's artefact: a staff-room wall of boxes the usual plan lacks.

## Assessment notes

The unit's formative work sits in the two topics' `Check your understanding` blocks
(ten items across the unit, above the five-to-eight floor). The `unit-assessment`
page carries the graded bank with worked responses and rubrics in its final bounded
section.

ERQ 5 is the capstone and integrates both topics by design; schedule it after the
practical work so the task design has a real subject. ERQ 1 rewards the
theory-identification skill the first session trains; set it as the week's written
homework.
