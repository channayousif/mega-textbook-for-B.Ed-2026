# Intelligence and Creativity

Every teacher in Sindh has used the word "intelligent" this month, and almost none have
had to say what it means. This one-week unit takes the word apart. What does it name -
one general ability, or many? What do the tests that claim to measure it actually
measure, and what do they miss? And what is creativity, which the tests barely touch,
that a Class 5 talent show reveals more reliably than any examination?

The unit's discipline is honesty about evidence: the claim that every pupil has a
 favoured "learning style" is tested against the research, and the theory that named
 eight intelligences is presented with both its classroom appeal and its critics.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- State the **theories of intelligence** on the one-ability-to-many-abilities axis,
  with named positions (Spearman, Gardner).
- Explain **multiple intelligences** - its claim, its classroom appeal, and the
  evidence caution the research community attaches to it.
- Describe the **measurement of intelligence**: what an IQ-style score is, what the
  mean and deviation mean, and what the tests do and do not establish.
- Explain **creativity** through its observable parts - divergent and convergent
  thinking - and the classroom conditions that grow it.
- Recognize and support **gifted learners** as an educational need rather than a prize.

## How to use this unit

- **Two topics, one week.** Read 5.1 before 5.2: the measurement limits set up the
  creativity material.
- **Hold the evidence honestly.** The unit asks you to hold two things at once - the
  classroom value of seeing multiple abilities, and the research caution about the
  theory that names them. Both are in the guide's own CLO 4: analyse.
- **Keep one pupil in mind** whose ability the tests would miss. The unit assessment
  asks for exactly that pupil.
- After the two topics, the **unit assessment** page carries a full question bank with
  answers and marking guidance.
