# Comprehension and Analysis

Unit 1 gave you the building blocks of English: words, grammar, sounds. This unit asks what
happens when you put those blocks together to read and listen. A text is not just a collection
of sentences; it is a communication shaped by who wrote it, for whom, and for what purpose. This
unit gives you the skills to read between the lines.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain how **purpose**, **audience** and **context** shape a text and influence the choices a
  writer makes.
- Identify **tone**, **bias**, **stereotypes**, **assumptions** and **inferences** in a text.
- Apply **reading strategies** (skimming, scanning, SQ4R, critical reading) to different kinds of
  text.
- Practise **active listening** and apply strategies for overcoming listening barriers.
- Analyse a described text for what it says, what it implies, and what it assumes.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1, in particular the vocabulary and grammar foundations. This unit assumes you can identify
sentence structures and word classes and applies those skills to the analysis of whole texts.

## In this unit

1. [Topic 2.1 - Reading for purpose, audience and context](./topic-01) - how purpose, audience
   and context shape a text; identifying tone, bias and assumptions.
2. [Topic 2.2 - Strategies for reading and listening](./topic-02) - skimming, scanning, SQ4R,
   critical reading; active listening and note-taking.
3. [Topic 2.3 - Critical reading and listening in practice](./topic-03) - applying comprehension
   and analysis skills to a described text.

## How to use this unit

The three topics follow the logic of a single reading event. Topic 2.1 asks what the writer
brought to the text; Topic 2.2 asks what strategies you bring as a reader; Topic 2.3 asks you to
combine both in practice. Work through them in order.
