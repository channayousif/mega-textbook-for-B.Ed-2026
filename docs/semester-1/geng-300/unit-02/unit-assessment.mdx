# Unit 2 assessment

## Unit summary

Purpose, audience and context shape every text. Tone, bias, stereotypes, assumptions and
inferences are the features a critical reader identifies. Reading strategies (skimming, scanning,
SQ4R, critical reading) are tools that good readers choose according to their purpose. Active
listening requires focus, strategy and engagement. Note-taking formats (linear, mind map, Cornell)
help you record and review what you hear. Analysing a complete text, written or spoken, takes four
moves: identify surface features, identify hidden features, evaluate the writer's strategies and
connect to your own context.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit's account, "purpose" refers to: *(Remember)*
   a) the main idea of a text
   b) why a text was written
   c) the audience of a text
   d) the context of a text

2. A reader who wants to decide whether a text is relevant should use: *(Understand)*
   a) scanning
   b) SQ4R
   c) skimming
   d) critical reading

3. A pupil says, "The text assumes that all parents value education." This is an example of:
   *(Understand)*
   a) a bias
   b) an inference
   c) a stereotype
   d) an assumption

4. Which step comes first in the SQ4R strategy? *(Remember)*
   a) Survey
   b) Recite
   c) Read
   d) Review

5. A news article describes a protest as "a small group of troublemakers". This is primarily an
   example of: *(Apply)*
   a) tone
   b) bias
   c) inference
   d) assumption

6. Cornell notes are characterised by: *(Remember)*
   a) branches radiating from a central idea
   b) headings and bullet points
   c) a linear sequence of numbered points
   d) a divided page with cues, notes and a summary

7. A pupil reads every word of a chapter three times but remembers little. The most likely
   problem is: *(Apply)*
   a) the text is too difficult
   b) the pupil is using an inefficient strategy
   c) the pupil has a poor memory
   d) the text is too long

8. While analysing a complete text, a reader asks, "Did the writer make good choices for this
   purpose and audience?" Which of the four moves is the reader making? *(Understand)*
   a) evaluating the strategies
   b) identifying the surface features
   c) identifying the hidden features
   d) connecting to their own context

9. Active listening is best described as: *(Remember)*
   a) hearing without distraction
   b) taking notes on everything the speaker says
   c) listening with the intention to understand
   d) agreeing with the speaker

10. A trainee teacher has found that a textbook passage assumes every pupil has the internet at
    home. She now decides to adapt the passage for her village school, where few pupils do. Which
    of the four moves is she making at this point? *(Apply)*
    a) identifying the surface features
    b) identifying the hidden features
    c) evaluating the strategies
    d) connecting to her own context

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. A Class 3 reader shows the mother cooking and cleaning while the father reads the newspaper,
   in every story in the book. Identify the stereotype, explain why it is a stereotype and not
   simply a description, and suggest a more neutral alternative. *(Analyze)*

2. A pupil reads a three-page text three times but cannot answer questions on it. Using this
   unit's account, explain what is going wrong and propose two specific strategies. *(Analyze)*

3. Name the four main reading strategies matched to purpose in this unit (not the supporting
   habits of predicting, questioning and summarising) and state when each is most useful.
   *(Remember)*

4. A text says, "Naturally, everyone agrees that education is important." Identify the assumption
   and explain why it is an assumption, not a fact. *(Analyze)*

5. A school prospectus says, "Our English-medium school prepares every child for success in the
   modern world." Using the four moves, identify one hidden feature of this sentence (an assumption
   or a bias) and evaluate whether the sentence works for its intended audience of parents.
   *(Analyze)*

6. Describe the six steps of SQ4R in order. *(Remember)*

7. A news article describes a protest as "a small group of troublemakers". Identify the bias, explain
   what a more neutral description would be, and explain what the original description reveals about
   the writer's purpose. *(Analyze)*

8. Explain why "listening is a passive skill" is a misconception, and describe two specific
   strategies for active listening. *(Understand)*

9. A friend says, "Critical reading means finding faults with everything." Using this unit's
   account, explain what that claim overlooks. *(Evaluate)*

10. Describe two note-taking formats and explain when each is most useful. *(Remember)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A teacher gives pupils a textbook passage and asks them to read it. The pupils read every word
   but cannot answer the questions. Using this unit's account of reading strategies, explain what
   is going wrong, propose two specific strategies the teacher could teach, and justify each by
   reference to the purpose of the reading. *(Analyze)*

2. Read the following two contrasting passages on girls' education in rural Pakistan:

   **Passage A (Government press release):** "The Provincial Education Department is pleased to announce the launch of the Girls' Education Uplift Initiative across sixty rural union councils. Through targeted budgetary allocations, upgraded boundary walls and sanitation facilities, and accelerated recruitment of female educators, the government reaffirms its commitment to eliminating gender disparity in basic schooling."

   **Passage B (Local community advocacy report):** "While official statements celebrate new funding initiatives, communities on the ground confront an unchanged reality. Boundary walls remain unbuilt, promised transport services never materialized, and primary school posts remain vacant due to bureaucratic inertia. Budget announcements mean little without transparent monitoring and community accountability."

   Compare the two passages for audience and purpose. In your response: (a) identify the intended audience for each passage and support your identification with textual evidence; (b) explain the primary purpose of each writer; (c) compare the tone, assumptions, and choices of evidence across both texts; and (d) evaluate how effectively each passage achieves its purpose with its intended readership. *(Analyze)*

3. A pupil says, "I understand the text but I do not know if it is good." Read the following
   short notice, which was constructed for this assessment (it is not from a real school):

   > "From Monday, mobile phones are banned in all classrooms. Every teacher knows that pupils who
   > carry phones cannot concentrate. Responsible parents will support this rule. Phones found in
   > bags will be kept by the office until the end of term."

   Using this unit's account: (a) explain what skill the pupil needs to develop; (b) describe the
   four moves for analysing a complete text; and (c) apply all four moves to the notice above,
   quoting words from it as evidence. *(Analyze)*

4. Evaluate the claim that "critical reading means finding faults." Use this unit's account of
   purpose, audience, context, tone, bias, assumptions and inferences to argue for or against the
   claim. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative.** You are placed in a government primary school. A Class 5 teacher asks you to
   help pupils who can decode words but cannot understand what they read. Using this unit's
   account of purpose, audience, context, reading strategies and active listening, design a
   30-minute lesson that teaches two specific strategies and explains why each strategy works.
   Justify each choice. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - purpose is why a text was written.
2. **c** - skimming is for deciding whether a text is relevant.
3. **d** - the text takes something for granted without stating it.
4. **a** - survey is the first step of SQ4R.
5. **b** - "troublemakers" is a loaded term that favours one perspective.
6. **d** - Cornell notes use a divided page with cues, notes and a summary.
7. **b** - reading without a strategy is inefficient; the problem is the strategy, not the memory.
8. **a** - judging whether the writer's choices suit the purpose and audience is move 3,
   evaluating the strategies.
9. **c** - active listening is listening with the intention to understand.
10. **d** - the assumption is already identified (move 2); deciding how to use the passage in her
    own school is move 4, connecting to her own context.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** Identifies the gender stereotype that women belong only in domestic roles (1);
explains that a stereotype is a fixed, oversimplified belief about a group (1); notes that the
repetition in every story, not one scene, turns a description into a stereotype (1); suggests a
neutral alternative, e.g. showing both parents in varied roles (1).

**2. (5 marks)** Explains that reading without a strategy is inefficient (1); explains that the
pupil is reading passively (1); proposes two specific strategies (e.g. SQ4R, critical reading)
(1 each); justifies each by reference to the purpose (1).

**3. (4 marks)** Names each strategy with its correct use case: skimming, scanning, SQ4R, and critical reading (1 mark each up to 4 marks total). Predicting, questioning or summarising earn no mark in place of one of the four.

**4. (5 marks)** Identifies the assumption: that everyone agrees (1); explains that an assumption
is taken for granted without evidence (1); notes that the word "naturally" signals the assumption
(1); the reasoning draws on Topic 2.1 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**5. (5 marks)** Identifies a hidden feature with the words that show it, e.g. the assumption that
English-medium schooling alone brings success, or that "every child" will succeed (1); explains why
it is hidden or unsupported (1); names the audience as parents and the purpose as persuading them
to enrol (1); gives a reasoned judgement of whether the sentence works for them, e.g. appealing to
hopes but offering no evidence (1); the judgement weighs a strength as well as a weakness (1).

**6. (6 marks)** One mark per step in order: survey, question, read, recite, review, reflect.

**7. (5 marks)** Identifies the bias: "troublemakers" favours one side (1); provides a more neutral
description (e.g. "a group of protesters") (1); explains that the original description reveals a
hostile purpose or audience alignment (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 2.1 (1); the reasoning is
coherent (1).

**8. (4 marks)** Explains that listening requires active engagement, not just hearing (1); describes
two specific strategies (e.g. focused listening, note-taking, overcoming barriers) (1 each up to 2);
the explanation is coherent (1).

**9. (5 marks)** Takes the claim at its strongest (1); explains that critical reading means
understanding how a text works (1); notes that evaluation includes judgement of quality, not just
fault-finding (1); argues a verdict with reasons (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**10. (4 marks)** Describes two formats (e.g. linear, mind map, Cornell) (1 each up to 2); explains
when each is most useful (1 each up to 2).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 on the four criteria below.

**ERQ 1 - Reading strategies for a struggling class**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis of what is going wrong | Asserts the pupils are lazy | Explains that reading without a strategy is inefficient | Explains the difference between decoding and comprehension |
| Proposal of two strategies | Vague or generic | Specific and practicable | Specific, practicable, and timed |
| **Justification by purpose (Analyze)** | **Asserts the strategies work** | **Links each strategy to the unit** | **Shows how the strategy addresses the specific comprehension problem** |
| Register and clarity | Generic | Refers to the description | Writes for a colleague, defines terms |

**ERQ 2 - Comparing two texts for audience and purpose**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identification of audience and purpose | Names audience or purpose vaguely without evidence | Identifies intended audience and primary purpose for both texts with evidence | Accurately identifies audience, purpose, and institutional context for both texts with specific textual evidence |
| Comparison of tone and assumptions | Lists tone words without comparison | Compares tone and identifies at least one assumption in each text | Thoroughly compares official vs advocacy tone and uncovers hidden assumptions in both texts |
| **Comparative analysis and evaluation (Analyze)** | **Asserts differences without textual backing** | **Explains how linguistic choices support each text's distinct purpose** | **Critically compares how each text constructs its message to influence its intended readership** |
| Connection to Pakistani educational context | Generic reference to schools | Connects differences in perspective to real rural schooling challenges | Evaluates the practical implications of both viewpoints for school-level policy and classroom reality |

**ERQ 3 - "I understand but I do not know if it is good"**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explanation of the skill needed | Vague | Names critical reading or evaluation | Explains why understanding a text is not the same as judging it |
| Description of the four moves | Incomplete | All four named in order | All four described, each with what it asks of the reader |
| **Application to the supplied notice (Analyze)** | **Lists features of the notice without analysis** | **Identifies surface and hidden features (e.g. the unsupported claim "every teacher knows", the judgement in "responsible parents") with quoted evidence** | **Also evaluates whether the writer's choices work for parents and pupils, and says whether the notice should be used, adapted or rewritten for a Pakistani school** |
| Register and clarity | Generic | Refers to the description | Writes for a colleague, defines terms |

**ERQ 4 - "Critical reading means finding faults"**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of critical reading | Conflates it with fault-finding | Distinguishes evaluation from fault-finding | Shows what each is for and what each cannot do |
| Use of the unit's framework | Names the concepts vaguely | Applies at least three concepts | Uses the full framework to argue the point |
| **Evaluative reasoning (Evaluate)** | **Asserts a verdict** | **Argues a verdict with reasons** | **Takes the claim at its strongest, then shows what it leaves out** |
| Quality of example | Generic | Concrete classroom example | The example genuinely illustrates the argument |

**ERQ 5 - Integrative: a 30-minute lesson**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality of the two strategies | Vague or generic | Specific and described | Specific, described, and timed |
| Justification of the choices | Asserts they work | Links each to the unit | Shows how each addresses the specific comprehension problem |
| **Lesson design (Create)** | **Lists activities without structure** | **Has a clear sequence** | **The 30-minute plan is realistic and each activity is justified** |
| Realism and register | Ignores the constraints | Acknowledges them | The plan survives the constraints: 30 minutes, Class 5, government school |
