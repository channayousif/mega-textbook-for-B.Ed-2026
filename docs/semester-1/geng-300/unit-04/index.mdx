# Professional Writing and Intercultural Communication

Units 1, 2 and 3 gave you the building blocks of English, the skills to read and listen with
understanding, and the principles of effective communication. This unit asks you to put all of
that to work in the kinds of writing and speaking you will do as a teacher and as a professional.
Not every workplace or community communicates the same way, and a message that works in one
setting can fail in another. This unit gives you the tools to write with purpose and to adapt
your communication to the context.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Identify the **structure**, **tone** and **conventions** of the main professional writing
  formats: business emails, memos, short reports and formal letters.
- Explain how **cultural context** shapes the way English is used, including high-context and
  low-context communication, directness, politeness and face-saving strategies.
- Analyse a described **cross-cultural communication breakdown** and identify what went wrong.
- Adapt the **style**, **register** and **content** of a message for a different cultural or
  professional audience.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 to 3. In particular, this unit assumes you can write clear, grammatically correct
sentences (Unit 1), read a text for purpose, audience and context (Unit 2), and apply the
principles of effective communication (Unit 3).

## In this unit

1. [Topic 4.1 - Professional writing in practice](./topic-01) - business emails, memos, short
   reports and formal letters; the writing process from draft to final version.
2. [Topic 4.2 - Intercultural communication](./topic-02) - high-context and low-context
   communication; directness, politeness and face-saving strategies.
3. [Topic 4.3 - Adapting communication for your context](./topic-03) - adapting style, register
   and content for diverse cultural and social contexts.

## How to use this unit

The three topics move from the page to the person to the situation. Topic 4.1 gives you the
formats you will actually write. Topic 4.2 gives you the cultural knowledge to understand why
the same words land differently in different settings. Topic 4.3 asks you to combine both in
practice. Work through them in order.
