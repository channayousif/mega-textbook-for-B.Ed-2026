# Topic 1.3 - Sounds of English

## A real classroom situation

<Figure id="fig-U1-3" src="/img/figures/geng-300/unit-01/fig-U1-3.svg" alt="Diagram showing the English sound system: vowels, diphthongs and consonants, with sounds that do not exist in Urdu highlighted." width={780} height={470} />

Miss Hina teaches Class 6 English in a government school in Islamabad. She asks her pupils to
pronounce "think". Several pupils say "tink". She asks them to pronounce "this". Several say
"dis". She asks them to pronounce "vet". Several say "wet". This topic is about why these
errors happen and what to do about them.

## Explanation

English has approximately 44 sounds (phonemes) but only 26 letters. This means that the
relationship between spelling and sound is not one-to-one. For Urdu speakers, the challenge is
greater because English has sounds that do not exist in Urdu, and Urdu has sounds that do not
exist in English.

### The English sound system

<Figure id="fig-U1-6" src="/img/figures/geng-300/unit-01/fig-U1-6.svg" alt="Table comparing common pronunciation challenges for Urdu speakers with the Urdu-influenced pronunciation, the correct English sound and a practice strategy." width={780} height={470} />

The figure above maps the main categories of English sounds and the challenges they pose for
Urdu speakers.

**Vowels.** English has twelve vowel sounds (monophthongs) and eight diphthongs (two vowel
sounds blended together in one syllable). Urdu has far fewer vowel sounds, which is why
distinctions like "ship/sheep", "full/fool" and "cat/cut" are difficult. The figure above
maps the most problematic distinctions.

**Consonants.** English has 24 consonant sounds. Several do not exist in Urdu: the "th" sounds
in "think" and "this", the "v" sound in "vet", the "w" sound in "wet", and the "zh" sound in
"measure". Urdu speakers often substitute the closest Urdu sound: "t" for "th", "f" for "v",
"w" for "v" (sometimes in the wrong direction).

**Stress.** In English, every word of more than one syllable has one syllable that is stressed
(louder, longer, higher in pitch). "REcord" (noun) and "reCORD" (verb) are distinguished only
by stress. Urdu stress patterns are different, which can make Pakistani English sound flat to
a native speaker.

**Intonation** is the rise and fall of the voice across a phrase or sentence. English uses
intonation to distinguish statements from questions, to show emphasis, and to signal attitude.
Urdu intonation patterns are different, and direct transfer can produce speech that sounds
uninterested or rude when no rudeness is intended.

### Common challenges for Urdu speakers

The figure above maps the most common pronunciation challenges. The most frequent are:

- **"th" sounds** (think, this, bath, bathe): Urdu has no equivalent; speakers substitute "t/d"
  or "s/z".
- **"v" and "w" distinction** (vet/wet, very/well): Urdu has a sound between the two; speakers
  confuse them.
- **"p" and "f" distinction** (pin/fin, pat/fat): Some Urdu dialects merge these sounds.
- **Final consonant clusters** (desks, texts, films): Urdu does not allow clusters at the end of
  syllables; speakers insert vowels ("deskes").
- **Vowel length** (ship/sheep, full/fool): Urdu does not distinguish these pairs.

**Common misconception.** "Pronunciation does not matter as long as people understand you." It
does matter. Poor pronunciation can cause misunderstanding, and in professional contexts it can
affect how competent you sound. The goal is not a British or American accent but clarity.

## Activity: Minimal pairs practice

Work in pairs. Take the following minimal pairs (pairs of words that differ by one sound). One
partner says one word from a pair; the other identifies which word was said. Then switch.

| Pair 1 | Pair 2 | Pair 3 | Pair 4 | Pair 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ship / sheep | vet / wet | think / sink | full / fool | pin / fin |
| bat / bet | this / dis | bad / bed | cat / cut | fan / van |

Allow 15 minutes. Then discuss: which pairs were hardest to distinguish? What strategy helped?

## Check your understanding

1. Explain why English has more sounds than letters, and give one example. *(Understand)*
2. A pupil says "tink" for "think". Identify the sound that is causing the difficulty and
   explain why it is difficult for Urdu speakers. *(Analyze)*
3. Explain the difference between stress and intonation, and give one example of each.
   *(Remember)*
4. Write three minimal pairs that would help a Urdu speaker practise the "v/w" distinction.
   *(Apply)*
5. A friend says, "I just speak loudly and they will understand." Using this topic's account,
   explain why volume is not the same as clarity. *(Evaluate)*

## Summary

English has approximately 44 phonemes, several of which do not exist in Urdu. The most common
challenges for Urdu speakers are the "th" sounds, the "v/w" distinction, final consonant
clusters and vowel length. Stress and intonation are as important as individual sounds. The
goal is clarity, not a native-speaker accent.

pronunciation features that might cause difficulty for pupils: sounds that do not exist in

each feature.
