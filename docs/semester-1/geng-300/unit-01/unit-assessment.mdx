# Unit 1 assessment

## Unit 1 summary

A strong vocabulary is built through contextual usage, not memorised from lists. Synonyms, antonyms
and idiomatic expressions add precision. Word formation - affixation, compounding, clipping and back
formation - is the system that connects words and lets you decode unfamiliar vocabulary.

Communicative grammar makes meaning clear: subject-verb agreement, consistent verb tenses,
complete sentences, correct articles and properly placed modifiers. The four sentence structures -
simple, compound, complex and compound-complex - give a writer the tools to vary pace and emphasis.

Editing is the process that brings vocabulary, grammar and structure together: read, identify,
correct, re-read aloud, repeat. Pronunciation supports writing accuracy because a pupil who hears
English correctly writes English correctly.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Learning a word through contextual usage means: *(Remember)*
   a) memorising its dictionary definition
   b) seeing it in a sentence, understanding its nuance and using it in a new sentence
   c) writing it out twenty times
   d) translating it into Urdu

2. "Happy", "joyful" and "delighted" are: *(Remember)*
   a) antonyms
   b) idioms
   c) synonyms
   d) compounds

3. The word "sunflower" is formed by which process? *(Remember)*
   a) Affixation
   b) Clipping
   c) Compounding
   d) Back formation

4. In the sentence "The team is playing well", the verb "is" agrees with the subject "team" because:
   *(Understand)*
   a) "team" is always plural
   b) the team is acting as one unit, so the singular verb is used
   c) "playing" requires "is"
   d) all sports teams take singular verbs

5. Which sentence is a complex sentence? *(Understand)*
   a) The teacher explained the lesson and the pupils took notes.
   b) When the teacher explained the lesson, the pupils took notes.
   c) The teacher explained the lesson.
   d) The teacher explained the lesson; the pupils took notes.

6. A pupil writes, "Running down the street." This is: *(Apply)*
   a) a simple sentence
   b) a compound sentence
   c) a fragment
   d) a run-on

7. An editor checking a text for word-level accuracy is checking for: *(Remember)*
   a) subject-verb agreement only
   b) whether each word is the right word, correctly spelled and in the right form
   c) whether the paragraphs are in the right order
   d) whether the sentences are all the same length

8. A pupil writes, "She have finished her homework." The error is: *(Apply)*
   a) tense shift
   b) subject-verb agreement
   c) misplaced modifier
   d) wrong article

9. Reading a text aloud during editing is useful because: *(Understand)*
   a) it makes the text shorter
   b) the ear hears errors that the eye skips when reading silently
   c) it helps with pronunciation only
   d) it is faster than reading silently

10. A paragraph made up entirely of simple sentences is likely to feel: *(Analyze)*
    a) sophisticated and mature
    b) heavy and difficult to follow
    c) flat and choppy
    d) formal and academic

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. State the difference between a synonym and an antonym, and give one example of each.
   *(Remember)*

2. A pupil writes, "The news are very bad today." Explain the error and correct it.
   *(Understand)*

3. Name the four word-formation processes and give one example of each. *(Remember)*

4. Identify the sentence structure (simple, compound, complex or compound-complex) in each of the
   following: "Although it rained, we went out and we enjoyed ourselves." *(Analyze)*

5. Explain why a misplaced modifier confuses the reader, and correct the following: "She almost
   drove the car for six hours." *(Analyze)*

6. A teacher says, "I teach grammar rules and my pupils still make errors." Using Topic 1.2,
   explain why rule-teaching alone may not be enough. *(Analyze)*

7. Describe the five-step editing process from Topic 1.4, in order. *(Remember)*

8. A pupil writes an essay using only simple sentences. Using Topic 1.3, explain what the essay
   loses and how you would advise the pupil. *(Analyze)*

9. Explain the difference between a compound sentence and a complex sentence, with one example of
   each. *(Understand)*

10. A friend says, "Vocabulary means memorising long word lists." Using Topic 1.1, explain what
    that claim overlooks. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A teacher asks pupils to learn 20 words over the weekend for a Monday test. Using Topic 1.1,
   analyse why this approach may not help pupils remember the words long-term, and propose one
   specific alternative. *(Analyze)*

2. A pupil writes the following paragraph. Edit it for accuracy at word, grammar and structure
   level, and explain each change you make.

   > "Me and my friend goes to the market yesterday. We buys some fruits. The shopkeeper was very
   > friendly he gives us a discount. We was happy because we get good fruits cheap." *(Apply)*

3. Using Topics 1.2 and 1.3, explain why variety in sentence structure matters in a piece of
   writing, and argue what a teacher should aim for when teaching sentence structure to
   lower-secondary pupils. *(Analyze)*

4. Evaluate the claim that "a long sentence is a good sentence." Use the four sentence structures
   from Topic 1.3 and the editing principles from Topic 1.4 in your answer. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative.** Write a paragraph of 100-120 words describing a memorable school event. Then
   edit your paragraph using the five-step process from Topic 1.4, showing the before and after
   versions and explaining the changes you made at each level (word, grammar, structure). *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - contextual usage means seeing a word in a sentence, understanding its nuance and using
   it in a new sentence.
2. **c** - synonyms are words with similar meanings.
3. **c** - "sunflower" is a compound of "sun" and "flower".
4. **b** - collective nouns acting as one unit take singular verbs.
5. **b** - a complex sentence has one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses;
   "When..." introduces a dependent clause.
6. **c** - "Running down the street" has no subject and no main verb; it is a fragment.
7. **b** - word-level editing checks whether each word is right, correctly spelled and in the right
   form.
8. **b** - "She have" should be "She has"; the error is subject-verb agreement.
9. **b** - reading aloud catches errors that silent reading skips.
10. **c** - a paragraph of only simple sentences feels flat and choppy.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** A synonym is a word with a similar meaning (1); an antonym is a word with an opposite
meaning (1); the synonym example is genuine (1); the antonym example is genuine (1).

**2. (4 marks)** Explains that "news" is an uncountable noun treated as singular (1); identifies that
"are" should be "is" (1); the correction is accurate (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**3. (4 marks)** Names all four processes: affixation, compounding, clipping, back formation (1 each).

**4. (4 marks)** Identifies the structure as compound-complex (1); explains that "Although it rained"
is a dependent clause (1); "we went out" and "we enjoyed ourselves" are independent clauses (1); the
reasoning is coherent (1).

**5. (5 marks)** Explains that a misplaced modifier creates ambiguity about what "almost" modifies
(1); identifies that "almost" modifies "drove" in the original, not "six hours" (1); corrects to
"She drove the car for almost six hours" (1); the correction removes the ambiguity (1); the
reasoning is coherent (1).

**6. (5 marks)** Explains that rule-teaching without context produces rules that are memorised and
forgotten (1); links this to Topic 1.2's account of grammar as a meaning-making system (1); argues
that pupils need to apply rules in their own writing (1); the explanation is grounded in the topic
(1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**7. (5 marks)** Names the five steps in order: read, identify, correct, re-read aloud, repeat (1
each).

**8. (5 marks)** Explains that a paragraph of only simple sentences feels flat and choppy (1); links
this to Topic 1.3's account of variety (1); advises the pupil to mix simple, compound and complex
sentences (1); the advice is specific and grounded in the topic (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**9. (4 marks)** A compound sentence joins two independent clauses with a coordinating conjunction
(1); a complex sentence has one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses (1); the
compound example is genuine (1); the complex example is genuine (1).

**10. (5 marks)** Explains that vocabulary building is a deliberate process grounded in context (1);
identifies that memorising lists produces temporary learning (1); argues that contextual usage,
synonyms/antonyms and word formation produce lasting learning (1); the explanation draws on
Topic 1.1 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 on the four criteria below.

**ERQ 1 - Memorising vocabulary vs contextual learning**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of contextual usage | Names the distinction vaguely | Distinguishes memorisation from contextual usage | Shows what each makes possible in a classroom |
| Analysis of the memorisation approach | Asserts it is wrong | Explains what it overlooks | Takes the approach at its strongest, then shows what it leaves out |
| **Proposed alternative (Analyze)** | **Asserts an alternative** | **Proposes an alternative with a reason** | **Proposes an alternative grounded in Topic 1.1 and explains why it would work better** |
| Register and clarity | Generic classroom | Refers to the described pupils | The evidence is drawn from specific details in the description |

**ERQ 2 - Editing a paragraph**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage of the three levels | Fewer than two levels addressed | Two levels addressed | All three levels addressed with evidence |
| Accuracy of the corrections | Corrections are incomplete or introduce new errors | Most corrections are correct | All corrections are correct and explained |
| **Explanation of changes (Apply)** | **Asserts the changes are correct** | **Links each change to an error type** | **Links each change to an error type and explains why the original was confusing** |
| Register and clarity | Generic | Refers to the described text | The evidence is drawn from specific details in the text |

**ERQ 3 - Variety in sentence structure**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the four structures | Names the structures vaguely | Lists the structures | Shows what each contributes to a paragraph |
| Analysis of why variety matters | Asserts variety is good | Explains what is lost without variety | Explains what is lost and what is gained with variety |
| **Argument for the teacher's aim (Analyze)** | **Asserts an aim** | **Argues for an aim with a reason** | **Argues for an aim using specific features of Topics 1.2 and 1.3** |
| Register and clarity | Generic classroom | Refers to the described pupils | The evidence is drawn from specific details |

**ERQ 4 - "A long sentence is a good sentence"**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the four structures | Treats length as the only variable | Distinguishes length from structure | Shows what each structure is for |
| Use of editing principles | Names the editing process without linking it to the claim | Links editing to the claim | Uses the editing process to show what "good" means |
| **Evaluative reasoning (Evaluate)** | **Asserts a verdict** | **Argues a verdict with reasons** | **Takes the claim at its strongest, then shows what it leaves out using the full evidence** |
| Quality of example | Generic | A described sentence | The example genuinely illustrates the difference |

**ERQ 5 - Integrative: write and edit**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality of the original paragraph | Fewer than 8 sentences or no errors to find | 8+ sentences with some errors | 8+ sentences with errors at all three levels |
| Quality of the editing | Fewer than three changes made | Three changes made, some explained | Five or more changes made, all explained by level |
| **Before-and-after presentation (Create)** | **Shows only the final version** | **Shows before and after** | **Shows before and after with a clear explanation of each change at each level** |
