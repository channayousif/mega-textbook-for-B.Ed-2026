# Unit 1 assessment

## Unit summary

Vocabulary in context means understanding register, collocation, connotation and denotation, not
just memorising word lists. Communicative grammar means understanding how grammar creates
meaning in real communication: subject-verb agreement, verb tenses, fragments, run-ons,
modifiers and articles. Word-formation processes (affixation, compounding, clipping, back
formation) explain how English builds new words. Sentence structures (simple, compound, complex,
compound-complex) explain how clauses combine. The English sound system has approximately 44
phonemes, several of which do not exist in Urdu, and the most common challenges for Urdu speakers
involve the "th" sounds, the "v/w" distinction, final consonant clusters and vowel length.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit's account, "register" refers to: *(Remember)*
   a) a list of vocabulary words
   b) the level of formality a situation demands
   c) the pronunciation of a word
   d) the spelling of a word

2. A teacher says, "The pupils in Class 7 reads quietly." This is an example of: *(Understand)*
   a) a fragment
   b) a run-on
   c) a subject-verb agreement error
   d) a modifier error

3. Which word-formation process creates "exam" from "examination"? *(Remember)*
   a) Affixation
   b) Compounding
   c) Clipping
   d) Back formation

4. A trainee says, "I am having two brothers." The most likely source of this error is:
   *(Apply)*
   a) L1 transfer from Urdu, which uses the present continuous for possession
   b) overgeneralisation of the present continuous
   c) confusion between "have" and "has"
   d) a spelling error

5. How many phonemes does English have, approximately? *(Remember)*
   a) 26
   b) 36
   c) 44
   d) 52

6. A pupil writes, "Walking to school, the rain started suddenly." The error is:
   *(Understand)*
   a) a run-on sentence
   b) a dangling modifier
   c) a subject-verb agreement error
   d) a fragment

7. A pupil pronounces "think" as "tink". The sound causing difficulty is: *(Apply)*
   a) /s/
   b) /t/
   c) /θ/
   d) /f/

8. Which sentence is a compound-complex sentence? *(Apply)*
   a) The pupils read their books.
   b) The pupils read their books and the teacher wrote on the board.
   c) When the bell rang, the pupils packed their bags.
   d) When the bell rang, the pupils packed their bags and the teacher left the room.

9. "Edit" was formed from "editor" by removing what looks like a suffix. This is an example of:
   *(Remember)*
   a) affixation
   b) compounding
   c) clipping
   d) back formation

10. The difference between "ship" and "sheep" is primarily a difference of: *(Understand)*
    a) stress
    b) intonation
    c) vowel length
    d) consonant voicing

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain the difference between register and connotation, and give one example of each.
   *(Remember)*

2. A pupil writes, "The group of students was arguing among themselves." Explain why this
   sentence is grammatically difficult and identify the rule that applies. *(Analyze)*

3. Name the four word-formation processes and give one example of each. *(Remember)*

4. Classify the following sentence as simple, compound, complex or compound-complex, and explain
   your reasoning: "After the meeting ended, the teachers went to the staffroom and discussed the
   new timetable." *(Apply)*

5. A pupil pronounces "vet" as "wet" and "wet" as "vet". Identify the sounds involved and explain
   why they are difficult for Urdu speakers. *(Analyze)*

6. Explain the difference between stress and intonation, and give one example of each.
   *(Remember)*

7. A teacher says, "I have been knowing him for ten years." Identify the error and explain why
   the verb "know" behaves differently from other verbs in this context. *(Analyze)*

8. Rewrite the following sentence so the modifier is not dangling: "Running to school, the rain
   started suddenly." *(Apply)*

9. Explain why English has more sounds than letters, and describe two challenges this creates for
   Urdu speakers. *(Understand)*

10. A friend says, "Grammar rules are fixed and universal." Using this unit's account, explain
    what that claim overlooks. *(Evaluate)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A pupil writes: "I am having two brothers and one sister. My father is doing job in a bank.
   We are living in Lahore since five years." Using this unit's account of communicative grammar,
   identify four grammatical issues, explain why each is an error, and rewrite the sentences so
   they are natural and correct. *(Analyze)*

2. A pupil pronounces "ship" and "sheep" the same way, says "tink" for "think", and says "wet"
   for "vet". Using this unit's account of the English sound system, explain why these errors
   happen, identify the specific sounds involved, and propose three specific strategies the pupil
   could use to improve. *(Analyze)*

3. Take a short paragraph from an English textbook used in a Pakistani school. Analyse the
   paragraph for: (a) five words formed by affixation, compounding, clipping or back formation,
   and (b) five sentences classified by structure. Write 400 to 450 words. *(Analyze)*

4. Evaluate the claim that "pronunciation does not matter as long as people understand you." Use
   this unit's account of the English sound system, register and connotation to argue for or
   against the claim. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative.** You are placed in a government primary school. The Class 5 English teacher
   asks you to help pupils who consistently make these errors: "I am having a book", "She go to
   school yesterday", and "tink" for "think". Design a 30-minute lesson that addresses all three
   errors, using this unit's account of vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation. Justify each
   choice. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - register is the level of formality a situation demands.
2. **c** - "pupils" is plural but "reads" is singular; this is a subject-verb agreement error.
3. **c** - "exam" is a clipping of "examination".
4. **a** - Urdu uses a continuous construction for possession; L1 transfer is the likely source.
5. **c** - English has approximately 44 phonemes.
6. **b** - the modifier "walking to school" has no clear subject; it is dangling.
7. **c** - the /θ/ sound (voiceless dental fricative) does not exist in Urdu.
8. **d** - two independent clauses ("the pupils packed their bags", "the teacher left the room")
   and one dependent clause ("when the bell rang").
9. **d** - "edit" was formed by removing "-or" from "editor"; this is back formation.
10. **c** - "ship" (/ɪ/) and "sheep" (/iː/) differ primarily in vowel length.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** Register is the level of formality a situation demands (1); connotation is the
feeling or association a word carries beyond its literal meaning (1); the register example is
genuinely about formality level (1); the connotation example is genuinely about association (1).

**2. (5 marks)** Identifies the difficulty: "group" is singular but "themselves" is plural (1);
explains the rule: collective nouns take singular verbs when the group acts as a unit (1); notes
that British English sometimes accepts plural verbs with collective nouns (1); the reasoning draws
on Topic 1.1 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**3. (4 marks)** Names all four processes: affixation, compounding, clipping, back formation (1
each). One mark per example that correctly illustrates the process (up to 4).

**4. (4 marks)** Classifies the sentence as compound-complex (1); identifies two independent
clauses: "the teachers went to the staffroom" and "discussed the new timetable" (1); identifies
the dependent clause: "after the meeting ended" (1); the explanation is coherent (1).

**5. (5 marks)** Identifies the sounds: /v/ and /w/ (1); explains that Urdu has a sound between
the two (1); links this to the confusion (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 1.3 (1); the reasoning
is coherent (1).

**6. (4 marks)** Stress is the relative emphasis on a syllable within a word (1); intonation is
the rise and fall of the voice across a phrase or sentence (1); the stress example is genuinely
about syllable emphasis (1); the intonation example is genuinely about voice movement (1).

**7. (5 marks)** Identifies the error: "know" is a stative verb and is not normally used in the
continuous form (1); explains that stative verbs describe states, not actions (1); contrasts with
dynamic verbs that can be used in the continuous (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 1.1 (1); the
reasoning is coherent (1).

**8. (3 marks)** The rewritten sentence has a clear subject for "running" (1); the modifier is
correctly placed (1); the original meaning is preserved (1).

**9. (4 marks)** English has approximately 44 phonemes but only 26 letters (1); the relationship
between spelling and sound is not one-to-one (1); names two challenges (e.g. "th" sounds, vowel
length, final clusters) (1 each up to 2).

**10. (5 marks)** Takes the claim at its strongest (1); explains that grammar describes actual
usage, not fixed rules (1); notes that usage varies by region, register and context (1); argues a
verdict with reasons (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).
