# Effective Communication

Unit 1 gave you the building blocks of English, and Unit 2 taught you to read and listen with
purpose. This unit asks you to put those skills to work: to speak, present and write in ways
that are clear, inclusive and suited to your audience. Communication is not just about what you
say, but how you say it, to whom, and in what situation.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain the **principles** that make communication effective (clarity, coherence, conciseness,
  correctness, courtesy and consideration) and apply them to spoken and written texts.
- **Structure** documents (emails, reports and letters) with a clear introduction, body and
  conclusion.
- Use **inclusive** language that avoids stereotypes and respects cross-cultural differences.
- Manage the demands of **public speaking**, including stage fright, voice modulation and body
  language.
- Plan and deliver a short **presentation** with visual aids, and handle questions confidently.
- Apply the conventions of **informal communication**, including small talk, networking and
  turn-taking.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 and 2. This unit assumes you can identify sentence structures, recognise purpose and
audience in a text, and apply the vocabulary and grammar foundations from Unit 1.

## How to use this unit

The three topics move from principle to practice. Topic 3.1 sets out the principles that govern
all effective communication. Topic 3.2 applies those principles to speaking, with attention to
inclusivity and confidence. Topic 3.3 asks you to combine everything in presentations and
informal settings. Work through them in order across the four weeks the content-spec allows.
