# Unit 4 teacher notes

## Before you teach: obtain the primary document

This unit references Pakistan's *National Professional Standards for Teachers* (2009) throughout
and does not reproduce it, following the course's citation policy. **Obtain a copy before Week 9.**

Topic 4.2's practicum task and the indicator-writing activity both assume trainees can read at
least one standard in full. If you cannot supply copies, the unit still teaches the architecture,
but the activities become hypothetical and lose most of their value.

The unit's own source record notes a verification limitation worth passing on: the document was
not retrievable from the authoring environment, and the names of the ten standards used in Topic
4.2 were corroborated from secondary sources rather than read from the primary text. **Check them
against your copy.** If any differ, that is a correction to make in the unit, not an error to
teach around.

## Sequencing across the three-week block

Four topics over three weeks: 4.1 in Week 9, 4.2 in Week 10, and 4.3 with 4.4 in Week 11.

Give 4.1 a full week even though it is conceptually the lightest. The four uses of a standard are
the analytical tool the rest of the unit runs on, and a cohort that has not internalised them
reads 4.4 as an opinion about bureaucracy rather than as a design argument.

Topics 4.3 and 4.4 belong together in the same week. Read separately, 4.3 sounds like an
exhortation to be honest and 4.4 like a complaint about administration. Read together, they are
one argument: honest self-evaluation is professionally valuable, and it only survives if the
system is built so that honesty is not punished.

## The misconceptions that recur

**"Standards and a code of conduct are the same thing."** Near-universal, because both are
official documents about teachers. The Topic 4.1 comparison table exists for this. Press on the
question each answers rather than on what each contains, since the contents do overlap.

**"A standard tells you how to teach."** Correct this early or trainees will read the NPST as a
prescribed method and resent it. The space a standard deliberately leaves is where Unit 3's
professional judgement operates.

**"Meeting the indicators is the goal."** The most consequential of the four, because acting on
it produces exactly the behaviour the framework was built to prevent. The indicator-gaming
activity in Topic 4.2 is the intervention; do not shorten it.

**"Appraisal and professional development are the same process."** Trainees have usually only met
systems where they are combined. Ask how many have ever told an appraiser something they were bad
at. The answer in the room makes the argument better than the text does.

## The activity that most often goes wrong

The Topic 4.2 indicator-writing activity depends on pairs genuinely trying to defeat each other's
indicators. Groups that are polite to one another produce indicators nobody attacked, and the
lesson does not land.

Set it up as adversarial and explicitly reward the pair that finds the cleverest way to satisfy an
indicator without the competence. Trainees are often reluctant, because it feels like teaching
dishonesty. Name that objection directly: you are teaching them to anticipate how a measure will
be gamed, which is exactly what whoever designs their appraisal should have done and frequently
has not.

## Practical work

**Read one standard in full (Week 10).** The Topic 4.2 practicum task. The reliable finding is
that the dispositions part is nearly impossible to evidence by observation, which sets up Topic
4.3's material on evidence types. Collect a show of hands on that question rather than the written
work; it is faster and the pattern is what matters.

**One full self-evaluation cycle (Week 11).** The Topic 4.3 task. Three lessons will usually be
too few for the re-collection stage to show change, and trainees should report that as a finding
rather than manufacture a result. Say so in advance, or you will receive five reports of
successful improvement in a fortnight.

Ask one question in the debrief and give it time: **were you tempted to improve the record rather
than the practice?** An honest cohort will say yes. That admission is the unit's most important
learning moment and does not survive being rushed.

**Appraisal enquiry (Week 11).** The Topic 4.4 task asks what really happens in the school's
appraisal. Prepare for the common finding of a process that is formally summative and decides
nothing. The unit does not cover that case, and it is worth ten minutes of discussion: an
appraisal with no consequence is not neutral, because it teaches that the standards are
decoration.

## Marking the unit assessment

The bank is on the unit assessment page with model answers and analytic rubrics.

ERQ 2 is the integrative item and the best single indicator of whether the unit landed, because
it requires the three-part internal division to be applied to specific described behaviours
rather than recited.

The rubrics cap a response that does not reach "Adequate" on the analysis, evaluation or creation
criterion at 10 out of 20. In this unit the characteristic strong-looking weak answer is a fluent
summary of what standards are for that never applies them to anything.
