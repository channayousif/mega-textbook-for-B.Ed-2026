# Unit 4 assessment

## Unit summary

A professional standard is a written, shared, evidence-specific statement of what competent
practice looks like. It differs from a code of conduct, which governs behaviour, and from a
curriculum, which governs content. It does not prescribe method. Standards serve four uses -
initial training, continuing development, appraisal and public accountability - and those uses
pull against one another, which is the source of most complaints that standards are irrelevant
bureaucracy.

Pakistan's National Professional Standards for Teachers, issued in 2009 by the Ministry of
Education with UNESCO and USAID support, are the framework this country's teacher education is
built to. They are set out in three levels: ten standards, each divided into knowledge
and understanding, dispositions, and performance and skills, with indicators stating observably
what meeting a part looks like. The three-part division separates knowing, valuing and being able
to do. Good indicators are observable, specific and achievable, and there is a standing trade-off
in which the easiest indicators to check are the most likely to measure a producible proxy.

Self-evaluation is a five-station cycle run on one indicator at a time: choose, collect, judge,
change, re-collect. It breaks by taking on too much and by improving the record rather than the
practice. Kinds of evidence are not equivalent, and triangulating two that fail differently beats
collecting one thoroughly.

Certification confirms preparation, licensing confirms current permission to practise, and
appraisal is the recurring judgement of serving practice. Appraisal serves formative and
summative purposes whose requirements pull against each other: candour against
comparability, a trusted appraiser against a distant one, weakness against strength. A combined
instrument will be treated as summative and the formative value lost. The remedy is separate,
deliberately connected processes rather than abandoning accountability.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. A professional standard answers which question? *(Understand)*
   - a) How should I behave?
   - b) What does competent practice look like, in a checkable form?
   - c) What should pupils learn?
   - d) Which teaching method is correct?

2. "Be a good teacher" fails as a standard chiefly because it is not: *(Remember)*
   - a) written down
   - b) shared across schools
   - c) approved by a ministry
   - d) specific enough to be evidenced

3. A framework built for continuing development is used to decide promotions. This is best
   described as: *(Apply)*
   - a) applying a standard for a use it was not designed for
   - b) efficient reuse of an existing instrument
   - c) a breach of the code of conduct
   - d) summative appraisal working as intended

4. Pakistan's National Professional Standards for Teachers were issued in: *(Remember)*
   - a) 1999 by the Higher Education Commission
   - b) 2015 by NACTE
   - c) 2009 by the Ministry of Education
   - d) 2009 by UNESCO alone

5. Each of Pakistan's standards is internally divided into: *(Remember)*
   - a) aims, methods and outcomes
   - b) knowledge and understanding, dispositions, and performance and skills
   - c) novice, proficient and expert levels
   - d) primary, elementary and secondary bands

6. Which is the strongest indicator for a learning-environment standard? *(Apply)*
   - a) Maintains a positive classroom climate
   - b) Displays pupils' work on the classroom walls
   - c) Keeps an up-to-date seating plan
   - d) Pupils volunteer answers, including incorrect ones, without prompting

7. In the self-evaluation cycle, evidence is collected before the judgement is formed because:
   *(Understand)*
   - a) it is quicker
   - b) the appraiser requires it in that order
   - c) a judgement formed first selects the evidence that confirms it
   - d) evidence is harder to collect afterwards

8. Which evidence type is easiest to produce without the underlying competence? *(Understand)*
   - a) Planning documents
   - b) Pupil voice
   - c) Pupil work
   - d) Observation by a colleague

9. Licensing differs from certification chiefly in that licensing asks: *(Understand)*
   - a) was this person prepared?
   - b) is this person currently fit to practise?
   - c) how is this person doing this year?
   - d) which subject may this person teach?

10. A single appraisal document serving both formative and summative purposes will in practice be
    treated by teachers as: *(Understand)*
    - a) formative, because development matters more
    - b) neither, because it will be ignored
    - c) summative, because that is the version with consequences
    - d) both equally

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Certification, licensing and periodic appraisal all apply the same standards at different
   career points. State what question each one asks, and explain why a teacher can hold a
   valid certificate and still fail a licensing requirement. *(Understand)*

2. Distinguish a professional standard, a code of conduct and a curriculum by the question each
   answers and what each governs, and name one way national standards frameworks differ from one
   another. *(Understand)*

3. Name the four uses a standard is put to, state what each use requires of the standard, and
   explain why any two of those requirements pull against each other. *(Understand)*

4. Explain why a standard designed for development and one designed for accountability pull
   against each other, and what a system must decide in advance if it wants one document to
   serve both. *(Analyze)*

5. Describe the three-level architecture of Pakistan's National Professional Standards, and
   explain why the ten standards cannot all be evidenced in the same way. *(Understand)*

6. Explain what separating knowledge, dispositions and performance within a standard buys that a
   single judgement would not. *(Understand)*

7. Give three tests of a good indicator, and explain why an indicator that is easy to check is
   often worse. *(Analyze)*

8. Name the five stations of the self-evaluation cycle, identify the two places it commonly
   breaks, and explain for each break why it makes the cycle unable to show whether anything
   changed. *(Understand)*

9. For any three evidence types, state what each shows well, what it cannot show, and how easily
   it can be produced without the competence. *(Understand)*

10. Explain why a mentor who also writes a teacher's appraisal is less useful as a mentor, and
    what this costs a school. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A district must decide whether to confirm two probationary teachers, one well-liked with a
   quiet class and one difficult with adequate results. Argue whether introducing a written
   standard would improve that decision, addressing the objection that evidence requirements
   favour the compliant over the effective. *(Evaluate)*

2. **Integrative.** A teacher is described as follows: she plans thoroughly, marks late, knows
   her subject well, rarely changes her approach when pupils struggle, and has a warm but quiet
   classroom in which four pupils do all the answering. Map this practice to specific standards
   and to the knowledge, dispositions or performance part in each case, then argue what her
   appraisal should say and what her development priority should be. *(Analyze)*

3. Write an indicator for any standard that is observable, specific and achievable in an
   under-resourced government school. Then explain in detail how a teacher could satisfy it
   without the underlying competence, and revise the indicator to close that gap. *(Create)*

4. Nadia's self-evaluation was complete and useless; Kashif's looked worse and was not. Evaluate
   both, diagnose what in a school's handling produces Nadia's behaviour, and design arrangements
   that would produce more Kashifs while preserving accountability. *(Evaluate)*

5. A province decides that the annual developmental self-evaluation will now also determine
   promotions. Evaluate the reform, trace the mechanism by which it degrades the record, and
   propose an alternative that still delivers evidence-based promotion. Address the objection that
   two separate processes are unaffordable. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - a standard governs practice. "How should I behave?" is the question a code of
   conduct answers, and "What should pupils learn?" is the curriculum's.
2. **d** - it fails the evidence-specificity test; it could be written and shared and still be
   unusable.
3. **a** - the four uses make this diagnosis available, and it is the province's error in Topic
   4.4.
4. **c** - Ministry of Education, 2009, with UNESCO and USAID support.
5. **b** - knowledge and understanding; dispositions; performance and skills.
6. **d** - observable, specific, and genuinely hard to produce without the competence. Displaying
   pupils' work on the walls is the proxy trap: it is easy to arrange and shows nothing about
   whether the climate is safe to be wrong in.
7. **c** - order protects against confirmation, which is why Kashif counted before concluding.
8. **a** - planning documents evidence an afternoon's writing, not a term's teaching.
9. **b** - licensing is about current permission and can be renewable; certification is about
   preparation.
10. **c** - rational response to where the consequences sit, not a failure of maturity.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** Certification asks whether this person was prepared (1); licensing asks whether
they are currently fit to practise (1); periodic appraisal asks how they are doing this year (1);
explains that certification is a judgement about past preparation and does not expire, while
licensing is a judgement about present fitness and can lapse or be withdrawn, so the two can
disagree about the same teacher (1).

**2. (7 marks)** Two marks per instrument: the question it answers and what it governs. Standard:
what competent practice looks like, governs practice. Code: how to behave, governs conduct.
Curriculum: what pupils learn, governs content. One further mark for naming a real way national
frameworks differ, for example in scope, in granularity, or in whether they carry licensing
consequences; accept any difference the unit's topic-01 supports.

**3. (8 marks)** One mark per use named (4); one mark per apt requirement (3); one mark for naming
a real tension between two requirements, for example that development needs a standard safe to
admit failure against while accountability needs one precise enough to carry a consequence (1).
Initial training
needs alignment with preparation; development needs generosity and safety; appraisal needs
precision and evidence; accountability needs a public claim.

**4. (4 marks)** Development needs candour and appraisal penalises it (1); development is best
with a trusted appraiser, accountability needs distance (1); development targets weakness,
accountability invites strength (1); concludes that a system serving both must settle in advance
which purpose each conversation carries, because otherwise the summative purpose wins by default
(1). Do not award an answer whose conclusion is that one document cannot hold both: topic-04
teaches, from Isore's own wording, that the purposes are conflicting but not incompatible.

**5. (5 marks)** Ten standards, each a broad area of professional competence (1); each divided
into knowledge and understanding, dispositions, and performance and skills (2); indicators beneath
the parts, stating observably what meeting a part looks like (1); explains that the ten mix
knowledge areas, practices, a values area and a subject-specific area, so they are not parallel
and cannot all be evidenced the same way (1).

**6. (4 marks)** Identifies three distinct shortfalls: not knowing, not valuing, not able (2);
explains that each has a different remedy (1); notes that a single judgement hides which applies
(1).

**7. (5 marks)** Observable, specific to the standard, achievable under real conditions (3); easy
indicators tend to measure proxies (1); proxies can be produced without the competence (1).

**8. (9 marks)** Choose one indicator, collect evidence, judge the gap, plan one change,
re-collect the same evidence (5); breaks by taking on too many indicators (1); breaks by changing
the evidence rather than the practice (1); explains that too many indicators leave each one
measured too thinly to attribute a change to anything (1); explains that changing the evidence
makes the comparison meaningless, because station five no longer measures what station two did
(1).

**9. (9 marks)** Three marks per evidence type: what it shows, what it cannot show, ease of
production without competence.

**10. (4 marks)** A teacher will not bring a real problem to someone who reports on them (1); the
mentoring relationship is the cheapest effective development mechanism a school has (1); its loss
is invisible because nothing visibly fails (1); the school retains the appearance of mentoring
without the function (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 across four criteria. A response not reaching "Adequate" on the
analysis, evaluation or creation criterion cannot exceed 10 overall.
