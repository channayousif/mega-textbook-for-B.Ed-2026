# Teaching Profession - course review

## Course summary

EFMP-302 asked one question from six directions: what does it mean to be a professional
teacher, and what does that ask of you? Five through-lines run across the six units.

- **Teaching is a profession.** It rests on a specialised knowledge base, formal training and
  certification, a shared code of conduct, and accountability to a professional community
  (Units 1, 3, 4). Pay and prestige are not the test; the four features are.
- **Professional practice is ethical practice.** Codes of conduct, moral reasoning models and
  reflective decision-making are not an addition to the job but part of it, and they hold
  wherever the work is done (Units 1, 2, 6).
- **Standards make the profession legible.** National and global professional standards turn
  competent practice into something a system can teach, evaluate and be held to, and they are
  most useful when you can use one on yourself (Unit 4).
- **The profession is under real pressure.** Workload, multi-grade and multilingual classrooms,
  accountability and the digital age make good teaching hard to sustain, and professionals
  respond by continuing to learn rather than by enduring in silence (Units 5, 6).
- **Teacher identity is developed, not fixed.** It is shaped by context, beliefs and reflection
  across a whole career, which is why the question *who am I becoming as a teacher?* belongs at
  the start of training and not only at the end (Units 1, 3, 6).

Read together, the units make a single argument. A profession's claim on public trust rests on
its members continuing to develop; the ethical obligation to teach competently implies keeping
competent; the components of effectiveness are not fixed; standards are a tool for
self-evaluation; and conditions are hard, which makes the question of what is within your
control urgent rather than academic. The six units end in the same place: a teacher who keeps
examining their own practice, with evidence, across a career.

## Practice questions

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of the following is the best test of whether an occupation is a profession?
   *(Remember)*
   - A) It is respectable and reasonably well paid.
   - B) It rests on specialised knowledge, extended training, a shared code of conduct, and
     accountability to a professional community.
   - C) It is performed mainly in an office or an institution.
   - D) It requires a uniform and fixed working hours.
2. Which statement best captures the difference between **professionalism in teaching** and
   **professionalisation of teaching**? *(Understand)*
   - A) They are two terms for the same collective process.
   - B) Professionalism in teaching is about how the field organises itself, while
     professionalisation is about how one teacher acts.
   - C) Professionalism in teaching is about how one teacher conducts themselves, while
     professionalisation is the slower collective process by which the field builds training,
     licensing, standards and associations.
   - D) Professionalism in teaching is a legal requirement, while professionalisation is a
     personal choice.
3. A teacher follows her plan exactly and keeps the class quiet, but stops mid-lesson when she
   notices most pupils adding denominators. She puts three wrong answers on the board and asks
   the class what rule would produce each. Her practice is best described as: *(Apply)*
   - A) industrial, because she follows a plan.
   - B) inquiry-based, because she notices how pupils think and adjusts on evidence.
   - C) neither, because inquiry-based teaching requires group work.
   - D) unprofessional, because she departed from her plan.
4. Which list names Rest's four components of moral behaviour? *(Remember)*
   - A) Empathy, Context, Reflect, Action.
   - B) Knowledge, dispositions, performance, skills.
   - C) Sensitivity, judgement, motivation, character.
   - D) Planning, teaching, assessing, reflecting.
5. Miss Rabia reads her Class 8 marks aloud in rank order. She breaks no rule and means well.
   Which statement best explains why her action is still a professional-ethics matter?
   *(Understand)*
   - A) The law requires teachers to protect pupils' examination results.
   - B) Personal morality is a private matter and cannot be taught.
   - C) Professional ethics occupy the space between personal morality and law, and a teacher
     does not trade a pupil's dignity for a motivational effect.
   - D) Reading marks aloud is forbidden by the school's service rules.
6. Mr Aslam copies forward last term's marks for six scripts he has not marked. Which domain of
   a code of ethical conduct does this breach most directly? *(Apply)*
   - A) Ethical conduct toward students.
   - B) Ethical conduct toward parents and the community.
   - C) Ethical conduct in practice and performance.
   - D) Ethical conduct toward colleagues and the profession.
7. Which of the following best states the three families of definitions of teacher effectiveness
   discussed in Unit 3? *(Remember)*
   - A) Achievement gain, observed practice, and breadth of learner outcomes.
   - B) Subject knowledge, pedagogical knowledge, and assessment skill.
   - C) Planning, teaching, and marking.
   - D) Attendance, results, and popularity.
8. Which of the following is **not** one of the seven personality traits the course guide names
   for effective teachers? *(Remember)*
   - A) Empathy.
   - B) Resilience.
   - C) Self-control.
   - D) Ambition.
9. A teacher's empathy becomes a liability when it leads her to excuse weak work and lower what
   she expects of a pupil. This is an example of: *(Apply)*
   - A) a fixed personality trait that cannot be changed.
   - B) a failure of subject knowledge.
   - C) a trait unbalanced by the others, where more of it is not better.
   - D) a trait that has become a different trait.
10. Each of Pakistan's National Professional Standards for Teachers is internally divided into
    which three parts? *(Remember)*
    - A) Planning; teaching; assessment.
    - B) Knowledge and understanding; dispositions; performance and skills.
    - C) Students; parents; colleagues.
    - D) Standards; domains; indicators.
11. A district official says a professional standard and a code of conduct are the same thing.
    Which reply is most accurate? *(Apply)*
    - A) A code of conduct answers "how should I behave?", while a professional standard answers
      "what does competent teaching look like, in a form that can be checked?".
    - B) They are the same, because both are written documents.
    - C) A curriculum answers "what should pupils learn?", while a professional standard answers
      "how should I behave?".
    - D) A code of conduct is national, while a standard is international.
12. Which list names the three dimensions of burnout? *(Remember)*
    - A) Exhaustion, cynicism or depersonalisation, and a reduced sense of personal
      accomplishment.
    - B) Stress, exhaustion, and cynicism.
    - C) Time pressure, low student motivation, and a dissonant value context.
    - D) Demands, resources, and autonomy.
13. A teacher decides she cannot change her school's class sizes, but she can change how she
    uses the first ten minutes of each lesson. Her decision is best described as: *(Apply)*
    - A) spending effort in the region she does not control.
    - B) treating resilience as the whole answer to a structural condition.
    - C) moving a challenge from the region she controls into the region she can only influence.
    - D) acting on the part of a challenge that is within her control while leaving the
      structural part unresolved.
14. Which list names the six principles that distinguish development which changes practice
    from development which does not? *(Remember)*
    - A) One-day, generic, off-site, individual, assigned, and certificate-led.
    - B) Sustained, embedded, content-focused, collaborative, feedback-supported, and
      teacher-chosen.
    - C) Attendance, satisfaction, certification, compliance, cost, and reach.
    - D) Preservice, new teacher, and experienced teacher.
15. A teacher writes a development plan with one gap, one goal, a chosen activity, the evidence
    that would show change, and a mid-year review date. Which of the four things that ordinarily
    destroy a plan does the single goal directly answer? *(Apply)*
    - A) Too many goals.
    - B) No time was made.
    - C) No evidence was decided in advance.
    - D) No review date.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In one or two sentences, define a **profession** and name its four distinguishing features.
   *(Understand)*
2. Explain, with a short example, why **accountability, autonomy, and collegiality** must be
   held in balance rather than maximised one at a time. *(Analyze)*
3. Explain the difference between **professional ethics**, **personal morality**, and **the
   law**, and give one teaching example that the law does not reach. *(Understand)*
4. A teacher knows a pupil is being bullied, plans to raise it at the staff meeting, and does
   not because the meeting runs late and the moment passes. Using Rest's model, identify which
   component failed and explain what would actually have helped. *(Analyze)*
5. Explain why a teacher can be judged effective on one definition of teacher effectiveness
   and ineffective on another, using the same evidence. *(Understand)*
6. A teacher asks "Does everyone understand?" and is satisfied when several hands go up.
   Explain why this is a poor comprehension check, and give a better alternative that discovers
   what pupils think. *(Analyze)*
7. Distinguish a **professional standard** from a **code of conduct** and from a **curriculum**
   by the question each answers. *(Understand)*
8. Distinguish **stress** from **burnout**, and name the three dimensions of burnout.
   *(Understand)*
9. Explain why offering **resilience** as the answer to structural conditions relocates a
   system's failure onto an individual teacher. *(Analyze)*
10. Mr Naeem has attended eleven workshops and can name their titles, but cannot say what he
    does differently because of any of them. Explain why the eleven certificates are not
    evidence of development, using what evaluation should reach beyond. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. You are given a one-page description of a teacher's classroom practice in a Sindh government
   school. Judge it against the **four dimensions of teacher professionalism** (Unit 1) and the
   **components of teacher effectiveness** (Unit 3), identify one strength and one weakness in
   each framework, and reach an overall verdict on whether this is professional, effective
   practice. Justify your verdict with specific details from the description. *(Evaluate)*
2. Miss Farah's pupil Ayesha submits an essay that is plainly not her own work, three weeks
   before the board examination. Work the case through **Rest's four-component model** and the
   **four-step Empathy, Context, Reflect, Action framework** (Unit 2), and use Unit 4's
   distinction between a **mark** and the **response** to it to reach and defend a decision.
   Name the pressure on Miss Farah that is not one of her obligations. *(Evaluate)*
3. Miss Shahnaz teaches Class 4, and her Tuesday includes eight periods, forty-three notebooks,
   a ninety-minute enrolment return, two parent conversations, and no preparation time. Using
   Unit 5's **demands-and-resources** idea and the **control-and-influence** distinction, design
   one response that is within her reach and defend it against the objection that it requires
   someone else to act. Then say which of Unit 6's six blocks a plan based on this response
   would need to state first. *(Create)*
4. Ms Sundas's school receives forty tablets with no budget for electricity, no training, and a
   drill application in English for a Class 5 that cannot yet decode. Apply Unit 5's three
   questions (what does it add, what does it cost, what does it replace that was working) and
   decide what she should do. Then explain why her obligations toward pupils (Unit 2) do not
   weaken because the photographs the district requests would be posted online. *(Evaluate)*
5. Build a **one-page professional development plan** for your first year of teaching. Use
   Unit 6's six blocks, Unit 4's self-evaluation cycle to choose and evidence one indicator,
   Unit 3's researcher role to decide in advance what would show the change worked, and Unit 5's
   demands-and-resources framing to say where the time comes from. Defend the plan against the
   conditions that ordinarily defeat one. *(Create)*

## Project ideas for your practicum school

Six briefs you can carry into placement. Each one asks for something you can actually do in a
real school, and each one brings something back that your coursework can use.

### 1. Profession audit

- **Purpose.** Test the four features of a profession against a real teacher's practice instead
  of against a definition.
- **Rough method.** Observe your cooperating teacher for a week. For each of the four features
  (specialised knowledge, extended training and a qualification, a code of conduct, and
  accountability to a professional community), note one concrete thing you saw or heard about.
- **Evidence to bring back.** A one-page annotated log with the four features as rows and one
  piece of evidence in each.

### 2. Ethics-in-action journal

- **Purpose.** Practise noticing the ethical dimension in ordinary decisions, not only in
  dramatic ones.
- **Rough method.** Record three real classroom or staff-room decisions with an ethical edge.
  Analyse each with the four-step Empathy, Context, Reflect, Action framework, and name which
  of Rest's four components each decision most depended on.
- **Evidence to bring back.** The journal, plus a short reflection on which component recurred.

### 3. Standards self-map

- **Purpose.** Learn to use a professional standard on yourself rather than only being measured
  by one.
- **Rough method.** Obtain the National Professional Standards for Teachers. Choose one
  standard and one indicator within it. Map your current practice against that indicator and
  assemble one piece of evidence for or against it.
- **Evidence to bring back.** The map, plus one development goal for that domain.

### 4. Communication observation

- **Purpose.** See how the verbal and non-verbal channels carry a lesson, and where one of them
  is neglected.
- **Rough method.** Ask a peer or mentor to observe one of your lessons recording only two
  things: where you stood, minute by minute, and which pupils you made eye contact with. Map
  the results onto a seating plan and note any neglected zone.
- **Evidence to bring back.** The map, and a short note on one change you will try as a result.

### 5. Challenge case study

- **Purpose.** Turn a real school difficulty into a named, bounded response rather than a
  complaint.
- **Rough method.** Pick one challenge your placement school actually faces (multi-grade
  teaching, resourcing, workload). Document the challenge and one growth-oriented response the
  staff use, and place the challenge in the three regions of control, influence, and what you
  do not control.
- **Evidence to bring back.** The write-up, including what you are not claiming to fix.

### 6. One-page professional development plan

- **Purpose.** Produce a plan that can survive the school year rather than one that is filed in
  September.
- **Rough method.** Build the six blocks from Topic 6.4 in about twenty minutes: the gap stated
  specifically, the evidence that it is real, one goal, the activity, the evidence that would
  show change, and a mid-year review date. Say what you will stop doing to make room for it.
- **Evidence to bring back.** The one-page plan, for peer review.

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section to check your own work after you have attempted the questions.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - the four features are the test; A describes status, and C and D describe surface
   conditions.
2. **C** - professionalism is how one teacher acts; professionalisation is the slower collective
   building of training routes, licensing, standards and associations.
3. **B** - the defining move of inquiry-based teaching is noticing and adjusting on evidence,
   whatever the seating or noise level.
4. **C** - Rest's four components are sensitivity, judgement, motivation and character; A is
   the four-step framework, and B is the internal structure of a standard.
5. **C** - the law is silent and her conscience is clear, so only professional ethics has
   anything to say.
6. **C** - a mark is a public claim about a pupil; copying forward unmarked marks is a failure
   of honest assessment in practice and performance.
7. **A** - the three families are achievement gain, observed practice, and breadth of learner
   outcomes.
8. **D** - the seven named traits are empathy, patience, self-control, optimism, humour,
   resilience and adaptability; ambition is not among them.
9. **C** - the failure mode of a trait at excess is the trait unbalanced by the others, not its
   opposite and not a fixed disposition.
10. **B** - each standard divides into knowledge and understanding, dispositions, and
    performance and skills.
11. **A** - a code of conduct governs behaviour; a standard describes competent practice in a
    checkable form and governs practice.
12. **A** - burnout's three dimensions are exhaustion, cynicism or depersonalisation, and a
    reduced sense of personal accomplishment.
13. **D** - she leaves the class size where it is and acts on the first ten minutes, which is
    within her control.
14. **B** - sustained, embedded, content-focused, collaborative, feedback-supported and
    teacher-chosen.
15. **A** - one goal answers the destroyer "too many goals"; the other three blocks answer the
    other three destroyers.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: a profession is an occupation built on specialised knowledge, entered after extended
   training and certification, governed by a shared code of conduct, and accountable to a
   professional community rather than only an employer. **Marks (3):** 1 for a usable
   definition; 2 for all four features named.
2. Model: accountability, autonomy and collegiality must be held together because each corrects
   the others' excess. Too much accountability with no autonomy pushes teaching back toward the
   industrial model; too much autonomy with no collegiality leaves a teacher isolated and
   unchecked. **Marks (4):** 1 for the balance principle; 2 for one worked example of what goes
   wrong when one is over-weighted; 1 for linking the example back to professional practice.
3. Model: personal morality is private and varies; the law is a floor that sets out what is
   intolerable and is enforceable; professional ethics are shared, aspirational standards that
   cover what neither reaches. Example: reading marks aloud in rank order breaks no rule and
   may spring from a clear conscience, yet trades a pupil's dignity for a motivational effect.
   **Marks (4):** 1 for each of the three distinctions; 1 for a teaching example that occupies
   the gap.
4. Model: the teacher noticed and judged correctly but did not follow through, so the failure is
   at moral character, the fourth component. What helps is rehearsal, allies and small
   commitments made in advance, rather than more instruction in what is right. **Marks (4):** 2
   for identifying character rather than sensitivity, judgement or motivation; 2 for a remedy
   matched to that stage.
5. Model: the definitions count different effects. Achievement gain counts what the test tests;
   observed practice counts what an observer can see and can be satisfied by performance;
   breadth of learner outcomes counts more of what matters but measures it less reliably. The
   same evidence can therefore support opposite verdicts. **Marks (4):** 2 for naming at least
   two definitions and what each counts; 2 for showing how the same evidence yields opposite
   verdicts.
6. Model: "Does everyone understand?" samples confidence, not comprehension, and over-samples
   the pupils who are already fine. A better check asks a pupil to explain the step to a
   neighbour, or to say which of two wrong answers is closer and why, which discovers what
   pupils actually think. **Marks (4):** 2 for the diagnosis; 2 for a workable alternative and
   why it works.
7. Model: a code of conduct answers "how should I behave?" and governs conduct, with
   disciplinary consequences; a curriculum answers "what should pupils learn?" and governs
   content; a professional standard answers "what does competent teaching look like, in a form
   that can be checked?" and governs practice, with developmental consequences before
   disciplinary ones. **Marks (4):** 1 for each question correctly matched.
8. Model: stress is the episodic response to demands that exceed resources in the moment and
   resolves when the demand passes or resources arrive. Burnout is what chronic, unresolved
   demand produces over time, with three dimensions: exhaustion, cynicism or depersonalisation,
   and a reduced sense of personal accomplishment. **Marks (4):** 2 for the distinction; 1 for
   each dimension.
9. Model: structural conditions such as class size, pay and paperwork are real and are not
   moved by individual effort. Offering resilience as the whole answer treats the teacher's
   capacity to endure as the remedy for a condition the system produced, so the failure is
   located in the person rather than in the condition. The useful question becomes what, within
   the teacher's control and influence, would reduce the load. **Marks (4):** 2 for the
   mechanism of relocation; 2 for the alternative question or lever.
10. Model: a training course is an event with a certificate; a qualification certifies a past
    threshold; an appraisal requirement produces compliance. Continuing professional development
    is driven by the practitioner, continuous rather than episodic, and measured by a change in
    what the teacher actually does. Eleven certificates therefore record eleven attendances, not
    development. **Marks (4):** 2 for the distinctions; 2 for applying them to the case.

### ERQ rubrics

1. **Analytic rubric (20).** *Four dimensions of professionalism applied* (0-6: each judged
   against a specific detail from the description) - *Components of teacher effectiveness
   applied* (0-5: at least three named and evidenced) - *Overall verdict justified* (0-6,
   **Evaluate**: weighs strengths and weaknesses across both frameworks rather than listing
   them) - *Evidence discipline* (0-2: distinguishes what the description states from what is
   inferred) - *Structure and clarity* (0-1).
2. **Analytic rubric (20).** *Rest's model applied* (0-5: the component the decision depends on
   is named, or the failure is located correctly) - *Four-step framework completed* (0-6: all
   four steps, with Context establishing facts rather than restating the problem) - *Mark and
   response separated* (0-5: the mark as a claim about the work; the response as where a
   teacher's care operates) - *Pressure distinguished from obligation* (0-4: the school's
   results pressure named and set aside).
3. **Analytic rubric (20).** *Demands and resources separated* (0-5: demands identified, and a
   resource named as the second lever) - *Control-and-influence used accurately* (0-5: the part
   inside her control named, and the part outside it acknowledged) - *One workable change*
   (0-6, **Create**: specific, small, and hers to make without permission) - *Answer to the
   dependence objection* (0-4: says what remains possible if nobody else acts).
4. **Analytic rubric (20).** *Three questions applied* (0-6: what it adds, what it costs, and
   what it replaces that was already working) - *Decision defended* (0-5: a concrete
   recommendation, not enthusiasm and not refusal) - *Obligations carried into the digital
   case* (0-5: the power imbalance and the pupil-image issue named) - *Donor-relations objection
   answered* (0-4: proposes something defensible that does not require pretending the tablets
   work).
5. **Analytic rubric (20).** *Six blocks present and specific* (0-5: one observable gap, one
   goal, a chosen activity, evidence decided in advance, a review date, and where the time comes
   from) - *Self-evaluation cycle used* (0-4: one indicator chosen and evidenced) - *Researcher
   discipline* (0-4: the evidence could show the goal was not met) - *Defence against the four
   destroyers* (0-5, **Create / Evaluate**: names the plan's own weakest point and answers it) -
   *Clarity* (0-2).
