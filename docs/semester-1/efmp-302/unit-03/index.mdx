# Becoming an Effective Teacher

<Figure id="fig-U3-11" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efmp-302/unit-03/fig-U3-11.webp" alt="A teacher crouches beside a desk to listen as a boy and a girl explain their work, while the rest of the class works in pairs in a lively Sindh classroom." width={1376} height={768} />

Unit 1 asked what a profession is. Unit 2 asked what it obliges you to do. This unit asks the
question those two lead to: what actually makes a teacher good at the work?

It is a harder question than it sounds, and the honest answer begins with a warning. There is no
single agreed definition of an effective teacher, and the definitions in use disagree with each
other in ways that matter. A teacher judged effective by one measure can be judged ineffective
by another, on the same evidence. This unit gives you the competing accounts rather than a tidy
one, because a trainee who leaves with a tidy one will be surprised by the first appraisal they
sit.

What the unit does offer is a set of components you can observe, and four things that carry
effectiveness in practice: the qualities you bring, the relationships you build, the way you
communicate, and the roles you take on.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain what **teacher effectiveness** means, describe at least two competing conceptions of
  it, and say what each captures and misses.
- Name the components used to evaluate teacher effectiveness and distinguish within-classroom
  processes from factors beyond the classroom.
- Describe the personal and professional qualities associated with effective teaching, including
  attitude toward the profession and professional image, and explain how a trait can help or
  harm depending on degree.
- Build and describe positive relationships with pupils, parents, colleagues and the community,
  and set out a defensible sequence for handling conflict without losing professionalism.
- Distinguish **verbal** and **non-verbal communication**, name the types of non-verbal channel
  and their classroom use, and explain what happens when the two channels contradict each other.
- Explain the evolving roles of the teacher as facilitator, researcher, change agent, moral agent
  and life-long learner, and give a concrete classroom act that expresses each.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 and 2. Unit 1 supplies the idea of professionalism and its dimensions. Unit 2 supplies the
ethical obligations that constrain what "effective" is permitted to mean, and that constraint
runs through this whole unit: a method that raises marks and damages pupils is not effective
teaching, it is a different thing wearing its name.

## In this unit

1. [Topic 3.1 - What "an effective teacher" actually means](./topic-01) - the competing
   definitions, why they disagree, and the components used to evaluate effectiveness.
2. [Topic 3.2 - The qualities an effective teacher brings](./topic-02) - attitude toward the
   profession, professional image, the named personality traits and how each fails when overdone.
3. [Topic 3.3 - Relationships, and what to do when they go wrong](./topic-03) - the stakeholder
   web around a teacher, and a workable sequence for conflict.
4. [Topic 3.4 - Saying it so it lands](./topic-04) - verbal and non-verbal communication, the
   types of non-verbal channel, and what learners do when the channels disagree.
5. [Topic 3.5 - The roles a teacher now has to play](./topic-05) - facilitator, researcher,
   change agent, moral agent and life-long learner.

## How to use this unit

Work through the topics in order. Topic 3.1 sets up a distinction the other four keep using: the
difference between what is measured and what is meant.

Topics 3.2 and 3.4 both ask you to look at yourself, and both are more useful done with someone
who has seen you teach. If you are on practicum, save the transfer tasks in those two topics for
a week when you have a mentor available.

The question bank at the end covers all five topics. Its answers and mark schemes are in a
clearly marked section at the foot of that page.
