# Unit 5 assessment

## Unit summary

A teacher's week is dominated by work that is invisible from outside and therefore elastic:
marking, returns, cover, parent contact, assigned duties. It lands on whoever absorbs it, so
workload falls hardest on the most conscientious, and preparation is dropped first because it
alone has no external deadline.

Stress is episodic and resolves. Burnout is what chronic unresolved demand produces, and it has
three dimensions: exhaustion, cynicism or distancing, and a reduced sense of accomplishment. It
comes from the relationship between demands and resources, so adding a resource is a lever when
removing a demand is not. Cynicism is the dimension that looks like realism. Missing resources are
physical, human, instructional and structural, and the last two are the most underestimated.
Multilingual classrooms impose a cognitive load easily misread as low ability, and excluding the
home language removes the pupil's best resource. Multi-grade teaching is a distinct, widely
required and barely taught skill.

Accountability has grown and the case for it is sound; what it does not supply is anything that
makes the measured work easier. Without autonomy it produces narrowing, effort shifted to the
record of the work, and managed exposure. Many parties make demands, and for nearly all of them
what they want and what they measure differ. The demands conflict, no party sees the whole job,
and the teacher is the only one who does. Teaching's status is uneven rather than low; status
shapes who enters, practice shapes the recognition a community extends.

Pressure to use technology rarely originates in a question about learning. Judge a use by what it
adds, what it costs, and what it replaces that was working. Obligations do not weaken because the
medium changed.

A teacher's situation divides into what they control, what they can influence, and what they do
not. Most frustration comes from effort spent in the third region, and the characteristic failure is using
an accurate judgement about conditions to justify inaction where there is room. Resilience offered
as the whole answer relocates a system's failure into a person's character.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The part of a teacher's workload that expands under pressure is characteristically: *(Understand)*
   - a) timetabled teaching
   - b) work specified in the service rules
   - c) examination duties
   - d) the work that is invisible from outside the school

2. The dimension of burnout that most often looks like competence to a colleague is:
   *(Understand)*
   - a) reduced sense of accomplishment
   - b) exhaustion
   - c) cynicism or distancing
   - d) time pressure

3. A pupil sends a teacher a private message on a messaging app. The professional default is to:
   *(Apply)*
   - a) reply on a channel a parent or colleague can see, rather than privately
   - b) reply privately but keep the exchange brief
   - c) not reply at all, in any form
   - d) reply privately and delete the thread afterwards

4. Excluding a pupil's home language from the classroom is a mistake mainly because it: *(Apply)*
   - a) breaks a school rule
   - b) removes the pupil's best resource for understanding a difficult idea
   - c) slows the syllabus
   - d) is unpopular with parents

5. An accountability mechanism that measures but supplies nothing will typically: *(Understand)*
   - a) reduce workload
   - b) improve teaching directly
   - c) be ignored entirely
   - d) change behaviour without improving teaching

6. For most parties making demands on a teacher, what they want and what they measure: *(Remember)*
   - a) are the same thing
   - b) differ, and the gap is where much of the difficulty lives
   - c) are both set by the district
   - d) are both invisible to the teacher

7. Teaching's occupational status is best described as: *(Understand)*
   - a) uneven across markers, so that pay, autonomy and recognition do not move together
   - b) determined entirely by pay
   - c) equal to medicine and law
   - d) uniformly low across every marker

8. The question most often missed when judging a classroom use of technology is: *(Apply)*
   - a) what does it cost in money
   - b) what does it add
   - c) what does it replace that was already working
   - d) who supplied it

9. A teacher who is accurate that class size cannot be changed, and uses that to justify changing
   nothing at all, has: *(Apply)*
   - a) acted as a change agent
   - b) correctly identified what is outside their control
   - c) failed at resilience
   - d) used a true judgement about one region to excuse inaction in another

10. Offering resilience as the whole answer to structural conditions chiefly: *(Understand)*
    - a) relocates a system's failure into an individual's character
    - b) applies only to multi-grade classrooms
    - c) contradicts the evidence on burnout dimensions
    - d) is good advice for new teachers

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain why workload falls hardest on the most conscientious teachers under the conditions
   described. *(Understand)*

2. Distinguish stress from burnout, name burnout's three dimensions, and state where each of this
   unit's two burnout sources was conducted. *(Understand)*

3. Name the four categories of missing resource and explain why the instructional and structural
   ones are the most underestimated. *(Analyze)*

4. Give two predictable effects of accountability arriving without autonomy or resources.
   *(Understand)*

5. For any three stakeholders, state what that party wants and what that party actually measures.
   *(Apply)*

6. Distinguish occupational status from public recognition, and explain what follows for a teacher
   who believes nothing they do affects how teaching is regarded. *(Analyze)*

7. State the three questions to ask of any proposed classroom use of technology, and explain why
   the third is the hardest to answer honestly. *(Understand)*

8. Give two problems with a class messaging group that are social rather than technical.
   *(Understand)*

9. Name the three regions into which a teacher's situation divides, with two examples in each, and
   explain why most challenges cannot be placed wholly in one region. *(Understand)*

10. Explain why "decide in advance what would show it worked" is the station most often skipped,
    and what goes wrong when it is. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Analyse Miss Shahnaz's Tuesday using the demands-and-resources idea. Identify which demands
   could realistically be removed and which could not, propose the two interventions most likely to
   help, and state which parts of your analysis rest on the cited research and which are reasoned
   from the described conditions. *(Analyze)*

2. Mr Rafiq receives four demands in one week from his head, a parent, the district and a
   newspaper. Rank them, state the criterion your ranking rests on, and address the objection that
   deprioritising the head is unrealistic for someone whose confirmation depends on that head's
   report. *(Evaluate)*

3. Ms Sundas is given forty tablets she cannot usefully deploy and a request for photographs of
   them in use. Decide what she should do, work at least two uses through the three questions, and
   address the objection that refusing a donated resource is ungrateful. *(Evaluate)*

4. Take one challenge described in this unit, place it in the three regions, and design a response
   that is within a teacher's control. State what your change does not fix, and answer the charge
   that individual adaptation lets the system off the hook. *(Create)*

5. **Integrative.** A teacher in a rural government school has a multi-grade Class 4 and 5 of
   fifty-one pupils across three home languages, no cover arrangement, a district requiring monthly
   returns, and a head asking for better results. She has begun telling trainees not to bother with
   group work. Analyse her situation across conditions, demands and response: identify what is
   happening to her, distinguish the structural from the personal, and say what could realistically
   change and who would have to act. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **d** - the invisible work is elastic, so it absorbs pressure; the timetable does not move.
2. **c** - a teacher who has stopped investing is no longer visibly distressed, which reads as
   competence or realism.
3. **a** - the obligations from Unit 2 do not change because the medium changed, and the power
   imbalance that makes a private conversation a matter of care does not weaken on a phone. A
   visible channel protects the pupil first and the teacher second. Refusing to reply at all
   abandons a pupil who may need the contact; deleting the thread destroys the record that
   protects both parties.
4. **b** - the home language is the pupil's working resource for a difficult idea, not an obstacle.
5. **d** - measurement changes behaviour; without supplied resources the behaviour change is not
   an improvement in teaching.
6. **b** - the want-measure gap is the topic's central analytical point.
7. **a** - uneven; teaching scores differently on pay, entry selectivity, autonomy and recognition.
8. **c** - the replacement question is the one nobody asks, and the one that catches a video
   replacing a teacher who can notice confusion.
9. **d** - the judgement about class size is correct and is doing work it cannot do.
10. **a** - it converts an institutional failure into a personal deficiency.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** The elastic work is invisible and unallocated (1); it lands on whoever absorbs it
(1); conscientious teachers absorb more (1); so the burden correlates with commitment rather than
with formal duty (1).

**2. (6 marks)** Stress is episodic and resolves (1); burnout follows chronic unresolved demand
(1); exhaustion, cynicism or distancing, reduced accomplishment (3); names Maslach and Leiter as
human-service occupations generally and Skaalvik and Skaalvik as 262 Norwegian high-school
teachers (1).

**3. (5 marks)** Physical, human, instructional, structural (2); instructional and structural
absences are invisible and undiscussed (1); they convert solvable problems into permanent ones (1);
an example such as having nobody to ask about a pupil who cannot decode (1).

**4. (4 marks)** Two of: what is measured becomes what is done; effort moves from the work to the
record of the work; teachers manage exposure rather than improving practice. Two marks per effect,
one for naming and one for the mechanism.

**5. (6 marks)** Two marks per stakeholder: what they want and what they measure, correctly
distinguished.

**6. (5 marks)** Status is the occupation's structural standing, recognition is what a community
extends (2); structural status is not shiftable by one teacher (1); recognition is affected by the
teachers people actually meet (1); so the belief is false of recognition even where true of status
(1).

**7. (5 marks)** What does it add, what does it cost, what does it replace that was working (3);
the third is hardest because it requires admitting that the existing practice had value (1); and
because nobody proposing the technology is asking it (1).

**8. (4 marks)** Two of: pupils without a phone are excluded from what is announced; availability
expectations expand without decision; messages are permanent and forwardable. Two marks each.

**9. (7 marks)** Names the three regions (3, one mark each); two apt examples in each (2); explains that most challenges divide across the regions rather than sitting wholly in one, with an illustration - the number of pupils in a class is outside a teacher's control while the response to it is inside (2).

**10. (4 marks)** It is skipped because it is not required to act (1); and because deciding
afterwards is easier (1); what goes wrong is that a measure chosen after the fact confirms
whatever was hoped (1); so the result cannot surprise you and no learning occurs (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 across four criteria. A response not reaching "Adequate" on the
analysis, evaluation or creation criterion cannot exceed 10 overall.

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use of the model | Demands and resources conflated | Separated accurately | Applied to the specific Tuesday, not to teaching in general |
| Realism about constraints | Treats all demands as removable | Distinguishes removable from fixed | Distinguishes what a teacher, head and district can each change |
| **Defence of interventions (Analyze)** | **Proposals asserted** | **Proposals defended** | **Answers the objection that they depend on others acting, and says what remains if nobody does** |
