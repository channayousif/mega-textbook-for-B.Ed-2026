# Unit 6 assessment

## Unit summary

Continuous professional development is the ongoing process by which a professional maintains and
extends the knowledge, judgement and skill the work requires. It is not a training course, a
qualification or an appraisal requirement: it is driven by the practitioner, continuous, and
measured by changed practice rather than attendance. It is obligatory because the knowledge base
changes, the pupils change, initial preparation cannot be complete by construction, and the
alternative to examined practice is drift toward convenience rather than stability.

Development that changes practice is sustained, embedded in the teacher's own classroom, focused
on specific content and pupils, collaborative, supported by feedback on real practice, and chosen
rather than assigned. Most available provision violates all six at once.

A career passes through three stages. Preservice ends when responsibility transfers; its
difficulty is that everything is new at once and it needs structured practice with feedback. The
new teacher stage begins abruptly with full responsibility; its difficulty is survival and it needs
a mentor, reduced load and help with what is actually blocking the teacher. The experienced stage
is longest and least served: attention is free so capacity is highest, while external pressure is
lowest, which is where drift operates. At every stage the support offered tends to mismatch the
support needed.

Five routes are suited to different things. Conferences buy exposure at the highest cost per
teacher; workshops transmit a technique and cannot produce the practice, though a participant can
supply the missing principles; online platforms buy reach with mixed evidence and cannot observe
you; professional learning communities satisfy more principles than any other route and cost only
protected time; reflective tools range from journals to lesson study, where observers watch the
pupils rather than the teacher.

A plan is one page with six blocks: the gap, the evidence for it, one goal, the activity, the
evidence of change, and a review date. Four things destroy a plan: too many goals, no time made,
no evidence decided in advance, and no review. At the review, continuing, narrowing or replacing
the goal are all legitimate; quiet abandonment is not.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Continuing professional development differs from a training course chiefly in that development
   is: *(Understand)*
   a) longer
   b) driven by the practitioner, continuous, and measured by changed practice
   c) certified by an external body
   d) required by an employer

2. Eleven attendance certificates and no change in teaching demonstrate: *(Apply)*
   a) a lazy teacher
   b) that evaluation stopped at attendance rather than reaching changed practice
   c) that the workshops were on the wrong subject
   d) that the teacher needs a qualification

3. "The alternative to development is not stability but drift" means that unexamined practice:
   *(Understand)*
   a) stays the same indefinitely
   b) slowly adapts toward convenience without anyone deciding
   c) improves automatically with experience
   d) becomes more demanding over time

4. Which principle does a one-day generic workshop most obviously violate? *(Remember)*
   a) chosen rather than assigned
   b) sustained over time
   c) collaborative
   d) all of the above

5. The new teacher transition is described as unusually abrupt because a new teacher: *(Understand)*
   a) has less subject knowledge than a trainee
   b) frequently carries the same load as a colleague of twenty years from the first week
   c) is not permitted to ask for help
   d) has no qualification

6. The experienced teacher stage is the least served because: *(Analyze)*
   a) experienced teachers refuse development
   b) capacity for development is highest while external pressure toward it is lowest
   c) no suitable provision exists anywhere
   d) experienced teachers have finished learning

7. Which route satisfies the largest number of the six principles? *(Remember)*
   a) a conference
   b) a workshop
   c) a professional learning community
   d) an online platform

8. In lesson study, observers watch the pupils rather than the teacher chiefly because it:
   *(Apply)*
   a) is easier
   b) removes defensiveness and directs attention to what a teacher cannot see while teaching
   c) avoids the need for planning
   d) produces a record for appraisal

9. The single most common reason a development plan fails is: *(Remember)*
   a) the goal was too difficult
   b) there were too many goals
   c) the school did not approve it
   d) the teacher changed schools

10. At a mid-year review, narrowing the goal is best understood as: *(Understand)*
    a) a failure of ambition
    b) a legitimate outcome and the commonest one
    c) evidence the plan was written badly
    d) grounds for abandoning the plan

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Distinguish continuing professional development from a training course, a qualification and an
   appraisal requirement, by what each is for and who drives it. *(Understand)*

2. Give four reasons continuing development is obligatory rather than optional, and explain the
   drift argument in your own words. *(Understand)*

3. Name the six principles of development that changes practice, and state what any three of them
   rule out. *(Remember)*

4. Name the three career stages and state what changes at each transition. *(Remember)*

5. Sana and Imran both know about wait time and neither uses it. Explain why, and show that the
   reasons differ by stage. *(Analyze)*

6. Explain why capacity for development and pressure toward it are inversely arranged in the
   experienced stage, and what follows for how that stage must be served. *(Analyze)*

7. Give the three things a participant can do to convert a workshop into development, naming the
   principle each supplies. *(Apply)*

8. State what the review of technology for teacher development in low- and middle-income countries
   reports, and name two constraints on online platforms in Pakistani conditions. *(Understand)*

9. Name the six blocks of a one-page development plan and state what each one prevents.
   *(Remember)*

10. Explain why a plan written for an appraiser will be useless, referring to the separation of
    development from judgement. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Mr Naeem has eleven certificates and no change in practice. Diagnose why against the six
   principles, separate the provider's failures from his own, and design one activity that a real
   Pakistani government school could support. *(Create)*

2. A district has budget for one professional development intervention, for all teachers, once a
   year. Using the three stages, argue what it should spend it on and answer the objection from
   whichever group you do not serve. *(Evaluate)*

3. A head teacher has twelve teachers and a small budget. Compare at least three routes on cost,
   reach and the six principles, make a recommendation, state what is given up, and specify how
   the arrangement is protected from what ordinarily destroys it. *(Evaluate)*

4. "Reflective practice means keeping a journal." Evaluate this claim, distinguishing the four
   reflective tools by what each can establish, and explain why peer feedback is simultaneously
   the most valuable and the most avoided. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative, and the closing task of the course.** Write your own one-page professional
   development plan and defend it. Justify each block: why this gap and what evidence establishes
   it, why one goal, why this activity given what it can actually deliver, what evidence you will
   collect and why it could show you were wrong, and where the time comes from. Draw on at least
   three earlier units of this course, and finish by naming the plan's own weakest point.
   *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - all three features together; length alone does not distinguish it.
2. **b** - the certificates are an accurate record of attendance and evaluation never reached
   practice.
3. **b** - drift is unchosen adaptation toward convenience, which is why it is invisible from
   inside.
4. **d** - a one-day generic assigned workshop violates sustained, collaborative and chosen at
   once, which is the point of the example.
5. **b** - the abruptness is about load and accountability, not knowledge.
6. **b** - the inverse arrangement of capacity and pressure is the topic's analytical claim.
7. **c** - sustained, embedded, focused, collaborative and capable of supplying feedback.
8. **b** - the rule removes judgement of the teacher and directs attention to the pupils.
9. **b** - too many goals; Miss Hina's six.
10. **b** - narrowing means the goal met reality, which is the plan working.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (8 marks)** Two marks per instrument: what it is for and who drives it. Development:
maintaining and extending capability, driven by the practitioner. Course: an event, driven by a
provider. Qualification: certifying a past threshold, driven by an awarding body. Appraisal
requirement: an employer obligation, driven by the employer.

**2. (6 marks)** Knowledge base changes (1); pupils change (1); initial preparation cannot be
complete by construction (1); drift (1); drift explained as unchosen adaptation toward convenience
(2).

**3. (9 marks)** Six principles named (6); three "rules out" statements (3).

**4. (6 marks)** Preservice, new teacher, experienced (3); transitions: responsibility transfers;
mechanics become automatic and attention is freed (3).

**5. (5 marks)** Sana's attention is consumed by mechanics, so there is no spare capacity (2);
Imran's block is a class he cannot yet hold, not knowledge (2); concludes that identical knowledge
gaps can have different stage-specific causes (1).

**6. (5 marks)** Mechanics automatic so attention is free, giving highest capacity (2); nothing
external requires change, giving lowest pressure (2); therefore development at this stage must be
self-driven or it does not occur (1).

**7. (6 marks)** Decide in advance what single thing to try (1, supplies focus); agree with a
colleague to try and compare (2, supplies collaborative and sustained); decide what to look at to
know it worked (2, supplies feedback); one mark for correctly pairing principles to actions.

**8. (5 marks)** Limited, often unsustained provision, not always research-informed; real potential
to extend formal and peer learning; outcomes mixed (3); two constraints from data cost, device
access, electricity, connectivity (2).

**9. (12 marks)** Six blocks named (6); what each prevents (6): vagueness, a list of virtues,
dilution across many goals, an unchosen activity, a March with nothing to look at, quiet
abandonment.

**10. (4 marks)** A plan feeding judgement is written to look good (1); honest gaps disappear (1);
Unit 4's mixed-purpose degradation (1); the remedy that the working document is the teacher's own
and a summary can be supplied (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 across four criteria. A response not reaching "Adequate" on the
analysis, evaluation or creation criterion cannot exceed 10 overall.

**ERQ 1 - Mr Naeem's eleven certificates**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use of the principles | Two or three applied | All six applied | Applied to the actual provision described, not to workshops in general |
| Attribution | Blames the teacher | Separates provider from teacher | Identifies what he could have supplied himself without permission |
| **Design (Create)** | **Generic proposal** | **Specific activity** | **Startable on Monday and supplies the principles the provision violated** |
| **Constraint (Evaluate)** | **Assumes resources** | **Notes constraints** | **Designs within the absence of time and colleagues rather than around it** |

**ERQ 2 - One intervention, three stages**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use of the stages | One or two stages | All three with their needs | Needs stated specifically rather than as "more support" |
| Recognition of constraint | Proposes three interventions | Accepts one | Explains what makes the choice genuinely exclusive |
| **Defended choice (Evaluate)** | **Preference stated** | **Choice with reasons** | **States the criterion and answers the group not served** |
| Realism | Not deliverable | Deliverable | Deliverable once a year on a district budget |

**ERQ 3 - The head's twelve teachers**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparison | One route | Three routes on some criteria | Three or more on cost, reach and principles |
| What is given up | Not stated | Stated | Stated precisely, including who loses |
| **Protection (Create)** | **None** | **General intent** | **Concrete defences against the three named failure modes** |
| **Constraint (Evaluate)** | **Assumes protected time** | **Notes the difficulty** | **Argues whether a head in an understaffed school can actually defend it** |

**ERQ 4 - Is reflective practice a journal?**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The four tools | Fewer than four | All four named | Each distinguished by what it can establish |
| Grasp of reflection | Treated as disposition | Treated as method | Shows why evidence, not feeling, is what makes it reflection |
| **Evaluating the claim (Evaluate)** | **Asserts it is wrong** | **Argues it is wrong** | **Concedes what is right in it before showing what it misses** |
| Peer feedback | Mentioned | Explained | Explains the value and the avoidance together, including the appraisal link |

**ERQ 5 - Your own plan, defended (integrative)**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The plan itself | Blocks missing | All six present | The gap is stated so an observer could confirm or disconfirm it |
| Fit of the activity | Chosen by preference | Justified | Justified against what that route can actually deliver |
| **Evidence design (Create)** | **Vague or retrospective** | **Decided in advance** | **Capable of showing the goal was not met, and the candidate says so** |
| **Use of the course and self-diagnosis (Evaluate)** | **No earlier units used** | **Draws on three units** | **Uses them substantively and names the plan's own weakest point honestly** |
