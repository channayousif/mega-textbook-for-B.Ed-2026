# Understanding Teaching - teacher notes

## Where this unit sits

This is the opening unit of EFMP-302 and, for most students, the opening unit of the B.Ed. It
covers **Weeks 1–3**. Students arrive with strong everyday opinions about teaching and almost
no technical vocabulary for it. The unit's job is to give them that vocabulary - *profession*,
*professionalism*, *professionalisation*, the *four dimensions*, *teacher identity* - and the
habit of examining a case rather than reacting to it. Units 2–4 build directly on the
"dimensions" and "identity" material.

The unit is built as **four topic cycles** plus a unit-assessment page:

| Topic | Focus | Sub-topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What makes teaching a profession | U1-01…U1-04 |
| 1.2 | Industrial model → inquiry-based teaching | U1-05, U1-06 |
| 1.3 | The four dimensions of teacher professionalism | U1-07…U1-10 |
| 1.4 | Becoming a teacher: developing your identity | U1-11…U1-14 |

Each topic is a complete lesson: a real classroom situation, an explanation, an activity, a
formative check, a summary, a self-assessment checklist, a practicum transfer task, a summative
task, and further reading. Teach them in order - 1.3 relies on 1.1, and 1.4 assumes the four
dimensions.

## Teaching strategies

The course description names **interactive lecture with local examples, case analysis,
reflective practice, and collaborative inquiry**. Use all four:

- **Open each topic with its "real classroom situation", not a definition.** For Topic 1.1, ask
  students to name one job they would call a "true profession" and one they would not, and why,
  *before* you give the four features. This surfaces the "well-paid job" misconception straight
  away.
- **Teach the industrial → inquiry shift (Topic 1.2) as a contrast, not a history lecture.**
  Describe one lesson two ways in two minutes of talk (scripted/procedural, then
  reflective/adjusting). Students grasp the distinction far faster from a concrete contrast.
  Hargreaves' "four ages" can be mentioned for students who want the fuller picture - do not
  make it the centre.
- **Use think–pair–share for the Topic 1.1 and 1.3 activities** so every student commits to a
  judgement before the whole-class comparison.
- **Protect time for the Topic 1.4 "First reflection" activity.** It is short but important -
  the first entry in a habit the course returns to in Unit 6. Do not let it be squeezed out.
- **The Topic 1.3 summative task needs a case.** Give students the one-page description of a
  teacher's practice at least a few days early; the same case type reappears in the
  unit-assessment ERQs.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

| Misconception | What to do |
|---|---|
| "A profession = a respectable, well-paid job." | Push for the four features; ask for a well-paid job that is *not* a profession and a modestly paid one that *is*. |
| "Teaching is a profession only because the state says so." | Separate *recognition* from *basis*: the knowledge base and ethical code exist whether or not policy names them. |
| "Inquiry-based teaching = group work / less structure." | Show a strict, teacher-led lesson that is still inquiry-based because the teacher notices and adjusts. The defining move is evidence-based adjustment, not the furniture. |
| "Reflection = keeping a diary." | Contrast a record with using the record to question an assumption and change the next action (Topic 1.4 activity). |
| "Professionalism is dress and discipline." | Acknowledge these matter, then weight the discussion toward knowledge, ethics, the accountability–autonomy–collegiality balance, and continued learning. |
| Treating "semi-profession" as an insult. | Frame it as a description of what teaching is still building (longer training, stronger bodies, more judgement), not a ranking. |

## Practical work

- **The Topic 1.2 summative doubles as practical work.** Each student describes one real or
  imagined lesson two ways - industrial framing, then inquiry-based framing - and names one
  concrete, do-able change between them. Collect a sample aloud so the class hears that
  "inquiry-based" is a habit of *noticing and adjusting*, not an abstract label.
- **Mini-observation (optional, if a practicum school is available).** Ask students to watch
  ten minutes of any lesson and note one moment that looked "industrial" and one that looked
  "inquiry-based", with no judgement of the teacher. Discuss patterns, not individuals. This is
  the Topic 1.2 "Try this at your practicum school" task.
- **The per-topic practicum tasks are cumulative.** By the end of the unit each student should
  have four short practicum notes and one dated identity reflection. Ask to see them before the
  unit-assessment.

## Assessment guidance

- The **per-topic "Check your understanding"** items are diagnostic - mark lightly, look for
  which key ideas are shaky, and reteach before the topic's summative task.
- The **per-topic summative tasks** reach **Apply/Analyze**. The Topic 1.3 task reaches
  **Analyze** in full - breaking a real case into parts and relating it to the four dimensions.
  The most common shortfall is asserting an industrial/inquiry judgement without quoting
  specific details; flag that explicitly when you set the task.
- The **unit-assessment page** carries the full 10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ bank with answers and
  marking guidance. Students can self-check; you may also use selected RRQs/ERQs as a graded
  end-of-unit test. Weighting follows the course default: **60% summative / 40% formative**.
