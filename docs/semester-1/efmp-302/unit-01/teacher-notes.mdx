# Teacher Notes

## Teaching strategies

Open with a short, low-stakes question before naming any terms: ask students to think of one job
they would call a "true profession" and one they would not, and say why — then introduce the
four features of a profession and let students check their own examples against the list. Use
paired discussion for Activity 1 so students hear a second opinion before the whole-class
comparison. For the industrial-vs-inquiry shift, a brief contrast of two teaching styles
(one scripted/procedural, one reflective) works better as a quick spoken example than a long
lecture — students grasp the distinction fastest from a concrete contrast.

## Practical work

Activity 2 doubles as light practical work: have students describe one real or imagined lesson
two ways (industrial framing, then inquiry-based framing) and name one concrete change between
them. Collecting a few of these aloud at the end of the session reinforces that "inquiry-based"
is a habit of noticing and adjusting, not an abstract label.
