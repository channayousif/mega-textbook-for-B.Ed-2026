# Activities

## Activity 1: Profession or occupation?

**Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.**

Pick three jobs you know well — for example a government-school teacher, a shopkeeper, and a
doctor at a health centre.

1. Draw a table with the three jobs as columns and the four features of a profession as rows:
   specialised knowledge, extended training and a qualification, a code of conduct,
   accountability to a professional community.
2. For each cell, write **yes**, **no**, or **partly**, with one reason.
3. Decide: which job is clearly a profession? Which is clearly not? Is there one you and your
   partner disagree about — and why?
4. Join another pair and compare tables. Where you differ, try to agree on what evidence would
   settle it.

**Bring to the whole class:** one job that is genuinely hard to classify, and the feature that
makes it hard.

## Activity 2: The same lesson, two ways

**Work individually, then discuss. About 25 minutes.**

Think of one lesson you have seen taught, or imagine one on a topic you know well.

1. **Describe it as an "industrial" lesson.** Fixed procedure, cover the syllabus point on
   time, keep the class quiet and orderly, mark against a fixed key.
2. **Describe the same lesson as an "inquiry-based" lesson.** The teacher watches how students
   are actually thinking, notices a common mistake, and changes the next step in response.
3. Name **one concrete change** that would move the industrial version toward the inquiry-based
   one. It must be something a teacher could actually do next week — not "care more".
4. In groups of four, read out your one change. As a group, pick the change that would make
   the biggest difference for the least effort.

## Activity 3: Balancing the three

**Work in groups of three or four. About 15 minutes.**

Professional practice needs **accountability**, **autonomy**, and **collegiality** together.
For each short situation below, decide which of the three is missing, and say what a school
could do about it.

- A teacher is told exactly what to say in every lesson and is checked daily against the
  script.
- A teacher plans alone, never sees another teacher teach, and never has their marking looked
  at by anyone.
- A teacher is free to teach however they like, and no one ever asks what their students have
  learned.

## Activity 4: First reflection — who am I becoming as a teacher?

**Individual writing. About 15 minutes. Keep this; you will add to it through the course.**

Write half a page answering these prompts honestly. It is not marked for "correct" answers.

1. Name one teacher from your own schooling who influenced how you think a class should be
   run. What did they do?
2. Complete the sentence: *"I believe students learn best when…"* — then give one reason you
   believe it.
3. Why did you choose teaching?
4. Name one thing about teaching you are unsure you can do well yet.

Date the page. You will look back at it in Unit 6 when you build your professional development
plan.
