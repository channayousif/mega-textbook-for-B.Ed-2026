# Understanding Teaching - assessment and review

## Unit summary

- A **profession** is defined by specialised knowledge, extended training and certification, a
  shared code of conduct, and accountability to a professional community - not by pay or
  prestige. Teaching meets all four features (Topic 1.1).
- **Professionalism** (how one teacher acts) and **professionalisation** (how the field
  organises itself) are related but different, and develop at different speeds. Against older
  professions, teaching is still building a longer training route, stronger bodies, and more
  room for judgement (Topic 1.1).
- Ideas about teaching have shifted from an **industrial** model - efficiency, control,
  compliance, covering the syllabus - toward **inquiry-based professionalism**, the habit of
  *noticing how pupils think and adjusting on evidence*. Hargreaves' "four ages" describe this
  as a long change, not a simple before-and-after; both models still appear in real schools
  (Topic 1.2).
- Teacher professionalism has **four dimensions**: a specialised knowledge base; a shared
  ethical code of conduct; the balance of **accountability, autonomy, and collegiality**; and
  organised professional bodies. Judging practice means looking across all four (Topic 1.3).
- **Teacher identity** is a developing sense of who you are as a teacher. It is shaped by
  sociocultural and policy context and by personal beliefs that filter what you take from
  training, and it keeps changing through **reflection** - examining practice from several
  viewpoints and questioning assumptions (Topic 1.4).

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of these is the best test of whether a job is a profession? *(Remember)*
   - A) It is respectable and reasonably well paid.
   - B) It rests on specialised knowledge, extended training, a code of conduct, and
     accountability to a professional community.
   - C) It is done mostly in an office or an institution.
   - D) It requires a uniform and fixed working hours.
2. A shopkeeper trains no one, follows no shared written code, and answers only to the owner.
   Against the four features of a profession, the shopkeeper's work is best described as: *(Understand)*
   - A) a profession, because it needs skill.
   - B) a semi-profession.
   - C) an occupation.
   - D) impossible to classify.
3. "Two years of B.Ed study builds knowledge of how particular learners understand particular
   topics." This is closest to which feature or dimension? *(Understand)*
   - A) A code of conduct.
   - B) Accountability to a professional community.
   - C) Pedagogical content knowledge within the specialised knowledge base.
   - D) Membership of a professional association.
4. A teacher shows strong personal skill, ethics, and judgement, but works in a system with
   weak licensing and few professional bodies. This situation best illustrates: *(Apply)*
   - A) high professionalism with still-developing professionalisation.
   - B) high professionalisation with low professionalism.
   - C) neither professionalism nor professionalisation.
   - D) that the teacher is not really a professional.
5. On the **industrial** model of teaching, success is measured mainly by: *(Remember)*
   - A) how well the teacher adjusts to evidence about learning.
   - B) covering the set syllabus on time and keeping order.
   - C) how much pupils collaborate.
   - D) the teacher's membership of an association.
6. A teacher runs a strict, quiet, teacher-led room but stops mid-lesson to address a mistake
   she notices most pupils making, changing her next step. Her teaching is best described as: *(Apply)*
   - A) purely industrial, because the room is strict.
   - B) inquiry-based, because she is noticing and adjusting on evidence.
   - C) neither, because inquiry-based teaching needs group work.
   - D) unprofessional, because she departed from her plan.
7. Which set names the three parts of the third dimension of teacher professionalism that must
   be held in balance? *(Remember)*
   - A) Efficiency, control, compliance.
   - B) Accountability, autonomy, collegiality.
   - C) Knowledge, ethics, associations.
   - D) Planning, teaching, assessing.
8. A teacher plans alone, is never observed, and never has her marking checked, though she is
   asked about pupil results. Which part of the third dimension is most clearly missing? *(Apply)*
   - A) Accountability.
   - B) Autonomy.
   - C) Collegiality.
   - D) Specialised knowledge.
9. Teacher identity, according to the unit, is best described as: *(Understand)*
   - A) a fixed set of personality traits a teacher is born with.
   - B) a developing sense of who one is as a teacher, shaped by context and beliefs.
   - C) the same thing as a teaching licence.
   - D) decided entirely by national policy.
10. A trainee keeps a page each week that names one belief a lesson challenged and what she
    will try differently. This practice is best described as: *(Apply)*
    - A) a diary, which is the same as reflective practice.
    - B) reflective practice, because the record is used to question an assumption and change
      what comes next.
    - C) not reflection, because it is written down.
    - D) reflection only if a mentor reads it.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In one or two sentences, define a **profession** and name its four distinguishing features.
   *(Understand)*
2. Give one example of a well-paid job that is **not** a profession and one modestly paid job
   that **is**, and justify each using the four features. *(Analyze)*
3. Explain the difference between **professionalism in teaching** and **professionalisation of
   teaching**, with one example of each. *(Understand)*
4. Name two things the industrial model of teaching values and one important thing it leaves
   out. *(Understand)*
5. A visitor says a strict, silent classroom cannot be inquiry-based. In two or three
   sentences, explain why the visitor may be wrong. *(Analyze)*
6. Name the four dimensions of teacher professionalism, and give one classroom example of
   *pedagogical content knowledge* that is not merely subject knowledge. *(Understand)*
7. Explain, with a short example, why accountability, autonomy, and collegiality must be held
   in balance rather than maximised one at a time. *(Analyze)*
8. Give two ways the sociocultural or policy context a teacher works in can shape their
   professional identity. *(Understand)*
9. Explain in two or three sentences why keeping a teaching diary is not, by itself,
   reflective practice. *(Understand)*
10. A teacher keeps perfect attendance, dresses smartly, and maintains strict discipline, but
    marks unfairly and never updates her methods. Analyse which dimensions of professionalism
    she is meeting and which she is failing. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Choose any job that is **not** teaching and that you know well. Evaluate it against the four
   features of a profession - feature by feature, with specific reasons - and reach a justified
   verdict of profession, occupation, or borderline. *(Evaluate)*
2. Using a lesson you know, write a short account of it as an **industrial** lesson and as an
   **inquiry-based** lesson, then argue for **one realistic change** that would move the first
   toward the second, explaining its likely effect on learning. *(Analyze / Create)*
3. You are given a one-page description of a teacher's practice. Analyse it against the **four
   dimensions** of teacher professionalism, identify one strong and one weak dimension with
   evidence, and recommend one change to strengthen the weak one. *(Analyze)*
4. Analyse **one influence** on your own developing teacher identity: describe it, explain how
   it is shaping your purposes and values, and name one belief from it you want to keep and one
   you want to question. *(Analyze)*
5. **Integrative.** You are given a description of a teacher in a Sindh government school.
   Classify the practice against *all four dimensions* of professionalism **and** the
   industrial-versus-inquiry frame, judge whether this is a "professional" practice overall,
   and justify your judgement using at least four specific details from the description.
   *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section to check your own work after you have attempted the questions.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - the four features are the test; A describes status, C and D describe surface
   conditions.
2. **C** - none of the four features is clearly met, so it is an occupation, not a
   semi-profession.
3. **C** - knowing how particular learners understand particular topics is pedagogical content
   knowledge.
4. **A** - strong individual practice (professionalism) in a system whose collective structures
   (professionalisation) are still developing.
5. **B** - the industrial model measures success by timely coverage and order.
6. **B** - the defining move of inquiry-based teaching is noticing and adjusting on evidence,
   regardless of how strict the room is.
7. **B** - accountability, autonomy, and collegiality are the three to balance.
8. **C** - she is answerable for results (accountability) and plans her own lessons (autonomy),
   but works with no colleague input, so collegiality is missing.
9. **B** - identity is developing and shaped by context and beliefs, not fixed or
   policy-decided.
10. **B** - using the record to question an assumption and change practice is what makes it
    reflection rather than a diary.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: a profession is an occupation built on specialised knowledge, extended training and
   certification, a shared code of conduct, and accountability to a professional community.
   **Marks (3):** 1 - usable definition; 2 - all four features named.
2. Model: names one well-paid non-profession (e.g. a property dealer - no required
   qualification, no shared code, no professional body) and one modestly paid profession (e.g.
   a government primary teacher - trained, certified, code-bound, standards-accountable), with
   feature-based reasons for each. **Marks (4):** 1 per example named; 1 per example justified
   against at least two features.
3. Model: professionalism = how an individual teacher acts (skill, ethics, judgement, learning);
   professionalisation = how the field builds training routes, licensing, standards, bodies;
   one example of each. **Marks (3):** 1 - distinction clear; 1 per apt example.
4. Model: values efficiency/timely coverage and order/control; leaves out responsiveness to how
   particular pupils are actually learning. **Marks (3):** 1 per value named; 1 for the
   omission.
5. Model: inquiry-based teaching is defined by noticing how pupils think and adjusting on
   evidence, not by seating or noise level; a strict teacher-led lesson can do exactly that.
   **Marks (3):** 1 - names the defining move; 2 - applies it to the strict-classroom case.
6. Model: specialised knowledge; ethical code of conduct; accountability–autonomy–collegiality
   balance; organised professional bodies. PCK example, e.g. teaching tenses to a multi-grade
   Class 3 through short spoken drills rather than written rules. **Marks (4):** 2 - all four
   dimensions; 2 - a genuine PCK example (not just subject knowledge).
7. Model: maximising accountability alone crushes autonomy and returns teaching to the
   industrial model; maximising autonomy alone with no collegiality leaves a teacher isolated
   and unchecked; balance keeps judgement and answerability together. **Marks (3):** 1 - states
   the balance principle; 2 - an example of what goes wrong when one is over-weighted.
8. Model: any two of - teacher status/trust in society; pay and conditions; pupils' language
   and cultural background; national policies and standards - each with a brief example.
   **Marks (2):** 1 per way with an example.
9. Model: a diary records events; reflection uses the record to surface and question an
   assumption and to change the next action. **Marks (3):** 1 - record vs use; 2 - the
   questioning-an-assumption step.
10. Model: she meets the surface expectations often mistaken for professionalism
    (punctuality, dress, discipline) but fails the ethical code of conduct (unfair marking) and
    the specialised-knowledge dimension's expectation of continued learning (never updates
    methods). **Marks (4):** 2 - dimensions met, with reasons; 2 - dimensions failed, with
    reasons.

### ERQ rubrics

1. **Analytic rubric (12).** *Feature-by-feature evaluation* (0–4: each of the four features
   judged with a job-specific reason) · *Verdict justified* (0–4, **Evaluate**: verdict follows
   from the four judgements; borderline named where features are partly met) · *Use of terms*
   (0–2) · *Structure and clarity* (0–2).
2. **Analytic rubric (12).** *Two contrasting accounts of one lesson* (0–3: the contrast is
   noticing-and-adjusting, not tone) · *Proposed change* (0–5, **Analyze/Create**: specific,
   do-able, with a reasoned effect on learning) · *Use of the industrial/inquiry terms* (0–2) ·
   *Clarity* (0–2).
3. **Analytic rubric (15).** *Analysis against all four dimensions* (0–6: each judged against a
   specific detail) · *One strong, one weak, justified* (0–5, **Analyze**: evidence from the
   text for both) · *Recommended change* (0–2: tied to the weak dimension) · *Structure and
   accurate terms* (0–2).
4. **Analytic rubric (10).** *Influence described concretely* (0–3) · *How it shapes purposes
   and values* (0–4, **Analyze**: a clear line from influence to the teacher being formed) ·
   *One belief to keep, one to question, with reasons* (0–3).
5. **Analytic rubric (20).** *Classification against all four dimensions* (0–6) · *Industrial
   vs inquiry judgement with evidence* (0–4) · *Overall judgement of "professional" practice*
   (0–6, **Evaluate**: weighs strengths and weaknesses across the frameworks rather than
   listing them) · *At least four specific details used* (0–2) · *Structure and clarity* (0–2).
