# Teacher Notes

## Teaching strategies

Introduce the Empathy–Context–Reflect–Action framework through Activity 1's grading scenario
before naming the model of moral behavior — students grasp the four-step process fastest when
it's tied to a concrete decision, not presented as an abstract list first. When discussing codes
of conduct, keep the four relationships (students, own practice, parents/community, colleagues)
visible as a simple list students can refer back to during Activity 2's sorting exercise. Handle
Activity 1's group-grading dilemma sensitively — some students may have their own experience with
unequal group work, so frame the discussion around the framework's steps rather than any one
student's situation.

## Practical work

Activity 2's sorting exercise doubles as light practical work: after pairs sort the four
scenarios, briefly compare answers across the room — dilemmas rarely fit one category cleanly,
and seeing where pairs disagree is itself a useful discussion about how real ethical situations
overlap.
