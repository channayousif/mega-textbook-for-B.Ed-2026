# Unit 2 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

This unit is deliberately concrete before abstract. Topic 2.1 names the parts you can
point at; Topic 2.2 explains the invisible behaviour that separates them (why saving
matters); Topic 2.3 moves from hardware to instructions; Topic 2.4 connects many machines.
If a cohort is weak, spend the extra session on 2.1 with real equipment in hand - the
later topics reuse its vocabulary constantly.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"The monitor is the computer."** The oldest one in the book. Probe by asking what the
   screen alone can do with the power on; then follow the cables. Name the system unit as
   the computer and the screen as its output device.
2. **"Memory and storage are the same thing."** The most consequential one for daily work.
   Probe with the power-cut story from Topic 2.2 before explaining; ask where an unsaved
   document lives, and make the group say "in RAM, and RAM forgets".
3. **"Software means only the programs you buy."** Probe with the boot-sequence question:
   who is in charge before any app opens? Lead them to the operating system as software.
4. **"The internet comes from the computer itself."** Probe by asking where the internet
   physically enters the building. Trace it to the router and the line; the lab's blinking
   boxes become real parts with names.
5. **"Daily app use equals machine knowledge."** Cite the Wallace and Clariana (2005)
   finding (average 60 percent on these concepts) and let trainees predict their own
   score before the unit's check questions.

## Managing the activities

**Topic 2.1 - Sort the donation (20 minutes, pairs or threes).** The trap is arguing by
brand or price. Insist the justification names the job. The two dual-role items
(interactive whiteboard, barcode reader if treated as input) are the point of the
activity; do not resolve them too early.

**Topic 2.2 - The power-cut drill (15 minutes, pairs).** The trap is vagueness ("it is
lost"). Require the hierarchy words: RAM, ROM, disk, cloud. The cloud-drive scenario
usually surfaces the best discussion - saved to the cloud means the local power cut does
not touch the copy.

**Topic 2.3 - Software census (20 minutes, groups of three).** The trap is counting only
visible icons. Push for the settings screen and the installed-apps list, where the system
software lives. The two-question test (runs the machine vs does a task) is the takeaway;
have groups write it on the board and compare wordings.

**Topic 2.4 - Map the lab's network (20 minutes, pairs).** If a real network exists, walk
it; the physical walk is worth more than the sketch. The diagnostic sentence at the end is
the assessment of the activity: it forces the cause-and-part connection.

## Working with the guide's strategies

This unit is the course's most natural home for the guide's **demonstrations** and
**hands-on computer exercises**. Demonstrate with the real thing wherever possible: open a
case or point at the ports, pull out a RAM stick and a flash drive and hold them side by
side, press save and then kill the power on a spare document (nothing teaches volatility
faster). **Lectures** carry the two hierarchies (memory, software); keep them to the
figures. **Interactive discussion** fits the misconception probes. **Question-answer
sessions** work well at the end of each topic using the Check your understanding items as
rapid rounds. **Case study analysis** is Topic 2.4's diagnostic sentence and the ERQ
scenarios: treat "the internet is down on computer 14" as a case to reason through aloud.

## Practical work and practicum links

The guide's practical work list maps onto this unit as follows.

- **Hands-on computer exercises:** this unit is the hands-on core of the course. Every
  trainee should, during the unit: identify the ports on a real machine, save a file to
  two different storage devices, open the installed-apps list and classify what they
  find, and (if any network exists) point at the switch and the router.
- **Individual assignments:** the digital-corner plan (Topic 2.1) and the spending
  evaluation (Topic 2.2) are individual.
- **Group assignments:** the donation sort, the software census and the network map are
  group tasks.
- **Internet research tasks:** the device prices for the digital-corner plan can be a
  short guided search; require a source for every price.
- **Presentations:** the staff-meeting explanation (Topic 2.3) is a ready-made short
  presentation; the network map (Topic 2.4) presents well on paper on the wall.

The practicum tasks (device inventory, RAM and storage numbers, OS and top applications,
network sketch) produce a school ICT profile that Unit 4's security audit and Unit 6's
emerging-technology briefing will reuse. Set them up before practicum and debrief after.
