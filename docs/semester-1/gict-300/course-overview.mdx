# Application of ICT - Course Overview

Welcome to GICT-300, Application of ICT: the Semester 1 course that makes you confident
with the technology your teaching life will run on. This page is the course's front
cover: what the course is about, what you will be able to do by the end, how it is
taught and assessed, and what to read. Each unit opens with its own page; start there.

## What this course is about

The course provides a foundational understanding of Information and Communication
Technology (ICT) and its applications for students across arts, sciences and social
sciences. It introduces fundamental concepts of computer literacy, computer systems,
hardware and software components, operating systems, internet usage, cybersecurity, and
ethical issues related to digital technologies. The course emphasizes practical use of
ICT tools for academic, professional and everyday purposes, while also exploring
emerging technologies and their impact on society and future careers.

## Course learning outcomes

By the end of this course you should be able to:

1. Explain the concept and importance of computer literacy in modern society.
2. Classify computers based on functionality and size.
3. Identify major hardware and software components of computer systems.
4. Describe basic operating system concepts.
5. Apply safe and responsible practices for cybersecurity and privacy.
6. Demonstrate ethical use of ICT and avoid plagiarism.
7. Use internet tools and digital communication platforms effectively.
8. Explain emerging technologies and their societal impact.

## The six units

1. **Introduction to Computer Literacy and ICT** - what computer literacy really means,
   how computers evolved through five generations, how they are classified, and their
   impact and applications across society.
2. **Computer Hardware and Software Fundamentals** - input, output and storage devices;
   memory and the storage hierarchy; system software versus application software; and
   the hardware and software that let computers communicate.
3. **Operating System Concepts** - what the operating system does and its types, file
   and process management, input/output management, and the kernel.
4. **Cyber security and Data Protection** - cyber threats and the habits that stop
   them, digital footprints, passwords and authentication, cloud computing basics, and
   the duty a school carries for the personal data it holds.
5. **Ethical and Responsible Use of ICT** - digital citizenship, online etiquette,
   intellectual property rights, and avoiding plagiarism.
6. **Internet Applications and Emerging Technologies** - browsers, search engines and
   communication tools; safe browsing; artificial intelligence, the Internet of Things,
   blockchain, and virtual and augmented reality.

## How the course is taught

The guide names five strategies, and this course uses all of them: **lectures** for the
foundational concepts, **interactive discussions** and **question-answer sessions** for
the misconceptions every topic opens, **demonstrations** on real machines wherever
possible (the process list, the save-and-power-cut lesson, a live search), and **case
study analysis** for the stories each unit is built on - the phished head teacher, the
three-people worksheet, the vendor visit.

The practical work runs alongside: **hands-on computer exercises** (this course's core),
**individual and group assignments**, **internet research tasks**, and
**presentations**. Every unit also carries a "Try this at your practicum school" task,
so the course builds a real picture of technology in one real school.

## Assessment

The default weighting for affiliated GECEs applies: **60 percent summative and 40
percent formative**. Each unit ends with a self-study bank of 10 multiple-choice, 10
restricted-response and 5 extended-response items with answers and marking guidance, and
each topic carries its own checks and summative tasks. The course guide itself sets no
separate assessment-criteria table; the pipeline default governs.

## Recommended reading

The five books and documents above are the course guide's recommended readings, listed
for scope and further reading only - never reproduced as content. They are print
monographs and official publications, so each unit also draws on verified open-access
sources (open textbooks, NIST and ITU standards, UNESCO frameworks and peer-reviewed
open-access articles), recorded per unit in the course's governance files.
