# Unit 5 assessment

## Unit summary

Digital citizenship is participation in digital society with rights and responsibilities
that arrive together: respect (etiquette, access), educate (literacy, communication) and
protect (rights, security, health) - and teachers carry it doubly, as citizens and as
the model pupils copy. Netiquette is the etiquette of written digital communication,
needed because text carries no tone and readers supply what is missing from their own
mood; its rules group into before you send (identify, state, check the recipient, do not
send heat), while you write (caps, punctuation, matter-not-person, match the channel)
and after you send (permanent, forwardable, screenshot included), checked at the moment
of sending by the tone triangle: reader, purpose, context. Intellectual property is the
work of the mind made protectable - copyright (automatic, long), patents (inventions,
brief), trademarks (identifiers, while used) - with fair use permitting educational
borrowing within weighed limits, and the public domain and Creative Commons opening free
ground between. Plagiarism is the academic wrong of presenting another's words or ideas
as your own, wider than word-for-word copying; the safe-assignment workflow (own words,
compare, paraphrase again, cite) and the visible-borrowing test make honesty a habit.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Digital citizenship is best defined as: *(Remember)*
   a) knowing how to use a computer
   b) participating in digital society safely, ethically and constructively
   c) having accounts on social media
   d) avoiding the internet at school

2. A school sets digital homework that requires a smartphone, and some pupils own none.
   The dimension this touches most directly is: *(Apply)*
   a) digital security
   b) digital access
   c) digital health
   d) digital literacy

3. In the rights-and-responsibilities view, the right to express yourself online arrives
   tied to: *(Understand)*
   a) the right to be anonymous
   b) the responsibility not to use expression to harm others
   c) the responsibility to post daily
   d) the right to report others

4. A whole message written in capitals is read as shouting because: *(Understand)*
   a) capitals are louder on the screen
   b) readers supply the missing tone, and capitals signal aggression
   c) the internet slows capitals down
   d) capitals are against the law

5. The best action when a message makes you angry is: *(Apply)*
   a) reply at once so the matter is settled
   b) reply in capitals so you are taken seriously
   c) write the reply, wait, then decide whether to send
   d) forward it to the group for support

6. The three corners of the tone triangle are: *(Remember)*
   a) reader, purpose, context
   b) sender, receiver, moderator
   c) speed, length, spelling
   d) caps, punctuation, signature

7. Copyright protection begins: *(Remember)*
   a) when the author registers the work
   b) automatically, when the work is created
   c) one year after publication
   d) only for works published commercially

8. A teacher photocopies an entire commercial workbook for the whole class. Under the
   fair-use factors, this is: *(Apply)*
   a) fair use, because it is a school
   b) fair use, because no money was charged
   c) unlikely to be fair use: whole work, market effect
   d) not covered by copyright at all

9. Copying a paragraph but changing a few words, with no citation, is: *(Understand)*
   a) fine, because the words are different
   b) paraphrasing
   c) plagiarism: the structure and ideas are another's
   d) fair use

10. The safe-assignment workflow's final step, for every borrowed idea, is: *(Remember)*
    a) memorise the source
    b) cite the source
    c) delete the source
    d) change the author's name

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain why digital citizenship is more than "being polite online", using one example
   that is not about politeness. *(Understand)*
2. A pupil forwards a classmate's photograph to other groups as a joke. Name the right
   violated and the responsibility ignored, and state the harm in one sentence. *(Apply)*
3. Explain why writing needs netiquette more than speech does. *(Understand)*
4. Diagnose the netiquette failures in this message: "URGENT!!! WHY IS THE MEETING
   CHANGED?? - sent 11:40 p.m. to the whole staff group". *(Analyze)*
5. State the difference between plagiarism and copyright infringement with one example
   of each. *(Understand)*
6. A teacher copies one poem, with the poet's name, into a class worksheet; another
   photocopies a whole workbook. Judge each case using the fair-use factors. *(Apply)*
7. Explain what "self-plagiarism" is and why resubmitting your own old essay is treated
   as dishonest. *(Understand)*
8. A survey of 485 students found easy web access to be the main driver of plagiarism.
   Analyse what this implies for how a school should teach about it. *(Analyze)*
9. Explain the safe-assignment workflow in order, naming all four steps and what each
   step protects against. *(Understand)*
10. A summary keeps another author's sentence structure with new words and no citation.
    Identify the form of plagiarism and write the two-part repair. *(Apply)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Draft the first five articles of a "class code of responsible ICT use" for a Class 7
   group, each pairing one right with its responsibility, each with the classroom
   situation it answers. *(Analyze)*
2. Prepare a "netiquette card" for the staff room: five rules each for email to parents
   and for class groups, plus one worked before-and-after example. *(Evaluate)*
3. Analyse a real or constructed plagiarism case: what was plagiarised and in which
   form, which rules were broken, what the institution did, and the one habit that
   would have prevented it. *(Analyze)*
4. Design a one-page "honest worksheet" guide for your colleagues: what a teacher must
   do to use others' materials fairly - sources, fair-use judgement, Creative Commons -
   and how to present borrowed material to pupils. *(Analyze)*
5. Using the whole unit, evaluate the statement: "Responsible ICT use is citizenship,
   not compliance." Draw on rights and responsibilities, netiquette's purpose, and the
   honest-use workflow as evidence. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. b - citizenship is participation with rights and responsibilities, not skill alone.
2. b - unequal device and data access is the access dimension.
3. b - expression arrives tied to non-harm; the photograph story is the standing example.
4. b - text strips tone; the reader supplies it, and capitals read as aggression.
5. c - draft, wait, decide: heat writes what calm would not.
6. a - reader, purpose, context.
7. b - copyright is automatic on creation.
8. c - whole-work copying with market effect fails the fair-use weighing.
9. c - synonym-swapping keeps the structure and ideas: plagiarism.
10. b - every borrowed idea gets a citation; kept words get quotation marks too.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: citizenship covers rights, responsibilities and harms beyond manners - for
   example the forwarded photograph is about consent and harm, not politeness; or the
   smartphone-only homework is about access and fairness. **Marks (2):** 1 for the
   distinction; 1 for a non-politeness example.
2. Model: the right to privacy (and to be free from harassment) is violated; the
   responsibility to respect others' data is ignored; the harm is the permanent,
   forwardable humiliation of the photographed pupil. **Marks (3):** 1 per element.
3. Model: speech carries tone, face and pause; writing carries only words, so the
   reader fills the gap from their own mood - netiquette exists to add deliberately
   what text leaves out. **Marks (2):** 1 for the stripping effect; 1 for the
   reader-supplies-gap point.
4. Model: capitals (shouting); multiple punctuation (impatience); urgency pressure;
   wrong hour (11:40 p.m.); wrong audience (whole staff group for a personal query).
   **Marks (3):** 1 for any three named failures.
5. Model: plagiarism is presenting another's words or ideas as your own (an
   uncited paraphrase in an essay); copyright infringement is the legal wrong of
   reproducing a protected work beyond fair use (photocopying a whole workbook).
   **Marks (2):** 1 per concept with an example.
6. Model: the poem is likely fair use - educational purpose, small portion, named
   source, negligible market effect; the whole workbook fails - the entire work, direct
   market substitution. **Marks (3):** 1 per judgement; 1 for naming the factors used.
7. Model: self-plagiarism is presenting your own earlier submitted work as new; it is
   dishonest because the new audience expects original work and the assessment claims
   fresh effort. **Marks (2):** 1 for the definition; 1 for the honesty rationale.
8. Model: if access drives plagiarism, detection and punishment alone will not work;
   schools must teach the workflow and set tasks that reward original thought, and
   teachers must model citation, because the environment makes copying the path of
   least resistance. **Marks (3):** 1 for the environment reading; 2 for the
   implication argued.
9. Model: take notes in your own words; compare your text with the source; if still
   close, paraphrase again; cite the source (quotation marks plus citation for kept
   words). **Marks (4):** 1 per step in order.
10. Model: the form is mosaic/patchwork plagiarism (structure kept, words swapped);
    the repair is paraphrase again until only the idea is borrowed, then cite - or
    quote directly with quotation marks and cite. **Marks (3):** 1 for the form; 2 for
    the two-part repair.

### ERQ rubrics

1. **Class code (Analyze).** Criteria: *Five one-sentence articles, workable* (0-3);
   *Each pairs a right with a responsibility, not a bare prohibition* (0-4, Analyse);
   *Each situation line realistic for Class 7* (0-2); *Clarity* (0-1). Total 10.
2. **Netiquette card (Evaluate).** Criteria: *Ten distinct imperative rules* (0-4);
   *The before-and-after example repairs real failures by the named rules* (0-4,
   Analyse); *Fits a card, staff-room language* (0-2). Total 10.
3. **Plagiarism case (Analyze).** Criteria: *Facts summarised accurately and neutrally*
   (0-2); *The form of plagiarism identified from this topic's list* (0-4, Analyse);
   *Academic and legal rules distinguished* (0-2); *The preventive habit concrete and
   from the workflow* (0-2). Total 10.
4. **Honest worksheet guide (Analyze).** Criteria: *Sources and citation practice
   specified* (0-3); *Fair-use judgement explained with the factors* (0-3); *Creative
   Commons and public domain included as legitimate ground* (0-2); *Presentation to
   pupils addressed* (0-2). Total 10.
5. **Integrative evaluation (Evaluate).** Criteria: *Draws on all three topics: rights
   and responsibilities, netiquette, honest use* (0-4); *Each drawn part used
   accurately* (0-3); *The citizenship-not-compliance judgement defended as a
   synthesis* (0-2); *Clarity* (0-1). Total 10.
