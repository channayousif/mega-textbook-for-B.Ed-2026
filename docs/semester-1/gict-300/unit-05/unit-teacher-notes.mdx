# Unit 5 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

Three topics, one arc: identity (citizenship), behaviour (netiquette), honesty
(intellectual property and plagiarism). Topic 5.3 is the longest and the most
exam-relevant; protect it if time is short. The unit's summative arc - a class code, a
netiquette card, an honest-worksheet guide - is designed to produce artefacts a trainee
can carry into practicum, so keep the products concrete.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Netiquette is just politeness."** Probe with the photograph story from 5.1 and the
   11 p.m. capitals exchange from 5.2: neither is about manners; both are about harm,
   tone and permanence.
2. **"If it is on the internet, it is free to use."** The classic. Probe with the
   worksheet case in 5.3: three people made it, one name is on it. Then introduce
   copyright's automatic arrival and fair use's limits.
3. **"Plagiarism is only copying whole paragraphs."** Probe with the synonym-swap case:
   why is it still plagiarism when the words differ? Lead to structure-and-ideas as the
   test.
4. **"Citation is decoration for weak work."** Reverse it: citation shows what the
   writer read and lets the reader check - it is a strength display, not an apology.
5. **"Angry replies settle matters."** The 11 p.m. exchange is the standing cure; ask
   what each side actually wanted, and whether the drafts served it.

## Managing the activities

**Topic 5.1 - Rights and responsibilities cards (15 minutes, pairs).** Make the ten
cards beforehand. The pairing task is deliberately concrete; the photograph-story
application at step 3 is the transfer test. Do not accept "the pupil was wrong" - require
the named right and the named responsibility.

**Topic 5.2 - The message clinic (15 minutes, pairs).** Write the three messages
yourself with realistic detail. The trap is rewriting that only softens tone; require
the same legitimate purpose achieved (the parent still gets the mark updated). The tone
triangle is the closing check.

**Topic 5.3 - The two-column workshop (20 minutes, pairs).** The careless-summary half
feels transgressive and memorable - let it. The marking swap teaches the five cases
better than a lecture. Finish by writing the citation line together on the board, in
full, slowly.

## Working with the guide's strategies

**Case study analysis** carries this unit: the photograph, the 11 p.m. exchange, the
three-people worksheet and the ERQ plagiarism case are all cases - open each topic with
its case. **Interactive discussion** suits the misconception probes. **Question-answer
sessions** fit the five-cases table in 5.3 as rapid rounds. **Demonstrations** work for
the workflow: run a live paraphrase-and-compare on screen, showing how the skeleton
disappears sentence by sentence. **Lectures** are needed only for the IP instruments -
keep them to the figure and one example each.

## Practical work and practicum links

- **Hands-on computer exercises:** the worksheet audit (5.3) against a real practicum
  worksheet; optionally, finding one Creative Commons resource and reading its licence
  conditions.
- **Individual assignments:** the class code (5.1) and the honest-worksheet guide (5.3).
- **Group assignments:** the rights cards, the message clinic and the two-column
  workshop.
- **Internet research tasks:** finding a reported plagiarism case for ERQ-3, with a
  source required.
- **Presentations:** the netiquette card (5.2) presents well; the class code can be
  presented as if to the Class 7 it was written for.

The practicum tasks (the school's online-behaviour rules, the message-thread audit, the
worksheet audit) close the loop with Unit 4's data inventory and feed Unit 6's
emerging-technology briefing, where each new technology is asked the same citizenship
questions.
