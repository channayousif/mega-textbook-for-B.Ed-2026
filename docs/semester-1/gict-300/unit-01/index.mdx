# Introduction to Computer Literacy and ICT

Almost every job a teacher does today touches a computer somewhere: attendance in a
spreadsheet, results in a database, lesson material from the internet, a message to parents
on a phone. This unit builds the foundation for the whole course. It explains what computer
literacy really means (it is much more than typing), how computers developed from calculating
tools into the machines in your pocket, how we sort them into kinds, and what they have
changed in society, especially in Pakistan.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain what **computer literacy** means and name the skills it covers beyond typing.
- Explain why **ICT** matters in education, work and society, with examples from Pakistan.
- Trace the **evolution** of computers from early calculating devices and describe the five
  **generations** of computers and the technology that defines each.
- **Classify** computers by functionality (analog, digital, hybrid) and by size and power
  (supercomputer to embedded computer).
- Analyse the **impact** of computers and the internet on society, both benefits and
  challenges, and name **applications of ICT** in education, health, agriculture, banking and
  government.

## Prerequisite knowledge

No prior computer course is assumed. The unit starts from the level of a student who has
completed intermediate education in Pakistan and has used a mobile phone.

## How to use this unit

Work through the four topics in order; each one builds on the last. Every topic is a
complete learning cycle: it opens with a real classroom situation, explains the ideas, gives
you a collaborative activity, checks your understanding, and ends with a task to try at your
practicum school. Use the glossary terms (marked with underlining) as your reference points,
and attempt every "Check your understanding" question before reading on.
