# Unit 6 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

Topics 6.1 and 6.2 sharpen tools the trainees already hold (search, communication,
addresses); 6.3 and 6.4 open the emerging boxes (AI, IoT, blockchain, VR/AR). The unit's
spine is the closing question - what does this do for this lesson, at this cost, with
these cautions? - which the final ERQ turns on the whole course. Start the briefing
notebook in 6.1 and keep it fed; the practicum task and ERQ-3/ERQ-4 both draw on it.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"The browser is the internet."** Probe with the journey figure: who asks, who
   carries, who answers? Name the search engine as itself a website.
2. **"The first result is the best result."** The Khairpur story is the cure; run the
   four-minute search activity before explaining.
3. **"The padlock means safe."** The classic half-truth: encryption is not honesty. A
   fake site can have a padlock.
4. **"AI understands like a person."** Probe with the fluent-error case: how can
   something sound right and be wrong? Pattern prediction, not comprehension.
5. **"Blockchain means bitcoin."** Separate the ledger design from its first
   application; then ask the trust question.
6. **"VR and AR are only for games."** One classroom example of each is enough.

## Managing the activities

**Topic 6.1 - The four-minute search (20 minutes, pairs).** The paired contrast (naive
vs planned) is the whole point; do not let Pair A "cheat" by planning. The swap round
with a new need consolidates. Collect the three search rules on the board.

**Topic 6.2 - Address reading drill (15 minutes, pairs).** Construct the eight addresses
beforehand, with real domains mixed among plausible fakes. Insist verdicts cite the
domain, not the words. The "which would phishing love" step teaches the attacker's view,
which is the memorable one.

**Topic 6.3 - The briefing notebook (20 minutes, groups of three).** This activity seeds
the unit's summative products directly. Keep the skeletons; the class assembles the
practicum briefing from them in the last session.

**Topic 6.4 - Slogan translation (15 minutes, pairs).** The slogans are deliberately
vendor-shaped. The test of success is that every translated claim is checkable. Extend
with one real advertisement if time allows.

## Working with the guide's strategies

**Demonstrations** lead this unit: live searches (planned vs naive), a live address
dissection, an AI drafting session with the errors found on screen, and - if any device
exists - a biometric machine named and questioned. **Case study analysis** is the
Khairpur search, the fee-portal SMS, the vendor offers in 6.4. **Interactive discussion**
suits the misconception probes. **Question-answer sessions** fit the address drill and
the check lists. **Lectures** are needed only for blockchain's seals and the VR/AR
distinction - keep each to its figure.

## Practical work and practicum links

- **Hands-on computer exercises:** the planned search, the address drill, and (if
  possible) one AI drafting session with error-finding.
- **Individual assignments:** the search-and-communicate guide (6.1), the
  read-the-address card (6.2), the emerging-technology briefing (6.3/6.4).
- **Group assignments:** the four-minute search and the briefing notebook.
- **Internet research tasks:** finding one real AR-in-education example with a source;
  finding a reported fake-portal case.
- **Presentations:** the three-offer assessment (6.4) presents well as a mock
  purchase meeting.

The practicum tasks (the school's communication tools, its portals and how staff reach
them, its smart devices and purchase pattern) complete the school ICT profile begun in
Unit 2 and audited in Unit 4 - the course ends with a full picture of one real school's
technology, seen through every lens the six units built.
