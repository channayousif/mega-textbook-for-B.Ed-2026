# Quantitative Reasoning in Everyday Life

The course closes where its students live: money, media and decisions. The tools are
all from earlier units - percentages from Unit 2, expressions from Unit 3, the
check-habit from Units 1 and 4, the data questions from Unit 5 - now pointed at a
fair stall's ledger, a toothpaste advertisement, and a father choosing a school. The
final lesson is the quietest one: numbers inform decisions; they never replace the
decider.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Compute profit, loss and their percentages from a ledger, simple interest for
  saving and borrowing, and build a five-part monthly budget with savings before
  spending.
- Audit a numeric claim in an advertisement or news item with the five questions,
  and rebuild a hidden interest rate from an instalment.
- Run the decision loop (question, evidence, options, trade-offs) on a school or
  policy choice, reading the standard education indicators with their bases and
  blind spots.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-5: the four-step strategy, percentages, expressions and inequalities, the
reasonableness check, and the data questions. This unit adds no new mathematics.

## How to use this unit

Work the topics in order; each builds directly on the last (the instalment example
opens Topic 6.2; the census figures return in 6.3). The practicum tasks collect a
canteen ledger, an advertisement and a school decision - ERQ work will use all
three. This is the closing unit: when you finish it, read your Unit 1 self-assessment
again and mark what changed. Glossary terms are underlined; hover or tap them for
the Urdu definition.
