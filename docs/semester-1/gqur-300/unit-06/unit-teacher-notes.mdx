# Unit 6 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

Topic 6.1 gives the financial arithmetic; 6.2 points it outward at persuasion; 6.3
points it at decisions. The instalment example is the bridge from 6.1 to 6.2, and the
census figures return from Unit 5 in 6.3 - teach the three topics in order and name
the bridges. The unit closes the course; reserve the last session's final twenty
minutes for ERQ-5's reflection on the Unit 1 checklist.

## Teaching strategies from the guide

- Use the guide's real-life case-study mode throughout: the fair stall, the
  toothpaste advertisement and the school choice are all real situations - invite the
  class's own before revealing each worked example.
- Run the fair stall audit (Topic 6.1) as guided practice with the ledger on every
  table; the recommendation step is where arithmetic becomes judgement.
- Use brainstorming for the advertisement audit (Topic 6.2): collect the class's
  questions before showing the five-question table, and let them discover they
  already asked most of them.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Profit is whatever money comes in."** The 7,200-rupee takings exist for this;
   ask the class what the stall made before showing the cost lines.
2. **"A budget is a restriction."** Ask who has ended a month with money left over
   without planning one. The budget is thirty decisions made once, calmly.
3. **"Numbers in the media must be true."** Show the two-panel toothpaste chart from
   Topic 6.2; both panels are arithmetically correct.
4. **"The data decided."** The committee phrase, met in Topic 6.3; ask the class who
   signed the order. Data informs; a person decides and owns the trade-off.

## Running the activities

- **The fair stall audit (Topic 6.1)**: each group's ledger differs slightly so the
  recommendations cannot be copied; the vote at the end is on the best-argued
  recommendation, not the highest profit.
- **The advertisement audit (Topic 6.2)**: insist on real-style claims (bring
  clippings if possible); invented advertisements are too honest.
- **The school choice panel (Topic 6.3)**: the group-varying extra fact is the point -
  it shows how one new piece of evidence re-runs the whole loop.

## Practical work

Group work in the three activities; the group assignment is the full decision-loop
dossier on a real school question; the individual assignments are the canteen ledger,
the advertisement audit and the practicum decision reconstruction; presentations come
from the school choice panels.

## Assessment guidance

The bank's traps are deliberate: MCQ-02 and RRQ-4 punish the takings-is-profit error,
MCQ-07 and RRQ-6 the hidden rate, MCQ-08 and RRQ-8 the small-base rate. ERQ-3 has no
single fixed answer - only correct reasoning - and the rubric grades the loop, the
computation and the named changing-evidence. Say this aloud before the class attempts
it; the unit's last lesson is that a defensible decision, not a single right one, is
the goal.

## Practicum links

The three artefacts (ledger, advertisement, decision reconstruction) feed ERQs 2-5.
The course closes with the Unit 1 self-assessment re-read; collect the trainees'
"what changed" notes - they are the course's own evidence, and the next cohort's
first reading.
