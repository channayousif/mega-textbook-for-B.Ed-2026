# Unit 5 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

The three topics are the data cycle's three turns: collect and organize (5.1), display
(5.2), summarize and interpret (5.3). Do not let trainees skip to displays before they
have collected something real; a chart of invented numbers teaches nothing about the
cycle's honesty lessons. The unit's three weeks close with the census figures, which
deserve a full session.

## Teaching strategies from the guide

- Use the guide's real-life case-study mode: the attendance-dip investigation and the
  marksheet clinic are real contexts; ask trainees for their school's equivalents.
- Run the travel-to-school survey (Topic 5.1) as genuine group work with real
  classmates as respondents - the bias discussion only lands when the bias was
  possible.
- Use question-answer on the display-choice diagram (Topic 5.2): give a question, take
  the display choice from the class before revealing the rule.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Data means numbers."** Hold up the attendance register: mostly ticks and words.
   Numbers arrive at the summarize step, when the facts are counted.
2. **"A graph's impression is the data."** Redraw one bar chart with a truncated axis
   on the board and ask the class what changed. Nothing in the data changed; the
   construction did.
3. **"The mean is the average."** The wedding-order takings example exists for this
   moment: let the class compute both averages and argue which one a newspaper should
   print.
4. **"The published number settles it."** The 57.54 percent literacy figure is the
   probe: ask what it hides before telling them. The female-male gap and the
   out-of-school rate are in the same report.

## Running the activities

- **The travel-to-school survey (Topic 5.1)**: enforce the swap so no group surveys
  its own question; the master-table combination shows sampling effects for free.
- **Display the same data three ways (Topic 5.2)**: the swap question ("which single
  question does this display answer best?") is the assessment; drawing neatly is not.
- **Marksheet clinic (Topic 5.3)**: keep one marksheet outlier-free so the class sees
  that the three averages can also coincide - and that the gap between them, not any
  one of them, is the diagnostic.

## Practical work

Group work in the three activities; a group assignment running the full data cycle on
a school question of the group's choosing; the individual assignment is the Topic 5.3
practicum marksheet; presentations come from ERQ-5's own-cycle reports.

## Assessment guidance

The bank's traps are deliberate: MCQ-03 tests the total check, MCQ-07 the truncated
axis, RRQ-9 and ERQ-3 the census reading. When marking ERQ-3, reward trainees who
notice that the 2.97-point rise is dwarfed by the 14.02-point gap: comparing a number
to its right comparator is the unit's highest skill. ERQ-5 must use real data; a
cycle run on invented numbers caps at half marks.

## Practicum links

Trainees collect three artefacts: a register tally (Topic 5.1), a displayed chart or
table with a construction note (Topic 5.2), and a marksheet with the honest-average
sentences (Topic 5.3). All three feed Unit 6's decision-making work, so date them.
