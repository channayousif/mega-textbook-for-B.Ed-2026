# Unit 5 assessment

## Unit summary

Data are raw facts collected for a question, by observation, survey or records; tallies
and frequency tables organize them so summary is simple counting, and a table whose
total does not close names a collection problem. Each display answers one kind of
question - bar charts compare categories, pie charts show shares of one whole, line
graphs track change over time - and reading a display means reading its construction.
The mean is the fair share outliers drag, the median the middle that ignores them, the
mode the most frequent value; the honest average depends on the data. A published
statistic is read with five questions: who collected it, how, compared to what, on what
base, and what is missing - and Sindh's 57.54 percent literacy against a 46.29 percent
out-of-school rate shows how much one number can hide.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The five stages of the data cycle, in order, are: *(Remember)*
   a) collect, question, summarize, organize, interpret
   b) question, collect, organize, summarize, interpret
   c) question, organize, collect, interpret, summarize
   d) collect, organize, question, summarize, interpret

2. To learn how many pupils arrive after the bell each day this week, the best method
   is: *(Apply)*
   a) a survey of pupils
   b) observation at the gate
   c) the census report
   d) a pie chart

3. A survey of 40 returns frequencies 12, 14 and 9. The table tells you: *(Apply)*
   a) the survey is complete
   b) 5 responses are unrecorded
   c) the mode is 14
   d) the mean is 35

4. The display that best shows each travel mode's share of the class is: *(Apply)*
   a) a bar chart
   b) a line graph
   c) a pie chart
   d) a tally

5. The display that best tracks attendance across a week is: *(Apply)*
   a) a pie chart
   b) a line graph
   c) a pictograph
   d) a frequency table

6. A category that is 30 percent of the whole takes a pie-slice angle of: *(Apply)*
   a) 30 degrees
   b) 90 degrees
   c) 108 degrees
   d) 144 degrees

7. A bar chart whose vertical axis starts at 10 instead of 0: *(Understand)*
   a) is correct if labelled
   b) exaggerates small differences between bars
   c) makes bars harder to read
   d) shows shares of a whole

8. For the takings 4,000, 4,200, 4,100, 4,300, 39,000 rupees, the honest "typical day"
   is the: *(Apply)*
   a) mean, 11,120 rupees
   b) median, 4,200 rupees
   c) mode, 4,000 rupees
   d) total, 55,600 rupees

9. The only average that works for categories (like travel modes) is the: *(Remember)*
   a) mean
   b) median
   c) mode
   d) range

10. Sindh's 2023 literacy rate of 57.54 percent is measured on the base: *(Understand)*
    a) all ages
    b) 10 years and above
    c) 15 years and above
    d) enrolled pupils only

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Name the three data collection methods and one school question each suits. *(Understand)*
2. A survey of 40 gives frequencies 12, 14, 9. State what the total tells you and the
   next move. *(Analyze)*
3. Compute the mean, median and mode of: 20, 45, 50, 55, 60, 62, 62, 70, 70, 70, 96.
   *(Apply)*
4. A pictograph shows 3 bus icons for Class A and 5 for Class B, key "one icon = 10
   pupils". State the counts and one way it misleads without the key. *(Analyze)*
5. For the class strengths 38, 42, 35, 40, 45: compute the median and the mode, and
   state which classes (if any) share it. *(Apply)*
6. A bar chart's axis starts at 10. State what the picture exaggerates, in one
   sentence. *(Understand)*
7. Compute the pie-slice angle for a category that is 22.5 percent of the whole,
   showing the working. *(Apply)*
8. Explain in two sentences why the median is preferred to the mean for house prices
   or incomes. *(Understand)*
9. Sindh's male literacy is 64.23 percent and female 50.21 percent. Compute the gap,
   and say what the single headline number 57.54 hides. *(Analyze)*
10. Name the five questions to ask of any published statistic. *(Remember)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. The headteacher wants to know the school's most common travel mode. Design and run
   a mini-survey of 20 pupils (fixed choices: walk, van, rickshaw, bicycle, other):
   present your frequency table with the total check, state the most common mode and
   its percentage, and identify one way your collection could be biased and how you
   would fix it in a rerun. *(Analyze)*
2. The school's five class strengths are 38, 42, 35, 40 and 45. Name the best display
   for "which class is largest and by how much" and draw or describe it precisely;
   name one display that would be a poor choice and say what it would hide or
   exaggerate; and compute the pie-slice angle for the largest class's share of total
   pupils, showing the working. *(Analyze)*
3. The 2023 census reports Sindh's literacy (10 years and above) as 57.54 percent
   overall, 64.23 percent male and 50.21 percent female, against 54.57 percent in
   2017; the out-of-school rate (ages 5 to 16) is 46.29 percent (PBS, 2023). Compute
   the male-female gap in percentage points; state the rise since 2017 and say whether
   it is large or small compared with the gap; and write the two interpretation
   questions a teacher should ask before quoting the 57.54 percent figure to a
   parents' meeting, and what each guards against. *(Analyze)*
4. Miss Farhana's class of 11 scored 20, 45, 50, 55, 60, 62, 62, 70, 70, 70, 96.
   Compute all three averages; explain what the ordering of mean below median below
   mode reveals about the class; and write the two sentences you would say at a
   parents' meeting about which average you are quoting and why. *(Analyze)*
5. Using the frequency table your group built in the Topic 5.1 activity (or a fresh
   survey of 20 classmates), present the whole data cycle for your own question: the
   question, the collection method and its bias risk, the organized table, the best
   display for it, the honest average (if the data are numerical), and your
   interpretation - then evaluate which stage of your cycle was weakest and what you
   would redo. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **b** - question, collect, organize, summarize, interpret.
2. **b** - watching arrivals records what actually happens; a survey asks what pupils
   remember.
3. **b** - 12 + 14 + 9 = 35, so 5 of 40 are unrecorded.
4. **c** - shares of one whole are the pie chart's job.
5. **b** - change over time is the line graph's job.
6. **c** - 30% x 360 = 108 degrees.
7. **b** - the truncated axis stretches small differences into large-looking ones.
8. **b** - one wedding-order outlier drags the mean to 11,120, which no ordinary day
   resembled.
9. **c** - the mode is the most frequent value and works for categories too.
10. **b** - the census literacy rate counts persons 10 years and above.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: observation (arrivals at the gate), survey (which snack to stock), records
   (last month's absences) - or equivalent pairings with reasons. **Marks (3):** 1 per
   method with a fitting question.
2. Model: the total 35 against the sample 40 shows 5 responses unrecorded; the next
   move is to find them (or rerun), not to summarize. **Marks (2):** 1 - the gap
   identified; 1 - the correct next move.
3. Model: total 660, mean 60; median 62 (sixth of 11 sorted); mode 70 (three
   times). **Marks (3):** 1 each.
4. Model: Class A 30, Class B 50; without the key the icons suggest a 3-to-5 count (a
   20-pupil error). **Marks (2):** 1 - both counts; 1 - the missing-key danger.
5. Model: sorted 35, 38, 40, 42, 45: median 40; no repeated value, so no mode.
   **Marks (2):** 1 - median 40; 1 - no mode stated.
6. Model: differences between bars look larger than the values justify, because the
   bars' visible heights start from 10. **Marks (2):** 1 - exaggeration named; 1 -
   mechanism.
7. Model: 22.5% x 360 = 81 degrees. **Marks (2):** 1 - method; 1 - value.
8. Model: a few very high prices drag the mean above what almost any house costs; the
   median reports the middle of the market, unaffected by the extremes. **Marks (2):**
   1 - outlier effect on mean; 1 - median's resistance.
9. Model: gap = 64.23 - 50.21 = 14.02 percentage points; the headline 57.54 hides the
   near-10-point-plus divide between male and female literacy (and the 46.29 percent
   out-of-school rate). **Marks (2):** 1 - gap computed; 1 - what is hidden named.
10. Model: who collected it, how was it collected, compared to what, what is the base,
    what is missing. **Marks (5):** 1 per question.

### ERQ rubrics

1. Travel survey. Analytic rubric: *Table with check* (0-2: correct table totalling
   20); *Mode and percentage* (0-2: most common mode with a correct percentage);
   *Bias identified* (0-2, **Analyze**: a real collection bias with a concrete fix).
   Total 6.
2. Class strengths. Analytic rubric: *Best display* (0-2: bar chart, precisely
   described, axis from zero); *Poor choice analysed* (0-2, **Analyze**: e.g. a pie
   chart hides "by how much"); *Slice angle* (0-2: total 200, 45/200 = 22.5%, angle
   81 degrees with working). Total 6.
3. Sindh literacy. Analytic rubric: *Gap* (0-2: 14.02 percentage points); *Rise
   compared* (0-2, **Analyze**: 2.97 points, dwarfed by the gap at more than four
   times its size); *Questions* (0-2: two interpretation questions with correct
   guards). Total 6.
4. Marksheet. Analytic rubric: *Averages* (0-3: mean 60, median 62, mode 70, 1 each);
   *Ordering explained* (0-2, **Analyze**: mean below median below mode reveals a low
   tail pulling the mean down); *Parents' sentences* (0-2: names the average quoted
   with an honest reason). Total 7.
5. Own data cycle. Analytic rubric: *Cycle presented* (0-3: question, method, table,
   display all present and fitting); *Honest average* (0-2: correct choice for the
   data type); *Interpretation* (0-2: the answer stated with its limits); *Weakest
   stage evaluated* (0-3, **Analyze**: the weakest stage named with evidence and a
   redo plan). Total 10.
