# Quantitative Reasoning-I - Course Overview

This course develops prospective teachers' quantitative reasoning skills required for
academic study, professional practice, and everyday problem solving. It emphasizes logical
thinking, numerical sense, data interpretation, and the application of mathematical
concepts in real-life contexts. The course strengthens foundational mathematical
understanding and prepares future teachers to model quantitative reasoning skills for
their students.

## Course learning outcomes

On completing this course, you should be able to:

1. Demonstrate numerical and algebraic reasoning.
2. Solve real-world quantitative problems.
3. Apply proportional and logical reasoning.
4. Communicate mathematical ideas clearly.
5. Interpret tables, graphs, and statistical information.

These outcomes are referenced throughout the course as SLO:GQUR-300-1-1 through
SLO:GQUR-300-5-5 (course outcome, then unit).

## Units

| Unit | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Quantitative Reasoning | what QR is, number sense, estimation, logical reasoning, problem-solving strategies |
| 2 | Numbers and Operations | the number system, ratios, proportions, percentages, powers and roots in daily life |
| 3 | Algebraic Reasoning | patterns to expressions, linear equations and inequalities, algebra in problem solving |
| 4 | Measurement and Geometry | metric units, perimeter, area, volume, basic shapes, real measurement tasks |
| 5 | Data Analysis and Statistics | the data cycle, displays, averages, reading published statistics |
| 6 | Quantitative Reasoning in Everyday Life | financial literacy, media claims, decision-making with data |

## Assessment

Assessment follows the GECE default: **60% summative / 40% formative** (Constitution Art.
III.7). Each unit ends with a bank of 10 multiple-choice, 10 restricted-response and 5
extended-response items with marking guidance, and each topic carries its own
check-your-understanding and summative task.
