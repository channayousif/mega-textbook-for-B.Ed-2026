# Measurement and Geometry

Measurement is where mathematics touches the physical school: the cloth, the tiles, the
tank, the shelf. This unit fixes the metric units and their conversions, separates the
three quantities a task can buy (boundary, cover, fill), gives each its formula, and
then runs whole measurement tasks end to end - choose, measure, compute, check - the
way a librarian, a mason or a headteacher actually needs.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Convert between metric units for length, mass and capacity by powers of ten, and
  convert before computing.
- Compute perimeter, area and volume for rectangles, triangles, circles and boxes, with
  units attached, and explain where each formula's key step comes from.
- Name the defining properties of the basic shapes and say which measurement each one
  needs.
- Run a real measurement task through its four moves, check the answer with a
  round-number estimate, and write the plan so a colleague can re-check it.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 2 (decimals and the operation-choosing flowchart) and Unit 1's reasonableness
habit. No geometry beyond school level is assumed.

## How to use this unit

Work the topics in order; each assumes the previous. Keep a ruler or tape (even a
paper strip marked in centimetres) at hand - this unit's activities measure real
objects. The practicum task in Topic 4.3 hands a real measurement plan to your
cooperating teacher, so keep it neat, dated and complete with units. Glossary terms are
underlined; hover or tap them for the Urdu definition.
