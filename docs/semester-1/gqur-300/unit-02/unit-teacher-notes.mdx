# Unit 2 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

Topic 2.1 builds the number system as one family (draw the family tree on the board in the
first ten minutes and keep it up all unit). Topic 2.2 teaches proportional reasoning, the
single most useful idea in the unit. Topic 2.3 adds powers and roots as area ideas and
closes with the operation-choosing flowchart, which quietly revises the whole unit. Across
the three weeks, tie every skill back to Unit 1's four-step strategy; the worked examples
are already written in that shape.

## Teaching strategies from the guide

- Use the guide's problem-solving sessions for the worked examples: put the situation on
  the board and let the class build the plan before you show the carry-out step.
- Run the bazaar ledger (Topic 2.1) and the tiling activity (Topic 2.3) as group
  activities with real mock materials: a printed ledger and paper tiles make the abstract
  concrete.
- Use question-answer for the percentage table: cover the examples column and have the
  class reconstruct it.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Multiplying always makes bigger."** Ask for 12 x 0.1 on the board. Trainees who
   answer 120 or 12 are still treating multiplication as repeated addition of whole
   numbers. The part-of-a-whole picture from Topic 2.1 is the repair.
2. **"Scale by adding."** The recipe misconception (Topic 2.2) is worth a full five-minute
   demonstration: mix two batches of sherbat, one correctly scaled and one with the sugar
   merely added, if your training room allows it.
3. **"8 squared is 8 x 2."** Return to the tile grid: an 8-by-8 floor, not an 8-by-2
   corridor. Ask the class to draw both; the picture defeats the error faster than the
   rule.
4. **"The remainder can be dropped."** The vans problem (Topic 2.3) exists to be got
   wrong once in class so it is never got wrong at a real school event. Let a trainee
   order 7 vans, then ask the 5 leftover pupils to stand up.

## Running the activities

- **The bazaar ledger (Topic 2.1)**: include at least one entry that invites a sign error
  (a loan repaid) so the integer discussion has something to bite on.
- **The marksheet percentage relay (Topic 2.2)**: the debrief question is the point - the
  raw score that looked biggest (42/60) performs worst. Do not reveal it until the groups
  have committed to a ranking.
- **Tile the courtyard (Topic 2.3)**: 12 x 9 metres with 0.5 m, 1 m and 1.5 m tiles gives
  clean numbers (432, 108, 48 tiles); the waste discussion is where the analysis lives.

## Practical work

Group work in the three activities; a group assignment collecting the class's practicum
notes into a "school quantities" register (part-used stationery, tiled areas); the
individual assignment is the Topic 2.1 practicum task; presentations come from the tiling
defences.

## Assessment guidance

The bank's traps are deliberate: MCQ-07 and RRQ-06 test the reverse-percentage and
add-to-scale misconceptions, and ERQ-5's look-back remainder is the unit's central
transfer. When marking ERQ-2, reward the trainees who notice the two deals tie; noticing
an unexpected equality is a stronger quantitative act than computing a difference that
was never there.

## Practicum links

Trainees collect a stationery entry in fractions/decimals (Topic 2.1), a marks-versus-
percentage reporting practice (Topic 2.2), and a tiled-area estimate (Topic 2.3). All
three feed Unit 5's data work later, so insist the notes are dated and specific.
