# Unit 3 assessment

## Unit summary

A pattern grows by a rule (fixed step, doubling, or repeating), and the growth type points
to the rule's shape. A variable names a quantity that varies; an expression is a machine
turning each input into its output, built from variables, coefficients and terms. An
equation is a balance: legal moves do the same thing to both sides, and the method is
simplify, isolate the variable term, isolate the variable, check. An inequality claims a
range; multiplying or dividing by a negative flips the sign because the number line turns
around. Algebra solves daily problems through a loop: translate the situation into symbols,
solve, interpret back into the situation, and decide - and comparing two expressions
settles a whole family of questions at once.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The first five terms of a sequence that starts at 7 and grows by a step of 4 are: *(Remember)*
   a) 7, 11, 15, 19, 23
   b) 7, 10, 13, 16, 19
   c) 4, 7, 11, 15, 19
   d) 7, 14, 21, 28, 35

2. For the matchstick rule 2t + 1, the sticks needed for 12 triangles are: *(Apply)*
   a) 24
   b) 25
   c) 36
   d) 13

3. In the expression 500 + 30k, the coefficient is: *(Understand)*
   a) 500
   b) 30
   c) k
   d) 530

4. The expression 2t + 1 is best described as: *(Understand)*
   a) an equation waiting to be solved
   b) a rule that turns any number of triangles into its stick count
   c) a pair of numbers added together
   d) an inequality in disguise

5. The solution of 30 + 12m = 150 is: *(Apply)*
   a) m = 5
   b) m = 10
   c) m = 12
   d) m = 15

6. The solution of 5x - 7 > 18 is: *(Apply)*
   a) x > 5
   b) x < 5
   c) x > 25
   d) x < 25

7. Solving -3y &lt;= 21 gives: *(Apply)*
   a) y &lt;= -7
   b) y >= -7
   c) y &lt;= 7
   d) y >= 7

8. A photocopy shop charges 30 rupees to open the machine plus 8 rupees per copy. The cost
   of 25 copies is: *(Apply)*
   a) 200 rupees
   b) 230 rupees
   c) 254 rupees
   d) 158 rupees

9. Van A costs 500 + 30k rupees and Van B costs 300 + 40k rupees for k kilometres. The two
   cost the same at: *(Apply)*
   a) k = 10
   b) k = 20
   c) k = 25
   d) k = 50

10. The four moves of algebraic problem solving, in order, are: *(Understand)*
    a) solve, translate, decide, interpret
    b) translate, solve, interpret, decide
    c) interpret, translate, solve, decide
    d) translate, interpret, solve, decide

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Write the first five terms of a sequence that starts at 12 and grows by a step of 5.
   *(Apply)*
2. For the matchstick rule 2t + 1, compute the sticks for 12 triangles, and explain in one
   sentence what the + 1 accounts for. *(Apply)*
3. In the expression 45g + 20 (juice glasses at 45 rupees each plus a 20-rupee box
   charge), name the variable, the coefficient, and state what the constant 20 represents.
   *(Understand)*
4. Solve 60 + 15k = 195 and prove the answer by substitution. *(Apply)*
5. Write and solve the equation for: a tutor charges 250 rupees for materials plus 400
   rupees per session, and a family paid 2,650 rupees in total. *(Apply)*
6. Solve -4p > 24 and explain in one sentence why the inequality sign flips. *(Apply)*
7. A trainee writes 60 + 15k = 195 = 135 = 9 on a single line. Name the misconception in
   one sentence. *(Analyze)*
8. Name the four moves of algebraic problem solving, and state which move the substitution
   check belongs to. *(Understand)*
9. Printer A costs 2,000 + 15n rupees and Printer B costs 1,200 + 25n rupees for n
   programmes. Find the value of n at which both cost the same. *(Apply)*
10. A friend says "algebra is separate from real maths". Correct the claim in two
    sentences using the rate-card idea. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A decorator hangs bunting in triangles along the school gate: one triangle needs 3
   metres of ribbon, each extra joined triangle adds 2 metres, and there is a fixed 5-metre
   tail at each end of the string. Write the ribbon expression for n triangles, compute the
   ribbon for 15 triangles, and state the largest whole number of triangles a 70-metre
   ribbon can carry, with the substitution check. *(Analyze)*
2. A school bus service charges 8,000 rupees per trip plus 120 rupees per pupil, and the
   transport budget for one event is 20,000 rupees. Write the cost expression for p pupils,
   write and solve the inequality for how many pupils the budget can carry, and state the
   largest whole number of pupils with the check that proves one more pupil would not fit.
   *(Analyze)*
3. A school event needs printed programmes. Printer A charges 2,000 rupees setup plus 15
   rupees per programme; Printer B charges 1,200 rupees setup plus 25 rupees per programme.
   Write both cost expressions, compute both totals for 150 programmes and state which is
   cheaper, and find the number of programmes at which both cost the same; then recommend
   which printer the school should choose if the print run might grow or shrink, with a
   reason. *(Analyze)*
4. A school can spend at most 2,000 rupees on geometry boxes costing 45 rupees each, and
   must first pay a 300-rupee debt to the supplier from the same money. Write and solve the
   inequality for the number of boxes, state the largest whole number of boxes with the
   substitution check, and write an expression for the money left over after buying that
   many boxes. *(Analyze)*
5. Using the real rate card you collected at your practicum school (Topic 3.3 practicum
   task), write its cost expression, compute the cost for three realistic quantities, and
   compare it with a flat-rate alternative: find the quantity at which the two cost the
   same, and recommend which option the school should prefer, with reasons. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **a** - start at 7 and add 4 each time: 7, 11, 15, 19, 23.
2. **b** - 2 x 12 + 1 = 25.
3. **b** - the coefficient 30 multiplies the variable k.
4. **b** - an expression is a rule or machine; nothing is claimed equal.
5. **b** - 150 - 30 = 120; 120 / 12 = 10.
6. **a** - 5x > 25, so x > 5.
7. **b** - dividing by -3 flips the sign: y >= -7.
8. **b** - 30 + 8 x 25 = 30 + 200 = 230.
9. **b** - 500 + 30k = 300 + 40k gives 200 = 10k, so k = 20.
10. **b** - translate, solve, interpret, decide.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: 12, 17, 22, 27, 32. **Marks (2):** 1 - first three correct; 1 - all five.
2. Model: 25 sticks; the + 1 is the starting stick of the first triangle that no later
   triangle shares. **Marks (2):** 1 - 25; 1 - a sensible account of the constant.
3. Model: g is the variable (glasses), 45 is the coefficient (price per glass), 20 is the
   fixed box charge. **Marks (3):** 1 - variable; 1 - coefficient; 1 - constant's meaning.
4. Model: 15k = 135; k = 9; check 60 + 15 x 9 = 195. **Marks (2):** 1 - k = 9; 1 -
   substitution check shown.
5. Model: 250 + 400s = 2,650; 400s = 2,400; s = 6 sessions. **Marks (2):** 1 - correct
   equation; 1 - solution.
6. Model: p < -6 (dividing by -4 flips the sign); the flip happens because multiplying or
   dividing by a negative turns the number line around. **Marks (2):** 1 - p < -6; 1 - the
   number-line explanation.
7. Model: the equals sign is being used to mean "the answer comes next"; each step should
   be on its own line because the equals sign claims balance, and 195 does not equal 135.
   **Marks (2):** 1 - names the misconception; 1 - states the correct practice.
8. Model: translate, solve, interpret, decide; the substitution check belongs to the decide
   step (step 4, where the answer is checked against the situation). **Marks (3):** 1 - four
   moves in order; 1 - check assigned to decide; 1 - a sentence on why.
9. Model: 2,000 + 15n = 1,200 + 25n; 800 = 10n; n = 80. **Marks (2):** 1 - equation set
   up; 1 - n = 80.
10. Model: algebra is the compression of daily arithmetic into reusable rules; the rate
    card, the fare and the budget are the same expression in different clothes, so algebra
    is exactly the maths of real situations. **Marks (2):** 1 - the compression idea; 1 -
    the rate-card example or equivalent.

### ERQ rubrics

1. Bunting. Analytic rubric: *Expression* (0-2: 2n + 11 built with parts labelled);
   *Computation* (0-2: 41 metres for 15 triangles); *Largest n with check* (0-3,
   **Analyze**: 2n + 11 &lt;= 70 gives n &lt;= 29.5, so 29 triangles, with 2(29) + 11 = 69 &lt;= 70
   shown). Total 7.
2. Bus budget. Analytic rubric: *Expression* (0-2: 8,000 + 120p); *Inequality solved*
   (0-2: p &lt;= 100); *Largest whole number with check* (0-3, **Analyze**: 100 pupils, with
   8,000 + 120 x 100 = 20,000 exactly and 101 pupils shown to exceed at 20,120). Total 7.
3. Printers. Analytic rubric: *Expressions* (0-2: A = 2,000 + 15n, B = 1,200 + 25n);
   *Totals and comparison* (0-2: 4,250 vs 4,950, A cheaper by 700); *Break-even and
   recommendation* (0-3, **Analyze**: n = 80 with the working, and a recommendation that
   weighs a possibly smaller run, below which B is cheaper, with a reason). Total 7.
4. Geometry boxes. Analytic rubric: *Inequality* (0-2: 45b &lt;= 1,700); *Solution and
   largest whole number* (0-2: b &lt;= 37.8, so 37 boxes); *Check* (0-2: 37 x 45 = 1,665 &lt;=
   1,700 while 38 x 45 = 1,710 exceeds); *Leftover expression* (0-2, **Analyze**: 1,700 -
   45b, or 35 rupees left at b = 37). Total 8.
5. Practicum rate card. Analytic rubric: *Expression* (0-2: a correct expression from the
   real card); *Three quantities* (0-2: three correct computations); *Break-even* (0-3,
   **Analyze**: the flat-rate comparison set up and solved, with the quantity where the two
   costs cross); *Recommendation* (0-2: justified against the school's likely usage);
   *Clarity* (0-1). Total 10.
