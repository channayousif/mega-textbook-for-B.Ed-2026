# Topic 3.2 - Linear equations and inequalities

## A real classroom situation

<Figure id="fig-U3-3" src="/img/figures/gqur-300/unit-03/fig-U3-3.svg" alt="Flowchart of solving a linear equation: simplify, isolate the variable term, isolate the variable, then check by substitution, looping back if the check fails." />

A rickshaw driver in Hyderabad posts his rates at the stand: "60 rupees to start, then 15
rupees for every kilometre." A passenger travelling to the station pays 195 rupees and
wants to check the fare. The situation hides an equation: 60 + 15k = 195, where k is the
distance in kilometres. The driver solves it without ceremony: "195 minus 60 is 135; 135
divided by 15 is 9; nine kilometres." He has performed the two classic moves - take the
fixed part away from the total, then divide by the rate - and every two-step equation you
will ever solve uses exactly those moves, in that order. This topic names the method,
adds the safety check the driver skipped, and then widens the idea to inequalities, where
the answer is a range rather than a single number.

## Explanation

### Linear equations

A <Glossary term="Linear equation">linear equation</Glossary> claims that two expressions
are equal for some value of the variable: 60 + 15k = 195. Picture a
<Glossary term="Balance model">balance</Glossary>: the left pan holds 60 + 15k, the right
holds 195. Whatever you do, you must do to both pans, or the balance lies. That one
picture generates every legal move:

- Add or subtract the same amount on both sides.
- Multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero amount.

The flowchart above turns the picture into an ordered method: simplify, isolate the
variable term, isolate the variable, check.

**Worked example.** Solve 60 + 15k = 195. Simplify: nothing to do. Isolate the variable
term: subtract 60 from both sides, giving 15k = 135. Isolate the variable: divide both
sides by 15, giving k = 9. Check: 60 + 15 x 9 = 60 + 135 = 195. True. The passenger
travelled 9 kilometres. The check is not decoration; it catches the sign slips that
silently corrupt fares, bills and budgets.

**A common misconception to correct.** Many trainees read the equals sign as "the answer
comes next" (a habit from primary arithmetic: 2 + 3 = 5). In an equation, the equals sign
is a claim of balance that stays true only under fair moves. Writing 60 + 15k = 195 = 135
= 9 (a chain seen on many scripts) is the misconception in action: 195 does not equal 135.
Keep each step on its own line, and let the equals sign mean balance.

### Linear inequalities

<Figure id="fig-U3-4" src="/img/figures/gqur-300/unit-03/fig-U3-4.svg" alt="Diagram of two number lines: x greater than 4 shaded to the right, and after multiplying by negative 2, the inequality flips to less than negative 8 shaded to the left." />

A <Glossary term="Linear inequality">linear inequality</Glossary> claims a range, not a
point: the school's stationery budget allows at most 2,000 rupees, so 45b &lt;= 2,000 for b
boxes at 45 rupees. Solving uses the same balance moves with one new rule: **multiplying
or dividing by a negative number flips the inequality sign**. The diagram above shows why.
If x > 4 (everything to the right of 4), then multiplying by -2 turns the whole picture
around: -2x < -8, everything to the left of -8. The numbers 5, 6, 7 (all greater than 4)
become -10, -12, -14 (all less than -8). The flip is not a rule to memorise blindly; it is
what the number line does when it is turned around.

**Worked example.** A school can spend at most 2,000 rupees on geometry boxes costing 45
rupees each, and already owes the supplier 300 rupees from last term, which must be paid
from the same money. Write and solve the inequality. Available for boxes:
2,000 - 300 = 1,700 rupees, so 45b &lt;= 1,700. Divide both sides by 45: b &lt;= 37.8 (to one
decimal). Since boxes come whole, b &lt;= 37. The school can buy 37 boxes, and the look-back
check confirms: 37 x 45 = 1,665 &lt;= 1,700, while 38 x 45 = 1,710 would exceed it.

## Activity: The fare desk

Work in pairs. Each pair writes three fare-card situations for the other: a fixed charge
plus a per-unit rate, with the total paid stated (for example, "150 rupees for a 4-km
trip on the 50-plus-25 rate"). The solving pair must write the equation, solve it with the
four steps, and check. The writing pair verifies the check, not just the answer. Rotate
after three problems. About 20 minutes.

## Check your understanding

1. Solve 30 + 12m = 150 and check your answer by substitution. *(Apply)*
2. Write the equation for: a tutor charges 400 rupees per session plus 250 rupees for
   materials, and a family paid 2,650 rupees in total. Then find the number of sessions.
   *(Apply)*
3. Solve 5x - 7 > 18 and describe the solution as a sentence ("x is greater than ...").
   *(Apply)*
4. Solve -3y &lt;= 21, showing the sign flip and explaining in one sentence why it happens.
   *(Apply)*
5. A trainee writes 60 + 15k = 195 = 135 = 9 on one line. Identify the misconception in
   one sentence. *(Analyze)*

## Summary

An equation is a balance: legal moves do the same thing to both sides, and the method is
simplify, isolate the variable term, isolate the variable, check. The equals sign means
balance, not "answer next". An inequality claims a range and uses the same moves, except
that multiplying or dividing by a negative flips the sign - the number line turning
around. Whole-number contexts (boxes, vans, seats) add a final look-back step: the
algebraic answer must be rounded in the direction the situation demands.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can solve a two-step linear equation and prove the answer by substitution.
- [ ] I can translate a fare-or-budget situation into an equation or inequality.
- [ ] I can solve inequalities, flip the sign correctly on negative multiplication, and
      explain why.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find one posted rate in or near the school (van fare, photocopy rate, canteen price list
with a fixed part). Write the equation for a total you or the clerk actually paid, solve
it, and check with the person who charged it. Note any disagreement and its cause.

## Summative task

A school bus service charges 8,000 rupees per trip plus 120 rupees per pupil. The school's
transport budget for one event is 20,000 rupees. In a short written answer: (a) write the
cost expression for p pupils, (b) write and solve the inequality for how many pupils the
budget can carry, and (c) state the largest whole number of pupils, with the substitution
check that proves one more pupil would not fit.

**Mini-rubric** (each criterion 0-2 marks):

- *Expression*: 8,000 + 120p.
- *Inequality solved*: 8,000 + 120p &lt;= 20,000 gives p &lt;= 100.
- *Largest whole number with check*: 100 pupils, with 8,000 + 120 x 100 = 20,000 exactly;
  the check must show the budget is exactly met (or that 101 would give 20,120 and
  exceed). **(Analyze)**
