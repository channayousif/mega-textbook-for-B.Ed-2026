# Unit 1 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

The three topics form one arc. Topic 1.1 changes what trainees think quantitative reasoning
is. Topic 1.2 gives them the first practical tools (number sense and estimation). Topic 1.3
gives them a method for unfamiliar problems. Teach them in order across the three weeks the
content-spec allows, and keep returning to Topic 1.1's definition: every later skill should
be introduced as an instance of "reasoning with numbers for a decision".

## Teaching strategies from the guide

The course guide names interactive lectures, problem-solving sessions, group activities,
real-life case studies, guided practice and discussions, plus lecture, discussion,
question-answer and brainstorming. In this unit:

- Use the interactive lecture shape for Topic 1.1: present the classroom situation, then
  question-and-answer your way to the definition rather than dictating it.
- Run Topic 1.2's "one-minute estimate challenge" exactly as timed: the time pressure is
  what forces estimation instead of counting.
- Use the guide's brainstorming mode for Topic 1.3's activity: collect the pairs' repairs
  on the board and let the group argue for the best one.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Quantitative reasoning means doing hard calculations."** Probe with a task that needs
   no calculation at all: "The ground fits 80 and 63 signed up - what else do you want to
   know before deciding?" Trainees who reach for a formula are stuck in the old frame.
2. **"Estimation is just guessing."** Ask for the method behind an estimate. If a trainee
   cannot name a strategy, it was a guess; make the class supply the missing strategy.
3. **"There is one correct method for every problem."** Deliberately solve one problem two
   ways on the board and ask the class to check both; when both survive the look-back, the
   misconception is refuted by demonstration.

## Running the activities

- **Sort the school day (Topic 1.1)**: prepare the twelve slips in advance and include at
  least two ambiguous ones (ordering chalk for the month) so the pairs have something to
  argue about. Debrief by asking for the decision each quantitative task serves.
- **The one-minute estimate challenge (Topic 1.2)**: award points for method, never for
  closeness, or the activity collapses into guessing. Keep the true figures hidden until
  the debrief.
- **Spot the broken step (Topic 1.3)**: the three flawed reasonings should each break a
  different move (a false if-then, a pattern over-read, a bad elimination). Ask pairs to
  name the move, not just the flaw.

## Practical work

The guide's practical work (group work, group assignments, individual assignment,
presentations) maps onto this unit as: group work in the three topic activities; a group
assignment collecting the class's practicum notes into one "school decisions" wall; an
individual assignment in the Topic 1.2 practicum task; and presentations in the unit's
debrief, where each group presents one reconstructed decision.

## Assessment guidance

The unit-end bank samples all three topics. When marking the ERQs, resist the temptation to
reward arithmetic alone: the rubrics weight the Analyze criteria (missing quantity,
alternative explanations, limit of the method) precisely because the unit's claim is that
reasoning outranks calculation. A note that checks the arithmetic but names no missing
quantity is a pass at best; the distinction goes to the trainee who asks what the numbers
leave out.

## Practicum links

Trainees collect two artefacts this unit: a teacher's real numeric decision (Topic 1.1) and
an estimate with its true figure (Topic 1.2). Both feed ERQ-5. Warn trainees to seek
permission before counting anything, and to anonymise names in anything they write up.
