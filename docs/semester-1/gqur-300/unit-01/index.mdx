# Foundations of Quantitative Reasoning

This unit lays the foundation for the whole course. Quantitative reasoning is not a bag of
formulas; it is a way of thinking with numbers, data, and logic that you will use in every
later unit, and every day as a teacher. Here you learn what it is, how to estimate when
exactness is unnecessary, and how to reason and solve problems in checkable steps.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain what quantitative reasoning is and why it matters for a teacher.
- Apply number sense to judge the size and reasonableness of quantities.
- Estimate and approximate using grouping, rounding, front-end digits, and compatible
  numbers, and judge when an estimate is good enough.
- Use the logical-reasoning moves (pattern, if-then, deduction, elimination) and the
  four-step problem-solving strategy on real school problems.

## Prerequisite knowledge

No prior course is assumed. The unit starts from the level of a student who has completed
higher secondary education in Pakistan and uses everyday arithmetic comfortably.

## How to use this unit

Work through the three topics in order; each builds on the last. In every topic, read the
classroom situation first, then work through the explanation with a pencil and paper, and
do the activity with your classmates rather than alone. The "Try this at your practicum
school" tasks collect real material you will use in the unit assessment, so keep your
notes. Glossary terms are underlined; hover or tap them for the Urdu definition.
