# Toxicology Essentials and Environmental Governance

Unit 4 managed hazards from the outside. This unit follows them inside the body: what a
toxic agent does once it is absorbed, how the body defends itself, and how the risk of
harm is managed. Then it turns outward again to the frameworks that govern whole
countries and the planet: the multilateral environmental agreements, the Sustainable
Development Goals, and the environmental laws that carry them.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain the toxic impacts of the major atmospheric agents.
- Trace the fate of absorbed toxins and xenobiotics, including detoxification and
  bioactivation.
- Describe the body's natural detoxification processes.
- Apply the risk management process to an environmental health risk.
- Explain what multilateral environmental agreements are and name the major ones.
- Explain the Sustainable Development Goals and their environment-health targets.
- Describe environmental laws, policies, guidelines and strategies related to public
  health.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-4 (the pollution chain; hazard classes; risk assessment; the PDCA cycle). Topic
5.4's risk management reuses Unit 3's cycle at environmental scale.

## How to use this unit

The topics run inward then outward: the agents (5.1), the body's handling of them (5.2,
5.3), the management of the risk (5.4's first half), and the governance frameworks that
manage it at scale (5.4's second half, which the topic list folds into one large topic).
The CO generator example threads through 5.1 to 5.3; keep it in view.
