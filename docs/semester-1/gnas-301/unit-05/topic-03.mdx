# Topic 5.3 - Natural detoxification processes

## A real classroom situation

After the sprayer's recovery, his neighbour sells him a "liver cleanse" tea, guaranteed
to "flush the toxins". The health worker, asked whether to buy it, gives the trainee
teacher watching the case a look that means: this one is yours. The trainee has just
learned what the liver actually does. She starts there.

This topic is the body's own detoxification system, the real one, and the honest answer
to the tea.

## Explanation

### Natural detoxification processes

The body runs a permanent, three-organ waste system, and it needs no help from a box.

<Figure id="fig-U5-5" src="/img/figures/gnas-301/unit-05/fig-U5-5.svg" alt="Flowchart of natural detoxification, the liver's two phases then exits by kidneys, lungs and gut, with a storage branch to fat and bone" width={780} height={470} />

- The **liver** is the hub, working in two phases. Phase 1 chemically reworks the
  xenobiotic, the biotransformation of Topic 5.2. Phase 2 attaches a handle to the
  product, a small molecule that makes it water-soluble and ready for export. The phases
  must stay in balance: a fast phase 1 with a slow phase 2 can raise the bioactivation
  risk.
- The **kidneys** filter the finished water-soluble products into urine, the busiest
  exit for many chemicals, which is why kidney function matters in every poisoning case.
- The **lungs** exhale gases, CO's exit, and volatile solvents.
- The **gut** carries out what the liver dumps in bile, and its contents, including its
  bacteria, can re-release some chemicals, a loop toxicologists account for.

What genuinely helps this system is unglamorous: **hydration** keeps the kidneys'
filtration running; **nutrition**, protein and the vitamins in ordinary food, supplies
phase 2's raw materials; **avoidance** removes the queue's workload, clean air, no
smoking, safe water, protective equipment at work; and **time**, because every exit has
a rate. What genuinely hinders it: repeated exposure that keeps the queue full,
malnutrition that starves phase 2, and liver disease that slows the whole plant.

<Figure id="fig-U5-6" src="/img/figures/gnas-301/unit-05/fig-U5-6.svg" alt="Table of what helps and what hinders the body's natural detoxification, with examples and an honest note on each" width={780} height={470} />

**A worked example.** The sprayer's recovery, read through the system: away from the
cotton field, his exposure stopped (avoidance); the drip rehydrated him (hydration);
food restored phase 2's supplies (nutrition); and over days his liver finished the
rework and his kidneys exported the products (time). The tea did none of these. Nothing
in the system's plumbing is flushed by a drink, and the word "cleanse" describes a
marketing hope, not a renal process.

**Common misconception.** "Detoxification means juice cleanses and special diets." The
body's detoxification is the organ system above, running since birth. Commercial
"detox" products claim to improve it; the honest evidence for those claims is absent,
while the evidence for hydration, nutrition and avoidance is the whole of physiology. A
teacher's job is not to sell the tea but to name the system it borrows.

## Activity: The honest label

Work in groups of three. Your teacher gives each group one "detox" product claim, a tea,
a foot pad, a juice week. In fifteen minutes, write the honest label: which organ, if
any, the claim touches, what would genuinely help that organ, and what the product's
price buys instead. Then present your label in ninety seconds. Allow 25 minutes in
total.

## Check your understanding

1. Name the liver's two phases and what each does, in one sentence each. *(Remember)*
2. Which exit does carbon monoxide use, and why not the kidneys? *(Understand)*
3. Why can a fast phase 1 with a slow phase 2 raise the bioactivation risk? *(Analyze)*
4. A friend spends a week's wage on a cleanse tea. Using the helps list, write your
   honest advice in two sentences. *(Evaluate)*

## Summary

Natural detoxification is the liver's two-phase rework-and-attach sequence feeding three
exits, kidneys, lungs and gut, with a storage branch to fat and bone. What helps is
unglamorous: hydration, nutrition, avoidance and time. What hinders is repeated
exposure, malnutrition and liver disease. The commercial "detox" borrows the system's
name without touching its plumbing, and the honest answer to the tea is the system
itself.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find out what the staffroom and the first-aid box offer for "heat" and "weakness", the
everyday word for feeling unwell. List two items that genuinely help the body's system,
water, rest, food, and two claims you would now question. Bring the list.

## Summative task

Write 150 to 180 words on the following.

> A colleague is about to spend a month's salary on a "liver detox programme". Write the
> note you would leave on their desk: what the body's system actually is, what genuinely
> helps it, and one question worth asking the seller.

| Criterion | What a strong response does |
