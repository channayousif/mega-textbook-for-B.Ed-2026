# Unit 5 assessment

## Unit summary

Atmospheric agents travel emission, inhalation, blood, target organ, effect, and differ
in destination and damage: CO takes the blood's oxygen seats, sulfur and nitrogen
dioxide inflame airways, ozone irritates on hot afternoons, PM2.5 causes cardiovascular
and respiratory disease and cancers, lead attacks the developing brain with no safe level. Dose, concentration times
time, and the route of entry decide the harm, and toxic agents arrive by water as well
as air. Absorbed xenobiotics follow absorption, distribution, biotransformation, whose
outcome is usually detoxification but sometimes bioactivation, then excretion or
storage. Natural detoxification is the liver's two-phase sequence feeding the kidneys,
lungs and gut, helped by hydration, nutrition, avoidance and time, and hindered by
repeated exposure, malnutrition and liver disease. Risk management at scale runs the
same cycle with the options avoid, reduce, transfer, accept. Multilateral environmental
agreements, Stockholm 1972 to Paris 2015, commit states through reporting cycles; the
SDGs turn goals into targets; and Pakistan's stack, PEPA 1997, the NEQS and the
provincial agencies, carries it down to the drain.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. b - CO binds haemoglobin's oxygen seats; the lungs themselves stay clean-looking.
2. c - dose is concentration times time; hours in a closed room is a high dose.
3. c - the Sindh study's arsenic arrives by drinking water, the non-atmospheric route.
4. a - a xenobiotic is any chemical foreign to the body, natural or manufactured.
5. b - the liver runs biotransformation; the other organs are exits.
6. b - bioactivation produces a more poisonous product, the sprayer's puzzle.
7. c - CO is a gas; the lungs exhale it once clean air replaces the exposure.
8. b - hydration, nutrition, avoidance and time; the products have no honest evidence.
9. b - well below 2 degrees, pursuing efforts toward 1.5, from 195 parties in 2015.
10. b - the NEQS are PEPA 1997's enforceable limits, the yardstick of Unit 2.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: the sensory system did not evolve for industrial chemistry; CO is colourless
   and odourless yet poisons, so detection is an instrument's job, which is why CO alarms
   exist. **Marks (2):** 1 for the CO fact; 1 for the general principle.
2. Model: any one of the six agents walked link by link, for example CO: emitted by the
   generator's incomplete combustion, inhaled at high concentration for hours, absorbed
   into the blood where it takes the oxygen seats, arriving at the oxygen-hungry brain,
   with headache to collapse as the effect. **Marks (4):** 1 per link in order (emission,
   entry, blood, target organ with its effect).
3. Model: biotransformation; the liver converted the weak organophosphate into its
   active, nerve-attacking form, so the harm outran the label. **Marks (2):** 1 for the
   stage; 1 for the mechanism.
4. Model: distribution (the blood parks it in bone) and excretion-or-storage (the
   storage branch, for years). **Marks (2):** 1 per stage.
5. Model: phase 1 chemically reworks the xenobiotic; phase 2 attaches a handle that
   makes it water-soluble and exportable. **Marks (2):** 1 per phase.
6. Model: phase 1's rework can itself create the more poisonous intermediate; if phase 2
   lags, the intermediate accumulates instead of being exported. **Marks (2):** 1 for
   the intermediate; 1 for the lag.
7. Model: helps, hydration, nutrition, avoidance, time; hinders, repeated exposure,
   malnutrition, liver disease. **Marks (4):** 2 for the helps; 2 for the hinders.
8. Model: avoid, relocate playing time away from traffic hours; reduce, filtration or
   green belts; transfer, health cover for asthmatic staff; accept, with the AQI watch
   running. **Marks (4):** 1 per option matched.
9. Model: states ratify and then report against commitments in cycles, and the lists and
   funding mechanisms change what states do, which a press release cannot.
   **Marks (2):** 1 for ratification and reporting; 1 for the consequence.
10. Model: any four of Stockholm 1972 (first conference), Basel 1989 (hazardous waste
    trade), Rio 1992 (climate and biodiversity conventions), Stockholm POPs 2001
    (persistent organic pollutants), Minamata 2013 (mercury), Paris 2015 (climate).
    **Marks (4):** 1 per correct year-subject pair.
