# Unit 2 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

The three topics run supply, pollution, waste. Topic 2.1 sets the water frame (the
100-droplet picture and water security); 2.2 follows the water out (pollution and
treatment); 2.3 turns to the solid stream. Hold the worked examples together: the
Hyderabad drain from 2.2 returns in 2.3's burning heap, and the Unit 1 waste log returns in
the bin audit. Teach in order across the two weeks the guide allows.

## Teaching strategies (from the course guide)

The guide names interactive lectures, seminars, group work, case studies, presentations,
guided reading, home study and individual tutorials. This unit leans on case study (the
flooded-but-scarce puzzle, the Chachro study, the burning heap) and guided reading of two
figures (the 100-droplet strip and the waste hierarchy staircase). The home-study element is
the water bill or tanker-price question in Topic 2.1, which needs one adult conversation at
home, not a special trip.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Floods mean no scarcity."** Probe with the 100-droplet picture: which droplet did the
   flood deliver, and which droplet does a household drink from? The answer separates
   volume from usable supply.
2. **"Clear water is safe water."** Probe with two invisible pollutant types, pathogens and
   dissolved metals. Ask what a sample bottle would need to test that the eye cannot.
3. **"Waste management means collection."** Probe with the five stages: what happens before
   the truck, and what happens after it? The heap at the gate is a storage failure, not a
   truck shortage.
4. **"Hospital waste is just like household waste."** Do not argue; walk the chain. Who
   touches the bag between the clinic and the dump? The sweeper and the picker answer the
   question for you.

## Managing the activities

**Topic 2.1, The 100-droplet class (25 minutes, groups of four).** Counters make the
abstract share physical. The argument to protect time for is the household split: groups
discover that the visible-water droplet cannot serve four uses generously, which is the
whole point.

**Topic 2.2, Trace the drain (20 minutes, pairs).** The map is the tool. Watch for pairs
that mark receptors (wells, pumps) but forget pathways (seepage into ground). Ask: how does
the pollutant travel from that pipe to that pump?

**Topic 2.3, Bin audit, three streams (30 minutes, groups of three).** Safety first: tongs
or gloved hands, a spread sheet, no clinical or sharp material, and handwashing after. If
the school's waste is not safe to audit, use photographs of a sorted heap instead. The
debrief question is: what share never needed to be generated?

## Practical work and practicum links

Topic 2.3's practicum extends the Unit 1 two-bin trial with a third stream, which is the
unit's main practical. The guide's (2-1) practical hour can also carry the water-source
walk in Topic 2.1. Debrief both in the unit's last session; the water findings feed Unit 3's
hazards work, and the waste findings return in Unit 5's governance discussion of the rules
behind the bins.
