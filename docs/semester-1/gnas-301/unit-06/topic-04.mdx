# Topic 6.4 - The greenhouse effect and global warming

## A real classroom situation

A Class 9 pupil looks up from his textbook and says, "Sir, if the greenhouse effect is
natural, why is everyone shouting about it?" The teacher walks to the window. "This
glass," he says, "keeps the room warm in winter. That is the natural effect, and we need
it. Now imagine someone seals the vents and adds another pane every year. The problem
is not the glass. The problem is the extra panes."

This topic is the physics of the glass and the extra panes, stated simply enough for a
Class 9 pupil to carry home.

## Explanation

### The greenhouse gas effect and global warming: causes and effects

The mechanism is short and physical. Sunlight arrives as short-wave energy and warms
the Earth's surface. The warm surface radiates the energy back as long-wave heat.
Greenhouse gases, water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane and others, let the short-wave
sunlight through but absorb part of the outgoing long-wave heat, returning some of it
downward. The trapped part warms the lower atmosphere. Without this natural effect the
Earth would average far below freezing; the glass keeps the room alive.

<Figure id="fig-U6-7" src="/img/figures/gnas-301/unit-06/fig-U6-7.svg" alt="Diagram of the greenhouse effect, shortwave sunlight in, longwave heat out, greenhouse gases trapping part of it, with natural and enhanced panels" width={780} height={470} />

The problem is the enhancement. Burning coal, oil and gas, clearing forests, and
raising livestock add carbon dioxide and methane to the atmosphere faster than the
natural sinks absorb them, so the gas layer thickens: the extra panes. The observed
results NASA summarises: global average surface temperature has risen about one degree
Celsius since the late nineteenth century, carbon dioxide has increased about 250 times
faster than the natural rises after the last ice age, the ice sheets are losing
hundreds of billions of tonnes a year, and the sea has risen about 20 centimetres over
the last century (NASA, 2024).

<Figure id="fig-U6-8" src="/img/figures/gnas-301/unit-06/fig-U6-8.svg" alt="Table of the main greenhouse gases with main sources and role in warming for each" width={780} height={470} />

**A worked example.** Pakistan's own connection to the gases, in one paragraph. The
country's emissions per person are low by world standards, but its energy, still
coal-and-gas heavy, its deforestation pressures, and its growing livestock all add to
the shared layer; and the floods and heatwaves of Topic 6.6 are what the shared layer,
thickened by everyone's extra panes, hands back. The justice question, smallest
emitters, heaviest losses, is real, and it belongs in the classroom honestly: it
explains the global negotiation of Unit 5.4, and it does not exempt anyone's next
kilowatt.

**Common misconception.** "The greenhouse effect is itself a problem." The effect is
natural and life-sustaining; the enhancement is the problem. The distinction is not
pedantry: it tells the pupil that the remedy is not to remove the glass but to stop
adding panes, which is why mitigation means cutting emissions, not banning a natural
process.

## Activity: The pane model

Work in pairs. Your teacher gives each pair a lamp, a thermometer, and two glass or
clear plastic sheets. Measure the temperature under one pane, then two, at one-minute
intervals for five minutes. Record the two curves and answer: what does the second pane
change, and what in the real atmosphere plays its role? Allow 20 minutes.

## Summary

The greenhouse effect is natural and physical: short-wave sunlight in, long-wave heat
out, gases returning part of it down, keeping the Earth warm enough for life. The
enhancement is the problem: fossil fuels, deforestation and livestock thicken the gas
layer, and the observed results include about one degree of warming since the late
nineteenth century, rapid carbon-dioxide growth, shrinking ice sheets and rising seas.
Pakistan's per-person emissions are low and its losses heavy, which frames the justice
of the global negotiation without exempting anyone's next kilowatt.
