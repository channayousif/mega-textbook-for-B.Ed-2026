# Unit 6 assessment

## Unit summary

Toxicology asks four questions, the agent, the route, the effect and the dose, with dose
the founding one and its caveat, the no-safe-level agents. Substances classify by
origin, anthropogenic against natural, by use and by effect; the origin axis decides
the control, banning the made and managing the natural. Effects divide by clock, acute
against chronic, then by target: genotoxic, mutagenic, teratogenic, carcinogenic and
sensitizing, with the dose-response curve rising from a threshold for most effects and
no known safe level for the genotoxic class. Organic pollutants linger in fat and
inorganic metals never break down; essentiality and toxicity share one ladder; the
three absorption doors deliver different doses; bioaccumulation builds within one
organism and bio-magnification up the chain, the multiplier that makes a trace in the
water a dose in the family. The greenhouse effect is natural and life-sustaining; its
enhancement, fossil fuels, deforestation, livestock, is the problem, with about one
degree of warming observed. Smog is polluted fog, sulfurous in winter and photochemical
in summer, and its controls act at the source and the receptor. Mitigation cuts the
causes for the shared future; adaptation softens the arriving present's harms; the two
are partners, and the course's tools, the chain, the hierarchy, the governance stack,
are how a school answers.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The founding question of toxicology is: *(Remember)*
   a) where was it made
   b) how much reaches the site
   c) who sells it
   d) what colour is it

2. Arsenic in Sindh's groundwater is a poison that is: *(Understand)*
   a) anthropogenic and bannable
   b) synthetic and replaceable
   c) biological and treatable
   d) natural and only manageable

3. Cutting emissions and installing cool shelters are, respectively: *(Understand)*
   a) adaptation and mitigation
   b) mitigation and adaptation
   c) both mitigation
   d) both adaptation

4. A teratogen's harm depends on: *(Understand)*
   a) timing as well as dose
   b) dose only
   c) the season only
   d) nothing measurable

5. Genotoxic carcinogens are regulated with no safe level because: *(Analyze)*
   a) they are always immediately fatal
   b) they cannot be measured
   c) their dose-response shows no known threshold
   d) they only harm workers

6. Iron is essential at one dose and toxic at another. This illustrates: *(Apply)*
   a) the origin axis
   b) the essentiality ladder
   c) bio-magnification
   d) sensitization

7. A farm worker takes in the same pesticide by skin, breath and hand-to-mouth. What
   changes across the three? *(Apply)*
   a) the dose reaching the blood
   b) the substance's chemistry
   c) the effect class
   d) nothing at all

8. The natural greenhouse effect: *(Understand)*
   a) is the problem to remove
   b) began with industry
   c) exists only in cities
   d) keeps the Earth warm enough for life

9. Lahore's winter grey is mostly: *(Understand)*
   a) photochemical smog
   b) ordinary fog
   c) sulfurous smog under an inversion
   d) desert dust alone

10. Heat-action plans and flood-proof schools are examples of: *(Apply)*
    a) mitigation
    b) bioactivation
    c) source control
    d) adaptation

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

5. Course capstone: the principal asks, "Heat and floods, which should we prepare for?"

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. b - the dose makes the poison is the discipline's first law.
2. d - arsenic dissolves naturally from rock; it can only be managed, tested, treated.
3. b - mitigation cuts the causes for the shared future; adaptation softens the
   arriving present's harms.
4. a - the unborn child's stage of development decides the teratogen's harm.
5. c - the no-threshold dose-response is the reason for no-level strictness.
6. b - the essentiality ladder: required at one rung, poisonous higher up.
7. a - the route changes the dose that reaches the blood; the substance is the same.
8. d - without it the Earth would average far below freezing.
9. c - coal and waste burning under an inversion, with photochemical leftovers.
10. d - adaptation softens the arriving present's harms.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: what is the agent; how does it enter (route); what does it do (effect); how
   much does it take (dose). **Marks (4):** 1 per question.
2. Model: anthropogenic poisons are made or concentrated by people, so they can be
   banned or replaced; natural poisons cannot be banned, only managed, tested,
   treated, avoided. **Marks (2):** 1 per half.
3. Model: the sprayer's collapse an hour after exposure is acute, the antidote clock;
   the thirty-year well drinker's biopsy is chronic, the testing clock.
   **Marks (2):** 1 per patient correctly classed.
4. Model: the first exposure teaches the immune system; later exposures trigger the
   trained overreaction, so the harm appears only after the lesson is given.
   **Marks (2):** 1 for the teaching; 1 for the later attack.
5. Model: lung, gut, skin; for spray drift, skin first (the wet spray on the body),
   lung second (the mist breathed), gut third (hand-to-mouth).
   **Marks (3):** 1 for the doors; 2 for a defensible ranking.
6. Model: bioaccumulation is the build-up within one organism, entry faster than exit;
   bio-magnification is the rise in concentration up the food chain as each predator
   eats many prey. **Marks (2):** 1 per sentence.
7. Model: short-wave sunlight in and warms the surface; the surface radiates long-wave
   heat out; greenhouse gases absorb part of the heat and return some downward,
   warming the lower atmosphere. **Marks (3):** 1 per sentence.
8. Model: any real pair, for example mitigation, joining a tree-planting drive or
   shifting a generator's load hours, which protects the future by cutting emissions;
   adaptation, shade shelters or a heat-day plan, which protects the present by softening
   the arriving harms. **Marks (2):** 1 for a correct mitigation-adaptation pair; 1 for
   the present-versus-future placement.
9. Model: the pathway is the air under the lid; dispersion cannot be increased while
   the inversion sits, so the only honest action is at the source and the receptor.
   **Marks (2):** 1 for the physics; 1 for the consequence.
10. Model: mitigation answers how we stop feeding the future crisis (cut the causes);
    adaptation answers how we soften the harms already arriving (protect the exposed).
    **Marks (2):** 1 per question.
