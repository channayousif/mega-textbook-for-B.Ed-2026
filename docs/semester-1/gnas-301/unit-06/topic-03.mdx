# Topic 6.3 - Pollutants in the body: essentiality, absorption, accumulation

## A real classroom situation

On a school trip to Keenjhar Lake, the guide points to the fishing families and says
what the pupils' textbook says not: "The big fish are the worst to eat, if the water is
dirty." A pupil asks why. The guide draws in the dust: small plants, small fish, big
fish, family. "What the water carries a little of," he says, "the big fish carries a
lot of, and the family eats the big fish."

He is drawing <Glossary term="Bio-magnification">bio-magnification</Glossary>, and this
topic is the explanation behind his dust diagram.

## Explanation

### Biological properties of organic and inorganic pollutants; essentiality and toxicity

Pollutants behave in the body according to their chemistry. **Organic pollutants**,
built on carbon, pesticides, solvents, dioxins, tend to dissolve in fat, which makes
them slow to leave and prone to the accumulation this topic builds to. **Inorganic
pollutants**, the metals and minerals, lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium, do not break
down at all; the body can store them in bone and tissue, and some bind the body's own
working parts, which is how they do harm.

Essentiality and toxicity sit on one ladder, not two shelves. <Glossary term="Essentiality">Essential</Glossary> substances, iron, zinc, copper, even fluoride
in small amounts, are required for life; the same substances at higher dose poison, and
the ladder's position differs by substance. Iron cures anaemia at one dose and
hospitalises a child at another. Arsenic, famously non-essential, is toxic at every
rung. The WHO's chemical-safety work treats the essential and the non-essential with
the same question, how much reaches whom (WHO, n.d.).

### Routes of absorption

Unit 5 named the three doors: the lung (fastest, the air pollutants' door), the gut
(food and water, arsenic's door), and the skin (the sprayer's door). The route changes
the dose that reaches the blood, and the same substance can be a different exposure by
a different door: a farm worker takes in the same pesticide by skin, breath and
hand-to-mouth, and the route mix decides how much rides Unit 5's journey.

### Bioaccumulation and bio-magnification

Two words, two scales. <Glossary term="Bioaccumulation">Bioaccumulation</Glossary> is
within one organism: a substance that enters faster than it leaves builds up over time
in the organism's fat or bone. Bio-magnification is up the food chain: each predator
eats many prey and inherits what they accumulated, so the concentration rises step by
step toward the top, the big fish, the fish-eating bird, the fishing family.

<Figure id="fig-U6-6" src="/img/figures/gnas-301/unit-06/fig-U6-6.svg" alt="Diagram of bioaccumulation and bio-magnification up a four-step lake food chain, plankton, small fish, large fish and the fishing family, with the concentration rising at each step" width={780} height={470} />

**A worked example.** The Keenjhar dust diagram, explained. If the water carries a
trace of a fat-loving pollutant, the plankton hold a little; each small fish eats many
plankton and holds more; each big fish eats many small fish and holds a lot; the family
eats the big fish and receives the whole chain's collections. The guide's warning is
the food-chain arithmetic, and it is why the top predators, birds of prey, big fish,
and people, are the monitoring stations of environmental poisoning, the story the
Minamata bay's mercury taught the world.

**Common misconception.** "If the water is only slightly polluted, the fish are only
slightly polluted." The chain is a multiplier, not a mirror: each step concentrates, so
a trace in the water can be a dose in the fish. This is why testing the water is not
enough, and why the fishing family's big fish, not the lake sample, is the exposure
that matters.

## Activity: Build the ladder

Work in groups of four. Each member takes one rung of the Keenjhar ladder, plankton,
small fish, large fish, family, and receives a card showing how many of the rung below
they eat and what each carries. Multiply up the ladder, write your rung's total on the
board, and the class reads the magnification factor from water to family. Then answer:
which single control would cut the family's dose most? Allow 25 minutes.

## Summary

Organic pollutants dissolve in fat and linger; inorganic metals never break down and
bind the body's working parts. Essentiality and toxicity sit on one ladder, iron
essential at one dose and poisonous at another, arsenic toxic at every rung. The three
routes of absorption, lung, gut and skin, each deliver a different dose to the blood.
Bioaccumulation builds within one organism and bio-magnification up the chain, so a
trace in the water becomes a dose in the top predator, the big fish and the family that
eats it. The chain is a multiplier, not a mirror.

from. Do not name the school.
