# Unit 3 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

Two pollution topics, then the framework, then the tool. Topics 3.1 and 3.2 deliberately
reuse Unit 2's source-pathway-receptor chain so the chain itself becomes the thing
students know best; 3.3 reframes the same exposures as hazard classes; 3.4 hands over the
risk cycle that Unit 4 will scale up to whole organisations. The hazard walk in 3.3 and
the risk brief in 3.4 are one continuous project across two weeks: do not let the pairs
change their five cards between them.

## Teaching strategies (from the course guide)

The guide names interactive lectures, seminars, group work, case studies, presentations,
guided reading, home study and individual tutorials. This unit leans on case study (the
Lahore smog episode, the Karachi site study, the Dadu relief camp) and on the two
measurement activities, the AQI reading and the sound meter, which convert abstract
scales into numbers the trainees produced themselves. Individual tutorials have their
natural place after the risk brief: each pair's ranking deserves five minutes of
argument.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Clean air means air you cannot see."** Probe with PM2.5: which pollutant carries
   the heaviest burden, and can you see it? The AQI reading settles the argument.
2. **"Noise is annoying but harmless."** Probe with the permanent, painless nature of
   hearing loss and the 430 million WHO figure. Ask: what would we accept if smoke
   damaged hearing instead of lungs?
3. **"Hazards mean dramatic accidents."** Probe with the quiet exposures: heat, dust,
   stress, standing water. The five-class table exists so the quiet ones are not missed.
4. **"Risk assessment is paperwork."** Probe with the order of events: when was the walk
   done, when was the form filled? The form nobody walked for is decoration.

## Managing the activities

**Topic 3.1, Read the index (20 minutes, pairs).** Have a real week of AQI values ready;
Pakistani cities are covered by the usual apps. The judgement to protect is the
cancel-or-move decision, which rehearses receptor-end thinking.

**Topic 3.2, Measure three places (20 minutes, pairs).** One phone per pair is enough.
The debrief question is the comparison with the ladder figure: where do our numbers sit
against the damage zone?

**Topic 3.3, Hazard-spotting walk (25 minutes, pairs).** Safety first: observe only,
handle nothing. The ranking argument at the end is the bridge to 3.4; do not skip it.

**Topic 3.4, Score and defend (25 minutes, pairs, then cross-examination).** The
cross-examination is the teaching move: another pair argues for a different order, and
the grid has to answer, not the loudest voice.

## Practical work and practicum links

The hazard walk and the risk brief are the unit's main practicals and feed directly into
Unit 4's workplace audit. The guide's practical hour can also carry one AQI week
(3.1) and one three-spot sound survey (3.2). Debrief all of them together: the same five
cards have now been through classification, scoring and control, which is the whole unit
in one artefact.
