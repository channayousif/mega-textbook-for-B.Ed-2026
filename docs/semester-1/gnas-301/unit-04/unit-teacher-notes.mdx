# Unit 4 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

Seven topics, one arc: the sectors and their failures (4.1), the system that answers
(4.2), the layers that make it real (4.3) and the people who carry it (4.4); then the
theory (4.5), the law and the plan (4.6), and the quiet exposures with the toolbox (4.7).
The mill family from 4.1 returns in every topic, so protect that first lesson; and 4.6's
one-page plan is the assessment's ERQ-4 rehearsal, so leave time for the fair-plan
activity before the unit test.

## Teaching strategies (from the course guide)

The guide names interactive lectures, seminars, group work, case studies, presentations,
guided reading, home study and individual tutorials. This unit leans on case study (the
mill, the Karachi fire, the ignored safety officer) and on group work for the triangle
negotiation in 4.4 and the tool match in 4.7. The home-study element is the near-miss
book in 4.5, which runs for one week at the practicum school and is the unit's most
transferable artefact.

## Common misconceptions to probe

1. **"Accidents happen to careless workers."** Probe with the locked exit and the
   wage-bound uncle: who chose the lock, and who could afford to refuse?
2. **"A management system is paperwork."** Probe with the extinguisher-on-the-wall story:
   which PDCA stage does an extinguisher without a check belong to?
3. **"Safety is the safety officer's job."** Probe with the ignored report: if the officer
   is ignored, who still holds the duty?
4. **"No accidents means safe."** Probe with the empty near-miss book: what does silence
   measure?

## Managing the activities

**4.1, Portrait of four sectors (25 minutes, four groups).** Push groups past the obvious
hazard to the quiet one, dust, stress, in each portrait; the ninety-second format keeps
the presentations honest.

**4.2, PDCA on your school (20 minutes, pairs).** The swap-and-mark-one-gap step is the
teaching move; gaps found by peers land harder than gaps found by tutors.

**4.4, The triangle in practice (25 minutes, three groups).** Assign the roles firmly.
The negotiation usually stalls on cost, which is the moment to introduce the uncle's
question: what is the worker's alternative to accepting?

**4.5, Find the holes (20 minutes, pairs).** Drawing matters; the fifth slice of their own
design is where the hierarchy from Unit 3 gets used without being named.

**4.6, The one-page fair plan (25 minutes, pairs).** Enforce the one-page limit. The
missing-name-and-date hunt in the swap teaches more than the drafting.

**4.7, Tool match (20 minutes, pairs).** Speed round; keep the tools on cards so the
matching is physical.

## Practical work and practicum links

The near-miss book (4.5) and the one-page plan (4.6) are the unit's practicals, and both
run at the practicum school. The guide's practical hour can also carry the sector-portrait
research in 4.1, an interview with one working relative. Debrief all three together: the
book supplies the hazard list, the plan organises it, and both feed Unit 5's governance
discussion of the law behind them.
