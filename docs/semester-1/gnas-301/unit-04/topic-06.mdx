# Topic 4.6 - HSE statutes, regulations and plans

## A real classroom situation

A school fair is planned: food stalls, a generator, a bouncy ride, four hundred visitors.
The head teacher asks the trainee teacher, "You did the safety course. Write me the safety
plan." The trainee freezes, then remembers Topic 3.4's risk brief, and says, "It is the
same walk, on paper, with names and dates." The head teacher says, "One page. Friday."

This topic is how that page gets written, and what stands behind it in law.

## Explanation

### HSE statutes and regulations

Pakistan's HSE statute book is built in layers. At the top, the **Pakistan Environmental
Protection Act 1997** governs discharges and waste, with the National Environmental
Quality Standards (NEQS) as its enforceable limits, the same yardstick Unit 2's effluent
treatment aimed at. For workplaces, the **Factories Act 1934** and the provincial factory
legislation built on it carry the classic duties: guarding, ventilation, hours, and the
welfare provisions. Labour law, including the Industrial and Commercial Employment
(Standing Orders) Ordinance and the provincial labour codes, carries the employment-side
duties. After the 18th Constitutional Amendment in 2010, labour and much environmental
competence devolved to the provinces, so Sindh, Punjab and the others maintain their own
acts and enforcement, for example Sindh's environmental protection legislation. The
practical consequence for a workplace is that it must know both its national floor and
its provincial detail.

### Establishing HSE plans

An <Glossary term="HSE plan">HSE plan</Glossary> is the document that turns the statute
book and the PDCA cycle into a named, dated commitment for one workplace. Its steps are
few and fixed.

<Figure id="fig-U4-11" src="/img/figures/gnas-301/unit-04/fig-U4-11.svg" alt="Flowchart of the six steps of establishing an HSE plan, scope, hazards, objectives, controls and resources, training and communication, monitoring and review, as a loop" width={780} height={470} />

1. **Scope**: one activity, one site, one period. A fair, a mill's dye house, a
   construction phase. Plans that try to cover everything cover nothing.
2. **Hazards**: the walk and the register from Unit 3, in this scope only.
3. **Objectives**: measurable, with dates. "Zero locked exits during operating hours,
   checked hourly" is an objective; "safety first" is a poster.
4. **Controls and resources**: each hazard matched to a control at its hierarchy level,
   with the person and the budget named.
5. **Training and communication**: who must know what, in which language, before the
   activity starts.
6. **Monitoring and review**: who checks what, when; and the date the plan is re-read.

**A worked example.** The fair plan, one page. Scope: the school grounds, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.
Hazards: the generator's exhaust and fuel (chemical, physical), the ride's anchorage
(mechanical), the food stalls' gas cylinders (chemical), crowd crush at the gate
(physical), lost children (psychological). Objectives: generator sited downwind and
fuelled cold; ride inspected before opening; cylinders on firm ground away from flames;
one gate open in and one out; a lost-child point staffed all day. Controls and resources:
caretaker for the generator, ride operator's certificate, stallholders' briefing, two
senior pupils at the gates, one teacher at the lost-child point. Training: a ten-minute
all-staff briefing on the morning. Monitoring: the head teacher walks the grounds at 11
and 1; review the Monday after. That is the whole method, and it fits on one page.

**Common misconception.** "A plan is a folder the inspector asks for." The inspector asks
for the statutes; the plan is what the workplace writes for itself, because the statutes
cannot know where the generator will stand. A folder nobody signed is a wish; a page with
names and dates is a plan.

## Activity: The one-page fair plan

Work in pairs. Using the worked example's six steps, write your own one-page HSE plan for
the school fair, but for your own school's layout: name the two gates, the generator
corner and the rides' surface. Swap with another pair, who must find one hazard you
missed and one name or date you left blank. Allow 25 minutes.

   sentence. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Pakistan's HSE statutes layer the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act 1997 with its
NEQS, the Factories Act 1934 and provincial factory law, the labour codes, sector rules
and the international instruments, with much competence devolved to the provinces since
2010. An HSE plan turns the layers into a one-workplace commitment through six fixed
steps: scope, hazards, objectives, controls and resources, training and communication,
monitoring and review. The test of a plan is names and dates, not pages.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask for the school's most recent event, a fair, sports day, exam series, and whether any
written plan existed. If yes, read it against the six steps and name the missing steps;
if no, write the missing one-page plan for the next event, with names and dates.

## Summative task

Write 170 to 200 words on the following.

> Write the HSE plan outline for your practicum school's annual sports day on one page:
> scope, the five most likely hazards across at least three classes, one measurable
> objective per hazard with its control level, the training line, and the monitoring line
> with names and times.

**Mini-rubric**

| Criterion | What a strong response does |
|---|---|
| Six steps present (Apply) | Scope, hazards, objectives, controls, training, monitoring, all named |
| Hazards classified (Understand) | Five hazards across three or more classes, correctly labelled |
| Measurable (Analyze) | Every objective has a number or a date, and every line a name |
| Clarity and register | One page, plain words, no padding; within the word count |
