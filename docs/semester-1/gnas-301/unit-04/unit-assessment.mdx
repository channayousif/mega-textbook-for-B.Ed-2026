# Unit 4 assessment

## Unit summary

Each workplace sector, industry, construction, agriculture and services, carries a
characteristic hazard portrait, and incidents grade from near-miss through injury and
fatality to the under-counted chronic illness; the 2012 Karachi factory fire shows small
failures lining up behind a locked door. An OSH management system runs the PDCA loop,
plan, do, check, act, with leading indicators preferred over injury counts, under the
international reference points of ILO Convention 155 and ISO 45001. Regulation supplies
the ladder of act, regulations, codes and guidance; internal control supplies the layers,
philosophy, policy, procedure, supervision, records, that make rules real, and the
philosophy layer decides the rest. The duties triangle, employer, worker, government,
carries the rights, know, protect, refuse, raise, and the OHS professional works the six-
role web while the employer holds the duty. The incident pyramid and the Swiss cheese
model explain why small failures and serious harm share causes. Pakistan's statutes layer
the 1997 Environmental Protection Act with its NEQS, the Factories Act 1934 and provincial
law, with much competence devolved since 2010; an HSE plan turns them into one page with
names and dates. The quiet exposures, heat, dust, noise, stress, ergonomics, are managed
by ordinary tools mapped to the system's elements.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In the Faisalabad spinning-mill study, the workers' burden was chiefly: *(Understand)*
   a) falls from height
   b) respiratory disease with lost work days
   c) hearing loss
   d) pesticide poisoning

2. The 2012 Karachi factory fire is described as "ordinary in its mechanism" because it:
   *(Understand)*
   a) was caused by an unusual chemical reaction
   b) resulted from several small failures lining up
   c) happened in an old building only
   d) was deliberately set

3. The four stages of an OSH management system's engine are: *(Remember)*
   a) predict, decide, correct, approve
   b) plan, do, check, act
   c) policy, drill, count, audit
   d) scope, hazard, control, record

4. On the regulatory ladder, codes of practice sit between: *(Understand)*
   a) acts and regulations
   b) regulations and guidance
   c) guidance and company rules
   d) statutes and the constitution

5. Under the duties triangle, the employer's core duty is: *(Remember)*
   a) following the safety officer's advice in all cases
   b) providing a workplace without avoidable harm
   c) reporting every near-miss to government
   d) paying for the worker's own protective equipment

6. In the incident pyramid, the widest band at the base is: *(Remember)*
   a) fatalities
   b) serious injuries
   c) near-misses and unsafe acts
   d) property damage

7. A director claims two accident-free years prove safety. The strongest reply uses:
   *(Apply)*
   a) the factory's equipment age
   b) the distinction between leading and lagging indicators
   c) the number of safety officers employed
   d) the size of the insurance policy

8. After the 18th Constitutional Amendment, a workplace's legal homework grew because:
   *(Understand)*
   a) national law was abolished
   b) labour and environmental competence largely devolved to the provinces
   c) ILO conventions became binding directly
   d) the courts stopped hearing safety cases

9. Which pairing of tool and system element is correct? *(Apply)*
   a) near-miss book and plan
   b) sound meter and act
   c) legal register and plan
   d) corrective-action tracker and do

10. The WHO's estimate of 12 billion working days lost yearly belongs to the challenge
    of: *(Understand)*
    a) heat
    b) dust
    c) ergonomics
    d) stress and mental health

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Name the four workplace sectors and one characteristic hazard of each. *(Remember)*
2. Explain why chronic illness is the incident grade that statistics under-count. *(Analyze)*
3. Why is a system steered only by injury counts "steered by its failures"? Name the
   alternative indicator type. *(Analyze)*
4. List the five layers of internal control, and name the one the others inherit from.
   *(Understand)*
5. Name the four worker rights and explain why the right to refuse needs the others to
   work. *(Understand)*
6. What does the shape of the incident pyramid tell a safety officer to count, and why?
   *(Understand)*
7. Explain the Karachi fire in Swiss cheese terms, in two sentences. *(Analyze)*
8. State the six steps of establishing an HSE plan, in order. *(Remember)*
9. Give two mechanisms by which the external environment crosses into a workplace. *(Apply)*
10. Match four HSE tools to the management-system element each supports. *(Apply)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Your cousin is joining a spinning mill. Using the sector portrait and the PDCA cycle,
   write what the mill should already have in place for dust, from policy to review, and
   the one question your cousin should ask on the first day. *(Analyze)*
2. A safety officer's report is ignored by the owner. Using internal control's layers and
   the duties triangle, analyse what has failed, who still holds the duty, and what the
   officer's remaining roles are. *(Evaluate)*
3. A director says, "No accidents for two years, so we are safe." Write the reply using
   the incident pyramid, the leading-versus-lagging distinction and the organizational
   environment, ending with two measurements to start this month. *(Evaluate)*
4. Write the one-page HSE plan outline for your practicum school's sports day: scope,
   five hazards across three classes, one measurable objective per hazard with its
   hierarchy level, the training line, and the monitoring line with names and times.
   *(Create)*
5. Integrative: the school's June examination series is the workplace. Write the full HSE
   plan outline for it, covering the quiet health exposures, heat, noise and stress, the
   external environment that feeds them, and the toolbox, naming actual tools, that makes
   each measurable, with the monitoring and review lines to close the loop. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. b - the study found respiratory disease and work absence in Faisalabad's spinning mills.
2. b - locked exits, no alarm, stored combustibles and no audit lined up; each alone is
   ordinary.
3. b - plan, do, check, act, the loop borrowed from quality management.
4. b - regulations say how; codes say what counts as doing it well; guidance advises.
5. b - the duty of care: a workplace without avoidable harm, with information, training
   and equipment.
6. c - near-misses and unsafe acts are the base; the serious injury is the apex.
7. b - injury counts are lagging; two quiet years may mean no reporting, not no failures.
8. b - devolution moved labour and much environmental competence to the provinces.
9. c - the legal register feeds planning; the near-miss book and meters feed checking; the
   tracker feeds acting.
10. d - depression and anxiety cost an estimated 12 billion working days a year.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: industry, dust and unguarded machines; construction, falls and excavation
   collapse; agriculture, pesticides and machinery; services, lifting, stress and
   infection. **Marks (4):** 1 per sector-hazard pair.
2. Model: fatality and injury statistics count events; chronic illness develops slowly,
   often after leaving the job, and is attributed to the person rather than the exposure,
   so it misses the registers, as the uncle's cough shows. **Marks (2):** 1 for the
   slow-development point; 1 for the attribution point.
3. Model: injury counts record what already went wrong, so the system reacts after harm;
   leading indicators, inspections done, near-misses reported, measure what the system is
   doing before harm. **Marks (2):** 1 for the reactive point; 1 for naming leading
   indicators.
4. Model: philosophy, policy, procedure, supervision, records; the others inherit from
   philosophy, what leadership actually rewards. **Marks (3):** 2 for all five layers; 1
   for naming philosophy as the parent.
5. Model: know, protect, refuse, raise; refusal without the right to know and to raise
   concerns is blind and punished, so the rights work as a set. **Marks (3):** 2 for four
   rights; 1 for the interdependence argument.
6. Model: count the base, near-misses and unsafe acts, because they share causes with the
   apex, so the base is where the next fatality is visible and cheapest to fix.
   **Marks (2):** 1 for what to count; 1 for the shared-causes reason.
7. Model: the locked door, missing alarm, stored combustibles and missing audit were four
   slices with holes; the harm passed when the holes lined up, and any working slice would
   have stopped it. **Marks (2):** 1 for slices and holes; 1 for the alignment.
8. Model: scope, hazards, objectives, controls and resources, training and communication,
   monitoring and review. **Marks (3):** all six in order for 3; four or five for 2; two
   or three for 1.
9. Model: any two of heat crossing into uncooled rooms, road noise and air entering from
   the street, floods interrupting travel and supply, power cuts stopping fans and pumps.
   **Marks (2):** 1 per mechanism.
10. Model: legal register to plan; training kit and checklists to do; inspection forms,
    near-miss book, sound meter to check; review agenda and corrective-action tracker to
    act. **Marks (4):** 1 per correct pairing.
