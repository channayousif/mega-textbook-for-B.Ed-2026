# The Writing Process

Good writing does not appear fully formed on the page. It is produced through a process - a series
of stages a writer moves through from first idea to final draft. This unit walks you through that
process: generating ideas before you write, drafting in stages, revising for content and editing for
form, proof reading for small errors, and using peer review to get fresh eyes on your work.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Use at least three pre-writing techniques (brainstorming, mind-mapping, free-writing, outlining) to generate and organize ideas before drafting.
2. Describe the three-stage drafting process and explain what each stage contributes.
3. Distinguish revising (content and organization) from editing (grammar and form) and apply both to a draft.
4. Proof read a text for errors in spelling, punctuation and formatting.
5. Give and receive structured peer feedback using a checklist.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 of this course (GENG-301). You should be able to write a single unified paragraph and understand the characteristics of effective expository writing (clarity, coherence, organization).

## How to use this unit

This unit teaches a process, so the order matters. Work through the topics in sequence - each one
builds on the last. The activities ask you to practise each stage on a real writing task, not just
read about it. By the end you will have moved a piece of writing from first idea to reviewed draft.
