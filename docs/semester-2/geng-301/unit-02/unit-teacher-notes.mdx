# Unit 2 Teacher Notes

These notes support the teaching of GENG-301 Unit 2. They assume you have read the unit content and
the course guide.

## Teaching strategies

This unit teaches a process. The most effective strategy is to have pupils *do* the process while
they learn it - not just read about it.

1. **Demonstrate each stage.** Before asking pupils to pre-write, demonstrate brainstorming on the
   board with a real topic. Write your thoughts as they come, without editing. Pupils see that
   pre-writing is messy and that is acceptable.
2. **Use think-pair-share** for the peer review activity (Topic 2.4). Pupils read their partner's
   draft, complete the checklist individually, then discuss feedback together.
3. **Use question-answer** to reinforce the difference between revising and editing. Ask: "If I change
   the order of paragraphs, am I revising or editing?" Pupils who can answer correctly understand the
   distinction.
4. **Use group work** for the three-stage drafting activity in Topic 2.2. Groups draft together on a
   shared topic, producing rough, structured and refined versions in sequence.

## Sequencing

Teach the four topics in order - each builds on the previous. A reasonable split for a four-week unit:

- Week 1: Topic 2.1 (pre-writing).
- Week 2: Topic 2.2 (drafting).
- Week 3: Topic 2.3 (revising and editing).
- Week 4: Topic 2.4 (proof reading and peer review) and the unit assessment.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

| Misconception | Topic | How to handle |
|---|---|---|
| "Pre-writing is a waste of time" | 2.1 | Time two pupils: one pre-writes for five minutes, one starts drafting immediately. Compare the quality and speed of their drafts. |
| "A draft should be nearly perfect" | 2.2 | Show a published writer's rough draft (with crossings-out and margin notes) to demonstrate that rough is normal. |
| "Revising and editing are the same thing" | 2.3 | Give two before-and-after examples: one where only sentences changed (editing), one where paragraphs were reordered (revising). Ask which is which. |
| "Asking for feedback is cheating" | 2.4 | Explain that professional writers, researchers and even textbook authors use peer review. It is a skill, not a shortcut. |

## Practical work

- **Group work**: the three-stage drafting activity (Topic 2.2) works well in groups of three.
- **Group assignment**: as a group, produce a short essay on a given topic, documenting the process -
  submit the pre-writing notes, the rough draft, the structured draft and the refined draft together.
- **Individual assignment**: each pupil produces one complete paragraph through all stages of the
  writing process and writes a brief reflection on what they changed at each stage.
- **Presentations**: pairs present their drafting process (Topic 2.2) showing how their draft changed
  from rough to refined.

## Connection to practicum

In Topic 2.4, the "Try this" task asks pupils to swap writing with colleagues for peer review. This
builds a habit of seeking feedback before submitting work - a professional practice that serves them in
their B.Ed assignments and later in their teaching career.
