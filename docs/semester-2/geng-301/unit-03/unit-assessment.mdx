## Unit summary

This unit taught you the architecture of a well-organized expository essay.

- Topic 3.1 covered hooks and the funnel structure of introductions.
- Topic 3.2 gave the features of a strong thesis statement.
- Topic 3.3 covered body paragraphs: topic sentences, evidence, explanation and transitions.
- Topic 3.4 covered conclusion types and cohesion across the whole essay.

Together these give you a complete picture: an essay is a unified structure where every part connects
to the thesis and leads logically to the next.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Where in an essay does the thesis statement usually appear?
   - A) At the very beginning, as the first sentence
   - B) At the end of the introduction
   - C) In the middle of the first body paragraph
   - D) In the conclusion

2. *(Remember)* How many types of conclusions did this unit introduce?
   - A) Two
   - B) Three
   - C) Four
   - D) Five

3. *(Understand)* A thesis statement says, "This essay will discuss several things about teaching."
   What is the main weakness?
   - A) It is too short
   - B) It announces the topic but states no controlling idea
   - C) It uses a hook
   - D) It has too many points

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following best describes the funnel structure of an introduction?
   - A) Narrow to wide: thesis first, then examples
   - B) Wide to narrow: hook, then context, then thesis
   - C) Wide to narrow: thesis, then hook, then examples
   - D) Narrow to wide: conclusion first, then body

5. *(Apply)* A paragraph begins, "School uniforms identify pupils by their school colours." The essay
   thesis is "Daily reading improves vocabulary by introducing new words in context." What is the
   most likely problem with this topic sentence?
   - A) The paragraph has no topic sentence
   - B) The paragraph's topic sentence does not connect to the thesis
   - C) The paragraph is too long
   - D) The paragraph uses a statistic

6. *(Understand)* Which hook type is this: "What if every child in Pakistan had access to a library?"
   - A) Anecdote
   - B) Bold statement
   - C) Question
   - D) Statistic

7. *(Apply)* A conclusion begins: "In conclusion, we have seen that reading matters. Schools should
   build libraries." What type is this?
   - A) Summary only
   - B) Call to action only
   - C) Summary + call to action
   - D) Full circle

8. *(Apply)* A body paragraph gives an example but does not explain how the example supports the point.
   What should the writer add?
   - A) Another example
   - B) A transition word
   - C) An explanation connecting the evidence to the point
   - D) A longer topic sentence

9. *(Understand)* Which transition word signals contrast?
   - A) Furthermore
   - B) However
   - C) In addition
   - D) As a result

10. *(Apply)* Every paragraph supports the thesis and the points are in a logical order, but there
    are no repeated key terms, referring words or transitions linking paragraphs. What needs strengthening?
    - A) The hook
    - B) Cohesion between paragraphs
    - C) The thesis length
    - D) The number of body paragraphs

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the funnel structure of an introduction using the hook,
   context and thesis.

2. *(Remember)* State four features of a strong thesis statement from the checklist in Topic 3.2.

3. *(Apply)* A pupil writes this thesis: "Games are very good for children and they should be used in
   schools." Rewrite it to be more specific and focused (one sentence).

4. *(Apply*) A body paragraph begins: "Another thing about teaching is that it is a noble
   profession." Explain why this is a weak topic sentence for a body paragraph about "how teacher
   training improves classroom practice."

5. *(Apply)* A pupil wants to connect a paragraph about "homework benefits" to one about "homework
   problems." Which transition word group (Topic 3.3) should they use and why?

6. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between a summary conclusion and a full circle conclusion in one
   sentence each.

7. *(Apply)* A pupil's essay begins: "In this essay I will talk about why homework matters." Rewrite
   this opening as a question hook followed by one sentence of context that narrows toward a thesis.

8. *(Analyze)* A pupil's essay has three body paragraphs, each starting with "Another thing is..." What
   is the cohesion problem and how would you fix it?

9. *(Apply)* Write a topic sentence for a body paragraph about "how group work builds communication
   skills" that connects to the thesis: "Collaborative learning develops skills that individual
   practice cannot."

10. *(Analyze*) An essay ends with a conclusion that introduces the topic of school funding (never
    discussed in the essay). Identify the problem, explain why it matters and suggest a fix.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (80-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit taught that an introduction should follow a funnel structure (hook → context →
   thesis). Compare this with the common practice of starting an essay with "In this essay I will
   discuss..." Explain, with an example, why the funnel structure is more effective for engaging a
   reader.

2. *(Evaluate)* Read this short practice essay and judge its overall organizational quality.
   Use at least three criteria from this unit. Quote evidence for each judgment, propose a specific
   repair for each problem, and explain which repair you would make first and why.

   > Education is important. In this essay I will discuss many things about education.
   >
   > School learning can prepare pupils for work. For example, reading instructions helps a pupil
   > follow the steps in a practical task. This connects classroom reading with a skill used at work.
   >
   > Another thing is the economy. Education helps it. This is very important for everyone.
   >
   > Furthermore, sports improve health. Running gives pupils exercise. They can run in the playground.
   >
   > In conclusion, education is important. School buildings also need more funding.

3. *(Analyze)* Read this body paragraph and analyse its strengths and weaknesses.
   > "Group work helps pupils. For example, when pupils work in groups, they talk to each other.
   > Talking helps them learn. Another example is that they can share ideas. Sharing ideas is
   > beneficial."
   Evaluate the paragraph against three criteria from Topic 3.3: topic sentence quality, evidence
   quality and explanation quality.

4. *(Evaluate)* A pupil writes a conclusion: "In conclusion, homework is good. It helps pupils learn.
   Teachers should give homework. Pupils should do it." Evaluate this conclusion using what you learned
   in Topic 3.4. What type or types is it trying to be? What are its main weaknesses?

5. *(Create)* You are teaching a Grade 7 class to write expository essays. Write a short explanation
   (50-70 words) of the funnel structure of an introduction, using language a Grade 7 pupil would
   understand. Then describe one activity you would use to help pupils practise writing hooks.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - The thesis usually appears at the end of the introduction, after hook and context.
2. **C** - Four: summary, call to action, full circle, forward-looking.
3. **B** - It announces the topic but states no controlling idea. It does not tell the reader what the essay will explain.
4. **B** - Wide to narrow: hook (wide), context (narrowing), thesis (narrow, specific).
5. **B** - The topic sentence does not connect to the thesis. "School uniforms" has no link to "how daily reading improves vocabulary."
6. **C** - It begins with "What if..." - a question hook.
7. **C** - "In conclusion, we have seen..." is summary; "Schools should build libraries" is call to action.
8. **C** - The evidence needs an explanation connecting it to the point.
9. **B** - "However" signals contrast. A signals addition, C signals addition, D signals cause/effect.
10. **B** - The ideas already form a coherent sequence, but the language links between paragraphs are missing.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** An introduction starts with a hook (wide, engaging), moves to context (narrowing) and ends with the thesis statement (narrow, specific). This funnel shape prepares the reader for the argument. **Marks (2):** 1 - names all three parts; 1 - explains the wide-to-narrow movement.

2. **Model:** One sentence with a clear main idea; states what the essay will explain or argue; specific in scope; does not merely announce the topic. **Marks (2):** 0.5 for each distinct correct feature, up to 2 marks.

3. **Model:** Any specific, focused rewrite. E.g. "Structured group games in primary school develop teamwork and communication skills that individual desk work does not." **Marks (2):** 1 - specific; 1 - focused on one controlling idea.

4. **Model:** It is weak because it does not connect to the thesis point about "how teacher training improves classroom practice." It says only that teaching is noble - a vague statement unrelated to the thesis. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the lack of connection; 1 - explains why it matters.

5. **Model:** Contrast (however, on the other hand). The two paragraphs take opposite views of homework, so a contrast transition signals the shift. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the correct group; 1 - explains the reason.

6. **Model:** A summary conclusion restates the thesis and main points in different words. A full circle conclusion returns to the hook from the opening. **Marks (2):** 1 per correct definition.

7. **Model:** Any relevant question hook followed by a narrowing context sentence. E.g. "What if the homework pupils receive every night is doing more harm than good? To answer this question, we need to examine the purpose and design of the tasks pupils take home." **Marks (2):** 1 - rewrites as a clear question hook; 1 - the context sentence narrows toward a thesis.

8. **Model:** The cohesion problem is that all three topic sentences use the same vague structure ("Another thing is"), giving no sense of progression. Fix: rewrite each topic sentence to state its specific point and connect to the thesis. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the problem; 1 - suggests a specific fix.

9. **Model:** Any specific topic sentence that connects to the thesis and states the communication-skills point. E.g. "Collaborative learning develops communication skills because pupils must explain, listen and negotiate meaning together." **Marks (2):** 1 - connects to thesis; 1 - states the point specifically.

10. **Model:** The problem is that the conclusion introduces a new topic (school funding) not discussed in the essay. This confuses the reader and weakens the closing impact. Fix: cut the new topic and restate the actual thesis and main points. **Marks (2):** 0.5 - identifies the new topic; 0.5 - explains the consequence for the reader; 1 - suggests a specific fix.
