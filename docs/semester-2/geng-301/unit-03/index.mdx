# Essay Organization and Structure

A well-written essay is more than good paragraphs placed side by side. It has a deliberate structure:
an introduction that draws the reader in and states the main idea, body paragraphs that develop that idea
with evidence, and a conclusion that leaves the reader with a clear final impression. This unit teaches
you that structure so you can both write well-organized essays and teach your pupils to do the same.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Write an effective hook and introduction that engage the reader and lead to a thesis statement.
2. Craft a clear, focused, arguable thesis statement.
3. Build body paragraphs with topic sentences, supporting evidence and transitional devices.
4. Write a conclusion that rounds off the essay without introducing new ideas.
5. Evaluate an essay's cohesion and coherence and identify where connections between paragraphs break down.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 and 2 of this course (GENG-301). You should be able to write a unified paragraph and understand
the writing process from pre-writing through proof reading.

## In this unit

1. [Starting strong: hook and introduction](./topic-01) - engaging the reader and setting up the thesis.
2. [The thesis statement](./topic-02) - crafting a clear central idea.
3. [Building body paragraphs](./topic-03) - topic sentences, evidence and transitions.

## How to use this unit

Each topic covers one layer of essay structure. Work through them in order. The activities ask you to
practise each component - writing thesis statements, building body paragraphs, analysing real essays -
so you learn by doing, not just by reading.
