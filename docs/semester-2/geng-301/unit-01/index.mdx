# Introduction to Expository Writing

Before you can write well in English, you need to understand what kind of writing you are being asked
to produce. In your B.Ed programme, in your practicum reports, and later in your professional work,
you will often be asked to *explain*, *inform* or *describe* clearly and logically. That is expository
writing. This unit gives you a working definition of expository writing, names the qualities that
make it effective, and introduces the basic building block of all expository texts: the paragraph.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Define expository writing and distinguish it from narration, description and argumentation.
2. Explain the roles of clarity, coherence and organization in effective expository writing.
3. Identify the components of a well-structured paragraph (topic sentence, supporting details, concluding sentence).
4. Draft a single paragraph that is unified, coherent and adequately developed.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Functional English (GENG-300) or equivalent HSC-level English. You should be able to write simple and
compound sentences in English and have a basic understanding of sentence-level grammar. No prior
experience with essay writing is assumed.

## In this unit

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the explanation,
and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your understanding" items ask you to retrieve
what you have learned - try them without looking back, then check your reasoning. The unit ends with
a summative assessment that draws the three topics together.
