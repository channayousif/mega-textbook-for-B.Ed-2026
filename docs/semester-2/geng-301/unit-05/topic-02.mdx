## A real classroom situation

A B.Ed student writes in an essay: "I really think homework is super bad for kids and every school
   should just stop it right now." Her tutor writes: "This is a personal opinion expressed in informal
language. Academic writing requires a more measured tone and evidence to support your claim."

<Figure id="fig-U5-2" kind="table" src="/img/figures/geng-301/unit-05/fig-U5-2.svg" alt="Table comparing informal and academic writing on tone, vocabulary, evidence and person." width={780} height={470} />

The student's ideas may be right, but her style was wrong for the audience. This topic teaches you the
academic style.

## Explanation

### What academic audiences expect

Academic readers (tutors, examiners, researchers) expect three things:

1. **Formality** - Avoid contractions (use "do not" not "don't"), avoid slang and avoid overly
   emotional language. Write "The evidence suggests" not "I really think."
2. **Objectivity** - Present evidence and reasoning rather than personal feeling. "Research indicates
   that..." is stronger than "I feel that..."
3. **Evidence-based reasoning** - Support every factual claim with a source, example or data. An
   unsupported claim is just an opinion.

### Academic conventions

Academic writing follows conventions your reader expects:

- **Citation**: When you use someone else's idea, data or words, cite the source (more on this in
   Unit 6).
- **Structure**: Clear introduction, organized body, explicit conclusion.
- **Hedging language**: Words like *suggests, may, appears to, in many cases* that show you are not
   overclaiming. "Homework always improves learning" overclaims. "Homework may improve learning when
   it is short and specific" is more careful and more honest.

<Figure id="fig-U5-6" kind="table" src="/img/figures/geng-301/unit-05/fig-U5-6.svg" alt="Table of hedging language phrases ranked by strength, with examples." width={780} height={470} />

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think academic writing means using the most complex vocabulary
possible. It does not. Academic writing is clear, precise and formal - not complicated. "The evidence
suggests" is better academic writing than "The empirical indicia demonstrate a correlation."

## Activity: Formalize an informal text

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Rewrite this informal paragraph in academic style. Address tone, vocabulary, evidence and hedging.

> "I really think that homework is super stressful for kids. Every child I know hates doing it. Schools
> should stop giving homework because it causes nothing but stress. Teachers who give too much homework
> are just being mean."

1. Remove informal language and emotional claims.
2. Add at least one hedging phrase.
3. Where the original makes a claim without evidence, add a placeholder like "[evidence needed]."
4. Compare with another pair. Discuss: what changed and why?

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name three things academic audiences expect from a writer.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why "I feel that homework is bad" is weaker in academic writing than "Research
   indicates that excessive homework may increase pupil stress."
3. *(Apply)* A pupil writes: "This proves that group work is the best method." Using what you learned
   about hedging, rewrite this sentence more carefully.

## Summary

Academic writing demands formality, objectivity and evidence-based reasoning. Use hedging language to
avoid overclaiming. Cite sources for factual claims. Academic writing is clear and precise, not
complicated.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can write in a formal academic tone.
- [ ] I can support claims with evidence rather than personal opinion.
- [ ] I can use hedging language to avoid overclaiming.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find a short academic text (a journal article abstract, a textbook section, a B.Ed assignment). Note
three features that make it academic: one vocabulary choice, one hedging phrase and one citation.

## Summative task

Rewrite this sentence in academic style: "I think every school should have a library because books
really help children learn." Your rewrite must: (1) use formal tone, (2) include a hedging phrase,
(3) include a citation placeholder. Then write one sentence explaining why your version is more
appropriate for an academic audience.

**Mini-rubric**

| Criterion | Levels |
|---|---|
| *Academic style* | 0-2: formal tone, no slang or contractions / partially formal / still informal. |
| *Hedging and citation* | 0-2: includes hedging phrase and citation placeholder / one of these / neither. |
| *Explanation* | 0-2: explains why the academic version is more appropriate / vague. |
