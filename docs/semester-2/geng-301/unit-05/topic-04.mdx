## A real classroom situation

A student writes a reflective journal entry in the same formal style as her academic essays. Her
tutor writes: "This is a reflection - I want to hear your voice, not a thesis statement. You can use
first person and a more personal tone here." The student did not realize that different tasks allow
different tones.

<Figure id="fig-U5-4" kind="table" src="/img/figures/geng-301/unit-05/fig-U5-4.svg" alt="Table showing how the same content changes in tone when written for an academic tutor, a parent newsletter and a personal reflection." width={780} height={470} />

Tone is not fixed. You adjust it to suit the task. This topic gives you practice in doing so.

## Explanation

### What shapes tone

<Tone> is the writer's attitude toward the reader and the topic, expressed through word choice,
sentence structure and level of formality. Three factors shape it:

1. **Audience** - A tutor expects formality; a parent newsletter expects warmth and clarity; a personal
   reflection allows intimacy.
2. **Purpose** - An argumentative essay needs a confident tone; an informative essay needs a neutral
   tone; a reflective journal may be exploratory and questioning.
3. **Form** - An academic essay uses third person and formal register; a blog post may use first person
   and conversational language; a notice uses direct, imperative language ("Attend the meeting on
   Monday").

### Register

<Glossary term="Register">Register</Glossary> is the level of formality in a piece of writing. It
operates on a spectrum from formal to informal:

| Register | Features | Appropriate for |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | Third person, complex sentences, technical vocabulary, no contractions | Academic essays, reports |
| Semi-formal | Mix of first and third person, moderate sentence length | Professional emails, newsletters |
| Informal | First person, short sentences, contractions, conversational | Reflections, blogs, notes |

The key skill is matching register to the task. Using an informal register in an academic essay
undermines your credibility. Using a formal register in a personal reflection creates distance where
the reader wants closeness.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think a single "good writing style" applies to all tasks. It
does not. A good writer adjusts tone and register to the audience and purpose. The same writer can
produce a formal essay, an engaging newsletter and a personal reflection - each in a different voice.

## Activity: Shift the register

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Take this sentence: "Pupils who read for twenty minutes every day show measurable improvement in
vocabulary and comprehension."

Rewrite it in three registers:
1. **Formal** (for a research report): use third person, hedging, precise vocabulary.
2. **Semi-formal** (for a parent newsletter): use clear, warm, accessible language.
3. **Informal** (for a personal blog): use first person, short sentences, a conversational tone.

Compare with another pair. Discuss: what specific words or structures changed between versions?

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name three factors that shape a writer's tone.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why the same content can be written in different registers for different
   audiences without changing the factual information.
3. *(Apply)* A pupil writes a reflective journal using third person and no first-person pronouns.
   Using what you learned, explain what is wrong and how to fix it.

## Summary

Tone and register are shaped by audience, purpose and form. A good writer adjusts tone to match the
task: formal for academic essays, semi-formal for professional communication, informal for personal
writing. The same information can be expressed in different registers without changing the facts.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can identify the register of a given text (formal, semi-formal, informal).
- [ ] I can rewrite a text in a different register.
- [ ] I understand why matching register to audience matters.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find two pieces of writing from your school: one formal (a report or official letter) and one informal
(a notice, a note on the staffroom board). Identify one vocabulary choice and one sentence structure
difference between them. Note how the register serves the audience.

## Summative task

Take this statement: "Regular assessment helps teachers understand what pupils have learned." Write
it in two registers - formal (for an academic essay) and semi-formal (for a staff meeting agenda).
Then write two sentences explaining which specific changes you made and why.
