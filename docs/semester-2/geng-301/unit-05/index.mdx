# Writing for Purposes and Audiences

The same topic can be written in very different ways depending on why you are writing and who will
read it. An essay on homework for your B.Ed tutor reads very differently from a paragraph on homework
for a parent newsletter. This unit teaches you to adjust your writing for purpose and audience.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Name four common writing purposes (to inform, analyze, persuade, entertain) and describe what each demands.
2. Distinguish writing for academic audiences from writing for public audiences.
3. Adjust tone, vocabulary and formality to suit a specific audience and purpose.
4. Analyse how a described writer's choices reflect their intended audience.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-4 of this course (GENG-301). You should understand expository writing, the writing process,
essay structure and expository types.

## In this unit

1. [Why are you writing?](./topic-01) - purposes: inform, analyze, persuade, entertain.
2. [Writing for academic readers](./topic-02) - formality, objectivity, conventions.
3. [Writing for public readers](./topic-03) - engagement, accessibility, public forms.
4. [Adjusting tone and style](./topic-04) - matching language to purpose and audience.

## How to use this unit

Each topic covers one dimension of audience-aware writing. The activities ask you to rewrite the same
content for different audiences - the most direct way to learn this skill.
