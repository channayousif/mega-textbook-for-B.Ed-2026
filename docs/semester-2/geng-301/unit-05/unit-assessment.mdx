## Unit summary

This unit taught you that writing is shaped by why you write and who reads it.

- Topic 5.1 covered four purposes: inform, analyze, persuade, entertain.
- Topic 5.2 covered academic audiences: formality, objectivity, evidence, hedging.
- Topic 5.3 covered public audiences: accessibility, engagement, scannability.
- Topic 5.4 covered tone and register: adjusting language to match audience and purpose.

Together these give you the skill of audience-aware writing: choosing the right tone, vocabulary and
structure for each task.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* How many writing purposes did this unit introduce?
   - A) Three
   - B) Four
   - C) Five
   - D) Six

2. *(Remember)* Which purpose does "argue" or "recommend" signal?
   - A) Inform
   - B) Analyze
   - C) Persuade
   - D) Entertain

3. *(Understand)* A tutor asks for an essay on "The causes of pupil dropout." Which purpose is
   primary?
   - A) Entertain
   - B) Persuade
   - C) Inform or analyze
   - D) None of these

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following is an example of hedging?
   - A) "This proves that..."
   - B) "Research suggests that..."
   - C) "I really think that..."
   - D) "Everyone knows that..."

5. *(Apply)* A parent newsletter uses the sentence: "It is recommended that guardians ensure the
   attendance of their wards." What is the problem?
   - A) Too informal
   - B) Too formal and complex for a parent audience
   - C) Missing a thesis
   - D) Too persuasive

6. *(Apply)* A pupil writes: "The data unequivocally demonstrates..." This tone is most appropriate
   for:
   - A) A personal reflection
   - B) A parent newsletter
   - C) An academic essay
   - D) A text message

7. *(Apply)* Which feature is most important for a public audience?
   - A) Long complex sentences
   - B) Accessibility and scannability
   - C) Formal citation style
   - D) Passive voice

8. *(Apply)* A writer uses first person, contractions and short sentences. Which register is this?
   - A) Formal
   - B) Semi-formal
   - C) Informal
   - D) Academic

9. *(Apply)* A pupil writes a reflective journal in the same formal style as their essays. What advice
   would you give?
   - A) "Make it even more formal"
   - B) "Use a more personal, less formal tone for a reflection"
   - C) "Add more citations"
   - D) "Write it as a persuasive essay"

10. *(Apply)* A notice to parents says: "The institutional timetable undergoes modification effective
    the first of the month." What is the main problem?
    - A) It is too persuasive
    - B) It uses vocabulary parents may not understand easily
    - C) It is too short
    - D) It uses first person

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain how the same topic might be written differently for an
   academic tutor and a parent audience.

2. *(Remember)* Name two features academic audiences expect and two features public audiences need.

3. *(Apply)* Rewrite this academic sentence in accessible public style: "The pedagogical intervention
   yielded statistically significant improvements in literacy outcomes."

4. *(Apply)* Identify the primary purpose: "Write a column for a local newspaper arguing that every
   school should plant trees on its grounds."

5. *(Apply)* A pupil writes in an essay: "I really think homework is bad." Using what you learned about
   academic style, rewrite this sentence more appropriately.

6. *(Understand)* Explain why hedging language ("suggests," "may," "appears to") is important in
   academic writing.

7. *(Apply)* Write one sentence in formal register and one in informal register, both conveying: "The
   school meeting is on Monday."

8. *(Analyze)* A newsletter uses long sentences, passive voice and technical terms. Using what you
   learned, identify two problems and explain why they matter for a parent audience.

9. *(Apply)* A pupil's reflective journal reads: "It is recommended that one engage in professional
   development activities." Explain the register problem and rewrite in appropriate style.

10. *(Analyze)* A column in Dawn argues for smaller class sizes. Identify the purpose, the likely
    audience and two features that make the writing suitable for that audience.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (80-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit taught that a writer should match tone to audience. Compare writing for an
   academic tutor with writing for a parent newsletter on the same topic. Identify, with examples, at
   least three specific differences.

2. *(Apply)* A B.Ed pupil has written an essay in an informal, conversational style with contractions
   and first person throughout. Describe the steps you would take to help them rewrite it in academic
   style. Name at least three specific changes.

3. *(Analyze)* Read this sentence and analyse its tone: "Every school in Sindh must immediately
   establish a functioning library - our children's futures depend on it." Identify the purpose, the
   likely audience and evaluate whether the tone is effective for that purpose.

4. *(Evaluate)* A pupil writes a parent newsletter using academic vocabulary and passive voice:
   "Attendance at the forthcoming parent-teacher interface is anticipated to facilitate enhanced
   pedagogical alignment." Evaluate this sentence using what you learned in Topics 5.2 and 5.3. Rewrite
   it in appropriate style.

5. *(Create)* Choose a topic you might write about as a teacher (e.g. homework, assessment, school
   events). Write two short versions - one academic (for a report) and one public (for a parent
   newsletter). Each should be 30-50 words. State one key difference between your versions.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Four: inform, analyze, persuade, entertain.
2. **C** - "Argue" and "recommend" signal persuasion.
3. **C** - Inform or analyze: the task asks for causes, which requires analysis.
4. **B** - "Research suggests that..." is hedging. A overclaims, C is personal, D is a sweeping claim.
5. **B** - Too formal and complex for parents. Simple language would work better.
6. **C** - The formal tone suits an academic essay.
7. **B** - Accessibility and scannability are most important for public audiences.
8. **C** - First person, contractions and short sentences signal informal register.
9. **B** - A reflection allows a more personal tone. The pupil is using the wrong register for the task.
10. **B** - The vocabulary ("institutional timetable undergoes modification") is too complex for a parent audience.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** An academic tutor expects formal tone, evidence and hedging. A parent audience needs accessible language, short sentences and clear key information. The same facts are presented differently for each. **Marks (2):** 1 - names academic expectations; 1 - names parent needs.

2. **Model:** Academic: formality, objectivity, evidence. Public: accessibility, engagement, scannability. **Marks (2):** 1 per correct pair.

3. **Model:** Any accessible rewrite. E.g. "The new teaching method helped pupils read and understand better." **Marks (2):** 1 - simpler vocabulary; 1 - shorter sentence.

4. **Model:** Persuade. The task asks the writer to argue a position ("arguing that"). **Marks (1).**

5. **Model:** "Research indicates that excessive homework may negatively affect pupil wellbeing." (or similar). **Marks (2):** 1 - formal tone; 1 - evidence-based rather than personal.

6. **Model:** Hedging shows the writer is careful not to overclaim. It signals that evidence supports but does not absolutely prove the claim. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the function; 1 - explains why it matters.

7. **Model:** Formal: "The school meeting has been scheduled for Monday." Informal: "Just a reminder - the meeting's on Monday!" **Marks (2):** 1 per correct register.

8. **Model:** Two problems: long sentences are hard to scan; passive voice makes the writing feel distant; technical terms exclude non-specialist readers. These matter because parents read quickly and need key information immediately. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies problems; 1 - explains the effect.

9. **Model:** The register is too formal for a personal reflection. Rewrite: "I should do more professional development this year." **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the register problem; 1 - provides appropriate rewrite.

10. **Model:** Purpose: persuade. Audience: general public / newspaper readers. Features: strong opening, direct language ("must immediately"), emotional appeal ("our children's futures"). **Marks (2):** 1 - names purpose and audience; 1 - identifies features.

| Criterion | 0-3 |

|---|---|

| Criterion | 0-3 |
|---|---|
| Identifies specific problems (vocabulary, passive voice) | 0-1 |
| Rewrite is accessible and appropriate | 0-1 |

| Criterion | 0-3 |
|---|---|
