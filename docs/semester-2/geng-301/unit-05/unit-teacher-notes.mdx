# Unit 5 Teacher Notes

These notes support the teaching of GENG-301 Unit 5.

## Teaching strategies

This unit teaches audience-aware writing. The most effective strategy is rewriting the same content
for different audiences.

1. **Use rewriting exercises.** Give the same paragraph and ask pupils to rewrite it for three
   audiences: academic, public, personal. This makes the difference concrete.
2. **Use question-answer** for purpose identification. Give writing tasks and ask: "What is the
   primary purpose? How do you know?" Pupils learn to read task instructions critically.
3. **Use group work** for the register-shifting activity. Groups rewrite in different registers, then
   compare and discuss what changed.
4. **Use brainstorming** for tone. Give a sentence and ask: "How would this sound to a tutor? To a
   parent? To a friend?" Pupils generate different versions.

## Sequencing

Teach the four topics in order. A reasonable split for a four-week unit:

- Week 1: Topic 5.1 (purposes).
- Week 2: Topic 5.2 (academic audiences).
- Week 3: Topic 5.3 (public audiences).
- Week 4: Topic 5.4 (tone and register) and the unit assessment.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

| Misconception | Topic | How to handle |
|---|---|---|
| "Academic writing means complex vocabulary" | 5.2 | Compare two sentences: one complex and unclear, one simple and precise. Ask which is better academic writing. |
| "Accessible means childish" | 5.3 | Show a well-written newspaper article. Note: the vocabulary is simple but the ideas are complex. |
| "One writing style fits all tasks" | 5.4 | Give one topic and ask pupils to write it in three registers. The difference makes the point. |

## Practical work

- **Group work**: the register-shifting activity (Topic 5.4) works well in groups of three.
- **Group assignment**: as a group, take one topic and produce three versions (academic, public,
   personal). Present all three and explain the differences.
- **Individual assignment**: each pupil rewrites a given academic paragraph in public style and vice
   versa, with a note explaining what they changed.
- **Presentations**: pairs present their purpose identification for five different writing tasks.
