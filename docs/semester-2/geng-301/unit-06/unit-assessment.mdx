## Unit summary

This unit taught you the ethical foundations of academic writing.

- Topic 6.1 covered finding and evaluating sources using the CRAAP test.
- Topic 6.2 covered citation basics: when to cite, what to cite and APA vs MLA.
- Topic 6.3 covered integrating sources: quoting, paraphrasing and summarizing.
- Topic 6.4 covered avoiding plagiarism: types and best practices.

Together these give you a complete ethical framework: find good sources, cite them correctly, integrate
them properly and avoid plagiarism in all its forms.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* What does CRAAP stand for?
   - A) Citation, Reference, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose
   - B) Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose
   - C) Clarity, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose
   - D) Currency, Register, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose

2. *(Remember)* Which citation style uses (Author, Year, p. page) for in-text citations?
   - A) MLA
   - B) APA
   - C) Both
   - D) Neither

3. *(Understand)* A source is credible but you only paraphrase one sentence from it. Do you need to
   cite it?
   - A) No, because you used your own words
   - B) Yes, because the idea came from the source
   - C) No, because it is only one sentence
   - D) Yes, but only in the reference list

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following is an example of mosaic plagiarism?
   - A) Copying a paragraph word-for-word without citation
   - B) Reusing your own previously submitted essay
   - C) Piecing together phrases from sources with minor word changes
   - D) Forgetting to include a reference list

5. *(Apply)* You are writing an essay and find a perfect sentence that captures your point. The best
   approach is to:
   - A) Copy it exactly without citation
   - B) Use it as a direct quote with quotation marks and a citation
   - C) Change two words and use it without citation
   - D) Use it only in the reference list

6. *(Apply)* A source has no named author, no date and no references. Which CRAAP criterion is most
   clearly violated?
   - A) Currency
   - B) Authority
   - C) Relevance
   - D) Purpose

7. *(Apply)* Which integration technique should you use MOST often in academic writing?
   - A) Quoting
   - B) Paraphrasing
   - C) Summarizing
   - D) Copying

8. *(Apply)* A pupil submits the same essay to two different courses. This is:
   - A) Direct plagiarism
   - B) Mosaic plagiarism
   - C) Self-plagiarism
   - D) Not plagiarism

9. *(Apply)* Which statement about citation is correct?
   - A) A reference list alone is enough
   - B) Common knowledge must always be cited
   - C) Every in-text citation must appear in the reference list
   - D) You only cite direct quotes

10. *(Apply)* A pupil changes every fifth word in a passage and adds a citation. This is:
    - A) Proper paraphrasing
    - B) Direct plagiarism
    - C) Mosaic plagiarism
    - D) Not plagiarism because there is a citation

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between direct plagiarism and mosaic
   plagiarism.

2. *(Understand)* A fictional report dated 2012 describes computer access in one school.
   You need evidence about current mobile-phone learning in Sindh. Explain which two CRAAP criteria
   most directly expose this report's limits and how each affects its use.

3. *(Apply)* A pupil writes: "According to research, homework is important" with no citation.
   Use this fictional exercise source to explain the problem and rewrite the sentence:
   Channa (2023), page 20: "Short homework tasks let pupils practise the day's lesson."
   This original practice text is not a verified research finding.

4. *(Apply)* Format this fictional exercise source in APA style: A book by M. Channa titled *Teaching and Learning in
   Pakistan*, published in 2023 by Oxford University Press.

5. *(Analyze)* Compare this fictional exercise source and a pupil's version. Source, Channa
   (2023), page 78: "Feedback helps most when it is prompt and tells pupils exactly what to improve."
   Pupil: "Channa (2023) argues that useful feedback identifies a specific next step without delay."
   Is the pupil quoting, paraphrasing or summarizing this supplied sentence? Explain using both texts;
   acceptability depends on preserving meaning as well as changing expression.

6. *(Understand)* Explain why self-plagiarism is a problem even though the words are your own.

7. *(Analyze)* Two fictional sources claim mobile phones improve learning. Source A reports
   interviews with 20 pupils at one school, includes its questions and notes that it did not measure
   test scores. Source B sells phones and says "all pupils double their marks", without data or
   references. Compare accuracy and purpose, then state a qualified judgement about which source
   could support which claim. Identify one further check.

8. *(Analyze)* A pupil cites a source but follows the original sentence structure exactly, changing
   only synonyms. Identify the problem and explain what the pupil should do.

9. *(Apply)* Paraphrase this original fictional exercise sentence and add an APA citation:
   "Paired games give pupils practice in taking turns." Assign the practice source to S. Hussain,
   2022, page 45. Do not present it as a verified finding.

10. *(Analyze)* A pupil's essay has six reference-list sources and two in-text citation groups.
    Can these counts alone prove four sources are uncited? Explain two possible situations and the
    check you would perform before judging the essay.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (80-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* Compare two fictional cases against the source "Paired games let pupils practise
   taking turns." Case A copies that sentence without quotation marks or citation. Case B writes
   "Paired activities let pupils practise taking turns" and adds a citation. Identify each problem,
   compare the evidence that makes it detectable and explain why each requires correction. Make no
   claim about how common either type is.

2. *(Analyze)* A B.Ed pupil finds a paragraph from a journal article. Draft A copies a sentence
   with quotation marks and a citation but gives no introduction or explanation. Draft B changes
   only synonyms and adds a citation. Compare what each draft handles correctly and poorly, then
   justify a revision plan for integrating the source into the pupil's argument. Include the choice
   between quotation and paraphrase, connection to the argument and attribution.

3. *(Evaluate)* Use these fictional source cards for an essay on "teacher training in Pakistan":
   (a) Noor, education researcher, 2024 journal article, "Mentoring novice teachers". Extract:
   "Interviews with 12 teachers in one Lahore school suggest mentors helped lesson planning. We did
   not observe lessons or measure pupil results." It supplies interview questions and describes its
   analysis; its purpose is to report exploratory research.
   (b) Admin, 2015 blog, "Training guarantees success". Extract: "Every trained teacher doubles pupil
   scores. Enrol in our paid course today." No data, references or author qualifications are supplied.
   Apply at least three CRAAP criteria, including accuracy and relevance. Give qualified ratings,
   distinguish what each can support and identify further evidence needed. All wording is original
   exercise data, not extracts from real publications.

4. *(Evaluate)* A pupil says, "I cited all my sources so I cannot be accused of plagiarism." Evaluate
   this claim using what you learned in Topics 6.3 and 6.4. Explain why citation alone is not always
   enough.

5. *(Create)* You are teaching a Grade 9 class about academic honesty. Write a short explanation
   (50-70 words) of what plagiarism is and why it matters, using language a Grade 9 pupil would
   understand. Then describe one classroom activity that would help pupils practise proper paraphrasing.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose.
2. **B** - APA uses (Author, Year, p. page). MLA uses (Author page).
3. **B** - Yes. Paraphrases require citation because the idea came from the source.
4. **C** - Mosaic plagiarism pieces together phrases with minor changes. A is direct, B is self, D is accidental.
5. **B** - A perfect sentence should be used as a direct quote with quotation marks and citation.
6. **B** - Authority. No named author means the authority cannot be verified.
7. **B** - Paraphrasing is the most common and preferred technique in academic writing.
8. **C** - Self-plagiarism: reusing your own prior work without permission.
9. **C** - Every in-text citation must appear in the reference list and vice versa.
10. **C** - Mosaic plagiarism. Changing occasional words while keeping the structure is not proper paraphrasing.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Direct plagiarism copies text word-for-word without citation. Mosaic plagiarism pieces together phrases from sources with minor word changes, often without proper paraphrasing. **Marks (2):** 1 per correct distinction.

2. **Model:** Currency: the 2012 report may not describe current technology. Relevance: computer access in one school does not directly answer a question about mobile-phone learning across Sindh. **Marks (2):** 1 per criterion linked to a specific limitation; 0.5 for naming a relevant criterion without explaining the link. Accept other criteria only with a defensible evidence-based explanation.

3. **Model:** The source is unspecified. Using only the fictional exercise data: "Channa (2023) describes short homework as practice in the day's learning." **Marks (2):** 1 for identifying the missing source; 1 for a cited rewrite faithful to the supplied practice text. Do not reward an invented finding.

4. **Model:** Channa, M. (2023). *Teaching and learning in Pakistan*. Oxford University Press. **Marks (2):** 1 - correct APA format; 1 - all elements present.

5. **Model:** Paraphrase: the pupil restates both promptness and specific guidance from the supplied sentence in different wording and structure. **Marks (2):** 1 for identifying a paraphrase; 1 for comparison of wording and retained meaning. Accept "brief summary" if the learner explains the compression using both supplied texts. It is not a direct quotation.

6. **Model:** Self-plagiarism is a problem because each assignment should be original work for that course. Reusing old work without permission is dishonest about the effort put in. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the issue; 1 - names the ethical problem.

7. **Model:** A has inspectable interview methods and acknowledges limits; it can support a claim about reported experience in that school, not improved scores. B's sales purpose and unsupported universal claim weaken its use as learning evidence. Check independent studies or A's full methods. **Marks (2):** 1 for comparing evidence and purpose; 1 for a qualified judgement plus a relevant further check. Award 0.5 for either part of that second mark.

8. **Model:** The problem is mosaic plagiarism - following the original structure with only synonym changes. The pupil should change both wording and sentence structure. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies mosaic plagiarism; 1 - explains the fix.

9. **Model:** Taking turns can be practised through games in pairs (Hussain, 2022, p. 45). **Marks (2):** 1 for a faithful paraphrase with changed wording and structure; 1 for the citation. The page locator is optional for an APA paraphrase.

10. **Model:** No. Two groups may together name all six sources, or may name only two sources and leave four unmatched. Match each reference-list entry to author and year in the full text before deciding. **Marks (2):** 1 for distinguishing both possibilities; 1 for explaining the matching check and why traceability matters.
