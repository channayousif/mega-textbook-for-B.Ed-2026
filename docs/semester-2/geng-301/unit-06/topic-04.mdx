## A real classroom situation

A B.Ed student submits an essay. The tutor runs it through a plagiarism checker and finds a paragraph
copied word-for-word from a website, with no quotation marks and no citation. The student says, "I did
not mean to cheat - I read so many sources I forgot which words were mine and which were theirs."

Intent does not matter - plagiarism is plagiarism. This topic teaches you the different types and how
to avoid all of them.

## Explanation

### Types of plagiarism

<Glossary term="Plagiarism">Plagiarism</Glossary> takes several forms:

1. **Direct plagiarism** - copying text word-for-word without quotation marks or citation. The most
   obvious type - and the easiest for tutors to spot.
2. **Self-plagiarism** - reusing your own previously submitted work in a new assignment without
   permission. Even your own work must be original for each task.
3. **Mosaic plagiarism** (patchwriting) - piecing together phrases from multiple sources with minor
   word changes. This is the most common type among trainees because it feels like "using your own
   words" when it is not.
4. **Accidental plagiarism** - forgetting to cite a source, or paraphrasing too closely without
   realizing. The effect is the same as intentional plagiarism even if the intent is different.

### Best practices for avoiding plagiarism

1. **Take notes carefully** - when researching, note which ideas come from which source. Use quotation
   marks in your notes for direct quotes so you do not later mistake them for your own words.
2. **Paraphrase properly** - change both wording AND sentence structure (Topic 6.3).
3. **Cite as you write** - add citations while drafting, not as an afterthought. It is easier to cite
   during writing than to find sources later.
4. **When in doubt, cite** - over-citing is a minor issue. Under-citing is plagiarism.
5. **Keep a research log** - list every source you consult, even if you do not end up citing it.

<Figure id="fig-U6-8" kind="table" src="/img/figures/geng-301/unit-06/fig-U6-8.svg" alt="Table showing plagiarism prevention strategies." width={780} height={470} />

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think "if I change a few words it is not plagiarism." As Topic 6.3
explained, a paraphrase must change both wording AND structure. Changing only a few words is mosaic
plagiarism, not original writing.

## Activity: Identify the plagiarism type

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Read these scenarios and identify the type of plagiarism (direct, self, mosaic or accidental):

1. A pupil copies a paragraph from a website and pastes it into their essay with no changes and no
   citation.
2. A pupil submits the same essay to two different courses without telling either tutor.
3. A pupil reads three sources and writes a paragraph mixing phrases from all three with a few words
   changed, but cites only one source.
4. A pupil paraphrases a passage but follows the original sentence structure exactly, changing only
   synonyms, with a citation present.

For each, state the type and explain what the pupil should have done differently.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name four types of plagiarism.
2. *(Understand)* Why is mosaic plagiarism the most common among trainees?
3. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "I cited the source so I cannot be accused of plagiarism." Using what you
   learned, explain why a citation alone is not always enough.

## Summary

Plagiarism takes four forms: direct, self, mosaic and accidental. Avoid it by taking careful notes,
paraphrasing properly (changing wording AND structure), citing as you write and keeping a research log.
Intent does not matter - if the words or ideas are not yours and are not cited, it is plagiarism.

Read this scenario and analyse it: "A pupil writes an essay using four sources. They paraphrase each
source but follow the original sentence structure closely, changing only a few words. They include
in-text citations for all four sources." Identify: (1) what type of plagiarism is most likely present,
(2) why the citations alone do not fix the problem, and (3) what the pupil should do to paraphrase
properly.
