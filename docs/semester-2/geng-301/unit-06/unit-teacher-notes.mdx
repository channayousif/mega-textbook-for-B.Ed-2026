# Unit 6 Teacher Notes

These notes support the teaching of GENG-301 Unit 6.

## Teaching strategies

This unit teaches ethics in writing. The most effective approach is to make the abstract concrete.

1. **Use real scenarios.** Give pupils scenarios (like those in the "Common misconception" sections) and
   ask: "Is this plagiarism? What type? Why?" Discussion is more effective than lecturing.
2. **Use think-pair-share** for paraphrasing. Pupils paraphrase individually, then compare in pairs, then
   share with the class. The comparison step is where learning happens.
3. **Use question-answer** for citation. Give sources and ask: "What needs to be in this reference?
   Why is this element here?" Pupils learn the logic behind the format.
4. **Use group work** for source evaluation. Give groups three sources to evaluate using the CRAAP test.
   Each group presents findings to the class.

## Sequencing

Teach the four topics in order. A reasonable split for a four-week unit:

- Week 1: Topic 6.1 (finding and evaluating sources).
- Week 2: Topic 6.2 (citing sources).
- Week 3: Topic 6.3 (integrating sources).
- Week 4: Topic 6.4 (avoiding plagiarism) and the unit assessment.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

| Misconception | Topic | How to handle |
|---|---|---|
| "If I change a few words it is not plagiarism" | 6.4 | Show a passage and a poor paraphrase side by side. Ask pupils to identify what is wrong. |
| "A reference list is enough" | 6.2 | Give an essay with a reference list but no in-text citations. Ask: "Which idea came from which source?" |
| "Anything on Google is credible" | 6.1 | Give pupils three sources (credible blog, Wikipedia, peer-reviewed article) and ask them to rank credibility. |

## Practical work

- **Group work**: the CRAAP evaluation activity (Topic 6.1) works well in small groups.
- **Group assignment**: as a group, evaluate five sources on a given topic using the CRAAP test and
   present findings with ratings and reasons.
- **Individual assignment**: each pupil paraphrases and cites three different sources on a self-chosen
   topic, with a note explaining what they changed to avoid plagiarism.
- **Presentations**: pairs present their paraphrasing before and after, explaining what they changed and why.
