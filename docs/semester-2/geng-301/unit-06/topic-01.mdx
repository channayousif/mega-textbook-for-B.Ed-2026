## A real classroom situation

A B.Ed student writes an essay citing a blog post titled "Education Tips for Parents" with no named
author, no date and no references. Her tutor writes: "Who wrote this? When? What are their
qualifications? If you cannot answer these questions, the source is not credible enough to cite."

<Figure id="fig-U6-1" kind="concept-map" src="/img/figures/geng-301/unit-06/fig-U6-1.svg" alt="A concept map showing the CRAAP test with five branches: currency, relevance, authority, accuracy and purpose, each with a question to ask." width={780} height={470} />

The student used a source without checking its credibility. This topic teaches you how to evaluate
before you cite.

## Explanation

### Finding credible sources

In Pakistani B.Ed research, credible sources include:

- **Academic books and textbooks** from recognized publishers.
- **Journal articles** from peer-reviewed education journals (e.g. Journal of Research and Reflections
  in Education, Pakistan).
- **Government documents** (Sindh education policy, national curriculum documents, Bureau of
  Statistics reports).
- **Reports from recognized organizations** (UNICEF, UNESCO, Cambridge Education).
- **University lecture materials** and course packs from your own programme.

Less credible sources include personal blogs, social media posts, Wikipedia (useful for background but
not citable) and news articles with no named author.

### The CRAAP test

The <Glossary term="CRAAP test">CRAAP test</Glossary> is a checklist for evaluating any source:

1. **Currency**: When was it published? Is the information still current? A 2010 report on classroom
   technology may be outdated.
2. **Relevance**: Does it relate to your topic? Is it appropriate for an academic audience?
3. **Authority**: Who is the author? What are their qualifications? Who published it?
4. **Accuracy**: Is the information supported by evidence? Can you verify it from other sources?
5. **Purpose**: Why does the source exist? To inform, persuade, sell or entertain? A company selling
  textbooks has a different purpose than a research institute.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think any source found through Google is equally credible. It
is not. A search engine returns results ranked by popularity, not by academic quality. You must evaluate
each source yourself using criteria like the CRAAP test.

## Activity: Evaluate a source

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

You are writing an essay on "The effects of mobile phone use on pupil learning." Evaluate these three
sources using the CRAAP test. Rate each as "credible," "partially credible" or "not credible."

1. A 2024 article in the Journal of Education and Educational Development (peer-reviewed), authored by
   Dr. Aisha Siddiqui, Assistant Professor of Education, University of Karachi.
2. A 2015 blog post on "EducationTips.pk" by "Admin" with no date or references.
3. A 2023 UNICEF Pakistan report on digital learning, authored by the UNICEF education team.

For each, state your rating and give one reason from the CRAAP test. Compare with another pair.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name the five CRAAP test criteria.
2. *(Understand)* Why is currency important when choosing a source?
3. *(Apply)* A pupil cites a 2012 report on ICT in schools. The essay is about current classroom
   technology. Using the CRAAP test, explain the potential problem.

## Summary

Credible sources include academic books, peer-reviewed journals, government documents and recognized
organization reports. Evaluate every source using the CRAAP test: currency, relevance, authority,
accuracy and purpose. A search engine result is not automatically credible.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can distinguish credible from non-credible sources.
- [ ] I can apply the CRAAP test to evaluate a source.
- [ ] I understand why citing non-credible sources weakens my writing.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask your cooperating teacher which sources they use when preparing lectures or reports. Ask whether
they check the credibility of online sources before using them. Note two sources they consider
reliable and why.

## Summative task

Find two sources on the topic "The importance of play in early learning" - one that is credible and
one that is not. For each, write three sentences applying the CRAAP test and explaining your
credibility rating.

**Mini-rubric**

| Criterion | Levels |
|---|---|
| *Credible source* | 0-2: correctly identifies a credible source with reasoning / identifies but reasoning weak / wrong. |
| *Non-credible source* | 0-2: correctly identifies a non-credible source with reasoning / identifies but reasoning weak / wrong. |
| *CRAAP application* | 0-2: applies at least three CRAAP criteria accurately / applies one or two / does not apply. |
