# Policies and Legal Framework in Pakistan

Unit 4 ended with a written request to a head teacher: a named pupil, a named barrier, and a
question about who pays. This unit is about what stands behind that request.

Up to now the argument for inclusion in this course has been a moral and a professional one. It is
also a legal one. A child with a disability in a Sindh government school is not asking for a
favour. There is an Act of the Provincial Assembly that tells her school to admit her without
discrimination, to make its building and campus accessible, and to provide reasonable
accommodation according to her individual requirements. Most teachers in Sindh have never read it.

That sentence is the reason this unit exists, and it is also a warning about how to read it. The
law being on a child's side and the child being in school are two different facts, and in Pakistan
they come apart often. This unit holds both: it teaches what the documents actually require, and
then it asks, without cynicism and without pretending, what a teacher does on the Monday after she
finds out.

## A note on which law

Your course guide names the "Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act (2018)". Pakistan has more
than one disability statute, and which one applies depends on where you teach.

This unit teaches the **Sindh Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2018**, because that
is the law in force for the schools this book is written for. Education and the welfare of persons
with disabilities are provincial subjects, and the Sindh Act says so in its own preamble. The
federal **ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020** extends to the Islamabad Capital
Territory only. You will meet it in reading and in conversation, so Topic 5.2 puts it beside the
Sindh Act for one passage and one figure, and then returns to the law that governs your classroom.
Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan each have their own provincial arrangements; if you
take a post outside Sindh, the method of this unit transfers even though the section numbers do
not.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. State what the National Education Policy 2009 commits Pakistan to on inclusion and on learners
   with disabilities, and say precisely where the policy is silent.
2. Explain the purpose of the Sindh Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2018, and
   distinguish it from the federal ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020 by extent and
   by the strength of their education duties.
3. Explain **reasonable accommodation** as the Act defines it, and show why refusing it is itself
   discrimination rather than a budget decision.
4. Apply the Act's education duties to a real admission and accommodation case, and produce the
   checklist a head teacher could audit a school against.
5. Evaluate the part a teacher's own attitude plays in whether a legal right becomes a school
   place, using evidence about what Pakistani teachers themselves report.
6. Analyze a specific policy-to-practice gap, separate what causes it at each level, and identify
   the part of it that is within a classroom teacher's power.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 and 4 are assumed. From Unit 1 you need the rights basis of inclusion and UNCRPD
Article 24, because the Sindh Act exists in order to give provincial effect to that Convention and
recites it in its preamble. From Unit 4 you need accessibility as four layers, because this unit
turns the accessibility audit you learned to run into a duty somebody owes. Unit 2's distinction
between impairment and disability is also in use throughout: the Act's own definition of a person
with disabilities is built on it.

## In this unit

1. [Pakistan's inclusive-education policy](./topic-01) - what the National Education Policy 2009
   says about inclusion and learners with disabilities, what it leaves out, and why the difference
   between a policy statement and a legal duty matters in a school office.
2. [The Sindh Act 2018 and school responsibilities](./topic-02) - the purpose and education
   provisions of the Act, the federal comparison, reasonable accommodation taught carefully, and
   the duties a school can audit itself against.
3. [The teacher's attitude and the policy-to-practice gap](./topic-03) - what attitude actually
   does to inclusion, what Pakistani teachers report, and how to analyze a gap instead of
   complaining about it.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. The temptation in a legal unit is to memorise and move on;
resist it. Nothing in this unit is assessed by reciting a section number, and the unit assessment
deliberately contains none.

Two things to do rather than read. First, when Topic 5.2 quotes the Act, read the quoted words
twice and in the order given, because the argument of the whole unit turns on one clause
("including denial of reasonable accommodation") sitting inside the Act's definition of
discrimination. Second, do the Topic 5.3 activity honestly. It asks you to write down your own
reservation about inclusion, in your own words, and then test it. A trainee who writes "I have no
reservations" has not done the activity.

One case runs through the unit: Bilal, a seven-year-old deaf boy in a village in Tando Muhammad
Khan whose father is refused admission for him at the nearest government primary school. You will
meet him first as a policy question, then as a legal one, then as the same case in an Islamabad
school, and finally as a question about one teacher's willingness. Nothing about his case is
resolved by knowing the law. That is the unit's point, and it is not a counsel of despair: by the
end you will have the four things that actually move a case like his, and three of them cost
nothing.

The "Check your understanding" items ask you to produce an answer rather than recognise one. The
unit ends with a 10/10/5 assessment drawing the three topics together.
