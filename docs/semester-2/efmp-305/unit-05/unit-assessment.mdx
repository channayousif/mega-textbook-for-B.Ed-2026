## Unit summary

Unit 4 ended with a written request to a head teacher. This unit was about what stands behind it,
and about the one thing standing behind it that no document can supply.

**Topic 5.1** separated a **policy** from an **Act**. A policy states what a government intends; it
creates no right a named child holds against a named school. The **National Education Policy 2009**
commits Pakistan, in two sentences, to promoting inclusive and child-friendly education and to
special measures for the inclusion of "special persons" in mainstream education, with equity and
social exclusion addressed in a third action. Its own annex concedes that discrimination exists in
the education system and blames "poor implementation and social customs". Its silences are the
lesson: in seventy-one pages there is no definition of inclusive education, no chapter on it, no
duty on any school, no disability disaggregation in its equity data, no accountability mechanism
and no mention of accommodation. A commitment with no definition, no duty, no data and no mechanism
does not survive a head teacher with fifty-five children and no training. Read any policy with four
questions: who is addressed, what verb, what mechanism, what is absent. The sequence matters too:
the 1981 Ordinance framed disability as employment and welfare, the UNCRPD was adopted in 2006 and
ratified by Pakistan on 25 October 2011, and the 18th Amendment in 2010 made education and the
welfare of persons with disabilities provincial subjects, which is why the instrument that binds a
Sindh school is provincial. The policy still legitimises the conversation, gives the department its
own vocabulary, and puts a fifteen-year-old promise on the record.

**Topic 5.2** read the **Sindh Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2018**, an Act passed
to give effect to the UNCRPD, extending to the whole Province of Sindh. Four of its definitions
carry the unit. A person with disabilities is one whose long-term impairment, "in interaction with
barriers", hinders full participation: the social model in provincial law. Inclusive education means
learning together **and** a system of teaching and learning "suitably adapted". Discrimination means
any distinction, exclusion or restriction with the "purpose or effect" of nullifying equal
enjoyment of rights, and it "includes all forms of discrimination, including denial of reasonable
accommodation". **Reasonable accommodation** means "necessary and appropriate modification where
needed in a particular case" so that a person enjoys rights "on an equal basis with others" - which
is a test against the pupil's need, not against the school's budget. The Act could not name denial
of accommodation as discrimination if poverty were a complete defence, and in practice seven of the
eight things a deaf child needs in a crowded Grade 1 cost nothing and the eighth costs a sheet of
chart paper. Accommodation is nonetheless not a lowering of the standard, not unlimited, individual
rather than categorical, and not a substitute for teaching.

The Act's education section places ten duties on institutions funded or recognised by Government:
admit without discrimination and give equal access to sports and recreation; make building, campus
and facilities accessible; provide reasonable accommodation according to the individual's
requirements; provide individualised support; educate blind and deaf pupils in the most appropriate
languages, modes and means of communication; detect specific learning disabilities early; monitor
participation, attainment and completion for every such pupil; provide transport; teach life and
social skills; and give access to all modes of education. The Government, in turn, is obliged to
train and employ teachers qualified in sign language and Braille, to supply books, materials and
assistive devices free to age eighteen, and to make examination modifications including extra time,
a scribe, exemption from second and third language courses and age relaxation. The head teacher's
three objections all have answers: the duty is on Government but runs through the institutions it
funds, and a separate provision flatly forbids any institution from discriminating; cost is not a
complete answer and the accommodations asked for are free; and the one duty a village school cannot
meet this term does not excuse the nine it can, but becomes a written request citing the
Government's own obligation. The federal **ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020**
extends to the Islamabad Capital Territory only; its admission bar and its personal liability for
staff are worded more strongly, and its definition of reasonable accommodation is the same as
Sindh's. Cite the Sindh Act in Sindh. Five responsibilities make the duties auditable: admission,
accessibility, accommodation case by case, individualised support with appropriate modes and early
detection, and monitoring.

**Topic 5.3** turned to the factor no Act can legislate. A teacher's **attitude** is the pattern of
her small decisions - who is called on, who is seated where, who is entered, what is expected - not
the sentence she would write on a survey, and being sincerely in favour of inclusion without
practising it is more common than opposition. Pakistani studies report generally favourable
attitudes with specific reluctance about pupils with visual impairment and pupils needing
individualised functional reading and mathematics, strongest concerns about shortage of time, other
parents' reactions and infrastructure, and four inseparable dimensions of attitude: professional
knowledge and preparedness, beliefs, resources and support, and collaboration. The
**attitude-action cycle** explains the leverage: a belief produces a decision, the decision produces
an experience, the experience produces behaviour, and the behaviour becomes the evidence that
confirms the belief, with no ill will required anywhere in it. A declared duty or procedure
interrupts the cycle at the decision; contact with a pupil succeeding interrupts it at the evidence.
A **policy-to-practice gap** has four levels - document, system, school, classroom - and the
professional move is to name the level and then name the action at that level, instead of blaming
whichever level is furthest away. Three of Miss Farzana's gaps were level 4 and free; two were not
hers at all.

Four things move a case, and three are free: an accurate citation of the right Act, a written
request naming the pupil and the clause, an audit that produces evidence rather than opinion, and
one teacher's decision to change one thing this week.

Unit 6 turns to the people this unit kept mentioning and did not treat properly: parents, families
and the specialists a school can actually collaborate with.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose one answer. Each question carries 1 mark.

1. *(Remember)* Which edition of Pakistan's National Education Policy does this unit analyse?
   - A) The National Education Policy 1998-2010
   - B) The National Education Policy 2009, revised 1 August 2009
   - C) The National Education Policy Framework 2018
   - D) The Single National Curriculum 2020

2. *(Understand)* Which statement best captures the difference between a policy and an Act?
   - A) A policy is written by the federal government and an Act by a province
   - B) A policy states what a government intends; an Act creates duties and rights that can be
     enforced
   - C) A policy is temporary and an Act is permanent
   - D) A policy applies to schools and an Act applies only to government departments

3. *(Understand)* A trainee lists what the 2009 policy does **not** contain on inclusion. Which of
   these belongs on that list?
   - A) Any reference to Education for All
   - B) Any mention of equity or social exclusion
   - C) A definition of inclusive education and any duty on a school
   - D) Any reference to marginalised groups or to girls

4. *(Remember)* The Sindh Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2018 describes its own
   purpose as which of the following?
   - A) To establish special schools in every district of Sindh
   - B) To give effect to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities
   - C) To provide employment quotas for persons with disabilities
   - D) To replace the federal disability legislation across Pakistan

5. *(Understand)* Under the Sindh Act, reasonable accommodation is best described as:
   - A) Whatever adjustment a school can afford in its current budget
   - B) A necessary and appropriate modification, where needed, in a particular case, so that the
     person enjoys rights on an equal basis with others
   - C) A reduction in what a pupil with a disability is expected to learn
   - D) A specialist service provided by the district on written application

6. *(Understand)* Why does it matter that the Act's definition of discrimination says "purpose or
   effect"?
   - A) Because it means only deliberate exclusion counts
   - B) Because a rule applied to everyone identically can still exclude, and the exclusion is
     enough
   - C) Because it limits the Act to private institutions
   - D) Because it requires a complaint to be filed before any duty arises

7. *(Apply)* A head teacher in Hyderabad refuses admission to a pupil with cerebral palsy, citing
   the federal ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020 as the law he follows. What is the
   correct response?
   - A) Accept it; the federal Act is the senior instrument
   - B) Point out that the federal Act extends to the Islamabad Capital Territory only, and that the
     Sindh Act of 2018 governs his school
   - C) Point out that the federal Act forbids the refusal, so he has misread his own authority
   - D) Both B and C are accurate statements of the position

8. *(Understand)* Which statement best describes how this unit defines a teacher's attitude?
   - A) The opinion she would record on an attitude survey
   - B) Her level of training in special education
   - C) The pattern of her small daily decisions about a pupil
   - D) Her willingness to accept a pupil with a disability on her register

9. *(Understand)* In the attitude-action cycle, what makes low expectation harder to correct than
   open objection?
   - A) Low expectation is more common among trained teachers
   - B) Objection is forbidden by law, so it is rarely expressed
   - C) Low expectation produces the behaviour that then appears to be the evidence for it
   - D) Low expectation is unconscious, so the teacher cannot be held responsible

10. *(Apply)* A school counts enrolment of pupils with disabilities but has no record of their
    attendance, attainment or completion. On this unit's four levels, where does that gap mainly
    sit, and what is the teacher-level action?
    - A) System level; wait for the departmental monitoring circular
    - B) Document level; the policy should have required it
    - C) School level; keep two lines a term on a named pupil's participation and attainment and
      bring them to the staff meeting
    - D) Classroom level; raise expectations of the pupil concerned

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question concisely, using the unit's terminology. Marks are shown.

1. *(Understand)* State what the National Education Policy 2009 commits the government to on the
   inclusion of learners with disabilities, and explain why that commitment cannot be enforced by a
   parent. (4 marks)

2. *(Understand)* The 2009 policy uses the term "special persons". Explain what the phrase tells
   you about the document, and why its dated vocabulary is not a reason to dismiss the commitment it
   carries. (4 marks)

3. *(Analyze)* Apply the four reading questions - who is addressed, what verb, what mechanism, what
   is absent - to the 2009 policy's commitment that inclusive and child-friendly education shall be
   promoted. (5 marks)

4. *(Understand)* Give the Sindh Act's statutory definition of inclusive education, and explain
   which part of it a school that seats a deaf pupil at the back of an unchanged class has failed to
   meet. (4 marks)

5. *(Understand)* Explain, in four steps, why refusing a reasonable accommodation on the ground of
   cost alone is not a complete answer under the Sindh Act. (5 marks)

6. *(Apply)* A pupil with a moderate hearing loss joins Grade 4 in a government school with no
   assistive devices and no trained support. List six accommodations the school could provide within
   a week, state the cost of each, and identify which of them is the one thing that genuinely
   requires money. (5 marks)

7. *(Apply)* Write the three-line accommodation note a head teacher should place on file for a
   pupil with low vision newly admitted to Grade 2. Use the form taught in this unit: pupil, need,
   what the school has changed, review date. (5 marks)

8. *(Understand)* Explain what Pakistani studies of teacher attitudes report, and state two limits
   on what those studies can support. (4 marks)

9. *(Analyze)* A teacher never asks a pupil who reads below grade level to read aloud, because the
   pupil was laughed at two years ago. Analyze this decision using the attitude-action cycle, and
   state the intervention that would break the cycle without humiliating the pupil. (5 marks)

10. *(Analyze)* "There are good policies in Pakistan but no implementation." Explain why this is not
    an analysis, and rewrite it as one for a named gap, assigning levels and owners. (5 marks)

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer in continuous prose. Each question carries 10 marks and has a band rubric below.

1. *(Analyze)* Bilal's father was refused admission for his deaf son by a head teacher who was not
   unkind, in a province where an Act requiring admission without discrimination had been in force
   for three years. Analyze that refusal. Identify what the head teacher got right, what he got
   wrong, which of his objections the 2009 policy could not answer and which the Sindh Act can, and
   what the four of them together tell you about why a legal right did not become a school place.

2. *(Evaluate)* "Reasonable accommodation is a European idea. In a Pakistani government school with
   sixty children and no budget, it cannot mean anything." Evaluate this claim. Take the objection
   seriously, use the Act's own definition and its named examination accommodations, distinguish
   what is free from what must be escalated, and reach a judgement you defend.

3. *(Create)* Design a **section-by-section compliance audit** that a head teacher could run on her
   own school in one morning, covering the Sindh Act's education duties. Give your five
   responsibility headings, the questions under each, the evidence that would answer each question,
   and the form of the one-page summary. Then apply it to a real school you know and report your
   three most significant findings, separating the free from the escalated.

4. *(Evaluate)* Miss Farzana has a favourable attitude to inclusion, two days of training, and
   seventy-one girls in a class. In her room, a pupil with hearing loss sits in the fourth row, a
   pupil who reads below grade is never asked to read aloud, and a pupil with a physical disability
   was left off a debate entry form. Evaluate how much of what is wrong in her classroom is properly
   attributed to her attitude, and how much to conditions she does not control. Reach a judgement
   about what she can fairly be held to.

5. *(Analyze)* Compare the Sindh Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2018 with the federal
   ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020 as instruments a teacher might rely on. Analyze
   their difference in territorial reach, in the strength of their education duties and in the
   mechanisms behind them; identify what is identical in both; and explain what a Sindh trainee
   loses by citing the wrong one.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B  2. B  3. C  4. B  5. B  6. B  7. D  8. C  9. C  10. C

A note on item 7. Both B and C are true statements about the position, which is why D is the
intended response: the federal Act does not reach Hyderabad **and** it would not have supported him
if it did. A response of B alone shows the more important half and should be given the mark in an
oral or written examination where the reasoning is visible.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. The 2009 policy commits the government, at system level, to promoting inclusive and
   child-friendly education in pursuit of Education for All, and to adopting special measures to
   ensure the inclusion of "special persons" in mainstream education as well as in literacy and
   technical and vocational programmes. A parent cannot enforce it because a policy is a statement
   of intent rather than law: the commitment is addressed to governments, names no duty owed to a
   particular child, and carries no mechanism, no timetable and no complaint route. (2 marks for
   the commitments; 2 marks for the reason, which must distinguish intent from duty rather than
   merely saying a policy is "not legal".)

2. "Special persons" was the respectful term in Pakistani official usage in 2009; the Sindh Act of
   2018 says "persons with disabilities", so the vocabulary moved in nine years. It tells you the
   document's period and the understanding of disability current when it was drafted. It is not a
   reason to dismiss the commitment, because the substance of the commitment is mainstreaming,
   which is what this course argues for, and because terminology is a practice that keeps moving:
   the phrasing a trainee uses today will itself be corrected later. (2 marks for the period point;
   2 marks for why the dated term does not discredit the substance, one of which must recognise
   that current usage is also provisional.)

3. Addressee: governments, specifically the federal government's statement of what provincial and
   area governments are to pursue. No institution and no pupil is named. Verb: "shall be promoted",
   passive, with no actor named for the promoting, which is weaker than "shall provide" and much
   weaker than "shall not be denied". Mechanism: none. There is no target, no date, no budget line,
   no reporting duty, no named officer and no penalty. Absent: a definition of inclusive education,
   any disability data in the policy's equity annex, and any complaint route for a parent.
   (1 mark each for addressee, verb and mechanism; 2 marks for absence, which must name at least
   two specific absences.)

4. The Act defines inclusive education as a system of education in which students with and without
   disabilities learn together **and** the system of teaching and learning is suitably adapted to
   meet the learning needs of different types of students with disabilities. A school that seats a
   deaf pupil at the back of an unchanged class has met the first half and failed the second: there
   is no adaptation of teaching or learning, so the pupil is co-located rather than included.
   A strong answer notes that this is the clause that makes Units 3 and 4 of this course legally
   relevant rather than merely good practice. (2 marks for the definition including the adaptation
   clause; 2 marks for locating the failure in the second half.)

5. Step one: the word "reasonable" in the Act's definition qualifies the modification against the
   need, since the definition asks whether a modification is necessary and appropriate in a
   particular case so the pupil enjoys rights on an equal basis with others. Cost is not the subject
   of the sentence. Step two: the Act's definition of discrimination expressly includes denial of
   reasonable accommodation, which would be incoherent if poverty were a complete defence, because
   a school could then discriminate lawfully by being poor. Step three: in practice most
   accommodations are not purchases at all but changes to how teaching is done - a seat, a written
   instruction, a faced class, a briefed partner, an adapted paper - so the request is usually for a
   decision rather than for money. Step four: where an accommodation genuinely does require money or
   a sanctioned post, the honest response is a written escalation naming the pupil and the clause,
   and that escalation does not excuse the free accommodations that were never attempted.
   (1 mark per step; 1 mark for the point that the third list does not excuse the first.)

6. Six available within a week: a front seat on the side of the better ear, free; the teacher facing
   the class when speaking and not talking while writing on the board, free; every instruction that
   matters written as well as spoken, the cost of chalk; a briefed partner for instructions, rotated,
   free; visual support for new vocabulary using objects and hand-drawn charts, about Rs 70 for
   chart paper and a marker; assessment that does not depend on hearing a dictated question, free
   but requiring a decision from whoever sets the paper. The item that genuinely requires money is a
   prescribed hearing aid, which also requires audiological assessment and is therefore an
   escalation rather than a classroom change. (1 mark per accommodation with a cost, to a maximum of
   4; 1 mark for correctly identifying the hearing aid as the escalation and noting that it is
   prescribed.)

7. A full-credit note is three or four lines, names the pupil and class, states the need in terms of
   what she cannot presently do, lists what the school has changed, and sets a review date. For
   example: "Ayesha, Grade 2. Low vision, no spectacles yet; cannot read normal board writing from
   beyond the second row or standard textbook print. Needs: front-centre seat away from window
   glare, board washed and written in a large hand with everything read aloud, A3 enlargement of
   assessment papers, reading window for textbook work. Changed: seat moved 12 August, enlargements
   agreed with head teacher, typoscope made. Review: end of Term 1, and refer for sight testing."
   (1 mark for pupil and class; 1 mark for need stated as a limitation rather than a label; 2 marks
   for changes that are specific and actually made; 1 mark for a review date or a referral.)

8. Pakistani studies report broadly favourable general attitudes among teachers and pre-service
   teachers, with markedly less favourable attitudes toward including pupils with visual impairment
   and pupils needing individualised functional reading and mathematics; the strongest concerns
   reported are shortage of time, the anticipated reaction of parents of children without
   disabilities, and inappropriate infrastructure; and one validation study recovers four roughly
   equal dimensions of attitude, namely professional knowledge and preparedness, beliefs, resources
   and support, and collaboration. Two limits: the samples are small and local - one university
   cohort in Lahore, fifteen interviewed primary teachers, three hundred teachers for the
   instrument - so none of them measures national prevalence; and reported attitude is what a
   teacher says on an instrument, which this unit argues is a weak proxy for the decisions she
   actually makes. (2 marks for the findings; 1 mark each for two distinct limits. An answer that
   cites a number as a national figure loses the limit marks.)

9. Belief: the teacher expects the pupil cannot read aloud without embarrassment, which two years
   ago was probably accurate. Decision: she does not call on her, which costs nothing and feels
   humane. Experience: the pupil spends two years in a room where she is never asked, which is a
   repeated signal rather than a neutral absence. Behaviour: the pupil stops offering and stops
   practising, because the class was the only place she read aloud. Evidence: she now reads worse
   relative to her peers and dreads being asked, which confirms the original belief using evidence
   the decision produced. The intervention that breaks it without humiliation is a prepared,
   low-stakes, scheduled turn: two sentences given to her the day before, once a fortnight, with a
   note of what she manages each time, so the first attempt cannot fail in public and the teacher
   collects the evidence that will move her own expectation. (3 marks for the cycle traced through
   this case; 2 marks for an intervention that is prepared, scheduled and low-stakes. An
   intervention that simply asks the pupil to read aloud without preparation earns one.)

10. It is not an analysis because it names no cause, no level and no action; it is a complaint with
    the grammar of a finding, and it allows everyone to locate the problem at whichever level they
    are not standing on. Rewritten for a named gap: "Pupils with disabilities at this school are
    enrolled but their attendance, attainment and completion are not recorded, although the Sindh
    Act's education duties require participation, attainment and completion to be monitored for
    every such pupil." Levels and owners: level 2, no departmental monitoring format or data
    requirement reaches the school, owned by the district; level 3, the school keeps no record and
    nobody has been asked to, owned by the head teacher; level 4, the class teacher does not record
    participation for the named pupil, owned by her and available this week at no cost. A strong
    answer also names the evidence that would show change in three months. (2 marks for why the
    original is not an analysis; 3 marks for a concrete gap with levels and owners correctly
    assigned.)

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked in four bands. Award the band that best fits the whole response; a response need
not meet every descriptor in a band.

**ERQ 1 - the refusal analysed**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** The refusal is analysed rather than condemned. What the head teacher got right
  is granted specifically: he gave the family time, he correctly read the education duty as
  addressed to Government, and his statement about having no teacher who signs is true. What he got
  wrong is identified precisely: redirecting the child to a distant special school is not
  compliance; cost is not a complete answer to an accommodation request; and refusal on the ground
  of disability has the effect of nullifying equal enjoyment of education, which is discrimination
  on the Act's own definition regardless of his intentions. The answer distinguishes the objections
  the 2009 policy cannot touch - training, class size, an instrument the father can bring - from
  what the Act supplies, and concludes with the real cause, which is that an Act nobody in the room
  had read cannot operate. A Band 4 answer resists both easy endings.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** Most of the above, with the policy and the Act correctly distinguished and at
  least two of the head teacher's objections answered from the Act.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** The head teacher is blamed or excused as a whole. The policy and the Act are
  treated as interchangeable, or the Act is described as ordering him personally to employ a signing
  teacher. Discrimination is treated as requiring intention.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** The story is retold with a statement that inclusion is a right.

**ERQ 2 - evaluating the "accommodation cannot mean anything here" claim**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** The objection's legitimate core is conceded: time, class size and
  infrastructure are real, and they are exactly what Pakistani teachers themselves report as their
  strongest concerns. The answer then dismantles the premise with evidence rather than assertion:
  reasonable accommodation is defined in Sindh law, not imported; the same law names extra time, a
  scribe, exemption from second and third language courses and age relaxation as examination
  accommodations, which is a Pakistani legislature being concrete; denial of accommodation is itself
  discrimination on the Act's own definition; and the accommodations a crowded class actually needs
  are overwhelmingly free. Free, budgeted and escalated items are correctly separated, nothing cheap
  is parked in the escalation list, and the judgement reached is defended without resolving into
  either slogan.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** The objection is addressed, the definition used correctly, several free
  accommodations named, a defended judgement reached. The named examination accommodations may be
  missing.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** The claim is rejected by assertion or accepted uncritically. Accommodation is
  described as a budget matter, or as a lowering of the standard. No separation of free from
  escalated.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** General remarks about resources with no use of the definition.

**ERQ 3 - design the compliance audit**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** Five responsibility headings that between them cover admission, accessibility,
  case-by-case accommodation, individualised support with appropriate modes and early detection, and
  monitoring of participation, attainment and completion. Every question under them is answerable by
  evidence a person could obtain in a morning - a register, a walk, a file note, a direct answer -
  rather than by opinion. The one-page summary form separates findings into free, budgeted and
  escalated. The application to a real school reports three findings that are specific, supported,
  and include at least one uncomfortable one; "cannot establish" appears where that is the truth.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** All five responsibilities covered, most questions evidence-based, a real
  application with three findings, the free and escalated separated.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** An audit of the building only, or a checklist of opinions ("is the school
  welcoming?"). Monitoring omitted, which is the duty schools most often skip. Findings asserted
  without evidence.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** A list of recommendations with no audit behind it.

**ERQ 4 - evaluating Miss Farzana's responsibility**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** The answer distinguishes clearly between what attitude explains and what
  conditions explain, and it does so item by item rather than in general. The seating, the reading
  turns and the debate form are shown to be level 4 decisions available to her at no cost and in
  minutes, and the attitude-action cycle is used to explain how a kindness became an exclusion
  without any ill will. Seventy-one pupils, four benches deep, and two days of training are
  correctly placed outside her control, with the shortage-of-time concern recognised as a true
  description of her week rather than an excuse. The judgement reached is proportionate: she can
  fairly be held to the decisions, not to the conditions, and holding her to the conditions is both
  unjust and ineffective. A Band 4 answer also notices that the school-level absence of a termly
  seating review is a head teacher's gap, not hers.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** The distinction is made and mostly applied item by item, with a proportionate
  judgement.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** She is condemned for the three facts with no account of conditions, or excused
  entirely because the class is large. The cycle is named but not applied.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** General remarks about teacher commitment.

**ERQ 5 - the two Acts compared**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** Territorial reach is stated correctly and is treated as the decisive point for
  a Sindh teacher: the federal Act extends to the Islamabad Capital Territory, the Sindh Act to the
  whole province. The comparison of duty strength is accurate and even-handed, noting that the
  federal Act's flat admission bar and its personal liability for staff are worded more strongly,
  while the Sindh Act itemises ten institutional duties and names examination accommodations, which
  is what makes a school audit possible. What is identical is correctly identified: the definition
  of reasonable accommodation, and the inclusion of its denial within discrimination. The loss from
  citing the wrong Act is argued in practical terms - a head teacher who knows the citation is wrong
  stops listening, and the credibility of the whole request goes with it - and the answer names the
  18th Amendment as the reason the provincial instrument governs.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** Reach, strength and the shared definition handled correctly, with a sensible
  account of the cost of a wrong citation.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** The two Acts are treated as a hierarchy, or the federal Act is described as
  applying in Sindh. The shared definition is missed.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** Two Acts named with no comparison that bears on a teacher's position.
