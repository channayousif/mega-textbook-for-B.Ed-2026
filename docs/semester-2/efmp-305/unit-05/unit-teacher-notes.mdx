# Teacher notes: Unit 5

These notes are for the teacher educator delivering this unit to B.Ed trainees. They are not part
of the learner-facing material.

## What this unit is really teaching

The content is a policy, two Acts and an attitude. The capability is **citing the right thing
accurately and then acting at your own level**.

A trainee who leaves this unit able to recite section numbers has learned the wrong thing, and will
be humiliated the first time a head teacher asks a follow-up question. A trainee who can say, in a
school office, "this is the Sindh Act of 2018, it applies to this school because the school is
government funded, it requires admission without discrimination and reasonable accommodation
according to the child's individual requirements, and here are the six things I am asking for, five
of which cost nothing" has learned the unit.

Weight your teaching accordingly. There are no marks anywhere in this unit for reciting a section
number, and the unit assessment deliberately contains no item that requires one. Say this to the
group in the first session, because the instinct in a legal topic is to memorise, and memorising is
what will make them sound incompetent.

## Sequencing across the four weeks

The guide allocates Weeks 10 to 13, which is the largest allocation in the course and the unit
needs it.

- **Week 10 - Topic 5.1.** Open with Mr Soomro's office and the four statements. Do not define
  policy first. Ask the group which of his four statements they could answer this morning; most
  will find they can answer none, which is the right place to begin. Then the four-question reading,
  which should be done on the quoted actions in front of the group before they do it in pairs.
- **Week 11 - Topic 5.2, first half.** The definitions and reasonable accommodation. This is the
  single most important session in the unit. Budget the whole period for reasonable accommodation
  and do not let it become a vocabulary exercise.
- **Week 12 - Topic 5.2, second half.** Section 9's duties, the federal comparison, the audit, and
  the role-play. The role-play needs at least forty minutes; do not start it with ten to go.
- **Week 13 - Topic 5.3.** The attitude material and the gap method. Keep the reservation task for
  the start of this session rather than the end, because everything after it lands differently once
  people have written their sentence.

If the term loses a week, compress Topic 5.1 into half a session and set the quoted passages as
reading. Do not compress the reasonable-accommodation session or the role-play. Those two are where
this unit earns its place.

## Teaching a statute without turning it into recitation

Four moves work.

**1. Read the words aloud, in the room.** Most trainees have never heard a Pakistani statute read.
Read the definition of reasonable accommodation aloud slowly, then read it again in four parts, as
the topic breaks it down. The second reading is where the understanding happens.

**2. Teach the definitions section, not the duties list.** The duties are a list and lists are
learnable. The definitions are where the argument lives, and three of them - person with
disabilities, inclusive education, discrimination - do the work of connecting this unit back to
Units 1, 2, 3 and 4. Spend your time there.

**3. Ask "who is addressed and with what verb" every single time.** Make it a reflex. It is the
habit that prevents both of the characteristic errors: overstating the duty to a head teacher, and
dismissing the Act because its education clause says "endeavor".

**4. Use the retrieved text, not a summary.** The unit's quoted passages were taken from the Act as
published by the Sindh Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, and from the gazette
text of the federal Act; both are recorded with checksums under
`specs/content/efmp-305/sources/texts/`. If you want to project the primary text, use those URLs
rather than a secondary summary, because the summaries in circulation are frequently wrong about
extent.

## Where trainees reliably go wrong

**1. They conflate the policy with the Act.** Expect this in every cohort. The clean diagnostic
question is: "who could a parent complain to, and under which document?" A trainee who answers the
policy has not got it yet.

**2. They read "reasonable" as "affordable".** This is the error with the highest cost outside the
classroom, because it is the one that will later let them accept a refusal they should have
challenged. Correct it with the Act's own structure rather than with an argument: the definition
measures the modification against the need, and the Act lists denial of accommodation inside its
definition of discrimination. Then move straight to the eight free accommodations, because the
abstract correction alone does not change behaviour.

**3. They over-claim against the head teacher.** The opposite error, and it appears as soon as they
are armed. A trainee who tells a head teacher that the Act orders him to employ a signing teacher
is wrong, will be told so, and will lose the admission as well as the argument. Use the role-play
observer role for this; a peer marking their citations accurate or inaccurate corrects it far faster
than you can.

**4. They cite the federal Act in Sindh.** Extremely common, because the federal Act is better known
online and its admission bar is more satisfying to quote. Make the territorial point three times
across the unit and test it in the role-play.

**5. They recite section numbers.** Discourage it actively. If a trainee cites a number in the
role-play, ask them what the clause says; if they cannot say it, that is the lesson. What a head
teacher needs is the substance and the name of the Act.

**6. They treat the policy-to-practice gap as a complaint slot.** Half of all first gap analyses are
"lack of facilities and lack of awareness" at level 2, with no level 4 content at all. Refuse any
analysis that assigns nothing to the classroom level, and refuse any that assigns everything there.

**7. They use the Pakistani attitude studies as national statistics.** Watch for "research shows
that Pakistani teachers have negative attitudes". The studies cited are small and local and three
of them were read here only at abstract level, which the sources file records. Hold the group to
"teachers in these studies reported".

## Running the activities

**The four-question policy audit (Topic 5.1)** scales to any group size. The valuable step is Part 3
and the peer deflection check: trainees discover that a busy officer can wave away almost any first
draft, and the second draft is where they learn to write a request.

**The section 9 audit and role-play (Topic 5.2)** is the centre of the unit. Three things to manage.

- Cast the head teacher role deliberately, at least once, with a confident trainee. A weak head
  teacher who surrenders in the first minute teaches nothing.
- Protect the observer role. The instinct is to join in. The citation list read aloud afterwards is
  the whole mechanism and it collapses if the observer has been arguing.
- Run the correction from the text, not from your authority. Keep the Act's quoted passages on a
  handout so that a disputed citation is settled by reading rather than by rank.

**The reservation task (Topic 5.3)** needs care, and it is the one activity in this course where a
tutor can do harm. Rules:

- It is private. Nobody collects it, nobody reads it aloud, and you do not ask anyone what they
  wrote.
- Only the classification - child, preparation, or conditions - is exchanged, and only with one
  chosen partner.
- Go first. If you are willing, say your own reservation to the group before they write. A tutor who
  admits to a real one gets real ones in return; a tutor who implies she has none gets a room full
  of safe sentences and the activity is wasted.
- Do not moralise about what anyone discloses. A trainee who says she is afraid of a child having a
  seizure and not knowing what to do has named a training gap, not a prejudice, and treating it as a
  prejudice guarantees that nothing honest is said in your sessions again.

## Marking the summative tasks

For the Topic 5.1 note, mark the **analysis of silence** and the balance. A note that ends "the
policy is useless" has not understood what the policy is still for; a note that treats the policy as
a legal duty has the central error of the unit.

For the Topic 5.2 compliance report and for ERQ 3, the two criteria that separate real work from
paper are **evidence** and the **honest paragraph on the unmeetable duty**. Look for "cannot
establish" where a trainee could not get an answer: its presence is a sign of honesty, not of
incomplete work. A report claiming full compliance should be queried as closely as one condemning
the school.

For the Topic 5.3 gap analysis and for ERQ 4, mark **level assignment and ownership**. The failure
mode is the gap that is assigned entirely to the system, which absolves the trainee, or entirely to
the teacher, which is unjust and also ineffective. Ask of every teacher-level action: is it really
free, and is it really available this week? Half of them are not, and the trainee should find that
out from you rather than from a staff room.

For ERQ 2, the strongest answers concede the objection's core before answering it. A response that
treats "sixty children and no budget" as an excuse to be swept aside has misread the unit's whole
posture.

## Answers and keys

The learner-facing assessment carries its own bounded answers and marking guidance in its final
section. Nothing additional is held here. If you set the unit assessment under examination
conditions, note that the MCQ key is visible to learners in the published material, so use the RRQ
and ERQ sections for anything summative that matters. Item 7 of the MCQ bank has two individually
true options and a combined key; if you set it orally, accept the reasoning.

## A caution on legal advice

Two boundaries to state to the group explicitly, early.

**You are not a legal adviser.** A trainee who tells a parent "the school is breaking the law and
you can sue" has gone beyond what this unit equips her to say and may cause a family to spend money
they do not have. What she can accurately say is what the Act requires, that refusal on the ground
of disability is discrimination under it, and that the school has a complaint route and the province
has an Authority. Where a family wants to go further, the right referral is to an organisation of
persons with disabilities or a legal aid body, not to the trainee's own confidence.

**This unit states the law as retrieved on 15 October 2026 and does not track amendments.** Rules
made under the Act, notifications and any amendment since are outside what has been verified here.
Tell trainees to check the Department's own page before relying on a provision in a real case, and
model that habit yourself.

## Links to other units

- **Back to Unit 1** for UNCRPD Article 24 and the rights basis. The Sindh Act's preamble recites
  the Convention, so Unit 1 is not background here; it is the Act's own stated reason for existing.
- **Back to Unit 2** for the impairment and disability distinction. The Act's definition of a person
  with disabilities is the social model in statutory language, and showing trainees that sentence is
  the fastest way to make Unit 2 feel consequential.
- **Back to Units 3 and 4** for the content of the adaptation the Act's definition of inclusive
  education requires. Point out explicitly that the differentiation and accessibility work they did
  is what the statute is naming when it says "suitably adapted" and "reasonable accommodation".
  Trainees who make that connection stop treating this unit as a separate subject.
- **Forward to Unit 6** for the parent and the specialist. This unit has a father in it who is
  refused twice and never treated as a partner; Unit 6 is where that is corrected, and the handover
  is worth stating.

## Practical work

The course guide lists no assessed practical component for this course, so nothing here is set as
one. The unit does contain two placement tasks in Topic 5.1, three in Topic 5.2 and three in
Topic 5.3. The three worth insisting on are the **register count**, the **five-responsibility
audit** and the **one changed decision**, because each produces an artefact: a number with its
source, a completed sheet, and a record of what one pupil did differently. Ask for the artefacts.
In this unit they are also the first professional documents most trainees will ever have written
about a child's entitlement.
