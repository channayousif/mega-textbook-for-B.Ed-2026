## A real classroom situation

The same office, three weeks later. Bilal's father has come back, and this time he has a paper.

A field worker from a small organisation in Hyderabad has given him two printed pages: the first
page of the Sindh Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2018, and the page carrying
section 9, headed "Equity in education". He cannot read the English fluently. He has been told that
one line of it means his son must be admitted.

Mr Soomro reads it. He is a careful man and he does not dismiss it. He reads it twice and then says
three things, each of which a trainee needs to be able to answer.

1. "This says the **Government** shall endeavour. I am a head teacher, not the Government."
2. "It says to provide reasonable accommodation. I have no money. So it is not reasonable for me."
3. "It says education for deaf persons in the most appropriate modes of communication. I have no
   teacher who signs. I cannot comply, so what do you want me to do, break the law?"

He is not being obstructive. He has read the text more honestly than most people who cite it, and
two of his three objections are the two most common misreadings in Pakistan, held by people on
both sides of the argument.

By the end of this topic you will be able to answer all three, in order, from the Act's own words.
The third answer is the hardest and the most important, and it is not "yes, comply or break the
law".

## Explanation

### What the Act is for

The **Sindh Empowerment of 'Persons with Disabilities' Act, 2018** is Sindh Act No. XLVIII of 2018.
It was passed by the Provincial Assembly of Sindh on 24 May 2018, assented to by the Governor on
7 June 2018, and notified on 11 June 2018.

Its own long title states its purpose in one line: it is

> An Act to give effect to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of 'Persons with
> Disabilities' and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

That is worth pausing on. The Act is not a welfare scheme and does not present itself as charity.
It is the province doing, in its own law, what the state undertook when it ratified the UNCRPD on
25 October 2011. Its preamble recites the Convention's principles in order, names the ratification
date, and states that "the matter of 'Persons with Disabilities' is a Provincial Subject under
Constitution of Islamic Republic of Pakistan". Everything Unit 1 taught you about the rights basis
of inclusion is in the first page of this Act, in the Assembly's own words.

It extends to the whole of the Province of Sindh and came into force at once.

#### What the Act sets up

Four pieces of machinery matter for context, because they are what distinguishes an Act from a
policy. The Act establishes an **Authority** for the empowerment of persons with disabilities, a
**Provincial Advisory Council**, a **Provincial Fund**, and **special courts** for persons with
disabilities. It also requires the Authority to conduct a **survey of school-going children every
three years** to identify children with disabilities and ascertain their needs, with the first
survey due within two years of commencement.

You are not asked to memorise that list. You are asked to notice that it exists, because it is the
answer to "the law means nothing here": a mechanism that exists on paper can be asked about, and
an officer can be asked what the last survey found. A mechanism that was never written cannot.

### The Act's four definitions, and why they are the lesson

An Act's definitions section is usually skipped. In this Act it is where the teaching is. Four
definitions in section 2 carry the whole topic.

**Person with disabilities.**

> "person with disabilities" means a person with long term physical, mental, intellectual or
> sensory impairment which, in interaction with barriers, hinders his/her full and effective
> participation in society equally with others

Read "in interaction with barriers". That is the social model of disability, which Unit 2 taught
you, written into Sindh law. The Act does not define disability as a property of a body. It defines
it as what happens when a long-term impairment meets a barrier. Bilal's deafness is the
impairment. The absence of any teacher who signs is the barrier. The Act's own definition says the
disability is in the interaction.

**Inclusive education.**

> "inclusive education" means a system of education wherein students with and without disabilities
> learn together and the system of teaching and learning is suitably adapted to meet the learning
> needs of different types of students with disabilities

Note the second half. The statutory definition does not stop at "learn together". It requires that
"the system of teaching and learning is **suitably adapted**". Placing a deaf child at the back of
an unchanged Grade 1 and calling it inclusion does not meet this definition. Everything in Units 3
and 4 of this course, the differentiation and the accessibility and the aids, is the adaptation
this definition is naming.

**Discrimination on the basis of disabilities.**

> "Discrimination on the basis of disabilities" means any distinction, exclusion or restriction on
> the basis of disabilities which has the purpose or effect of impairing or nullifying the
> recognition, enjoyment or exercise, on an equal basis with others, of all human rights and
> fundamental freedoms [...] It includes all forms of discrimination, **including denial of
> reasonable accommodation**

The emphasis is added. That final clause is the most load-bearing sentence in this unit. We come
to it in the next section.

Note also "purpose **or effect**". Discrimination under this Act does not require that anybody
intended to exclude. A rule applied to everybody identically can have the effect of excluding, and
the effect is enough. Mr Soomro intends nothing against Bilal. He is a kind man with a crowded
school. "Purpose or effect" is the Act declining to make his kindness the test.

**Reasonable accommodation.**

> "reasonable accommodation" means necessary and appropriate modification where needed in a
> particular case, to ensure that the 'Persons with Disabilities' enjoy or exercise on an equal
> basis with others of all human rights and fundamental freedoms at home, workplace and in social
> life

### Reasonable accommodation, taught properly

This is the most misunderstood term in this course, and the misunderstanding has a cost that is
easy to name: it is the reason children are turned away from schools that could have taken them.
Read the definition again, slowly, in four parts.

**"necessary and appropriate modification"** - a change. Not a resource, not a budget, not a
specialist. A change to how something is done.

**"where needed"** - only when it is needed. An accommodation is not a standing entitlement
attached to a label. It is triggered by a need.

**"in a particular case"** - for this child, in this situation. Not a policy for all deaf children.
Not a scheme. One case at a time.

**"to ensure that persons with disabilities enjoy or exercise on an equal basis with others"** -
with a purpose, and the purpose is equality of access, not advantage. An accommodation that gives
an advantage is not accommodation; and an accommodation that leaves the child still unable to
access what others access has not done its job.

#### The misconception: "reasonable" means "reasonable for the school's budget"

This is the misreading that does the damage, and it is Mr Soomro's second objection. He read
"reasonable accommodation" and heard "accommodation that is reasonable for me to afford". On that
reading, a school with no money owes nothing, and the duty evaporates exactly where it is needed
most.

Three things are wrong with it.

**First, "reasonable" is attached to the modification, not to the budget.** The definition says
"necessary and appropriate modification where needed in a particular case". The test it sets is
whether the modification is necessary and appropriate **for the need** - whether it actually gives
this child equal access. Cost is not the subject of the sentence.

**Second, the Act forecloses the cost-only answer directly.** Its definition of discrimination
"includes all forms of discrimination, including denial of reasonable accommodation". If cost alone
were a complete answer to an accommodation request, then a school could discriminate lawfully by
being poor. The Act does not permit that reading: the denial is named as discrimination, not as a
budget outcome.

**Third, and this is what actually unlocks the practice: most accommodations are free.** The whole
argument is usually conducted as though accommodation meant equipment. Unit 4 spent a topic
proving otherwise. Look at what Bilal actually needs in Grade 1:

- A seat in the front row, on the side he reads faces from. Free.
- A teacher who faces the class when she speaks and does not talk while writing on the board.
  Free, and better teaching for the other fifty-four.
- Every instruction that matters written on the board as well as said. Free, costs chalk.
- One classmate briefed as his partner for instructions, rotated weekly. Free.
- Visual material for new vocabulary: objects, pictures, the thing itself where possible. The cost
  of chart paper and a marker.
- Assessment that does not depend on hearing a dictated question. Free, and a change to a paper.
- Permission to use his signs, and the class taught ten of them. Free, and takes one period.
- Extra time in examinations, and a scribe where writing is the barrier. Free, and already named in
  the Act.

Not one of those is equipment. All of them are "necessary and appropriate modification", and seven
of eight cost nothing. A head teacher who says "I have no money, so no accommodation" has usually
not been asked for money. He has been asked for a decision.

#### What accommodation does not mean

Teach the limits as carefully as the duty, because a trainee who overstates it will be corrected
in public and will then undersell it forever.

- **It is not a lowering of the standard.** Unit 3 drew this line already: an accommodation changes
  how a pupil accesses or demonstrates learning; a modification changes what is learned. Extra time
  is accommodation. Marking a different, easier paper and recording the same grade is not.
- **It is not unlimited.** The word "reasonable" is doing some work. An accommodation that is not
  necessary, not appropriate to the need, or that fundamentally alters what the institution does is
  outside the definition. A request to abolish all written examinations for a whole class is not an
  accommodation.
- **It is individual, not categorical.** Two deaf children in the same class may need different
  accommodations, and one of them may need very few. The duty is triggered "in a particular case".
- **It is not a substitute for teaching.** A front seat and a written instruction give Bilal
  access. They do not teach him to read. The adaptation of "the system of teaching and learning",
  which the Act's own definition of inclusive education requires, is the rest of your job.

#### Cost is not irrelevant, and pretending otherwise is dishonest

A ramp costs money. A hearing aid costs money and must be prescribed. A sign-language teacher is a
post, and posts are sanctioned in Karachi and not in a village school. None of this is free and
none of it is within a head teacher's power.

The honest account is the one Unit 4 built: the accommodations split into three lists. **This week,
by me**, which is nearly all of the eight items above. **This term, with the head teacher**, which
is the chart paper budget, the examination arrangements and the seating policy. **Not by me**,
which is the hearing aid, the trained teacher and the building work, and which gets written down
and sent up rather than swallowed.

The legal point is that the third list does not excuse the first. A school that has done nothing on
list one has not been defeated by poverty. It has not started.

### Section 9: what the Act requires of a school

Section 9 is titled "Equity in education". Its first sub-section is the one to know:

> (1) The Government shall endeavor that all educational institutions funded or recognized by it
> provide inclusive education to the children with disabilities and towards that end shall -

followed by ten duties. Here they are in the Act's order, in its own words where it matters, with
what each means for a classroom:

1. **"admit them without discrimination and provide education and opportunities for sports and
   recreation activities equally with others"** - admission is first, and the duty does not stop at
   the gate: games and activities are named.
2. **"make building, campus and various facilities accessible"** - the whole site, not the
   classroom. Unit 4's four layers, as a duty.
3. **"provide reasonable accommodation according to the individual's requirements"** - the duty
   discussed above, and note "according to the individual's requirements", which is the Act
   insisting on the particular case.
4. **"provide necessary support individualized or otherwise in environments that maximize academic
   and social development consistent with the goal of full inclusion"** - support, and the
   environment, judged against "the goal of full inclusion".
5. **"ensure that the education to persons who are blind or deaf or both is imparted in the most
   appropriate languages and modes and means of communication"** - this is Bilal's clause.
6. **"detect specific learning disabilities in children at the earliest and take suitable
   pedagogical and other measures to overcome them"** - early identification, which is Unit 2's
   observation work, as a duty.
7. **"monitor participation, progress in terms of attainment levels and completion of education in
   respect of every student with disabilities"** - not just enrolment. Participation, attainment
   and completion, for **every** such pupil.
8. **"provide transportation facilities"** to the child and, where support needs are high, to the
   attendant.
9. **"enable 'Persons with Disabilities' to learn life and social development skills"** for full
   and equal participation in education and in the community.
10. **"ensure that all 'Persons with Disabilities' are given full access to all modes and means of
    education"**, including vocational training, online education, distance learning and adult
    education.

Sub-section (2) then lists measures the Government is to take, several of which answer objections
head teachers actually raise: teacher-training institutions for inclusive education; the training
and employment of teachers qualified in **sign language and Braille**; books, learning materials
and assistive devices **free of cost up to the age of eighteen**; scholarships in appropriate
cases; and

> to make suitable modifications in the curriculum and examination system to meet the needs of
> students with disabilities such as extra time for completion of examination paper, facility of
> scribe or amanuensis, exemption from second and third language courses, age relaxation to sit in
> particular examinations

That is a legislature naming concrete examination accommodations. When a trainee is told that
"accommodation" is a foreign idea imported from Europe, this is the paragraph to produce: extra
time, a scribe, exemption from second and third language courses, age relaxation. It is Sindh law,
written in Sindh, in 2018.

#### Answering Mr Soomro's three objections

**"This says the Government shall endeavour. I am a head teacher, not the Government."**

Half right, and the half that is right matters. The duty in s. 9(1) is addressed to the
Government, and the verb is "shall endeavor", which is softer than a flat command. A trainee who
tells a head teacher that the Act orders **him** personally is overstating, and he will know it.

But the duty runs through him. It is a duty that the Government "endeavor that all educational
institutions **funded or recognized by it** provide inclusive education", and a government primary
school is funded by it. The person who provides or withholds is the school. And two further things
are not endeavour at all:

- Section 3(2) is flat: "No person or institution, whether public or private, shall be allowed to
  discriminate against 'Persons with Disabilities' or violate their rights or restrict benefits in
  any manner." That is not aspirational and it names institutions.
- Refusing admission to Bilal on the ground of his deafness is a "distinction, exclusion or
  restriction on the basis of disabilities" with the effect of nullifying his enjoyment of
  education on an equal basis with others. That is the Act's own definition of discrimination.

So the accurate answer is: the Act puts the endeavour on the Government, and it puts the
non-discrimination on the institution. The school cannot be ordered by this clause to employ a
signing teacher. It can be told not to refuse the boy.

**"I have no money. So it is not reasonable for me."**

Answered above. The modification must be necessary and appropriate for the need; denial of
reasonable accommodation is itself named as discrimination; and the eight things Bilal needs in
Grade 1 cost, in total, a sheet of chart paper and a marker. Ask for the decision, not the money.

**"I have no teacher who signs. I cannot comply, so what do you want me to do?"**

This is the honest objection and it deserves the honest answer, which has three parts.

*First, the duty to teach in the most appropriate mode is real and he cannot meet it today.* Say so
plainly. Pretending a mainstream village school can deliver Pakistan Sign Language instruction this
term is not advocacy, it is a promise that will fail Bilal in November.

*Second, partial compliance is not the same as none.* The clause he cannot meet is one of ten.
Admission without discrimination, an accessible site, reasonable accommodation, individualised
support, early detection, monitoring, transport, life skills and access to all modes of education
are the other nine, and most of them are available to him this month. The law is not a switch. A
school moves along it.

*Third, the clause he cannot meet is a written request, not a dead end.* "I require a teacher
trained in sign language for a pupil on my register" is a sentence that creates a record. Section
9(2) obliges the Government to train and employ such teachers. One request from one village school
changes nothing; a record of requests is how a district eventually gets a post. Unit 4 taught you
to write that request. This topic tells you which duty to cite in it.

### The federal comparison, kept short

You will meet the **ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020** (Act No. XXXV of 2020,
assented 22 September 2020). It is a federal Act and it is frequently cited in Pakistani writing
on inclusion, so you should be able to place it. Note the gazetted short title says "Disability",
singular.

Its section 1 is decisive for our purposes: "It shall extend to the whole of the Islamabad Capital
Territory (ICT)." It does not apply in Sindh.

Three comparisons are worth carrying, and Figure 5.3 sets them out in full.

**Where the federal Act is stronger in wording.** Its s. 9(4) is a flat bar: persons with
disabilities "shall not be denied admission into any educational institution, whether state owned
or private, on ground of their disabilities". Its s. 9(5) goes further and makes discrimination at
a place of education "illegal" and punishable for "both the institution and such violating
person". Its s. 9(2) promises free education from pre-primary to higher education. The Sindh Act's
education duties open with "shall endeavor".

**Where they are identical.** The definition of **reasonable accommodation** is the same in both
Acts, almost word for word, and both Acts' definitions of discrimination end with the same clause:
"denial of reasonable accommodation". This is the practically important point of the comparison. If
you learn reasonable accommodation correctly for Sindh, you have learned it for Islamabad too, and
for the UNCRPD both Acts derive it from.

**Where the Sindh Act gives a school more to work with.** Sindh's s. 9(1) sets out ten specific
institutional duties, which is what makes the audit in the next section possible; and its s. 9(2)
names the examination accommodations. The federal Act's education section is shorter and less
itemised.

#### Worked example: the same case in Islamabad

Suppose Bilal's family moves to a settlement in the Islamabad Capital Territory and his father
goes to the nearest government primary school. What changes?

The **argument gets simpler and sharper.** Under s. 9(4) the school cannot deny him admission on
the ground of his disability, full stop. There is no "endeavour" to argue about. Under s. 9(5), a
member of staff who discriminates against him at his place of education is personally exposed, not
only the institution. Under s. 9(2) his education is to be free from pre-primary upwards.

What does **not** change: the definition of reasonable accommodation, which is the same; the fact
that denying it is discrimination, which is the same; and the hard practical problem, which is that
neither Act produces a signing teacher in September. The eight free accommodations are the same
eight.

And the thing a trainee must not do: cite s. 9(4) or s. 9(5) at a head teacher in Tando Muhammad
Khan. It is not the law there, he may well know it is not, and a wrong citation costs you the whole
conversation. Cite the Sindh Act in Sindh. That is the rule, and it is the reason this comparison is
one passage long.

#### The misconception: "Pakistan has one national disability law that applies everywhere"

This is why the comparison is here at all. The belief is extremely common, including among trained
teachers, and it produces two opposite errors. Some teachers in Sindh believe no law applies to
them because they have only heard of a federal Act. Others cite the federal Act's strong admission
bar in a province where it has no force.

The correction is one sentence: **since the 18th Amendment, the law that governs your school is
your province's law**, and in Sindh that is the Act of 2018. The federal Act of 2020 reaches
Islamabad only. Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan have their own arrangements.

### The school responsibilities audit

Section 9's ten duties can be turned into something a head teacher can actually use: a list he can
walk around his own school with. This is the practical product of the topic and it is what
Figure 5.4 maps.

Group the duties into five responsibilities.

**1. Admission without discrimination.** Is any child in the catchment being turned away, directed
elsewhere, or quietly discouraged on the ground of a disability? Is admission refused for the
absence of a document the school does not require of other children? Is a child admitted to the
register and then not expected to attend?

*Audit evidence:* the register, and the honest answer to "has anyone been sent to Hyderabad this
year?"

**2. Accessible building, campus and facilities.** The route from the gate, the step at the
classroom door, the aisle, the toilet, the water point, the ground where games are played. Unit 4's
four layers, with the Act's own deadline behind them: s. 6(11) requires existing public buildings
to be made accessible in accordance with the Accessibility Code of Pakistan 2006 within five years
of the Act's promulgation. For an Act notified in June 2018, that period has passed.

*Audit evidence:* the accessibility walk from Unit 4, written up, with times and distances.

**3. Reasonable accommodation, case by case.** For each pupil with a disability on the register, is
there a short written note of what she needs and what the school has changed? Not a file, a note.
Three lines is enough.

*Audit evidence:* the notes. Their absence is the finding.

**4. Individualised support, appropriate modes of communication, and early detection.** Is there
support beyond the seat: a partner, an adapted material, a modified task? For a blind or deaf
pupil, what has been done about mode of communication, even partially? Is anybody looking for
specific learning difficulties early, as duty six requires, or are such pupils simply recorded as
weak?

*Audit evidence:* a named pupil, and what the class teacher can describe without notice.

**5. Monitoring participation, progress and completion.** Duty seven is the one schools skip. It is
not enough that the pupil is enrolled. Does she attend? Does she take part? Is her attainment
recorded and moving? Did she complete the stage? A school that cannot answer these four questions
for a named pupil is not monitoring, whatever the register says.

*Audit evidence:* attendance, assessment records and the leaving data for pupils with disabilities,
compared with the rest of the cohort.

### Worked example: the head teacher's admission and accommodation decision

Mr Soomro decides, after reading the two pages, to do this properly. Here is what a defensible
decision looks like. Nothing in it needs money that he does not have.

**Step 1 - admit.** Bilal is entered on the Grade 1 register on the day he presents. No order from
the Education Officer is required to admit a child of catchment age, and the duty in s. 9(1) and
the prohibition in s. 3(2) both point the same way. The admission is unconditional; it is not
"subject to his coping".

**Step 2 - find out what he needs, from the people who know.** Twenty minutes with the father, and
if possible the mother, in the courtyard, not across a desk. Three questions: how does he make
himself understood at home, what does he already understand, and what frightens or confuses him.
The family has forty signs; this is the single most valuable piece of information in the school and
it is free.

**Step 3 - write the three-line note.** "Bilal, Grade 1, profound hearing loss since age 2, no
hearing aid, uses about 40 family signs and lip-reads partially. Needs: front seat right of
centre, instructions written as well as spoken, a briefed partner, visual vocabulary support,
examination without dictation. Reviewed: end of Term 1." That note is the school's compliance
record for duty three, and it took four minutes.

**Step 4 - change the teaching, not just the seating.** The Grade 1 teacher faces the class when
she speaks; the key instruction goes on the board; the class is taught ten of Bilal's signs in one
period, which also does more for his social inclusion than a term of good intentions. The sign
lesson is the step teachers are most surprised by and the one that works best.

**Step 5 - escalate the clause he cannot meet, in writing.** One paragraph to the Taluka Education
Officer: a named pupil, the duty under s. 9(1) to educate deaf pupils in the most appropriate mode,
the Government's own obligation under s. 9(2) to train and employ teachers qualified in sign
language, and a request for guidance on provision. Copy kept. This is duty five honoured as far as
his power extends, and it is also the record that makes a post possible in five years.

**Step 6 - monitor and write it down.** Attendance weekly. Two lines at the end of each term on
participation and attainment. This is duty seven, it takes ten minutes a term, and it is the
evidence that will matter if anyone ever asks whether Bilal was included or merely enrolled.

**Step 7 - ask for the free entitlements.** Section 9(2) obliges the Government to provide books,
learning materials and appropriate assistive devices free of cost up to the age of eighteen. Ask.
The answer may be no, and asking creates a record. The same applies to the examination
accommodations, which are already named in the Act and therefore do not need to be invented by a
sympathetic examiner.

Count the cost of the whole sequence: a sheet of chart paper, a marker, about ninety minutes of
Mr Soomro's time across a term, and one period of the Grade 1 teacher's. The parts that need money
are on the escalation list, where they belong.

Bilal is now in school. He is not yet well taught. That is Topic 5.3, and Units 3 and 4.

## Activity: The section 9 audit and the admission role-play

Work in threes. About fifty-five minutes.

**Part 1 - build the audit sheet (10 minutes).** From the five responsibilities in this topic,
write an audit sheet on one page: five headings, and under each, two or three questions a visitor
could answer by looking or asking. Keep every question answerable by evidence rather than by
opinion. "Is the school inclusive?" is not a question for this sheet.

**Part 2 - audit a school (20 minutes).** Use your practicum school if you have one, or the
institution you are sitting in. Walk and ask. Complete the sheet. Where you cannot get an answer,
write "cannot establish" rather than guessing, and note who would know.

**Part 3 - the role-play (25 minutes).** Three roles, rotating so each of you plays each role once
for about seven minutes.

- **The parent.** You have a deaf child of seven, two printed pages of the Act, limited English,
  and you have been refused once already.
- **The head teacher.** You have 212 pupils, four rooms, no training, and the three objections from
  the start of this topic. Use them. Do not be a villain and do not surrender in the first minute.
- **The observer.** Say nothing. Write down every time the parent's advocate cites a document, and
  mark each citation accurate or inaccurate. Mark separately every time cost is offered as a
  complete answer, and whether it was challenged.

After each round, the observer reads out only the citation list. The group's job is to correct any
inaccurate citation from the Act's text in this topic. A trainee who cites the federal Act in
Sindh, or who tells the head teacher the Act personally orders him to employ a signing teacher,
should be corrected here rather than in a real office.

## Summary

The **Sindh Empowerment of 'Persons with Disabilities' Act, 2018** (Sindh Act No. XLVIII of 2018,
notified 11 June 2018) is an Act "to give effect to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of
'Persons with Disabilities'". It extends to the whole Province of Sindh and came into force at once.
It establishes an Authority, a Council, a Fund, special courts and a three-yearly survey of
school-going children. This is the Act your course guide means, and it is the law in force where
you will teach.

Four **definitions** in section 2 carry the topic. A person with disabilities is a person whose
long-term impairment, "in interaction with barriers", hinders full participation: the social model,
in Sindh law. Inclusive education means learning together **and** a system of teaching and learning
"suitably adapted". Discrimination means any distinction, exclusion or restriction with the
"purpose or effect" of nullifying equal enjoyment of rights, and "includes all forms of
discrimination, including denial of reasonable accommodation". Reasonable accommodation means
"necessary and appropriate modification where needed in a particular case" so that the person
enjoys rights "on an equal basis with others".

**Reasonable accommodation does not mean accommodation the budget finds convenient.** "Reasonable"
qualifies the modification against the need, not against the school's purse; the Act names denial
of it as discrimination, which it could not do if poverty were a complete defence; and in practice
most accommodations are free. Seven of the eight things a deaf child needs in Grade 1 cost nothing,
and the eighth costs a sheet of chart paper. Accommodation is not a lowering of the standard, is
not unlimited, is individual rather than categorical, and is not a substitute for teaching.

**Section 9** places ten duties on institutions funded or recognised by Government: admit without
discrimination, make the building and campus accessible, provide reasonable accommodation according
to the individual's requirements, provide individualised support, educate blind and deaf pupils in
the most appropriate modes of communication, detect specific learning disabilities early, monitor
participation and attainment and completion for every such pupil, provide transport, teach life and
social skills, and give access to all modes of education. Section 9(2) obliges the Government to
train and employ teachers qualified in sign language and Braille, supply books, materials and
assistive devices free to age eighteen, and make examination modifications including **extra time,
a scribe, exemption from second and third language courses and age relaxation**.

The head teacher's three objections have answers. The duty is on the Government but it runs through
institutions funded by it, and section 3(2) flatly forbids any institution from discriminating.
Cost is not a complete answer, and the accommodations he is actually being asked for are free. And
the one duty he genuinely cannot meet this term does not excuse the nine he can: partial compliance
is movement, and the unmeetable clause becomes a written request that cites the Government's own
obligation to supply the teacher.

The **federal ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020** extends to the Islamabad Capital
Territory only. Its admission bar and its personal liability for staff are worded more strongly;
its definition of reasonable accommodation is the same as Sindh's, almost word for word. Cite the
Sindh Act in Sindh. Pakistan does not have one disability law that applies everywhere, and since
the 18th Amendment the governing instrument for your school is provincial.

Five **responsibilities** make the ten duties auditable: admission without discrimination;
accessible building, campus and facilities; reasonable accommodation case by case; individualised
support with appropriate modes and early detection; and monitoring of participation, attainment and
completion. A school that cannot answer the monitoring questions for a named pupil has enrolled
her, not included her.

## Try this at your practicum school

Three tasks. Half a page each.

1. **Complete the audit.** Run the five-responsibility audit sheet from the activity on your
   placement school. Record "cannot establish" where that is the truth. Note which of the five is
   weakest and what the cheapest first step would be.
2. **Find one accommodation already in place.** Every school has at least one, usually
   unrecognised: a pupil moved to the front, a question repeated, a task shortened, extra time
   quietly given. Find it, describe it, and ask the teacher why she does it. Most teachers
   accommodate without knowing the word, and recognising that is the fastest route to a staff room
   conversation that goes somewhere.
3. **Complete the blank from Topic 5.1.** Go back to the hypothetical you asked about in Topic 5.1
   ("if a parent brought a deaf child of seven, what would happen?") and write underneath it what
   section 9 requires. Do not go back and argue with the teacher. Write the contrast for yourself;
   it is your own answer in a real office next year that this is preparing.

## Summative task

**Task.** Produce a **school compliance report** of no more than three pages on one real school,
addressed to its head teacher, assessing it against section 9 of the Sindh Act, 2018.

Your report must contain:

1. A one-paragraph statement of the legal position: which Act applies, to which institutions, and
   on what basis. Correct attribution matters more than length here.
2. The five responsibilities as headings, with findings under each, each finding supported by
   evidence you obtained rather than by impression.
3. For at least one named pupil (use initials), a three-line accommodation note in the form taught
   here, with each item tied to the duty it serves and its cost.
4. A separation of findings into what the school can do this month at no cost, what needs the head
   teacher's decision or a small budget, and what must be escalated with the clause to cite.
5. One paragraph that states, honestly, which duty the school cannot presently meet and why, and
   what the written escalation should say.

**Mini-rubric** (25 marks)

| Criterion | Marks | What a strong response does |
|---|---|---|
| Legal accuracy | 5 | Names the Sindh Act correctly, states its extent, attributes duties to the right provision, and does not cite the federal Act as governing. Distinguishes the endeavour clause from the flat prohibition without either softening or inflating the duty. |
| Reasonable accommodation handled correctly *(Understand)* | 5 | Treats accommodation as modification against need, not against budget. Names the clause that makes denial discrimination. The accommodations proposed are specific, individual and mostly free. |
| Evidence quality | 5 | Findings rest on a register, a walk, a document or a direct answer. "Cannot establish" appears where it is the truth rather than being filled with a guess. |
| Three-way separation *(Apply)* | 5 | Free, budgeted and escalated items are correctly sorted; nothing cheap is parked in the escalation list, and nothing costly is promised as free. |
| Honesty about the unmeetable duty | 5 | States plainly what the school cannot do, resists pretending otherwise, and converts it into a written request that cites the Government's own obligation. |

The mark that is hardest to earn is the fifth. A report that claims full compliance is as weak as
one that condemns the school; the professional document says what has been done, what can be done
by Friday, and what has to be asked for in writing.
