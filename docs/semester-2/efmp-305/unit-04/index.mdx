# Creating an Inclusive Classroom Environment

Unit 3 changed the lesson. This unit changes the room the lesson happens in.

That sounds like the smaller job. It is usually the larger one, because the room is where most
exclusion actually lives. A pupil can be given a differentiated worksheet and still spend the
year unable to see the blackboard, unable to reach her bench without two classmates standing up,
unable to ask a question because the only rule she has been taught is to be quiet.

Here is the difficulty, stated honestly at the start. You will teach in rooms you cannot
structurally change. Fifty-eight pupils, fifteen benches bolted to the floor or too heavy for
one person to move, one door, three windows, a blackboard, no electricity for half the day. You
will not be given a ramp because you asked for one, and you will not be given a teaching
assistant at all.

So this unit is about the part of the room that **does** move: who sits where, how pupils get in
and out, what the routines are, where the light falls, how much noise there is, where you stand,
and what you can make from cardboard and a marker in twenty minutes. Every solution in this unit
is costed. Most of them cost nothing.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain what classroom management is **for** in an inclusive classroom, and why a silent class
   is not evidence that it is working.
2. Describe a positive behaviour routine for a large primary class, and explain how it changes the
   environment rather than only the pupil.
3. Produce a seating plan for a fixed-bench classroom that spreads participation, and justify each
   placement by the barrier it removes.
4. Audit the accessibility of a real classroom across four layers - the route, the room, the
   material and the routine - and name what you can change this week, this term and not at all.
5. Match teaching aids and assistive solutions to named learner needs, and cost them in rupees,
   distinguishing what you can make yourself from what the school or district must provide.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 2 and 3 are assumed. You should be able to describe the range of learner needs an ordinary
Pakistani classroom contains, including sensory, physical and intellectual disability, learning
difficulties and social difference (Unit 2), and you should be able to differentiate the content,
process and product of one lesson and tell an accommodation from a modification (Unit 3). This
unit does not reteach either. It starts from the point where your lesson is well planned and the
room is still in the way.

## In this unit

1. [Managing an inclusive classroom](./topic-01) - what classroom management is for, the positive
   behaviour routines that carry it, and how to arrange a fixed-bench room so that participation
   is spread rather than concentrated.
2. [Accessibility, aids and arrangement](./topic-02) - auditing the route, the room, the material
   and the routine; teaching aids that carry content through another sense; and low-cost and
   no-cost assistive solutions, costed in rupees.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Read the classroom situation, work through the explanation, and
do the activity before you move on. In this unit the activities are the point: a trainee who has
read about an accessibility audit has learned a word, and a trainee who has walked one has learned
the skill.

One room runs through both topics: Room 4 at Government Girls Primary School, Tando Allahyar.
Grade 3, fifty-eight pupils, fifteen fixed benches, one door with a step at it, three windows on
one side. You will meet the room first as its teacher sees it, then as six of its pupils
experience it, then as something you can change by Friday. The pupils recur by name. Keep track of
them; the assessment expects you to.

The "Check your understanding" items ask you to produce an answer, not to recognise one. Try them
before you look back. The unit ends with an assessment that draws both topics together, and the
final extended-response question asks you to cost your own plan - so keep a note of prices as you
read.
