# Inclusive Teaching Strategies

Units 1 and 2 argued that the school should adapt to the child, and gave you the vocabulary to
describe who is in your classroom. This unit is about the adapting. It is the most practical
unit in the course, and the one you will use on Monday morning.

Three strategies do most of the work: **differentiated instruction**, **cooperative learning**
and **flexible assessment**. Each is useful on its own. Together they let one teacher, in one
room, with one set of materials, teach a class whose pupils are not all in the same place.

A warning about the objection you are probably already making. Almost every teacher who meets
these strategies says some version of "that is impossible with sixty pupils and no resources".
It is a fair objection and this unit takes it seriously: every example here is set in a class of
forty to sixty, with fixed benches, one textbook per bench and no teaching assistant. Where a
strategy costs time or money, the cost is stated.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain what differentiated instruction is, and why an inclusive classroom cannot do without
   some form of it.
2. Differentiate a single lesson by **content**, by **process** and by **product**, and say which
   of the three a particular pupil's barrier calls for.
3. Describe the main cooperative learning structures and explain what each one does for the
   pupils an ordinary lesson leaves behind.
4. Distinguish an **accommodation** from a **modification**, and choose assessment adaptations
   that keep the standard intact.
5. Plan one complete lesson that uses differentiation, cooperative learning and flexible
   assessment together, and justify each decision in it.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 and 2 are assumed. You should be able to state the principles of inclusion (Unit 1) and
to describe the range of learner needs a Pakistani classroom contains, including learning
difficulties and social difference as well as disability (Unit 2). This unit does not reteach
either; it starts from the point where you have noticed that a pupil is not learning and asks
what you do next.

## In this unit

1. [Differentiating for every learner](./topic-01) - what differentiated instruction is, and how
   to differentiate content, process and product in one lesson.
2. [Cooperative learning and flexible assessment](./topic-02) - the structures that make pupils
   teach each other, and the assessment adaptations that measure learning rather than disability.
3. [Planning an inclusive lesson](./topic-03) - putting all three strategies into a single plan
   you could teach tomorrow.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the
explanation, and do the activity before moving on; in this unit the activities are where the
learning actually happens, because these are skills rather than facts.

One lesson runs through the whole unit: a Grade 6 science lesson on how plants make food, taught
to fifty pupils. You will meet it first taught the ordinary way, then differentiated, then with
cooperative structures and adapted assessment, and finally as one complete plan. Watching the
same lesson change is the point. Do not skip to Topic 3.3 for the finished plan: it will look
like a form to fill in rather than a set of decisions you made.

The "Check your understanding" items ask you to produce an answer, not to recognise one. Try
them without looking back, then check. The unit ends with an assessment drawing the three topics
together.
