## Unit summary

This unit took you from the principle that the school adapts to the child (Unit 1) to the
practical question of how one teacher does that on a Monday morning in a class of fifty.

**Topic 3.1** defined **differentiated instruction**: one lesson planned so pupils in different
places reach **the same objective** by **different routes**. You learned to change **content**
(the route in), **process** (how the pupil works) or **product** (how she shows what she knows),
and to diagnose which of the three a particular barrier calls for. You also met the distinction
that protects the standard: an **accommodation** changes access, a **modification** changes the
standard, and you try every accommodation first.

**Topic 3.2** separated **cooperative learning** from ordinary group work. The two conditions that
make it cooperative are **positive interdependence** and **individual accountability**; without
them, seating pupils together produces the result Mr Ashfaq got, where four boys did the work of
twenty-nine. You met four structures that need no space or materials - think-pair-share, jigsaw,
numbered heads together, pairs-check - each matched to a specific exclusion. The topic then turned
to **flexible assessment**, whose principle is to assess the objective rather than the pupil's
ability to cope with your format, and whose discipline is to hold one set of criteria across
every product format. Flexible assessment is not easier assessment.

**Topic 3.3** integrated the three through an **order of decisions**: objective, barriers, content
and process, structure, evidence and criteria, check. The activity is the fourth decision, not the
first. The barrier table of named pupils is the step that makes everything else possible, and the
final check is what catches an adaptation that has quietly lowered the objective.

Across the unit, one lesson - Grade 6 photosynthesis, fifty-one pupils, one blackboard - was
taught the ordinary way, then differentiated, then restructured, then rebuilt as a complete plan.
The arithmetic of its cost was stated honestly: about twenty minutes of extra planning the first
time, about five thereafter, one photocopy or none.

Unit 4 turns from the lesson to the room it happens in.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose one answer. Each question carries 1 mark.

1. *(Remember)* Which pair of elements defines differentiated instruction?
   - A) Easier work for weaker pupils and harder work for stronger pupils
   - B) The same objective for all pupils, reached by different routes
   - C) Separate lessons written for each pupil
   - D) A reduced syllabus for pupils who are behind

2. *(Remember)* In the terms used in this unit, what does differentiating **product** mean?
   - A) Changing the objective the pupil is working towards
   - B) Changing the material the pupil meets
   - C) Changing the evidence the pupil produces to show what she knows
   - D) Changing the pupil's group

3. *(Remember)* Which two conditions distinguish cooperative learning from group work?
   - A) Positive interdependence and individual accountability
   - B) Small groups and a shared worksheet
   - C) Mixed ability and a time limit
   - D) Teacher circulation and a final mark

4. *(Understand)* A teacher gives a pupil with low vision an enlarged copy of the same
   examination paper, marked against the same criteria. This is best described as:
   - A) a modification, because the paper is different
   - B) an accommodation, because access changed and the standard did not
   - C) a modification, because the pupil is disadvantaged
   - D) neither, because the paper must be identical for all pupils

5. *(Understand)* Why does this unit insist that flexible assessment is not easier assessment?
   - A) Because easier assessment is against school rules
   - B) Because pupils dislike easier work
   - C) Because lowering the syllabus leaves the pupil unprepared for the next real examination
   - D) Because accommodations take more of the teacher's time

6. *(Understand)* In the six-decision planning order, why is the activity the fourth decision
   rather than the first?
   - A) Because activities are less important than assessment
   - B) Because fixing the activity first turns every pupil who does not fit it into a problem
     rather than someone planned for
   - C) Because the school's planning form requires that order
   - D) Because cooperative structures take longest to prepare

7. *(Apply)* A pupil understands the mathematics but writes so slowly that she completes a third
   of any written task. Which should you differentiate first, and what would you try?
   - A) Content: give her simpler problems
   - B) Process: put her in a group
   - C) Product: accept an oral answer or bullet points against the same criteria
   - D) Objective: expect her to complete fewer problems to a lower standard

8. *(Apply)* In a class of sixty, the same four pupils answer every question. Which cooperative
   structure most directly addresses this, and by what mechanism?
   - A) Jigsaw, because the material is divided into parts
   - B) Pairs-check, because feedback is immediate
   - C) Numbered heads together, because any pupil may be called and so the bench must prepare
     everybody
   - D) Group work, because pupils sit together

9. *(Apply)* A teacher plans a jigsaw and randomly assigns the six parts of a text. One pupil
   reads two years below level and is given the densest part. What should the teacher do?
   - A) Let her manage, since cooperative learning means pupils help each other
   - B) Assign the parts deliberately, giving her the part carried by the diagram
   - C) Exclude her from the jigsaw and give her separate work
   - D) Give her part to a stronger pupil and let her listen

10. *(Apply)* You run Decision 6 and find that one named pupil's version of the task asks her to
    name two of the three inputs to photosynthesis while the class must name three. What has
    happened, and what should you do?
    - A) Nothing; this is normal differentiation
    - B) The objective moved, so this is a modification; return to the route decision and look for
      an accommodation that keeps the objective whole
    - C) The objective moved, so lower it for the whole class to keep things fair
    - D) The pupil should be referred for assessment

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question concisely, using the unit's terminology. Marks are shown.

1. *(Understand)* Define differentiated instruction in one sentence, then explain why dropping
   either half of your definition produces something that is not differentiation. (4 marks)

2. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between an accommodation and a modification, and state
   the rule this unit gives about which to try first and why. (4 marks)

3. *(Understand)* Explain why product is described as the most neglected of the three in Pakistani
   classrooms, and what a lesson actually measures when written Urdu is the only accepted
   evidence. (4 marks)

4. *(Understand)* Describe two of the four cooperative structures from Topic 3.2. For each, name
   the specific excluded learner it is designed to reach. (4 marks)

5. *(Apply)* A Grade 7 class of 52 is learning to write a formal letter. Zubair understands the
   structure but cannot spell; Hira finishes in five minutes; Faiza cannot start a blank page.
   Give one differentiation for each, naming whether you are changing content, process or
   product. (6 marks)

6. *(Apply)* You want every pupil in a class of 55 to answer a question rather than the usual
   volunteers, and you have fixed benches and no materials. Describe exactly what you would say
   and do, minute by minute, for one question. (5 marks)

7. *(Analyze)* A head teacher says, "If we let that girl answer orally while everyone else writes,
   her result does not mean the same thing." Identify the assumption in this objection, and
   explain what would have to be true of the teacher's practice for the objection to be answered.
   (5 marks)

8. *(Analyze)* A teacher reports that her group work "does not work - the clever ones do
   everything". Using the five conditions of cooperative learning, identify which conditions are
   most likely missing and explain how you can tell from her description alone. (5 marks)

9. *(Analyze)* A lesson plan contains a tiered worksheet, two cooperative structures, three
   product formats and an exit slip, for a single forty-minute period. Analyse what is likely to
   happen and identify which planning decision was mishandled. (5 marks)

10. *(Analyze)* Two teachers both claim to differentiate. Teacher A gives her lowest group
    fifteen simple sums while the class does fifteen harder ones. Teacher B gives her lowest group
    the same fifteen problems with a worked example beside the first three. Analyse the difference
    between what these two teachers are doing, using the unit's terms. (5 marks)

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer in continuous prose. Each question carries 10 marks and has a band rubric below.

1. *(Analyze)* Miss Rubina's homework pile from Topic 3.1 contained six distinct patterns of
   response from fifty-one pupils. Analyse that pile as a diagnostic document: for each of four
   patterns you choose, identify the probable barrier, locate it in content, process or product,
   and state the variation you would try first. Conclude by explaining what the pile shows about
   the lesson as it was taught.

2. *(Evaluate)* "Cooperative learning is a luxury for small, well-resourced classes. In a class of
   sixty on fixed benches it is simply not realistic." Evaluate this claim, drawing on Topic 3.2.
   Your answer should take the objection seriously, use at least two specific structures, and
   reach a judgement you defend.

3. *(Create)* Design a complete inclusive lesson for a class you know, following the six-decision
   order from Topic 3.3. Present the objective, a barrier table of at least four named pupils,
   the differentiation, one cooperative structure with its interdependence and accountability
   mechanisms, the evidence with a single set of criteria, and your Decision 6 check. Include a
   costing in preparation minutes and materials.

4. *(Analyze)* A school introduces a policy that all pupils identified as having special
   educational needs will sit a shorter version of each end-of-term paper, covering fewer topics,
   with a lower pass mark. Analyse this policy using the accommodation/modification distinction
   and the unit's position on flexible assessment. Identify who is protected by the policy, who is
   harmed by it, and over what timescale the harm appears.

5. *(Evaluate)* Miss Rubina says the integrated approach costs her twenty minutes of extra
   planning the first time she teaches a lesson and five minutes thereafter. Evaluate whether this
   is a reasonable claim and whether the approach is sustainable for a teacher with five periods a
   day and fifty pupils in each. Your answer should identify what makes the cost fall, what could
   make it rise, and what you would advise a teacher to do in their first term.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B  2. C  3. A  4. B  5. C  6. B  7. C  8. C  9. B  10. B

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Differentiated instruction is one lesson planned so that pupils who are not in the same place
   can all reach **the same objective** by **different routes**. Drop "the same objective" and you
   have a reduced curriculum - different pupils learning different, lesser things. Drop "different
   routes" and you have an ordinary uniform lesson, addressed to the pupils in the middle.
   (2 marks for the definition with both halves; 1 mark for each consequence.)

2. An **accommodation** changes *how* a pupil accesses learning or shows it, leaving the standard
   identical - extra time, an enlarged paper, an oral response. A **modification** changes *what*
   is learned or to *what standard* - fewer topics, simpler questions, a lower pass mark. The rule
   is to exhaust every accommodation before modifying, and to record any modification made. The
   reason is cumulative: a pupil given modifications year on year arrives at a public examination
   several years behind, with the gap created by the adaptations rather than by her ability.
   (2 marks for the distinction, 1 for the rule, 1 for the reason.)

3. Product is neglected because the written paragraph is so standard a form of evidence that
   teachers stop seeing it as a choice. When written Urdu is the only accepted product, the lesson
   measures written Urdu: any pupil whose barrier is handwriting speed, spelling, or fluency in the
   language of instruction registers as a pupil who has not learned the subject. The pupils who
   draw the diagram beautifully and write nothing are the clearest case - they have evidence of
   learning that the format refuses to count. (2 marks for the reason, 2 for what gets measured
   instead, with an example.)

4. Any two, each with its matched learner. **Think-pair-share** - the pupil who will not speak in
   front of fifty, because she rehearses with one partner first, and the slow processor, because of
   the silent thinking time. **Jigsaw** - the pupil who contributes nothing to a group task, because
   the bench cannot succeed without her part. **Numbered heads together** - the pupil whose hand is
   never chosen, because any number may be called. **Pairs-check** - the pupil who needs feedback
   within seconds rather than days. (1 mark per structure described, 1 mark per matched learner.)

5. Zubair: **product** - accept the letter with spelling unmarked, or let him dictate while a
   partner writes, marked against the letter-structure criteria only. Hira: **content or process** -
   an extension that asks her to write the same letter for a different audience, or to identify
   what would make it inappropriate. Faiza: **process** - a scaffold, specifically a sentence
   starter and a skeleton of the letter's parts, removed once she can start unaided. (1 mark per
   named variation, 1 mark per correct location. Accept other defensible locations if the reasoning
   is given; "give extra attention" earns nothing.)

6. Credit a minute-by-minute account of think-pair-share or numbered heads together with the actual
   words. For example: "Here is the question. Thirty seconds, think on your own, do not write."
   (silence, timed) "Now tell the person beside you your answer and listen to theirs." (one minute)
   "Numbers threes, stand and answer." Total about two and a half minutes. (2 marks for a
   structure that genuinely reaches everybody, 2 for the actual words and timings, 1 for workability
   with fixed benches and no materials.)

7. **The assumption:** that the mark's meaning comes from the uniformity of the format rather than
   from the standard applied. **What must be true to answer it:** the teacher must have written the
   criteria down in advance and used the same ones for the oral and the written responses, and must
   have recorded the adaptation. If she can produce the criteria and show that the oral answer was
   judged against them, the mark means exactly what the written marks mean. If she cannot, the head
   teacher's objection stands - not because oral assessment is invalid, but because an unrecorded
   standard is unverifiable. (2 marks for the assumption, 3 for the conditions, with credit for
   recognising that the objection is answerable rather than simply wrong.)

8. Most likely missing: **positive interdependence** - a task the group can complete without every
   member, so the confident pupils rationally do it all; and **individual accountability** - no
   pupil is ever asked to answer for their own learning, so there is no cost to contributing
   nothing. The tell is in her own words: "the clever ones do everything" describes an outcome that
   is only possible when the task permits it and nothing checks the individual. Likely also absent:
   **social skills taught**, since "ask, do not tell" has to be taught before a confident pupil
   stops supplying answers. (2 marks per condition identified with reasoning; 1 for reading the
   evidence out of her description.)

9. It will overrun badly and probably collapse: the pupils will spend the lesson learning the
   procedures rather than the content, and the teacher will conclude that integration is impossible.
   The mishandled decision is **Decision 4** - the structure choice, where the rule is **one**
   structure per lesson - with the overloading failure mode from Topic 3.3. The remedy is the
   ceiling: one cooperative structure, two or three differentiated routes, two product formats.
   (2 marks for the predicted outcome, 2 for naming the decision, 1 for the ceiling.)

10. Teacher A has **moved the objective**: her lowest group is doing different, easier mathematics
    and will end the lesson knowing less. That is a modification, and if repeated it is a reduced
    curriculum. Teacher B has **held the objective and changed the process**: the same fifteen
    problems, with a scaffold - a worked example - that can be withdrawn as it stops being needed.
    Only Teacher B is differentiating. The distinction is not the amount of help but whether the
    destination moved. (2 marks for Teacher A's diagnosis, 2 for Teacher B's, 1 for naming the
    destination as the criterion.)

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked in four bands. Award the band that best fits the whole response; a response
need not meet every descriptor in a band.

**ERQ 1 - the homework pile as diagnosis**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** Four patterns treated as evidence rather than as pupil deficits. Each barrier
  is plausibly inferred and correctly located in content, process or product, with the location
  argued rather than asserted. Variations are specific and preparable. The conclusion recognises
  that the pile describes the **lesson**, not the class - that thirty-two pupils received a lesson
  addressed to somebody else - and says so without blaming Miss Rubina.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** Four patterns analysed, most barriers sound and correctly located, variations
  workable. The conclusion makes a point about the lesson rather than only about the pupils.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** Patterns described rather than diagnosed, or barriers given as deficits ("weak
  pupils", "lazy"). Locations are asserted or often wrong. Variations are generic. The conclusion
  is about which pupils need help rather than about the lesson.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** The pile is restated. Pupils are sorted into strong and weak. No use of the
  content/process/product framework.

**ERQ 2 - evaluating the large-class objection**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** The objection is taken seriously and its legitimate core conceded. At least
  two structures are named with their actual mechanics in a fixed-bench room, including timings or
  the teacher's words. The answer distinguishes what large classes genuinely prevent (floor
  movement, materials per pupil, individual attention) from what they do not (pair talk, called
  numbers). A clear judgement is reached and defended, with evidence used carefully - citing
  Vernon et al. (2020) for the structure-is-the-intervention point and Serman and Ningrum (2026)
  as illustration rather than proof.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** The objection is addressed, two structures named and shown to be workable, and
  a defended judgement reached. Evidence may be used loosely.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** The claim is rejected by assertion or accepted uncritically. Structures are
  named but not shown working in a crowded room. No real engagement with the resource objection,
  or alternatively total surrender to it.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** General remarks about group work. No structures, no judgement, or a judgement
  with no support.

**ERQ 3 - design an inclusive lesson**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** All six decisions present and in order. The objective is precise, single and
  identical for every pupil. The barrier table names real pupils and real obstacles rather than
  deficits. Every adaptation traces to a named barrier and is correctly located. One cooperative
  structure, chosen for a stated reason, with interdependence and accountability described
  operationally. One set of criteria genuinely serves every product format. The Decision 6 check is
  done pupil by pupil, with any modification identified and justified. The costing is honest and
  the lesson teachable in the stated time in a real Pakistani classroom.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** Six decisions present, objective sound and held for all pupils, barrier table
  mostly specific, most adaptations traceable, one structure with accountability addressed, shared
  criteria, check performed, costing present.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** Objective vague or drifting for some pupils. Barrier table lists deficits.
  Adaptations good but unconnected to named pupils, or generic. The cooperative element is group
  work without interdependence or accountability. Separate criteria per format, so the standard
  cannot be shown constant. Probably not teachable in the time.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** No usable objective, or a reduced objective for pupils who struggle. No barrier
  table. The three strategies appear as labels rather than decisions.

**ERQ 4 - analysing the shorter-paper policy**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** Correctly identifies the policy as a blanket **modification** rather than a
  set of accommodations, and explains precisely why: it changes what is assessed and the standard,
  for a whole category of pupils, without reference to any individual barrier. Identifies who is
  protected - the school's pass statistics, and teachers from difficult individual decisions - and
  who is harmed: the pupils, whose attainment is capped by the policy rather than their ability,
  and particularly those whose barrier was access and who needed only an accommodation. Shows the
  timescale: invisible or even flattering in the short term, catastrophic at the first external
  examination that offers no shortened paper. Proposes the accommodation-based alternative.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** Identifies it as a modification with sound reasoning, names beneficiaries and
  those harmed, and notes the external-examination consequence.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** Treats the policy as a kindness with some drawbacks, or calls it an
  accommodation. Harm is noted vaguely. No timescale.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** Endorses the policy as inclusive, or rejects it without using the
  accommodation/modification distinction.

**ERQ 5 - evaluating the cost claim**

- **Band 4 (9-10).** Tests the claim rather than accepting or dismissing it. Identifies what makes
  the cost fall - reusable materials, the barrier table changing little within a class, the
  planning habit becoming automatic - and what makes it rise: a new class, a new subject, a
  syllabus change, or trying to integrate everything at once. Reaches a defensible judgement on
  sustainability that distinguishes the first term from the steady state. Advice for the first term
  is concrete and modest, for example one differentiated lesson per week in one class rather than
  everything everywhere, with a reason.
- **Band 3 (7-8).** Evaluates the claim with reasons on both sides, reaches a judgement, and gives
  workable first-term advice.
- **Band 2 (4-6).** Agrees or disagrees with little analysis. Advice is encouragement rather than a
  plan. No distinction between first-time and later cost.
- **Band 1 (1-3).** Asserts that teachers have no time, or that the claim is obviously right, with
  no engagement with what drives the cost.
