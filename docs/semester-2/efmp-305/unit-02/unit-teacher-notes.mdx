## Unit 2 - Teacher Notes

### Purpose of this unit

This unit moves from the conceptual foundations of Unit 1 to the practical work of seeing and
responding to diversity in the classroom. Trainees learn to name what they see (categories of
diversity), to gather information before labelling (observation and assessment), and to build
a classroom culture that respects difference. The unit is essential preparation for the
teaching strategies in Unit 3 and the classroom environment work in Unit 4.

### Sequencing

Topic 2.1 (categories of diversity) should come first. Trainees need the vocabulary before
they can practise identification or discuss stigma. Topic 2.2 (identification and respect)
builds on the categories and applies them to the teacher's daily work.

| Session | Activity |
|---|---|
| Session 1 | Introduce with the classroom situation from Topic 2.1. Work through the three categories. Set the Sort and examine activity. |
| Session 2 | Debrief the activity. Address the impairment-disability-handicap distinction explicitly. Introduce Topic 2.2. |
| Session 3 | Work through observation and assessment techniques. Use the What is the label doing? activity as group work. Debrief. |
| Session 4 | Address the labelling cycle and respect practices. Ask trainees to rewrite a real report-card comment. Set the unit assessment. |

### Teaching strategies

1. **Use real, local examples.** The categories are abstract until trainees connect them to
   pupils they have actually taught or observed. Ask them to describe a pupil they have found
   difficult to teach, then use the categories to name what kind of diversity might be
   involved.

2. **Distinguish before you judge.** The impairment-disability-handicap distinction is the
   most important conceptual move in this unit. Give trainees several scenarios and ask them
   to sort the impairment, the disability and the handicap in each one. Repeat until it is
   automatic.

3. **Role-play the labelling cycle.** Have one trainee play a teacher attaching a label and
   another play the pupil internalising it. The rest of the class observes and then discusses
   what happened. Experiencing the cycle is more memorable than reading it.

4. **Practicum-based observation.** The "Try this at your practicum school" tasks are the
   bridge between theory and practice. Trainees should complete at least one observation and
   report back.

### Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

| Misconception | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| "Diversity means disability." | Point to the three categories. Ask trainees to name a pupil whose difficulty is social, not disability-related. |
| "I need to diagnose before I can help." | Emphasise observation and informal assessment. The teacher's job is to identify and support, not to diagnose. |
| "Labelling is just being honest." | Use the labelling cycle activity. Show that labels are not neutral descriptions; they shape outcomes. |
| "Treating everyone the same is fair." | Revisit the equality-equity distinction from Unit 1. Fairness is giving each pupil what they need. |

### Common trainee difficulties

- **Sorting impairment from disability from handicap.** Trainees confuse these because
  everyday language uses them as synonyms. The "who adjusts?" question helps: if the child
  must adjust, it is a handicap; if the teacher must adjust, it is a disability they are
  responding to; the impairment is what a doctor would name.

- **Confusing learning difficulty with low intelligence.** Emphasise that dyslexia and
  dyscalculia occur across the intelligence range. A pupil of high intelligence can have a
  specific reading difficulty.

- **Thinking stigma is only about disability.** Stigma attaches to any difference: poverty,
  language, caste, gender. Broadening the concept helps trainees see the full picture.
