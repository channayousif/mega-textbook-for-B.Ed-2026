## Unit summary

This unit built two connected ideas: **learner diversity is wide and every classroom contains
it**, and **the way teachers respond to diversity either builds respect or reinforces stigma**.

- Topic 2.1 gave the categories: disability (sensory, physical, intellectual), learning
  difficulty (dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD) and social difference (language, gender, poverty,
  ethnicity). It distinguished impairment (the condition), disability (the limitation) and
  handicap (the disadvantage the environment creates).
- Topic 2.2 gave the identification tools (observation, informal assessment) and examined
  the labelling cycle: how a label becomes self-fulfilling and how respect breaks the cycle.

Together these give you a usable professional habit: when you meet a pupil who struggles,
you observe before you label, you distinguish the handicap from the disability, and you
change the environment before you change the child.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following is a sensory disability?
   - A) Dyslexia
   - B) Visual impairment
   - C) ADHD
   - D) Poverty

2. *(Remember)* What does the term "handicap" describe?
   - A) The medical condition itself
   - B) The social disadvantage created by an unaccommodating environment
   - C) The pupil's general intelligence
   - D) The teacher's attitude

3. *(Understand)* A pupil who speaks Pashto at home struggles in an Urdu-medium school. Which
   category of diversity does this most show?
   - A) Sensory disability
   - B) Physical disability
   - C) Social difference
   - D) Learning difficulty

4. *(Understand)* What is the first step in the labelling cycle described in this unit?
   - A) The pupil internalises the label
   - B) The teacher attaches a label to the pupil
   - C) The pupil's performance drops
   - D) The peers tease the pupil

5. *(Apply)* A teacher notices that a pupil reads well when seated near the board but struggles
   at the back. What is the most likely explanation?
   - A) The pupil is lazy
   - B) The pupil may have a visual impairment
   - C) The pupil has an intellectual disability
   - D) The pupil is not intelligent

6. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to identify whether a pupil's reading difficulty is dyslexia or
   simply a lack of practice. Which action is most appropriate for the teacher to take first?
   - A) Refer the pupil for clinical diagnosis
   - B) Observe the pupil's reading behaviour and try targeted support
   - C) Label the pupil as "dyslexic" and move on
   - D) Tell the parent the pupil is "slow"

7. *(Analyse)* A report card says "Saima is a weak pupil who cannot learn science." Analyse
   this statement using the concepts from this unit. What is the main problem with it?
   - A) It is too short
   - B) It uses a fixed label and gives no specific information about what Saima can do
   - C) It is too kind
   - D) It mentions science

8. *(Analyse)* A school places all pupils with learning difficulties in one section and uses
   a simplified curriculum. Which principle of inclusion does this most violate?
   - A) All learners participate
   - B) Diversity is not valued
   - C) The child must adapt
   - D) Equality is unfair

9. *(Evaluate)* Which of the following teacher actions best promotes respect for diversity?
   - A) Saying "This class has too many weak pupils this year"
   - B) Using a pupil's medical condition as the main way to describe them
   - C) Pairing a strong reader with a struggling reader for a shared task
   - D) Avoiding calling on pupils who might struggle

10. *(Evaluate)* A head teacher says, "We cannot include because we have no special educator."
    Evaluate this claim using the misconceptions discussed in Unit 1 and the identification
    tools from this unit. Which response is most accurate?
    - A) Agree; without a special educator, nothing can be done
    - B) Disagree; the unit showed that observation and simple adaptations are within every
       teacher's reach
    - C) Agree; only a specialist can identify learner diversity
    - D) Disagree; diversity does not exist in this school

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question concisely.

1. *(Remember)* Name three categories of learner diversity and give one example of each.

2. *(Remember)* List two observation techniques a teacher can use to identify a pupil's
   needs without formal testing.

3. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between impairment, disability and handicap. Give a
   brief example.

4. *(Understand)* Describe two signs that a pupil in your classroom might have dyslexia.

5. *(Apply)* A girl in Grade 4 has started refusing to read aloud. She reads silently at
   grade level when asked privately. Using the categories from this topic, name two possible
   explanations and one action you would take for each.

6. *(Apply)* A teacher writes "weak in Maths" on a pupil's report card. Rewrite this comment
   to be specific, avoid labelling, and give the parent something actionable.

7. *(Analyse)* A pupil is labelled "naughty" in Grade 2. By Grade 5, he has been excluded
   from two schools. Using the labelling cycle, explain how the label may have contributed
   to his exclusion.

8. *(Analyse)* A government school in rural Sindh has 60 pupils per class, one textbook and
   no screening for vision or hearing. Analyse one barrier this creates for identifying learner
   diversity.

9. *(Evaluate)* A teacher says, "I treat all my pupils the same. That is fairness." Evaluate
   this statement using the equality-equity distinction from Unit 1.

10. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the statement: "If a pupil is not diagnosed, they do not have a
    learning difficulty." Give two reasons why this is incorrect.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a well-structured response of 150-250 words.

1. *(Analyse)* A boy in Grade 3 is disruptive, restless and cannot finish written work in the
   time given. Some teachers call him "naughty"; one suggests he may have ADHD. Analyse this
   situation using the concepts from both topics in this unit. In your response:
   - name two possible categories of diversity that might apply
   - explain why "naughty" is a label, not an explanation
   - describe two observation techniques the teacher could use before referring the pupil

2. *(Evaluate)* A school decides to screen every pupil for vision and hearing at the start of
   the year. Evaluate this decision using the identification approaches from this unit. In
   your response:
   - explain why screening is useful
   - name one limitation of screening
   - describe what the school should do with the results

3. *(Create)* You are asked to lead a 30-minute session with your colleagues on "How to see
   learner diversity in our school." Prepare an outline of the session. In your outline:
   - state one clear objective
   - describe one activity that helps colleagues see diversity in their own classrooms
   - give one takeaway message for each colleague to use

4. *(Analyse)* Compare the experience of two pupils: a boy with a physical disability in a
   school with ramps and accessible toilets, and the same boy in a school with steps and
   no accessible facilities. Using the impairment-disability-handicap distinction, analyse
   how the same impairment leads to different outcomes.

5. *(Evaluate)* "In Pakistan, we do not have the resources to identify every pupil's needs.
   Teachers should focus on the average pupil and let specialists handle the rest." Evaluate
   this claim. In your response:
   - identify the misconception it represents (drawing on Unit 1)
   - give two arguments from this unit that challenge the claim
   - suggest one practical, low-cost step a teacher could take tomorrow

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Any three of: disability (e.g. visual impairment), learning difficulty (e.g. dyslexia),
   social difference (e.g. language mismatch). (1 mark per category + example, max 3.)

2. Any two of: observing what a pupil can do independently; asking the pupil to explain
   their reasoning; noting when the pupil struggles most (time of day, task type, seating
   position). (1 mark each.)

3. Impairment is the medical condition (e.g. hearing loss); disability is the functional
   limitation (cannot hear the teacher); handicap is the social disadvantage if the
   environment does not accommodate (excluded from learning). Example: a pupil with hearing
   loss seated at the back of a noisy classroom is handicapped; seated near the front, the
   handicap is reduced. (2 marks for the distinction, 1 mark for the example.)

4. Two of: reversing letters; reading very slowly; unable to sound out unfamiliar words;
   reading comprehension below listening comprehension; avoiding reading tasks. (1 mark
   each.)

5. Possible explanations: social anxiety (social difference); dyslexia (learning difficulty);
   bullying or shaming by peers (social difference). Actions: observe when the difficulty
   occurs; try offering oral response as an alternative; check reading in a quiet setting.
   (1 mark per explanation, 1 mark per action, max 3.)

6. Rewrite: "Your child understands the number concepts in Maths but needs more practice
   with written calculations, especially under time pressure. Practicing five problems a
   evening at home with a timer will help build speed." (1 mark for specificity, 1 mark for
   avoiding the label, 1 mark for actionability.)

7. The label "naughty" lowered teacher expectations; the pupil internalised it; his
   behaviour confirmed it; each school saw the label rather than the child; the cycle
   repeated and escalated to exclusion. (2 marks for the cycle, 1 mark for linking to
   exclusion.)

8. Barrier: without vision or hearing screening, a pupil whose real difficulty is sensory
   may be labelled "slow" or "weak"; the school addresses the wrong problem. (2 marks for
   the barrier, 1 mark for the consequence.)

9. Treating everyone the same is equality, not equity. Fairness means giving each pupil
   what they need to succeed. A pupil with a visual impairment needs to sit near the front;
   treating her the same as everyone else leaves her handicapped. (2 marks for the
   distinction, 1 mark for the evaluation.)

10. The statement is incorrect because: many pupils with learning difficulties are never
    diagnosed, especially in resource-limited settings; a teacher's observation can identify
    a need without a clinical label; waiting for a diagnosis before acting leaves the pupil
    unsupported. (2 marks per reason.)

| Criterion | 0 | 1-2 | 3-4 | 5 |

| *Concept use* | No relevant terms | Uses 1-2 terms loosely | Uses 3+ terms correctly and connects them to the scenario | Uses 3+ terms precisely, connects them, and shows nuance |
| *Reasoning* | No reasoning | Some reasoning, partially grounded | Clear reasoning with specific examples | Strong reasoning with specific examples and addresses complexity |
| *Structure* | Unstructured | Some structure, may be over/under length | Well-structured, within word band | Well-structured, within word band, clearly signposted |
