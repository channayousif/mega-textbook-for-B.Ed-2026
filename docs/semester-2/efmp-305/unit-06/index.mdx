# Collaboration and Parental Involvement

Unit 5 left you with four things that move a case like Bilal's, and the news that three of them are
free. It also left something out.

Every one of those four things needs another person to agree to it. The accurate citation has to be
made to a head teacher. The written request has to be read by somebody at the taluka office. The
audit has to be believed. And the one decision a teacher changes this week changes the life of a
child whose mother has never been inside the school building, who is raised day to day by her
grandmother because her father is working in Karachi, and whose village has opinions about
disability that no Act of the Provincial Assembly has touched.

This unit is about those people. It is the last unit of the course, and it is the one that decides
whether the other five amount to anything.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Describe a supportive classroom culture in terms of what reliably happens in the room, and name
   the four markers an observer can actually check.
2. Explain why empathy is a teachable skill rather than a fixed trait, and identify the lever a
   teacher controls, with honest reference to what the evidence does and does not show.
3. Apply a first-contact protocol to a parent who cannot read a note home, cannot travel to the
   school, or has never been invited except to hear about a problem.
4. Produce the four questions that get a parent's knowledge of her own child into your teaching,
   and record the answers in a form that survives your transfer.
5. Apply shared planning to a specialist who visits once a month or not at all, and distinguish a
   shared working support plan from both a legal artefact and a handover.
6. Analyze a real barrier in a Pakistani school by level, by who holds it, and by what it costs,
   and separate the part that is yours from the part you escalate in writing.
7. Create a peer-support scheme for a class of sixty or eighty that is free, trainable in fifteen
   minutes, and checkable at the end of a term.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This is the closing unit and it draws on all five before it rather than repeating them.

- From **Unit 1** you have inclusion as a process of removing barriers to presence, participation
  and achievement, and the distinction between integration and inclusion. Topic 6.3 uses that
  distinction to test whether a peer-support scheme includes a child or merely parks her.
- From **Unit 2** you have stigma and labelling. Topic 6.1 needs them because a classroom culture
  is exactly where stigma either operates or stops, and Topic 6.3 needs them because the stigma a
  Pakistani family meets is often inside its own community, not inside the school.
- From **Unit 3** you have cooperative learning. Topic 6.3 argues that in a class of eighty it
  stops being a good idea and becomes the only mechanism that works.
- From **Unit 4** you have the participation tally and the accessibility walk. This unit reuses
  both as evidence-collecting tools, pointed at new questions.
- From **Unit 5** you have the four-level gap analysis, the attitude-action cycle, and the written
  escalation that names a pupil and a clause. Topic 6.3 is built on the first of those and Topic
  6.2 on the last.

If one of those is thin, read the relevant topic again before you start. This unit will not reteach
them.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Two warnings specific to this unit.

**The first is about tone.** A unit on collaboration can easily become a sermon about caring more.
Caring more is not a skill and cannot be assessed. Everything taught here is a routine, a protocol,
a form of words or a design, and all of it is meant to be done by a tired teacher at the end of a
long day in a room with sixty children in it. If a passage ever reads as though it is asking you to
be a better person rather than to do a specific thing, you have misread it, or it is badly written.

**The second is about the parent meeting.** Topic 6.2 contains the one piece of practice in this
course that trainees most often perform rather than learn. The role-play in that topic has a brief
for the parent as well as the teacher, and the parent's brief contains a real obstacle. Do not
soften it for each other. A meeting that goes well because both sides were trying to be agreeable
teaches nothing.

One school runs through the unit: Government Primary School in Goth Allah Dino, a village in taluka
Johi, district Dadu. Four teachers, around two hundred and thirty children on the register, Grades
1 to 5. You will spend most of your time in Miss Hameeda's Grade 4, with Sahiba, who has a hearing
loss that nobody at the school has ever asked a question about. Topic 6.3 moves to a town school in
Qasimabad, Hyderabad, where Master Imtiaz has eighty-one boys in Grade 3 and one entirely
reasonable objection to everything you have read so far.

The "Check your understanding" items ask you to produce an answer rather than recognise one. The
unit ends with a 10/10/5 assessment, and its last extended-response question is about the whole
course rather than this unit.
