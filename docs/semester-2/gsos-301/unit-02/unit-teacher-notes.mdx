## Teaching strategies for Unit 2

### Opening the unit

This unit introduces two concepts that are both familiar and unfamiliar to trainees. Everyone has culture and has been socialized, but few have thought about these processes sociologically. Use the opening vignette (Topic 2.1) to surface the problem: cultural judgments happen in every classroom, and teachers need the vocabulary to address them.

### Topic 2.1 - What culture is

**Key pedagogical move:** Make the abstract concrete. Use the "Mapping a cultural practice" activity early. Trainees will struggle most with the distinction between values and beliefs - values are what we hold important; beliefs are what we hold to be true. Use the mehndi example from the topic to illustrate.

**Likely wrong answer:** "Ethnocentrism is just another word for racism." Correct by distinguishing: ethnocentrism is a broader tendency to judge by one's own cultural standards; racism is specifically about racial hierarchy. Ethnocentrism can operate within a single racial or ethnic group (one province looking down on another).

**Good discussion prompt:** "Think of a time someone judged your cultural practice as 'strange' or 'backward'. How did it feel? What would cultural relativism have required of them?"

### Topic 2.2 - How society makes us who we are

**Key pedagogical move:** Use the "Socialization map" activity as the main learning event. Trainees will find it easier to understand agents of socialization through their own experience than through abstract description. The activity also builds empathy for pupils whose socialization differs from the trainee's own.

**Likely wrong answer:** "Family is the only agent that really matters because children spend most time with family." Correct by explaining: family is first and powerful, but school, peers, media, and religion all contribute, and their influence grows as children age. A pupil who spends six hours a day in school is being socialized by that environment regardless of family influence.

**Good discussion prompt:** "Which agent of socialization has changed most in Pakistan in the last twenty years, and why?" (Most will name media - this opens a discussion about how socialization is itself shaped by social change.)

## Sequencing and pacing

The unit is designed for approximately 5-6 hours of contact time:

| Topic | Contact hours | Main activity |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 What culture is | 2-3 hours | "Mapping a cultural practice" pairs activity |
| 2.2 How society makes us who we are | 2-3 hours | "Socialization map" individual + pairs activity |

The unit-assessment is designed for independent study or a supervised examination session.

## Practical work and assignments

1. **Cultural observation (individual):** Each trainee observes one cultural practice in their community and writes a one-page analysis using the four elements of culture. This develops the analytical skill Topic 2.1 introduces.

2. **Socialization timeline (individual):** Each trainee draws a timeline of their own socialization, marking key moments where each of the five agents was influential. This deepens Topic 2.2.

3. **Group discussion:** Small groups discuss a case of conflicting socialization (for example, a girl whose family wants early marriage but whose teacher encourages further education). Groups present their analysis to the class.

## Handling trainee misconceptions

1. **"Culture is just tradition."** Reframe: culture includes tradition but also language, values, norms, symbols, and material objects. It is the whole way of life, not just old customs.

2. **"Socialization ends when you grow up."** Reframe: socialization is lifelong. Starting a new job, getting married, becoming a parent, and migrating all involve learning new roles and norms.

3. **"Cultural relativism means accepting everything."** Reframe: cultural relativism is a method of understanding, not a moral stance. It means understanding a practice in context before making a judgment, not abandoning all standards.
