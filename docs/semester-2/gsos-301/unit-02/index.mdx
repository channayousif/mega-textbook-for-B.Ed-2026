# Culture and Socialization

Every society has a way of life that its members share: the language they speak, the values they hold, the norms they follow, the beliefs that guide them. Sociology calls this shared way of life **culture**. Culture is not something we are born with fully formed; it is something we learn through a lifelong process called **socialization**. This unit examines both ideas and shows why they matter for a teacher working in Pakistan's diverse classrooms.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Define culture and identify its key elements: norms, values, beliefs, and symbols.
2. Distinguish ethnocentrism from cultural relativism and explain why the distinction matters in a diverse society.
3. Name the major agents of socialization and describe what each contributes to personality development.
4. Explain the main stages of socialization and how they apply across the life course.
5. Analyse a described classroom or community situation using the concepts of culture and socialization.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 (Introduction to Sociology and Sociological Perspectives) is recommended but not required. The concepts of social structure and sociological imagination from Unit 1 will help, but this unit can be read independently.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your understanding" items are retrieval practice - try them without looking back, then check your reasoning. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws the two topics together.
