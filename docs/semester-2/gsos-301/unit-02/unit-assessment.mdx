## Unit summary

This unit built two connected ideas: **culture is the shared way of life of a group, and socialization is the process by which individuals learn it.**

- Topic 2.1 defined culture and its elements (norms, values, beliefs, symbols), explained cultural diversity, and distinguished ethnocentrism from cultural relativism.
- Topic 2.2 explained the five agents of socialization (family, school, peers, media, religion), their role in personality development, and the stages of socialization from preparatory through adult.

Together these give you a framework for understanding how pupils bring the cultures they have learned into the classroom, and how schools and other agents continue to shape who those pupils become.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following is one of the four key elements of culture as described in this unit?
   - A) Technology
   - B) Norms
   - C) Geography
   - D) Biology

2. *(Remember)* Which agent of socialization is usually the first and most influential in a child's life?
   - A) Media
   - B) School
   - C) Family
   - D) Peer group

3. *(Understand)* A Pakistani teacher says, "Children from our village are naturally more hardworking than city children." Which concept does that statement most show?
   - A) Cultural relativism
   - B) Ethnocentrism
   - C) Cultural universal
   - D) Socialization

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following is an example of a symbol in the sociological sense used in this unit?
   - A) The weight of a school bag
   - B) The crescent on Pakistan's flag
   - C) The height of a school building
   - D) The temperature of a classroom

5. *(Apply)* A school in Karachi celebrates Sindhi Cultural Day with music, dress, and food from Sindh. This is an example of:
   - A) Ethnocentrism
   - B) Cultural diversity in practice
   - C) Media socialization only
   - D) Preparatory stage of socialization

6. *(Apply)* A child plays "teacher" at home, correcting her younger brother using the phrases her teacher uses. Mead would place this in the:
   - A) Preparatory stage
   - B) Play stage
   - C) Game stage
   - D) Adult socialization stage

7. *(Apply)* Which pair correctly matches an agent of socialization to what it transmits?
   - A) Family - only academic knowledge
   - B) School - only entertainment
   - C) Peer group - belonging and identity
   - D) Media - only religious teaching

8. *(Apply)* Cultural relativism means:
   - A) Judging every culture as equal in value
   - B) Understanding a culture on its own terms before making judgments
   - C) Believing your own culture is the best
   - D) Refusing to study other cultures

9. *(Apply)* A teacher notices that a pupil behaves differently at school than his peers say he behaves in his neighbourhood. Which sociological idea best explains this?
   - A) Culture is the same everywhere
   - B) Different agents of socialization may teach different norms
   - C) Personality is fixed at birth
   - D) Media socialization overrides all other agents

10. *(Apply)* A girl learns to cook from her mother, learns to read at school, and learns about fashion from social media. This shows:
    - A) Only one agent of socialization can work at a time
    - B) Multiple agents of socialization work simultaneously
    - C) Socialization ends in early childhood
    - D) Family is the only agent that matters

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between ethnocentrism and cultural relativism, using the idea of "judging by one's own standards".

2. *(Remember)* Name the four elements of culture and give one example of each.

3. *(Apply)* A teacher in Quetta says, "I treat all my pupils the same - I don't see their ethnicity or language." Is this cultural relativism? Explain in one or two sentences.

4. *(Apply)* Describe one thing a child learns from the family as an agent of socialization and one thing learned from the peer agent that may conflict.

5. *(Analyze)* A boy is well-behaved at home and at mosque but aggressive with his school peers. Name at least two agents of socialization at work and explain what each may be teaching in two or three sentences.

6. *(Apply)* Name Mead's three childhood stages of socialization in order and give one characteristic of each.

7. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between material culture and non-material culture, using examples from a Pakistani school.

8. *(Apply)* A family in rural Punjab wants their daughter to leave school after class 5. The school says education is her right. Analyse this situation using the concept of conflicting agents of socialization in two sentences.

9. *(Analyze)* A teacher assumes that a quiet pupil is "shy by nature". What would a sociological view of personality say about that assumption in two sentences?

10. *(Apply)* Give one example of how media socialization might conflict with family socialization for a teenager in Pakistan.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (100-150 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* A government school in Karachi has pupils from five different linguistic communities. Analyse this situation using the concepts of cultural diversity and ethnocentrism. Explain what cultural relativism would require of the teacher and why this matters for classroom practice.

2. *(Evaluate)* A teacher says, "Parents who don't value education are the reason their children fail." Evaluate this claim using the concept of socialization agents. What agents beyond the family may be at work, and what is the teacher overlooking?

3. *(Analyze)* A girl in a small town in Sindh watches social media influencers who promote independence and career ambition, but her family expects her early marriage and domestic life. Analyse this situation using the concepts of agents of socialization and the stages of socialization. Which stage is she likely in, and how are the conflicting messages affecting her?

4. *(Evaluate)* "Culture is just tradition. It doesn't affect how pupils learn." Evaluate this claim using evidence from both topics in this unit. Give a specific classroom example.

5. *(Create/Analyse)* Describe a realistic situation in a Pakistani school where a pupil's behaviour reflects the influence of at least two different agents of socialization. Analyse the situation using the concepts of culture and socialization, then suggest what a culturally aware teacher might do.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Norms. Technology, geography, and biology are not among the four key elements named in this unit.
2. **C** - Family. It is the first agent and usually the most influential. The others come later or are less foundational.
3. **B** - Ethnocentrism: the teacher is judging village children by a standard that flatters his own group. Cultural relativism would suspend such judgment.
4. **B** - The crescent on the flag: it carries cultural meaning recognized by those who share Pakistani culture. The others are physical facts, not symbols.
5. **B** - Cultural diversity in practice: the school is making space for one of Pakistan's many cultures. A would be claiming one culture is best; C and D are wrong.
6. **B** - Play stage: she is taking on the role of "teacher" she has observed. A would be imitation without understanding of the role; C would require coordinating multiple roles.
7. **C** - Peer group transmits belonging and identity. A is wrong (family teaches much more than academics); B is wrong (school teaches more than entertainment); D is wrong (media is broader than religion).
8. **B** - Cultural relativism is understanding before judging. A is wrong (relativism does not mean all cultures are equal in every way); C is ethnocentrism; D is the opposite of what the concept requires.
9. **B** - Different agents may teach different norms. The family and mosque may teach restraint; the school peers may reward aggression. A is wrong; C is a biological view, not sociological; D overstates media's role.
10. **B** - Multiple agents work simultaneously. A, C, and D are contradicted by the scenario and the unit's teaching.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Ethnocentrism judges another culture by the standards of one's own; cultural relativism requires understanding a culture on its own terms before making any judgment. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the own-standards idea for ethnocentrism; 1 - names the own-terms idea for cultural relativism.

2. **Model:** Norms (rules of behaviour), Values (shared ideals), Beliefs (accepted truths), Symbols (meaningful objects/gestures). **Marks (4):** 1 for each correct element with a recognisable example.

3. **Model:** Not necessarily. Cultural relativism requires understanding the specific cultures in the classroom, not pretending they do not exist. Treating everyone "the same" can mean ignoring real cultural differences. **Marks (2):** 1 - distinguishes "same treatment" from cultural relativism; 1 - explains why ignoring difference is not relativism.

4. **Model:** Family teaches obedience to elders; peer group may reward independence or defiance of adult norms. These can conflict when a child is torn between family expectations and peer pressure. **Marks (2):** 1 - names what each agent teaches; 1 - explains the conflict.

5. **Model:** Family/mosque may teach respect, obedience, and restraint. School peers may reward toughness or aggression as a way of gaining status. The boy has learned different norms from each agent. **Marks (3):** 1 - names two agents; 1 - explains what each teaches; 1 - ties it to the scenario.

6. **Model:** Preparatory stage (imitation without understanding), Play stage (taking on specific roles), Game stage (coordinating multiple roles and rules). **Marks (3):** 1 per stage with a characteristic.

7. **Model:** Material culture is physical objects (textbooks, uniforms, buildings); non-material culture is intangible aspects (values, norms, beliefs, the expectation that pupils stand when the teacher enters). **Marks (2):** 1 - defines both; 1 - gives a school example of each.

8. **Model:** Family is socializing the girl into the role of daughter-to-be-married; school is socializing her into the role of educated citizen. The two agents are transmitting different values. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the conflict; 1 - names the agents involved.

9. **Model:** A sociological view says personality is shaped by social forces, not just biology. The pupil's quietness may reflect socialization (family norms about girls' speech, school norms about obedience), not a fixed nature. **Marks (2):** 1 - challenges the "nature" assumption; 1 - names a socializing force.

10. **Model:** Media may promote individual career ambition and romantic choice in marriage; family may prioritize family honor and arranged marriage. A teenager who follows media models may clash with family expectations. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the conflict; 1 - gives a concrete example.
