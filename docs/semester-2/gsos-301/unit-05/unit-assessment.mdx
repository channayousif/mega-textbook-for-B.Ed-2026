## Unit summary

A social institution is a lasting pattern of norms, roles and values that meets a basic need of society. It is wider than any building or single organisation.

- **Family** raises and supports children and passes on identity. Its form, joint or nuclear, changes with housing, work and income.
- **Education** transmits knowledge, sorts people through examinations and certificates, and teaches a hidden curriculum. Its manifest and latent functions do not always match.
- **Religion** gives meaning, moral guidance and community. Sociology studies these functions without judging belief.
- **The economy** organises production and distribution. Informal, insecure work leaves many families exposed to sudden loss of income.
- **Politics** makes shared decisions and distributes power. Authority is accepted power, and Weber described traditional, charismatic and legal-rational types.
- **Healthcare** prevents and treats illness, but health is also shaped by income, schooling, family decisions and trust.

The thread through the unit is interdependence: a change in one institution moves the others, so a single-institution fix rarely solves a social problem. Institutions also change over time, as the spread of low-fee private schooling shows, when several institutions push in the same direction.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* What is a social institution?
   - A) A large building where official work is done
   - B) A lasting pattern of norms, roles and values that meets a basic need of society
   - C) A single organisation with a clear chain of command
   - D) A group of friends who meet regularly

2. *(Understand)* A trainee says, "The District Education Office is the institution of education." What is the best correction?
   - A) The office is an organisation inside the wider institution of education
   - B) The office is an institution because it has a building
   - C) The office belongs to politics, so it has nothing to do with education
   - D) The office is an institution only if it has more than one hundred staff

3. *(Remember)* Which is a **latent** function of schooling?
   - A) Teaching pupils to read and write
   - B) Awarding a certificate after the board examination
   - C) Helping pupils form friendships and social networks
   - D) Teaching the national curriculum in science

4. *(Apply)* In a mohalla in Hyderabad, a grandmother minds the children so that her son and daughter-in-law can both work, and the household pools three incomes. What does this example best show?
   - A) The joint family is the only natural family form
   - B) A family form can meet needs such as childcare and income in a particular way
   - C) Family and economy are unconnected institutions
   - D) Nuclear families cannot provide childcare

5. *(Apply)* Neighbours share sacrificial meat at Eid ul Adha with relatives and the poor. A sociologist would most likely describe this as religion performing which function?
   - A) Proving that one belief is true
   - B) Building social cohesion and a norm of sharing
   - C) Sorting people into future jobs
   - D) Producing goods for the market

6. *(Remember)* According to Weber, what is authority?
   - A) Any use of force
   - B) Power that people accept as rightful
   - C) Wealth held by a family over generations
   - D) The number of votes a party receives

7. *(Apply)* A Deputy Commissioner's orders are obeyed because the post was created and filled under law. Which type of authority is this?
   - A) Traditional
   - B) Charismatic
   - C) Legal-rational
   - D) Informal

8. *(Understand)* Which statement best explains why daily-wage workers are hit hard when a mill closes?
   - A) Informal, unprotected work leaves them with little to fall back on
   - B) They prefer to change jobs often
   - C) The government pays all wages during a closure
   - D) Health services close whenever a mill closes

9. *(Apply)* Mothers in a village stop bringing babies for vaccination after the bus fare rises. Which pair of institutions does this link most directly?
   - A) Religion and politics
   - B) Economy and healthcare
   - C) Education and religion
   - D) Politics and education

10. *(Apply)* Which of these is an example of **institutional change**?
    - A) One teacher retires and a new teacher replaces her
    - B) Over many years, low-fee private schools spread to small towns and change what parents expect of a school
    - C) A pupil moves from Class 6 to Class 7
    - D) A family buys a new motorbike

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two or three sentences, explain the difference between an institution and an organisation, using one example from Pakistan.

2. *(Understand)* A joint household and a nuclear household live in the same street. Give two reasons, linked to housing, work or income, why they might have taken different forms.

3. *(Understand)* Explain one manifest function and one latent function of the Class 10 board examination.

4. *(Analyze)* Explain, in three or four sentences, how the hidden curriculum of a morning assembly can teach norms about authority. Give one specific routine as evidence.

5. *(Understand)* In two sentences, describe one social function of religion in a Pakistani community without judging whether any belief is true.

6. *(Understand)* Define power and authority, and give one example of each from a Pakistani district.

7. *(Analyze)* A village elder, a popular local leader and a magistrate all influence a land dispute. Classify each one's authority using Weber's three types, and give the reason for each classification.

8. *(Understand)* List three ways the economy can affect a child's schooling.

9. *(Analyze)* "Health is only a medical matter." Analyse this claim in three or four sentences, using two non-medical causes of poor child health.

10. *(Analyze)* Using the Lady Health Worker example, explain how two institutions other than healthcare help the programme to work.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in 150-250 words unless stated otherwise.

1. *(Analyze)* Sana, a Class 4 pupil in Larkana, misses school in the first week of each month. Analyse her absence using family, education and religion. Show at least two connections between these institutions and explain why "her family does not value education" is an incomplete explanation.

2. *(Evaluate)* "The family has always looked the same everywhere." Evaluate this claim. Compare family forms in two different settings, such as a joint household in rural Sindh and a nuclear household in Karachi, or a Pakistani household and one from another culture, and explain what conditions produce each form.

3. *(Analyze)* A textile mill near Kotri closes. Trace the effects through at least four institutions. For each link, name the institution and explain the mechanism.

4. *(Evaluate)* A provincial government plans to build one Basic Health Unit in every union council. Evaluate whether this will improve child health on its own, using institutional interdependence, and recommend two additions to the plan with reasons.

5. *(Create)* Choose one change in a Pakistani institution in your lifetime, such as the spread of private schooling, mobile phone banking or more girls in secondary school. Write an analysis that explains which institutions drove the change, how it has affected at least two other institutions, and what one further effect you predict in the next ten years. Justify your prediction.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you read it.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - The definition rests on a lasting pattern of norms and roles that meets a need. A confuses the institution with a building; C describes one organisation; D describes a friendship group.
2. **A** - The office is one organisation inside the wider pattern of schooling, examinations and expectations. B repeats the building error; C ignores the overlap of politics and education; D invents a size rule.
3. **C** - Friendships and networks are an unintended result of schooling. A, B and D are intended, openly recognised (manifest) functions.
4. **B** - The joint household meets childcare and income needs in a way suited to its conditions. A is the "natural family" misconception; C and D are contradicted by the case.
5. **B** - Sharing builds bonds and sets a norm, which is a social function. A is outside sociology's task; C and D belong to education and the economy.
6. **B** - Weber defined authority as accepted, legitimate power. A describes force; C describes wealth; D describes election results.
7. **C** - Power that rests on laws and official posts is legal-rational. A rests on custom; B rests on personal qualities; D is not one of Weber's types.
8. **A** - Informal work is unregistered and unprotected, so income stops at once. B and C are not true of the case; D is unconnected.
9. **B** - Cost (economy) limits access to vaccination (healthcare). The other pairs are not shown in the case.
10. **B** - A lasting change in norms and expectations across many schools is institutional change. A, C and D are individual events within an unchanged pattern.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** An institution is a wide, lasting pattern of norms and roles that meets a need; an organisation is one body inside it. Healthcare is an institution, while a particular Basic Health Unit is an organisation within it. **Marks (3):** 1 - institution defined; 1 - organisation defined as part of it; 1 - correct Pakistani example.

2. **Model:** The nuclear household may have moved near a new job and could afford only a small rented home. The joint household may own an inherited house and gain from pooled income and shared childcare. **Marks (2):** 1 for each reason linked to housing, work or income.

3. **Model:** Manifest: it measures learning and awards a certificate. Latent: it sorts pupils in ways that often follow family resources, such as private tuition and school quality. **Marks (2):** 1 - manifest function; 1 - latent function with a reason.

4. **Model:** Pupils stand in straight lines, face the head teacher, and stay silent until told to move. This routine teaches that adults in authority speak and pupils obey, and that order matters more than discussion. No lesson states this, so it is a hidden curriculum. **Marks (3):** 1 - names a specific routine; 1 - states the norm it teaches; 1 - explains why it is "hidden".

5. **Model:** Shared Eid prayers bring neighbours together and remind them that they belong to one community. This builds cohesion, whatever view an observer holds about the faith itself. **Marks (2):** 1 - correct function; 1 - concrete example stated without judgement.

6. **Model:** Power is the ability to make others do what you want; authority is power that people accept as rightful. A landlord who threatens tenants uses power; a judge whose ruling is obeyed because of the law has authority. **Marks (3):** 1 - power defined; 1 - authority defined; 1 - suitable examples.

7. **Model:** The elder holds traditional authority, because families follow custom in turning to him. The popular leader holds charismatic authority, resting on personal appeal. The magistrate holds legal-rational authority, resting on law and office. **Marks (3):** 1 per correct classification with its reason.

8. **Model:** Families may be unable to pay fees, uniforms or transport; children may be needed to earn; and families may migrate for work, which breaks schooling. **Marks (3):** 1 per valid way.

9. **Model:** The claim is too narrow. A child may fall ill because the family cannot afford clean water or enough food, which is an economic cause. A mother with little schooling may not know the signs of dehydration, which is an educational cause. Medicine treats disease, but these social causes decide who gets ill and who reaches care. **Marks (3):** 1 - judges the claim; 1 per non-medical cause explained (2).

10. **Model:** Politics funds the programme and decides where workers are posted. The family is where the service is delivered; it works when mothers-in-law and husbands welcome the visits. (Education, which trains the workers, or the economy, which pays them, are also acceptable.) **Marks (2):** 1 per institution with a clear mechanism.
