## Unit summary

This unit introduced the idea of a social institution - a stable pattern of norms, roles, values and expectations that meets a core social need - and examined six institutions in Pakistani society.

- Topic 5.1 covered family, education and religion. Family socializes, supports and gives identity. Education transmits knowledge, sorts people through credentials and teaches norms. Religion provides meaning, moral guidance and social cohesion.
- Topic 5.2 covered economy, politics and healthcare. Economy organizes production, distribution and consumption. Politics makes and enforces collective decisions and distributes power. Healthcare maintains and restores the population's wellbeing.

A central theme runs through both topics: institutions are interdependent. A change in one reshapes the others. The factory that closes, the law that is passed, the new school that opens - each sends ripples across the whole institutional system. Understanding these connections is what gives sociological analysis its power and what makes single-institution solutions to social problems incomplete.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following best defines a social institution?
   - A) A large building that houses government offices
   - B) A stable pattern of norms, roles and expectations that meets a core social need
   - C) A single organization with a clear chain of command
   - D) Any group of people who know each other personally

2. *(Remember)* How many social institutions does this unit examine in detail?
   - A) Three
   - B) Four
   - C) Six
   - D) Eight

3. *(Understand)* A sociologist says that "education sorts people." What does this mean?
   - A) Schools arrange pupils in alphabetical order
   - B) Examinations and credentials channel people into different occupations and social positions
   - C) Teachers choose which pupils they like best
   - D) Pupils sort themselves into groups by ability without any help from the school

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following is the best example of institutional interdependence?
   - A) A pupil studies hard and passes the exam
   - B) A factory closure leads to family stress, increased clinic visits and a local political protest
   - C) A teacher teaches the same lesson every year
   - D) A mosque holds prayers five times a day

5. *(Apply)* A joint family in rural Punjab pools income from farming and a son's job in Dubai. The son sends money home. The family uses it to pay school fees for younger children. Which institutional connection does this example show?
   - A) Economy to education through family
   - B) Religion to politics through healthcare
   - C) Education to religion through economy
   - D) Healthcare to family through politics

6. *(Apply)* A new government programme sends trained women into villages to teach mothers about clean water and vaccination. This programme involves healthcare and which other institution most directly?
   - A) Family - because the workers visit homes and speak to mothers
   - B) Religion - because the workers pray with families
   - C) Economy - because the workers produce goods
   - D) Politics - because the workers run in elections

7. *(Apply)* Which of the following would a sociologist classify as institutional change?
   - A) One teacher retiring and another being hired
   - B) A pupil moving from one school to another
   - C) The growth of private schooling over three decades, reshaping family spending and employment
   - D) A family deciding to have fewer children this year

8. *(Apply)* A widowed mother sends her daughter to a private school on a scholarship but cannot afford transport to the nearby mosque. This situation shows that:
   - A) Institutions are connected and a change in one (economy) affects access to others (education and religion)
   - B) Religion is unimportant to poor families
   - C) Private schools are always better than government schools
   - D) Transport is the only barrier to education

9. *(Apply)* Weber's ideal type of bureaucracy (Unit 3) was used in this unit to explain what aspect of social institutions?
   - A) The way institutions like schools and hospitals are often organized through rules, hierarchy and formal roles
   - B) Why institutions should be replaced by informal networks
   - C) Why religion is more efficient than politics
   - D) How families are organized around a single leader

10. *(Apply)* "Building more government schools will solve Pakistan's education crisis." Why would a sociologist be cautious about this claim?
    - A) Because schools are buildings and buildings are expensive
    - B) Because the education crisis is connected to the economy, family and politics, and schools alone cannot resolve all of those
    - C) Because government schools are always bad
    - D) Because more schools mean more teachers and teachers are hard to find

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Remember)* In one sentence, state what a social institution is, and name the six institutions covered in this unit.

2. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between an institution and a single organization, using one example from the unit.

3. *(Understand)* List two functions of the education institution besides transmitting knowledge.

4. *(Apply)* A family in Karachi is nuclear; a family in rural Sindh is joint. Explain two reasons why these different forms might exist, using economic and social factors.

5. *(Apply)* Explain how religion functions as a social institution in two sentences, using one concrete example from Pakistani society.

6. *(Apply)* A new government increases health funding by 20 per cent. Explain one political decision behind this change and one economic consequence.

7. *(Analyze)* A mobile phone company builds a tower in a remote village in Gilgit-Baltistan. Analyse two ways this economic change might affect another institution (choose from family, education, religion, politics or healthcare).

8. *(Analyze)* "The Lady Health Worker programme is a healthcare programme, not a political one." Analyse why this claim underestimates the role of politics in the programme.

9. *(Apply)* Distinguish between the manifest function and a latent function of a school examination, using the unit's framework.

10. *(Analyze)* The same mill closure scenario from Topic 5.2: five hundred workers lose jobs. Analyse how the economy institution connects to the family institution in this scenario in two sentences.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (100-160 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* Choose one of the six institutions covered in this unit. Describe its core functions and explain how it connects to at least two other institutions in a Pakistani context. Give a concrete example.

2. *(Evaluate)* "Social institutions never change - they have always been the same in Pakistan." Evaluate this claim using the concept of institutional change and one concrete example from the unit.

3. *(Analyze)* Compare the functions of family and education as social institutions in two areas: what they transmit to the next generation, and how they are shaped by the economy. Give one Pakistani example for each comparison.

4. *(Evaluate)* A provincial government announces a policy: every union council must build one new Basic Health Unit. Evaluate this policy using the idea of institutional interdependence. Name two other institutions that must be involved and explain why the health units alone may not improve community health.

5. *(Create/Analyse)* Describe a plausible Pakistani community scenario (real or constructed) in which a change in one institution creates a chain reaction across at least two others. Name each institution, explain the chain, and state which institution you think is most important in the chain and why.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - A stable pattern of norms, roles and expectations that meets a core social need. A confuses institution with a building; C confuses it with a single organization; D describes a personal network, not an institution.
2. **C** - Six: family, education, religion, economy, politics and healthcare.
3. **B** - Sorting refers to the way examinations and credentials channel people into different positions. A is literal, C is about personal preference, D ignores the school's role.
4. **B** - The factory closure affects economy, family, health and politics, showing institutional interdependence. A and D show single events within one institution; C shows routine, not interdependence.
5. **A** - Economic activity (the son's job and remittances) flows through the family to fund education. The other options misread the chain.
6. **A** - The Lady Health Worker programme involves healthcare and directly visits families. B is not stated; C and D are not supported by the description.
7. **C** - The growth of private schooling over decades is a change in the institution of education. A and B are individual events; D is a single family decision.
8. **A** - Economic constraints affect access to both education and religion, showing institutional connection. B is not supported; C is a judgement, not analysis; D is too narrow.
9. **A** - Weber's bureaucracy explains the formal organizational structure common in institutions like schools and hospitals. B, C and D misapply the concept.
10. **B** - A sociologist would point out that education is connected to other institutions and schools alone cannot address all the factors behind the crisis. A focuses on cost; C is a judgement; D addresses only one practical barrier.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** A social institution is a stable pattern of norms, roles and expectations that meets a core social need. The six institutions are family, education, religion, economy, politics and healthcare. **Marks (2):** 1 - correct definition; 1 - names all six.

2. **Model:** An institution is a broad pattern of social behaviour; a single organization is one body within it. Example: the institution of education includes schools, colleges, universities, the examination system and shared expectations about schooling - a single school is just one organization within that institution. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the distinction; 1 - gives a concrete example.

3. **Model:** Two functions besides knowledge transmission: social sorting (using credentials to channel people into occupations) and norm transmission (teaching punctuality, obedience, national identity). **Marks (2):** 1 for each correct function.

4. **Model:** The Karachi nuclear family may reflect the high cost of urban housing (economic factor) and the mobility required by service-sector jobs (social factor). The rural joint family may reflect the pooling of agricultural labour (economic factor) and the cultural value placed on living with and caring for elders (social factor). **Marks (3):** 1 - identifies an economic factor; 1 - identifies a social factor; 1 - connects each to a specific family form.

5. **Model:** Religion functions as a social institution by providing a shared moral framework and creating social bonds through ritual. For example, in Pakistan, the observance of Ramadan brings families and communities together in shared fasting and evening prayers, reinforcing both religious identity and social cohesion. **Marks (2):** 1 - names a function; 1 - gives a concrete example.

6. **Model:** The political decision is the government's choice to prioritize health spending over other demands. One economic consequence is increased employment for health workers and increased demand for medical supplies, which may stimulate local economies. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the political decision; 1 - names an economic consequence.

7. **Model:** The mobile phone tower (economy) may improve education by giving pupils and teachers access to online learning resources. It may affect family by enabling family members who migrated for work to stay in touch, reducing the pressure to choose between income and family unity. **Marks (3):** 1 - names economy as the starting institution; 1 - links to a second institution with a plausible mechanism; 1 - gives a concrete, plausible outcome.

8. **Model:** The claim underestimates politics because the Lady Health Worker programme was created by a government decision, funded by the state and deployed through the political system. Without political support - funding, policy and authorization - the healthcare intervention could not exist. Politics is the institution that makes the healthcare programme possible. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the political role; 1 - states why calling it "only healthcare" is incomplete.

9. **Model:** A manifest function of a school examination is to assess what a pupil has learned (intended and recognized). A latent function is to sort pupils by social class, because wealthier pupils have more support at home and perform better (unintended and often unrecognized). **Marks (2):** 1 - defines manifest function with the example; 1 - defines latent function with the example.

10. **Model:** The mill closure (economy) reduces household income. Families respond by pulling children from school to work, sending members to migrate for work, or cutting back on food and healthcare. The economic shock reshapes family structure and daily life. **Marks (3):** 1 - identifies the economic shock; 1 - links it to a family response; 1 - explains the connection clearly.
