# Social Institutions

Every society faces the same basic needs: it must raise and socialize children, pass on knowledge, give its members a sense of meaning and purpose, produce and distribute goods, make collective decisions, and care for people when they are ill. Society meets these needs not through single acts but through stable, organized patterns of behaviour called **social institutions**. An institution is not a building or an office. It is a set of norms, roles, values and expectations that work together to meet a core social need. This unit examines the six major institutions - family, education, religion, economy, politics and healthcare - and shows how they connect, how they maintain social life, and how they change.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Define a social institution and distinguish it from a single organization or building.
2. Explain the core functions of family, education and religion as social institutions.
3. Explain the core functions of economy, politics and healthcare as social institutions.
4. Analyse how a change in one institution affects at least one other institution in a described Pakistani community.
5. Describe one example of institutional change in Pakistani society and explain its causes.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 introduced the functionalist and conflict perspectives (Topics 1.3). You will use those lenses in this unit to analyse what institutions do and who benefits. Unit 3 introduced the idea of organizations (Topic 3.1). Institutions are larger and more diffuse than any single organization, so this unit builds on that foundation.

## In this unit

1. [Family, education, and religion](./topic-01) - the institutions that socialize, teach and give meaning.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. The first topic covers the institutions most familiar from daily life: family, education and religion. The second covers economy, politics and healthcare. Together they give you a full picture of how Pakistani society is organized. Read the topics in order, try the activities, and use the unit assessment to check your understanding at the end.
