## Unit summary

This unit built a single idea across three topics: **sociology is the systematic, evidence-based study of human social life, and it offers distinct ways of seeing the world.**

- Topic 1.1 defined sociology and distinguished it from common sense, psychology, and social work. Sociology studies social patterns using evidence, not personal opinion.
- Topic 1.2 introduced the four founders: Comte (positivism and social order), Marx (class conflict and exploitation), Durkheim (social facts and solidarity), and Weber (verstehen, culture, and bureaucracy).
- Topic 1.3 explained the three major perspectives: functionalism (order and functions), conflict theory (inequality and power), and symbolic interactionism (meaning and interaction).

Together these give you a usable analytical toolkit: when you meet a school situation, you can ask what function it serves (functionalism), who benefits and who pays (conflict theory), and what meanings are being made (symbolic interactionism).

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following best defines sociology as described in this unit?
   - A) The study of individual personality and motivation
   - B) The systematic, evidence-based study of human social life, groups and societies
   - C) The practical art of helping people with social problems
   - D) Common sense applied to social issues

2. *(Remember)* Who coined the term "sociology"?
   - A) Karl Marx
   - B) Emile Durkheim
   - C) Auguste Comte
   - D) Max Weber

3. *(Understand)* A teacher says, "My pupils fail because their families do not value education." How would a sociologist most likely respond?
   - A) Agree, because families are responsible for children's attitudes
   - B) Ask what social conditions shape families' capacity to support education
   - C) Suggest the teacher try harder in the classroom
   - D) Accept the explanation because it matches common sense

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following is an example of a "social fact" in Durkheim's sense?
   - A) A pupil's personal dislike of mathematics
   - B) The rule that all government school pupils must wear a uniform
   - C) A teacher's decision to use group work today
   - D) The colour of the classroom walls

5. *(Apply)* A study finds that in District X, 80 per cent of pupils from landlord families complete secondary school, compared with 30 per cent of tenant families. Which founder's ideas most directly explain this pattern?
   - A) Comte
   - B) Marx
   - C) Durkheim
   - D) Weber

6. *(Apply)* A symbolic interactionist studying a school would most likely focus on:
   - A) The economic background of the pupils' families
   - B) The daily interactions and meanings negotiated between teachers and pupils
   - C) The function the school serves for society as a whole
   - D) The state policy that funds the school

7. *(Apply)* Which pair correctly matches a founder to their key contribution?
   - A) Marx - social facts
   - B) Durkheim - class conflict
   - C) Weber - verstehen and the role of ideas
   - D) Comte - the Protestant ethic

8. *(Apply)* A functionalist analysing the morning assembly in a government school would most likely say it:
   - A) Reinforces the power of the head teacher over the pupils
   - B) Builds shared identity and social cohesion among pupils
   - C) Excludes pupils who cannot afford the required clothing
   - D) Has no important social function

9. *(Apply)* Weber's concept of verstehen refers to:
   - A) Measuring social facts statistically
   - B) Understanding the meanings people attach to their actions
   - C) Exposing exploitation in economic systems
   - D) Building social solidarity through shared rituals

10. *(Apply)* "Sociology and common sense both study social life, so there is no important difference between them." Which unit idea best rejects this claim?
    - A) Sociology is broader than common sense because it studies more countries
    - B) Sociology replaces personal judgement with systematic, evidence-based methods
    - C) Sociology is harder than common sense because it uses longer words
    - D) Sociology agrees with common sense most of the time

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between sociology and common sense, using one example from a school context.

2. *(Remember)* Name the four founders of sociology and the key concept associated with each.

3. *(Apply)* A rural school in Sindh finds that girls' attendance drops sharply after age 12. Would a functionalist and a conflict theorist explain this differently? Give one sentence for each perspective.

4. *(Apply)* Durkheim argued that suicide rates are social facts. In your own words, explain what that means and why it was an important finding.

5. *(Analyze)* Marx argued that "the ruling ideas of any society are the ideas of the ruling class." Apply this claim to the Pakistani education system in two or three sentences.

6. *(Apply)* Distinguish between a manifest function and a latent function, using any school practice as your example.

7. *(Understand)* Explain why symbolic interactionism is called a "micro-level" perspective, while functionalism and conflict theory are called "macro-level".

8. *(Apply)* A government school requires parents to provide a birth certificate for enrolment. Many families in the catchment area do not have one. Analyse this situation from the conflict theory perspective in two sentences.

9. *(Analyze)* A head teacher says, "Our morning assembly builds discipline and school spirit." A conflict theorist replies, "It also teaches pupils to obey authority without question." Explain what the conflict theorist is doing that the head teacher is not.

10. *(Apply)* Choose one of the three perspectives and explain, in two sentences, what it would focus on when studying a multi-grade classroom in a mountain village in Gilgit-Baltistan.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (100-160 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* A government school in Punjab has two streams: science (mostly boys from wealthier families) and arts (mostly girls and children from poorer families). Analyse this situation using **two** of the three perspectives from Topic 1.3. For each, name the perspective, state its core assumption, and explain what it would focus on.

2. *(Evaluate)* "Sociology is just common sense with a fancy name." Evaluate this claim using evidence from Topic 1.1 (the nature of sociology as a social science) and Topic 1.3 (the three perspectives). Explain what sociology offers that common sense does not.

3. *(Analyze)* Choose **one** of the four founders (Comte, Marx, Durkheim, or Weber). Explain their key contribution to sociology and discuss one strength and one limitation of their ideas for understanding education in Pakistan today.

4. *(Evaluate)* A policymaker says, "If we build more schools, educational inequality will disappear." Evaluate this claim from the conflict theory perspective. What would a conflict theorist say the policymaker is missing?

5. *(Create/Analyse)* Describe a school or community situation you have observed (real or plausible) that involves a clear pattern of social behaviour. Analyse it using all three perspectives from Topic 1.3. For each, name the perspective and explain what it reveals about the situation. Then state which perspective you find most illuminating and why.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - The systematic, evidence-based study of human social life. A describes psychology, C describes social work, D describes common sense; all rejected in Topic 1.1.
2. **C** - Auguste Comte coined the term in 1838. The other three are founders but not the namer of the discipline.
3. **B** - A sociologist asks about social conditions, not just individual attitudes. A accepts common sense; C blames the teacher; D rejects sociology's core stance.
4. **B** - A uniform rule is a social fact: it exists outside any individual and constrains behaviour. A is personal, C is an individual decision, D is a physical fact, not a social one.
5. **B** - Marx's ideas about class conflict and exploitation most directly explain differences in educational outcomes by family class position. Comte would look for order, Durkheim for integration, Weber for meaning.
6. **B** - Symbolic interactionism focuses on interactions and meanings. A is conflict theory, C is functionalism, D is policy analysis.
7. **C** - Weber developed verstehen and the role of culture and ideas. A is Durkheim, B is Marx, D is Weber but about the Protestant ethic specifically; the pairing of Weber to verstehen and ideas is the most complete match.
8. **B** - A functionalist focuses on how a practice contributes to social cohesion and shared identity. A is conflict theory, C is also conflict theory, D rejects functionalism entirely.
9. **B** - Verstehen is empathetic understanding of meaning. A is positivism (Comte), C is Marx, D is Durkheim.
10. **B** - The unit's core distinction is that sociology uses systematic, evidence-based methods rather than personal judgement. A is about scope not method, C confuses difficulty with method, D is false.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Sociology studies social patterns using systematic evidence and methods; common sense relies on personal belief and tradition. For example, common sense says "girls are naturally quiet", while sociology asks what classroom norms produce girls' silence. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the evidence vs. belief distinction; 1 - gives a concrete school example.

2. **Model:** Comte (positivism), Marx (class conflict / exploitation), Durkheim (social facts / solidarity), Weber (verstehen / bureaucracy). **Marks (4):** 1 for each founder with a recognisable concept.

3. **Model (functionalist):** A functionalist might say the drop serves a function - perhaps the community arranges roles so that girls' domestic labour is valued. **Model (conflict theorist):** A conflict theorist would say the drop reflects gender inequality and the community's (or the patriarchy's) interest in limiting girls' education. **Marks (2):** 1 - names a function; 1 - names an inequality or power relation.

4. **Model:** Durkheim showed that suicide rates are stable across time and differ between groups (Protestants vs Catholics, married vs single). This proved that even the most personal act is shaped by social forces - social integration and regulation. **Marks (3):** 1 - explains what a social fact is; 1 - mentions the stability/variation finding; 1 - states why it matters (proves social forces shape even personal acts).

5. **Model:** The curriculum, the language of instruction, and the knowledge that counts as "education" tend to reflect the values and experiences of dominant groups. Elite schools offer international curricula; government schools may be underfunded. The system reproduces the ideas of those who hold economic power. **Marks (3):** 1 - applies the ruling-ideas claim to education; 1 - gives a concrete Pakistani example; 1 - explains how it reproduces advantage.

6. **Model:** A manifest function is an intended, recognized consequence; a latent function is an unintended, often unrecognized one. Example: the manifest function of a school exam is to assess learning; a latent function is to sort pupils by social class, because wealthier pupils have more support at home. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines manifest; 1 - defines latent; 1 - applies both to a concrete example.

7. **Model:** Symbolic interactionism studies face-to-face interactions (micro-level); functionalism and conflict theory study whole societies, institutions, and large-scale structures (macro-level). **Marks (2):** 1 - explains micro; 1 - explains macro.

8. **Model:** A conflict theorist would say the birth certificate rule, while neutral on its surface, disadvantages families without documentation - often the poorest and most marginalised. It reproduces inequality by creating a barrier that falls hardest on those with least power. **Marks (2):** 1 - applies conflict theory; 1 - identifies who is disadvantaged.

9. **Model:** The head teacher describes what the assembly does (its manifest function). The conflict theorist asks whose interest it serves and what it conceals - that it also teaches obedience to authority. The conflict theorist looks beneath the stated purpose to the power relations. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the difference; 1 - names power or hidden interest.

10. **Model:** A functionalist would focus on how the multi-grade classroom serves the community's need for education where there are few teachers. A conflict theorist would focus on the inequality that forces one teacher to manage multiple grades. A symbolic interactionist would focus on how pupils and teacher negotiate meaning across grade levels. **Marks (2):** 1 - chooses a perspective; 1 - applies it to the example.
