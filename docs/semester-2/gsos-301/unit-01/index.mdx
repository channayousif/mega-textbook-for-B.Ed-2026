# Introduction to Sociology and Sociological Perspectives

Before you can use sociology in your teaching, you need a clear picture of what it actually is. The word is sometimes used loosely in everyday talk to mean "thinking about people" or "knowing how society works". This unit uses a more precise meaning: sociology is the systematic, evidence-based study of human social life, groups, and institutions. It is a discipline with its own history, methods, and ways of seeing the world. This unit introduces you to that discipline, the thinkers who built it, and the three major perspectives that still shape how sociologists explain social life today.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Define sociology, explain its scope and nature, and distinguish it from common sense and from related disciplines such as psychology and social work.
2. Describe the contributions of Auguste Comte, Karl Marx, Emile Durkheim, and Max Weber to the founding of sociology.
3. Explain the core assumptions of functionalism, conflict theory, and symbolic interactionism.
4. Apply at least one sociological perspective to analyse a described classroom or school situation.

## Prerequisite knowledge

None beyond HSC-level general study skills. This is the opening unit of the GSOS-301 course. No prior study of sociology is assumed. If you have ever wondered why some schools in your district succeed while others struggle despite having similar resources, you already have the kind of question sociology tries to answer.

## In this unit

1. [What sociology is, and why it matters](./topic-01) - definition, scope, nature, and importance of sociology.
2. [The founders of sociology](./topic-02) - Comte, Marx, Durkheim, and Weber and what each contributed.
3. [The three major perspectives](./topic-03) - functionalism, conflict theory, and symbolic interactionism.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your understanding" items are retrieval practice - try them without looking back, then check your reasoning. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws the three topics together.
