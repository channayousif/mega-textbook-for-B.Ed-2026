## Unit summary

This unit developed one argument across two topics: **societies are constantly changing, and the change produces both gains and new social problems that must be understood structurally.**

- Topic 6.1 explained the causes of social change: technology, economic shifts, population movement, environmental pressure, conflict and new ideas, all interacting. It introduced modernization, globalization and technology as three major drivers and showed that each brings both benefits and new inequalities.
- Topic 6.2 turned to the social problems that result: poverty, crime and deviance, unemployment, media, human rights and environmental issues. It argued that each is a structural product, not only an individual failing, and that the problems are interconnected.

Together these give you a way to analyse any social issue you encounter as a teacher: what caused it, who it affects, which institutions are involved and what structural response might address it.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following best defines social change?
   - A) A short-term shift in fashion or public opinion
   - B) A significant and lasting alteration of social structures, institutions and behaviour
   - C) One individual changing their mind about a social issue
   - D) A government announcing a new policy

2. *(Remember)* Which of the following is a major driver of social change discussed in Topic 6.1?
   - A) Astrological events
   - B) Technology
   - C) Individual personality
   - D) Genetic inheritance

3. *(Understand)* A functionalist studying crime would most likely say that deviance:
   - A) Should be eliminated entirely
   - B) Has no social consequences
   - C) Can reinforce shared norms by making the boundaries of acceptable behaviour visible
   - D) Is always harmful to society

4. *(Understand)* Which of the following is an example of deviance that is not necessarily a crime?
   - A) Stealing from a shop
   - B) A teacher publicly challenging a powerful community leader's view
   - C) Driving above the speed limit
   - D) Refusing to pay taxes

5. *(Apply)* A village in Sindh sees a rapid shift from farming to factory work after an industrial zone opens nearby. Which concept from Topic 6.1 best describes the underlying process?
   - A) Modernization
   - B) Symbolic interactionism
   - C) Functionalism
   - D) Social Darwinism

6. *(Apply)* A conflict theorist analysing media in Pakistan would most likely focus on:
   - A) The entertainment value of television programmes
   - B) Who owns the media and whose viewpoints are amplified or silenced
   - C) The technical quality of broadcast signals
   - D) The number of channels available in urban areas

7. *(Apply*) A government school stops requiring exam fees, and enrolment rises sharply among poor families. Which sociological idea does this best illustrate?
   - A) Individual effort overcomes poverty
   - B) A structural change (removing a financial barrier) can alter access to education
   - C) Technology is the only effective driver of change
   - D) Globalization automatically improves schooling

8. *(Apply)* Pakistan's vulnerability to flooding is worsened by deforestation, weak regulation of industry and settlement patterns in flood-prone areas. Which sociological framing best captures this?
   - A) Environmental issues are purely natural and have no social cause
   - B) Environmental problems result from social arrangements and affect groups unequally
   - C) Environmental problems affect every person equally regardless of wealth
   - D) Environmental issues are a matter of personal lifestyle only

9. *(Apply)* A young university graduate in Lahore has been looking for formal employment for two years despite having good marks. Which explanation is most clearly structural?
   - A) The graduate is lazy
   - B) The graduate's family is not supportive
   - C) The economy is not generating enough formal jobs, and the education system may not match labour-market needs
   - D) The graduate is overqualified for every position

10. *(Apply)* Which statement best reflects the sociological view of poverty presented in this unit?
    - A) Poverty is always the result of individual laziness or bad decisions
    - B) Poverty is produced and maintained by social structures, and can therefore be reduced by changing those structures
    - C) Poverty is natural and cannot be changed by human action
    - D) Poverty exists only in rural areas of Pakistan

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Remember)* Name the three major drivers of social change discussed in Topic 6.1.

2. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between modernization and globalization, using one example.

3. *(Understand)* What is the difference between crime and deviance? Give one example of each that is different from the ones used in the topic.

4. *(Apply)* A friend says, "Poor people are poor because they do not work hard." Using the structural explanation of poverty from Topic 6.2, explain in two or three sentences what that claim misses.

5. *(Apply)* Explain how unemployment connects to at least one other social problem from Topic 6.2. Use a concrete example.

6. *(Analyze)* A new mobile-money service reaches a rural district in Punjab. Analyse this change using at least two causes of social change from Topic 6.1, and name one institution (from Unit 5) that is likely to be affected.

7. *(Apply*) Why do sociologists treat environmental issues as social problems rather than purely scientific ones? Give two reasons with brief explanations.

8. *(Understand)* What is the digital divide, and why is it a concern for the sociological study of media?

9. *(Analyze)* A conflict theorist and a functionalist both study crime in a low-income neighbourhood. Explain in two or three sentences what each would focus on and how their explanations would differ.

10. *(Apply)* Human rights are guaranteed in Pakistan's constitution but remain unrealised for many people. Using one example from the unit, explain the gap between a right on paper and a right in practice.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (120-180 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* Choose one social problem from Topic 6.2 (poverty, crime, unemployment, media, human rights or environment). Analyse it using the structural approach from this unit, explaining at least two structural causes and connecting it to at least one institution from Unit 5. Use a concrete Pakistani example.

2. *(Evaluate)* "Globalization only brings progress to developing countries like Pakistan." Evaluate this claim using evidence from Topics 6.1 and 6.2. Explain what the claim gets right and what it misses.

3. *(Analyze)* Describe a Pakistani community or school situation (real or plausible) in which a new technology (mobile phones, social media, online learning or another) produced both a benefit and a problem. Analyse the situation using the concepts of modernization, technology and the digital divide from Topic 6.1.

4. *(Evaluate)* A policymaker proposes to reduce poverty by offering short-term cash transfers to poor households. Evaluate this proposal using the structural understanding of poverty from Topic 6.2. What does it address, and what structural causes might it miss?

5. *(Create/Analyse)* Choose one social problem from this unit and one driver of social change from Topic 6.1. Propose a structural response to the problem that works through the driver you chose. Explain how the response addresses a structural cause rather than only an individual one, and discuss one potential difficulty in putting it into practice.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Social change is a significant, lasting alteration of structures and behaviour, not a fashion (A), an individual decision (C) or a policy announcement (D).
2. **B** - Technology is one of the major drivers named in Topic 6.1. The other three options are not discussed as drivers.
3. **C** - A functionalist sees deviance as potentially reinforcing shared norms. A and D are more common-sense than sociological; B is false.
4. **B** - Challenging a powerful figure may violate social norms without breaking a law. A, C and D are crimes.
5. **A** - A shift from farming to factory work and urban-centred livelihoods is modernization in action. B and C are perspectives from Unit 1, not the concept the question asks for; D is not a concept from the unit.
6. **B** - A conflict theorist focuses on power: who owns media and whose voices are heard or silenced. A and C are not sociological angles; D is factual, not analytical.
7. **B** - Removing a financial barrier is a structural change that alters access. A is the individual explanation the unit rejects; C overstates technology's role; D is unsupported.
8. **B** - Environmental problems result from social arrangements (deforestation, regulation, settlement) and affect groups unequally. A and C contradict the topic; D is an individual-level explanation.
9. **C** - The structural explanation looks at the economy and the education system. A and B are individual explanations; D is not a sociological explanation.
10. **B** - The unit's core argument is that poverty is structural and therefore can be addressed structurally. A is the individual-blaming view the unit rejects; C is defeatist and unsupported; D is factually wrong.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Modernization, globalization and technology. (Accept any order. Other causes named in the topic, such as economic shifts or population movement, may also be credited if the student notes them as additional causes.) **Marks (3):** 1 for each correct driver named.

2. **Model:** Modernization is the shift toward industrial, urban and technologically intensive forms of life within a society. Globalization is the growing interconnection of economies, cultures and societies across national boundaries. Example: a Pakistani textile factory adopts modern machinery (modernization) and sells its products to a retailer in Europe (globalization). **Marks (3):** 1 for a correct modernization definition; 1 for a correct globalization definition; 1 for a concrete example that distinguishes them.

3. **Model:** Crime is behaviour that breaks formal laws; deviance is behaviour that violates social norms, whether or not a law is broken. Example of crime: stealing a mobile phone from a shop. Example of deviance: a woman in a conservative community choosing to work outside the home despite family disapproval. **Marks (3):** 1 for distinguishing crime and deviance correctly; 1 for a valid crime example; 1 for a valid deviance example that is not also a crime.

4. **Model:** The claim treats poverty as individual laziness. The structural explanation says poverty persists because of social arrangements: unequal distribution of land, uneven school quality, limited access to healthcare and formal employment. A person can work very hard and still be poor if those structures are unchanged. **Marks (3):** 1 for naming the structural explanation; 1 for naming at least two structural causes; 1 for explaining why individual effort alone is not sufficient.

5. **Model:** Unemployment deepens poverty because without formal income a household cannot accumulate savings, fund education or afford healthcare. Example: a laid-off factory worker in Faisalabad falls behind on rent and withdraws his children from private tuition, worsening their educational prospects. **Marks (3):** 1 for the unemployment-poverty connection; 1 for naming a second problem (e.g., education, crime); 1 for a concrete Pakistani example.

6. **Model:** The spread of mobile money is driven by technology (the mobile platform itself) and by economic pressure (families need to move money without travelling to a bank). The institution most affected is the economy, because the informal financial system is partly replaced by a formal one; the family is also affected, because remittances arrive faster and household budgeting changes. **Marks (3):** 1 for naming technology as a cause; 1 for naming economic pressure (or another valid cause from the topic); 1 for naming and briefly explaining the institutional effect.

7. **Model:** (1) Environmental problems result from social choices about industry, energy and land use, not only from nature. (2) Their effects fall unequally on different groups, with the poor suffering more from pollution, floods and water scarcity. **Marks (2):** 1 for each valid reason with a brief explanation.

8. **Model:** The digital divide is the gap between those with access to digital tools and the skills to use them and those without. It matters for media sociology because those without access are excluded from the information, conversations and opportunities that digital media create, which reinforces existing inequalities. **Marks (2):** 1 for defining the digital divide; 1 for explaining why it is a sociological concern.

9. **Model:** A functionalist would focus on how crime affects social order: whether it disrupts norms or, paradoxically, reinforces them by clarifying boundaries. A conflict theorist would focus on how laws are made and enforced: whose interests they serve, and why the survival strategies of the poor are often criminalised while the harmful practices of the rich are not. **Marks (3):** 1 for the functionalist focus; 1 for the conflict focus; 1 for explaining how the two explanations differ.

10. **Model:** The right to education is guaranteed, but children in rural Sindh may attend schools with no trained teacher, no building and no learning materials. The right exists on paper but not in practice because the institutional capacity (funding, teacher deployment, accountability) is too weak to make it real. **Marks (3):** 1 for naming a specific right; 1 for a concrete Pakistani example; 1 for explaining the gap in institutional terms.
