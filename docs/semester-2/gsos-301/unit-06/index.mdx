# Social Change and Social Problems

Societies are never still. The school you teach in, the community around it and the country itself are all changing, often faster than we notice. Some changes feel like progress, others bring new problems. This unit gives you the tools to understand *why* societies change and to analyse the social problems that change produces, not as a list of ills but as patterns with causes that can be studied and, in principle, addressed.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain the main causes of social change, including modernization, globalization and technology.
2. Distinguish modernization from tradition and describe how they interact in Pakistani society.
3. Identify the major social problems studied by sociologists and explain their structural causes.
4. Apply sociological knowledge to analyse a real-world social issue in a Pakistani context.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 4's concepts of stratification and inequality (U4-01 to U4-03) and Unit 5's social institutions. This unit treats social problems as products of structure and institutions, so both earlier units must be secure. If you can explain why poverty is a social arrangement rather than only individual misfortune, you are ready for this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Why societies change](./topic-01) - the causes of social change, modernization, globalization and the role of technology.
2. [Social problems and contemporary issues](./topic-02) - poverty, crime, deviance, unemployment, media, human rights and environmental issues.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. Topic 6.1 gives you the engine of change; Topic 6.2 applies that understanding to the problems change leaves behind. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws both topics together.
