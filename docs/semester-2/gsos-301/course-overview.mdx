# Social Science (Sociology) - Course Overview

This course introduces students to sociology as the systematic study of society, social
relationships, and social institutions. It examines how social structures, cultures, and
interactions shape individual behaviour and collective life. The course covers classical and
contemporary sociological theories, socialization, culture, social stratification, institutions,
and major social issues at local and global levels. Emphasis is placed on developing sociological
imagination and critical thinking skills.

## Syllabus

The 16-week course is organized into six units:

1. **Introduction to Sociology and Sociological Perspectives** - What sociology is, the four founders (Comte, Marx, Durkheim, Weber), and the three major perspectives (functionalism, conflict theory, symbolic interactionism).
2. **Culture and Socialization** - Elements of culture, cultural diversity, ethnocentrism vs cultural relativism, agents and stages of socialization.
3. **Social Structure and Social Interaction** - Status and roles, social groups, organizations and bureaucracy, cooperation/competition/conflict, and communication.
4. **Social Stratification and Gender** - Class, caste, and hierarchy; social mobility; inequality; gender roles and stereotypes; feminist perspectives.
5. **Social Institutions** - Family, education, religion, economy, politics, and healthcare as core social institutions.
6. **Social Change and Social Problems** - Causes of social change, modernization and globalization, poverty, crime and deviance, unemployment, media, human rights, and environmental issues.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Understand core concepts, theories, and perspectives in sociology.
2. Analyse social behaviour using sociological frameworks.
3. Explain how social institutions influence individual and group life.
4. Identify patterns of inequality and social change in society.
5. Apply sociological knowledge to real-world social issues.
6. Develop critical thinking, analytical, and research skills.

## Assessment

Assessment follows the General Education default: **60% summative / 40% formative** (Constitution Art. III.7).
Each unit carries a summative assessment bank (10 MCQ + 10 RRQ + 5 ERQ) and formative retrieval
practice through topic-cycle checks for understanding.
