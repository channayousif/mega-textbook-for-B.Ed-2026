# Social Structure and Social Interaction

Every day you occupy many positions at once: teacher, daughter, union member, neighbour, committee head. Each position comes with expectations about how you should behave, and each affects how others treat you. Sociology explains this invisible architecture of social life. This unit examines two connected ideas: **social structure** - the organised pattern of statuses, roles, groups and institutions that shapes what people do - and **social interaction** - the face-to-face processes (cooperation, competition, conflict and communication) through which people carry out their daily lives.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Define status and role, distinguish between them, and explain how multiple statuses produce role strain.
2. Distinguish primary from secondary groups and explain the key features of each.
3. Describe the main features of bureaucracy and evaluate both its strengths and its limitations.
4. Identify and explain the three major social interaction processes: cooperation, competition and conflict.
5. Analyse a described classroom or community situation using the concepts of social structure and social interaction.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 2 (Culture and Socialization) is recommended. The idea that roles are learned through socialization (Topic 2.2) is the foundation for the status-and-role concepts in this unit. The three sociological perspectives from Unit 1 (functionalism, conflict theory, symbolic interactionism) are applied to social structures in Topic 3.2.

## In this unit

1. [How society is structured](./topic-01) - status and roles, social groups (primary and secondary), and organizations and bureaucracy.
2. [How people interact](./topic-02) - cooperation, competition, conflict, social interaction patterns, and communication in society.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the explanation, and try the activity before moving on. The "Check your understanding" items are retrieval practice - try them without looking back, then check your reasoning. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws both topics together.
