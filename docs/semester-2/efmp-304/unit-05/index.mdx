# Engaging in Reflective Practice

Unit 4 gave you the vocabulary (reflecting, the process, the practice) and the method (the six-stage
cycle). This unit answers a question the first four topics left open: *where does that cycle come
from, and which version of it should I use?*

The answer matters because not all reflective cycles are the same. Some were designed for nurses, some
for social workers, some for managers. They share a family resemblance - experience, examination,
learning - but they differ in what they emphasise, what they assume about the learner, and what they
leave out. A teacher who picks a cycle without understanding its origin is like a doctor who prescribes
a medicine without reading the label: it may help, it may not, and they will not know why.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain why experience alone does not produce learning and how the reflective cycle closes that gap, using the experiential-learning framework.
2. Take a single teaching incident through a named reflective cycle and name what each stage produces.
3. Identify the main theoretical strands beneath reflective practice and what each contributes.
4. Compare at least two frameworks or models of reflection and choose one for a described situation, with a justification against an alternative.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 4 in full. This unit operationalises what Unit 4 defines - the cycle in Topic 5.2 is the same
cycle Topic 4.3 walked through, now with its theoretical basis and its variants named. Opening this
unit first would put the models before the reason for them.

## In this unit

1. [Learning from experience, and why experience alone does not teach](./topic-01) - reflection as experiential learning.
2. [The cycle](./topic-02) - the reflective cycle in detail, with its stages and what is carried between them.
3. [The theory beneath the practice](./topic-03) - where the ideas came from and what each was originally answering.
4. [Knowing yourself, and choosing a model](./topic-04) - frameworks and models compared, and how to choose between them.

## How to use this unit

This unit is the theoretical half of the course's practice section. Topic 5.1 and 5.2 give you the
cycle and its basis; Topic 5.3 and 5.4 give you the intellectual history and the menu of models. If
you are preparing to write your assessed reflection file (Unit 6), pay particular attention to
Topic 5.4: the model you choose shapes the journal you keep.
