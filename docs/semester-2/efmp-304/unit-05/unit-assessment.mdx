## Unit summary

This unit gave the theoretical basis for the reflective practice Unit 4 introduced. The single idea
running through all four topics is this: **reflection is a mechanism with a history, and the model
you use should match the question you are asking.**

- **Topic 5.1** explained why experience alone does not produce learning and how reflection closes the gap. The bypass route (repetition without reflection) is the default in most schools; the main route (experience examined and re-evaluated) is what turns a career into growth.
- **Topic 5.2** walked the reflective cycle end to end and named the return arrow as the element that closes the loop. Each stage has a characteristic way of being skipped; the most damaging is skipping the return arrow, because that is what prevents learning from accumulating.
- **Topic 5.3** named the theoretical strands: experiential learning (Dewey, Kolb), reflective consciousness (Schön), and critical reflection (Brookfield, Freire). Each answers a different question, and the critical strand is the one most Pakistani training programmes leave out.
- **Topic 5.4** compared the models and gave three criteria for choosing: the question you are asking, what the assessment requires, and what you can sustain. For most teachers, a combination (Gibbs for structure, Brookfield for depth) is the most sustainable.

Three habits should survive this unit. First, know the strand behind the model you are using, because a model used for the wrong question gives the wrong answer. Second, close the return arrow every time, because an open loop is a wasted cycle. Third, know your default level of reflection and push against it, because comfort is the enemy of growth.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* The three steps of the experiential-learning mechanism (Boud et al.) are:
   - A) Description, analysis, action
   - B) Returning to the experience, attending to feelings, re-evaluating
   - C) Planning, teaching, assessing
   - D) Observing, recording, reporting

2. *(Understand)* "Experience alone does not produce learning" means that:
   - A) Experience is useless for teachers
   - B) A reflective process is required to turn experience into learning
   - C) Teachers should attend more workshops
   - D) Only new teachers need to learn

3. *(Apply)* A teacher has taught for twelve years and says, "I have seen it all. Nothing changes." This teacher is most likely on:
   - A) The main route from experience to learning
   - B) The bypass route (repetition without reflection)
   - C) The critical reflection strand
   - D) The reflection-in-action stage

4. *(Remember)* The stage of the reflective cycle that produces a finding to carry to the next cycle is:
   - A) Description
   - B) Analysis
   - C) Re-examination
   - D) Alternatives

5. *(Apply)* A journal entry describes a problem, analyses it, chooses an alternative, tries it, and concludes "it worked". What is missing?
   - A) A description of what was tried
   - B) A finding that can be carried to the next entry
   - C) An alternative
   - D) Nothing - the cycle is complete

6. *(Remember)* The theoretical strand that asks "what assumptions and power relations shape my practice?" is:
   - A) Experiential learning
   - B) Reflective consciousness
   - C) Critical reflection
   - D) Technical reflection

7. *(Understand)* Gibbs' six-stage model is best suited for:
   - A) Examining assumptions about social justice
   - B) Producing structured, technique-focused reflection with clear action points
   - C) Understanding how professionals think during practice
   - D) Replacing the need for any other model

8. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to understand how she adjusts her explanation mid-lesson when she sees confusion. The most relevant thinker is:
   - A) Kolb
   - B) Dewey
   - C) Schön
   - D) Gibbs

9. *(Remember)* Brookfield's four lenses include all of the following EXCEPT:
   - A) The autobiographical lens
   - B) The students' eyes lens
   - C) The theoretical literature lens
   - D) The statistical evidence lens

10. *(Apply)* A teacher-training programme teaches only Gibbs' model and presents it as "the complete
    framework for teacher reflection." The main limitation of this approach is that it:
    - A) Leaves out the critical strand that examines assumptions and power
    - B) Has too few stages for thorough reflection
    - C) Was designed for nursing, not teaching
    - D) Is too difficult for most teachers

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, name the difference between the main route and the bypass route from experience.

2. *(Understand)* Explain in two or three sentences why the return arrow matters in the reflective cycle. What does it carry?

3. *(Apply)* A teacher writes in her journal: "The pupils failed the test. I will teach more carefully next time." Using the cycle from Topic 5.2, name the stage this reaches and what the next stage would add.

4. *(Apply)* Name the theoretical strand each thinker belongs to: (a) Kolb, (b) Schön, (c) Brookfield.

5. *(Understand)* A trainee says, "I will use the model with the most stages because it will be the most thorough." Using Topic 5.4, explain in two sentences why that reasoning is wrong.

6. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to examine why she feels more confident teaching one class than another. Which model is the best fit and why?

7. *(Analyze)* A journal entry describes an incident, analyses it, generates three alternatives, and chooses one. The next entry begins with a completely different incident and makes no reference to the previous one. What has the teacher missed, and what does it cost?

8. *(Apply)* Name one reason a Pakistani teacher might choose Gibbs' model and one reason they might also need Brookfield's lenses.

9. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between reflection-on-action and reflection-in-action.

10. *(Analyze)* Take this claim: "After ten years of teaching, a teacher automatically has ten years of professional growth." Using the experiential-learning framework, explain what is wrong with this claim and what the correct version would say.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* Here is a teacher's journal entry:

    > "Grade 9, poetry. I taught the poem with a line-by-line explanation. The pupils were quiet and
    > copied everything. The test showed they could recall the meanings but not explain the imagery on
    > their own. I will try a different approach next time."

    In 150 to 200 words, name which stage of the reflective cycle this entry reaches, rewrite it so it
   reaches the re-examination stage with a clear success criterion, and write the first sentence of the
   next entry showing the return arrow.

2. *(Evaluate)* A head teacher tells the staff: "Keep a Gibbs journal every week and your teaching
   will improve." In 200 to 250 words, evaluate this claim against the theoretical strands from Topic
   5.3, name what Gibbs can and cannot deliver, and say what would have to be added for the claim to be
   fully true.

3. *(Analyze)* A teacher wants to reflect on why pupils from one neighbourhood consistently
   underperform in her class. In 200 to 250 words, name which theoretical strand and which model is the
   best fit, justify the choice against a named alternative, and name one activity that would help her
   reach the critical level on this issue.

4. *(Evaluate)* Two teachers both keep reflective journals. Teacher A uses Gibbs every week and
   produces neat, structured entries that rarely surprise her. Teacher B uses Brookfield's lenses
   and produces entries that sometimes make her uncomfortable but occasionally change how she sees her
   pupils. In 200 to 250 words, compare the two approaches by theoretical strand, say which is more
   likely to produce lasting growth, and justify your judgement from the content of each approach.

5. *(Create)* **Integrative.** Choose one incident from your own teaching or practicum. In 250 to 300
   words, produce a reflective account that (a) takes the incident through at least three stages of the
   reflective cycle, (b) names which theoretical strand your reflection draws on, (c) applies one of
   Brookfield's lenses to the same incident and names what it surfaced that the cycle did not, and (d)
   names the finding you would carry to the next entry.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Work each question before reading it.

### MCQ answer key

1. B - the three steps are returning to the experience, attending to feelings and re-evaluating; A is a simplified version of the reflective cycle, not the experiential-learning mechanism.
2. B - reflection is the process required; A overstates the claim.
3. B - the teacher is repeating experience without examining it; "I have seen it all" is the bypass route's characteristic statement.
4. C - re-examination produces the finding that is carried forward.
5. B - the teacher has reached Stage 4 (trial) but not Stage 5 (re-examination with a finding).
6. C - critical reflection examines assumptions and power; A is Kolb/Dewey, B is Schön.
7. B - Gibbs is structured and technique-focused; A is Brookfield's domain, C is Schön's.
8. C - Schön's reflection-in-action describes thinking during practice.
9. D - statistical evidence is not one of Brookfield's lenses; the four are autobiographical, students' eyes, colleagues' perceptions and theory.
10. A - the main limitation is the missing critical strand; C is true but secondary; B and D are false.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: the main route goes from experience through reflection (returning, attending, re-evaluating) into learning. The bypass route goes straight from experience to repetition without examination. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines the main route; 1 - defines the bypass route; 1 - contrasts them.

2. Model: the return arrow carries the finding from the re-examination stage back to the next cycle. Without it, every entry starts from scratch and learning does not accumulate. **Marks (2):** 1 - names what the return arrow carries; 1 - says what happens without it.

3. Model: description and decision. The analysis stage was skipped - the teacher moved from "failed" to "teach more carefully" without asking why. The next stage (analysis) would ask what "more carefully" means and what evidence supports that as the right response. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the stage reached; 1 - names the skipped stage; 1 - says what it would add.

4. Model: (a) Kolb - experiential learning; (b) Schön - reflective consciousness; (c) Brookfield - critical reflection. **Marks (3):** 1 per correct pairing.

5. Model: thoroughness is about the depth of the question, not the number of stages. Brookfield's four lenses can produce deeper reflection than Gibbs' six stages because they ask questions Gibbs does not ask. **Marks (2):** 1 - states the principle; 1 - gives the example.

6. Model: Brookfield (autobiographical lens). The question is about the teacher's own feelings and assumptions, which is the autobiographical lens's domain. Kolb/Gibbs would treat it as a technique problem and miss the assumption dimension. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the model/lens; 1 - says why; 1 - names why the alternative is weaker.

7. Model: the return arrow. The teacher completed the cycle but did not carry the finding forward. It cost them the accumulated learning: the next entry starts from scratch rather than building on what was discovered. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the return arrow; 1 - says what was missed; 1 - says what it cost.

8. Model: Gibbs is easy to structure and assess, which suits the assessed reflection file. Brookfield is needed because Gibbs alone does not examine assumptions, and a teacher who only refines techniques never questions whether the techniques are the right ones. **Marks (3):** 1 - one reason for Gibbs; 1 - one reason Brookfield is also needed; 1 - ties the reason to the critical strand.

9. Model: reflection-on-action is thinking about a past action (after the lesson). Reflection-in-action is thinking during the action (adjusting mid-lesson). **Marks (2):** 1 - defines reflection-on-action; 1 - defines reflection-in-action.

10. Model: the claim assumes experience automatically produces learning. The correct version is that experience *plus reflection* produces learning, so ten years of teaching produces growth only if the teacher has reflected on those ten years. A teacher who has repeated one year ten times has experience but not growth. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the error; 1 - gives the correct version; 1 - a concrete example.

### ERQ rubrics

1. Analytic rubric - *Stage identification* (0-2: correct stage named / a general answer / wrong) · *Rewrite to re-examination* (0-2: includes a specific, testable success criterion / a vague criterion / none) · *Return arrow sentence* (0-2: shows what was learned and carries it forward / a general sentence / none) · *Clarity* (0-2).

2. Analytic rubric - *Claim evaluation* (0-2: evaluates against the three strands with named distinctions / a general evaluation / wrong) · *What Gibbs can and cannot do* (0-3: names both - structure, action points vs. missing critical examination (**Evaluate*: the answer turns on the specific theoretical content, not on general caution) / one side only / neither) · *What would have to be added* (0-2: names the critical strand or a specific activity / a general suggestion / none) · *Clarity* (0-2).

3. Analytic rubric - *Strand and model naming* (0-2: correct strand and model with justification / one correct / neither) · *Justification against alternative* (0-3: justifies against a named alternative using the strand definitions / a general reason / a verdict asserted) · *Activity for critical level* (0-2: names an activity tied to the critical strand / a general activity / none) · *Clarity* (0-2).

4. Analytic rubric - *Strand identification* (0-2: both correctly identified by strand / one correct / neither) · *Which produces lasting growth* (0-2: names Teacher B with a reason tied to the critical strand / a general reason / wrong) · *Justification* (0-3: justifies from the specific content of each approach, showing why the critical strand matters for lasting change (**Evaluate*: the comparison turns on the actual content, not on a general preference for depth) / general reasons / a verdict asserted) · *Clarity* (0-2).

5. Analytic rubric - *Cycle stages* (0-2: at least three stages clearly distinguished / two or fewer / none) · *Theoretical strand named* (0-2: correct with evidence / wrong) · *Brookfield lens applied* (0-2: a lens applied with a finding that differs from the cycle's finding / a lens named but not applied / none) · *Finding carried forward* (0-2: a specific finding named / a vague one / none) · *Clarity* (0-2).
