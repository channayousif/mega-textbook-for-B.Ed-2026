# Becoming a Reflective Teacher

Units 1 to 3 gave you the tools to judge reasoning: standards, argument structure, and the tests for
validity and strength. This unit turns those tools on your own teaching. A teacher who can spot a
weak argument in a staffroom is thinking critically; a teacher who can spot the weak reasoning inside
their *own* lesson plan and do something about it is thinking *reflectively*. The two are not the same,
and this unit is about the second one.

The first thing to get right is the vocabulary, because the three words this unit runs on are used as
if they mean the same thing. They do not, and the difference between them is what makes reflection a
disciplined practice rather than a vague resolution to "think about your teaching".

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Distinguish reflective thinking, the reflective process and reflective practice, and explain what each adds to the one before it.
2. Explain why reflective practice matters for a teacher's professional growth, with evidence rather than assertion.
3. Carry a single teaching incident through a complete reflective process and name what each stage produced.
4. Distinguish the types of reflection and match at least three activities to the type each promotes.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1's standards and barriers. Reflection without the standards is what this unit has to prevent: a
teacher who writes "the lesson went badly next time I will try harder" has described a problem but has
not evaluated it against any standard. The standards give reflection its rigour.

## In this unit

1. [Three words that are not the same](./topic-01) - reflective thinking, the reflective process and reflective practice distinguished.
2. [Why reflect at all](./topic-02) - what reflection is claimed to buy, and what the evidence actually shows.
3. [How it is actually done](./topic-03) - the reflective process end to end, applied to a real lesson.
4. [Types of reflection, and what promotes it](./topic-04) - levels of reflection and the activities that produce each.

## How to use this unit

Each topic builds on the one before. Topic 4.1 gives you the vocabulary; Topic 4.2 gives you the
reason to care; Topic 4.3 shows you the method; Topic 4.4 shows you that the method has levels, and
that most teachers stop too early. Work through them in order, and keep a single teaching incident in
mind throughout - the method lands better when you have a real lesson to apply it to.
