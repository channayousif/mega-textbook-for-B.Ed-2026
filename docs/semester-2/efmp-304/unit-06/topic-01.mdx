## A real classroom situation

A trainee teacher at a college in Hyderabad is told to submit a "reflective journal" at the end of
her practicum. She hands in a notebook with an entry for every day of the eight weeks. Each entry
reads like this:

> "Monday: Taught grade 7, chapter 4, section 2. Completed the exercise. Homework set. Tuesday:
> Taught grade 7, chapter 4, section 3. Pupils were noisy. Gave extra work. Wednesday: Test conducted."

The supervisor marks it as "poor - no reflection shown". The trainee is upset. "I wrote every day,"
she says. "What more did she want?"

What the supervisor wanted was not more writing. It was a different *kind* of writing. The trainee had
kept a diary. The supervisor wanted a journal. This topic explains the difference.

## Explanation

### A reflective journal is not a diary

A <Glossary term="Reflective journal" /> records teaching experiences *in order to learn from them*.
A diary records events *in order to remember them*. The difference is the purpose, and the purpose
determines everything else. A diary entry says what happened. A journal entry says what happened, why
it mattered, and what the writer will do differently as a result.

The trainee's notebook was a diary. It answered the question "what did I do each day?" A reflective
journal answers the question "what did I learn from what I did, and how will that change what I do
next?" Both are useful records. But only one is a journal.

### A reflective journal is not a lesson plan

A lesson plan prepares you for a lesson that has not happened yet. A journal reflects on a lesson
that has already happened. The two are related - a good journal entry may change your next lesson plan
- but they are different documents with different timings and different purposes. A trainee who writes
"tomorrow I will do X" in a journal is writing a lesson plan, not a reflection.

### A reflective journal is not a scheme of work

A scheme of work maps coverage across a term: which chapters, in which order, by which week. A
journal examines the *experience* of teaching those chapters. The scheme of work says "week 3, chapter
4". The journal says "chapter 4 did not go the way I expected, and here is why". The scheme of work is
a map. The journal is an analysis of the journey.

### A reflective journal is not an incident report

An incident report documents a specific event (an accident, a disciplinary issue, a complaint) for an
official record. A journal examines incidents for the writer's own learning. An incident report is
written for someone else (a head, a board). A journal is written primarily for the writer - though
on this course, it is also read and marked by a supervisor, which is a complication Topic 6.2
addresses.

### What a journal actually does

Jasper (2003) describes three things a reflective journal does that no other document does:

1. **It makes thinking visible.** Writing forces you to be specific. A vague thought ("that lesson was
   bad") has to become a specific claim ("the pupils could not answer question 3 because I had not
   taught the prerequisite concept") in order to be written down. That specificity is itself a form
   of analysis.

2. **It creates a record that can be revisited.** A teacher who keeps a journal for a term can look back
   across weeks and see patterns they could not see day to day. "The same kind of question confuses
   them every time" is a finding that only exists across entries, not within one.

3. **It supports the reflective cycle.** A journal is the medium in which the cycle from Unit 5 runs.
   The description is written, the analysis is written, the decision is written, and the next entry's
   opening sentence (the return arrow) is written. Without a journal, the cycle has no container.

**Common misconception: "a journal is a diary."** It is the most common confusion in assessed
reflection files across Pakistani teacher education. Trainees write diaries because diaries feel
natural and journals feel demanding. The mark scheme distinguishes them: a diary gets credit for
regularity and coverage; a journal gets credit for analysis and learning. On this course, the
assessed file is a journal, and a diary submitted as a journal is marked down for lacking reflection.

## Activity: Journal or something else?

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Each pair takes the five written extracts below. For each one, decide whether it is a **reflective
journal entry** or something else (diary, lesson plan, scheme of work, incident report). Write one
sentence naming the evidence.

1. "Week 5: chapter 4 (fractions), sections 1-3. Assessment: exercise 4.1."
2. "The group task took longer than planned. I think the instructions were unclear. Next time I will write them on the board before the groups start."
3. "Grade 8, period 3. Zainab and Sana argued during the lesson. Sent both to the head teacher."
4. "Monday: grade 7 period 2. Tuesday: grade 8 period 4. Wednesday: grade 7 period 1."
5. "I asked a question and nobody answered. I think I have been asking questions that only the top pupils can answer. I need to design questions at three difficulty levels."

Then swap with another pair. Where you disagree, say which of the three purposes of a journal (making thinking visible, creating a revisible record, supporting the cycle) is present or absent.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name the four things a reflective journal is not.
2. *(Understand)* In two or three sentences, explain the difference between a diary and a reflective journal. Use a school example.
3. *(Apply)* A trainee writes, "Tomorrow I will teach chapter 5 using group work." Is this a journal entry? Justify in one sentence.
4. *(Apply)* A teacher writes, "The pupils failed the test. The test was hard." Which kind of document is this most likely to be, and what would make it a journal entry?

## Summary

A reflective journal is not a diary (which records events), a lesson plan (which prepares for
teaching), a scheme of work (which maps coverage), or an incident report (which documents an event).
A journal examines teaching experience in order to learn from it: it makes thinking visible, creates
a revisible record, and supports the reflective cycle. The trainee who confuses a diary with a journal
is the most common failure in assessed reflection files. The difference is the presence of analysis,
not the regularity of the writing.

- [ ] I can take a written extract and classify it as a journal or something else, with evidence.
- [ ] I can explain the three purposes a journal serves.
- [ ] I can say why regularity alone does not make a diary a journal.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find one piece of writing from your own practicum so far - a lesson plan, a diary, a note to yourself.
Read it and ask: is this a journal? If not, what would I have to add to make it one? Write one sentence
naming the missing element.

## Summative task

Here is a page from a trainee's notebook:

> "Monday 6 March: Taught grade 7 English. Lesson: essay writing. Did the introduction together. Pupils
> wrote their own. Most introductions were copied from the example. I told them not to copy."
>
> "Tuesday 7 March: Taught grade 7 English. Lesson: essay writing, the body paragraph. Used the board.
> Pupils copied the structure. Better than Monday."

Write 150 to 200 words that (a) names what kind of document this is and gives two pieces of evidence,
(b) says what a reflective journal entry on the same Monday lesson would include that this does not,
and (c) rewrites the Monday entry as a reflective journal entry that reaches the analysis stage.

**Mini-rubric**

| Criterion | Levels |
|---|---|
| *Document classification* | 0-2: correctly identified as a diary with two pieces of evidence / identified with one piece / wrong. |
| *What a journal would add* | 0-2: names analysis (why copying happened, what to try, how to know it worked) / a general addition / none. |
| *Rewrite* | 0-3: a rewrite that reaches the analysis stage with a testable hypothesis (**Analyze*: the rewrite turns on the specific content of the Monday lesson) / a rewrite that stays descriptive / no real rewrite. |
| *Clarity* | 0-1: within the word band / not. |
