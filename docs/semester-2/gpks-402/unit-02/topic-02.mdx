the textbook lists the milestones but does not explain the struggle between them. The pupil is asking

Those seven years were not empty. They were the most intense period of political struggle in the

## Explanation

### The formation of the All-India Muslim League

The All-India Muslim League was founded in 1906 in Dhaka, in response to the political needs of
Muslims after the partition of Bengal and the growing influence of the Indian National Congress.
The Muslim League's original purpose was to safeguard Muslim political interests within a united
India, not to demand a separate state. Its early demands were for separate electorates (the right
for Muslims to vote for their own representatives) and for guaranteed representation in legislatures.

Over time, the Muslim League's position changed. The experience of Congress rule in the provinces
after 1937 convinced many Muslim League leaders that Muslims would be permanently disadvantaged in
a democratic India where Hindus were the majority. This shift led to the adoption of the Lahore
Resolution in 1940, which formally demanded independent states for Muslims.

### Key events and milestones

The Pakistan Movement can be traced through a small number of decisive moments (Rabbani, n.d.):

- **1906** - All-India Muslim League founded in Dhaka. The political voice of Muslims is organised.
- **1930** - Allama Iqbal's Allahabad address proposes a separate Muslim state in the northwest.
  The idea moves from political protection to territorial demand.
- **1940** - Lahore Resolution (also called the Pakistan Resolution) is passed at the Muslim
  League's session in Lahore. It formally demands "independent states" for Muslims in the northwest
  and east of India. This is the founding political document of Pakistan.
- **1945-46** - Provincial elections. The Muslim League wins the vast majority of Muslim seats,
  demonstrating that it speaks for the Muslim masses. This electoral mandate strengthens its claim.
- **1946** - The Cabinet Mission Plan proposes a united India with grouped provinces. The Muslim
  League initially accepts but then rejects the plan when it becomes clear that the grouping will
  not give Muslims the autonomy they sought. Direct Action Day (16 August 1946) marks the start of
  communal violence that makes partition seem inevitable.
- **1947** - Lord Mountbatten, the last Viceroy, presents the plan for partition. India and
  Pakistan become independent dominions on 14 and 15 August 1947 respectively.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees arrive believing that "the Pakistan Resolution of 1940
immediately created Pakistan." It did not. The Resolution was a demand, not a reality. It took
seven years of elections, negotiations, communal violence and the final partition plan for that
demand to become a state. A teacher who treats 1940 as the end of the story misses the political
struggle that actually produced Pakistan.

### The creation of Pakistan

The creation of Pakistan was not only a political achievement; it was accompanied by one of the
largest mass migrations in history. Millions of Muslims moved from India to Pakistan, and millions
of Hindus and Sikhs moved in the opposite direction. The migration was marked by widespread
violence, displacement and loss. For a teacher, this human dimension matters: Pakistan was not only
born in the Constituent Assembly on 14 August 1947; it was also born in the refugee columns and the
trauma of partition that millions of families experienced.

## Activity: Seven years, seven events

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Take the seven years from 1940 to 1947. For each year from 1940 to 1947, write one sentence saying
what happened and one sentence saying why it mattered for the creation of Pakistan. Then answer:
which year do you think was the turning point, and why?

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name three milestones of the Pakistan Movement between 1906 and 1947.
2. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between the Muslim League's original purpose (1906) and its demand in 1940.
3. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "Pakistan was created on 23 March 1940." Using what you learned in this topic, explain why that date is significant but not the date of Pakistan's creation.

## Summary

The All-India Muslim League (1906) gave Muslims a political voice. Iqbal's address (1930) and the
Lahore Resolution (1940) turned that voice into a demand for a separate state. Elections (1945-46),
the failure of the Cabinet Mission (1946) and the Mountbatten Plan (1947) turned the demand into a
state. Pakistan was created on 14-15 August 1947, accompanied by mass migration and partition
violence.
