# Unit 2 Assessment - Reform Movements and the Pakistan Movement

## Unit summary

This unit examined the two-century historical continuum that culminated in the creation of Pakistan on 14 August 1947. Following the decline of Mughal authority and the 1857 War of Independence, South Asian Muslims faced political disempowerment, economic distress, and intellectual stagnation. The response emerged in two major waves of reform:

1. **The Reformers and Intellectual Awakening (Topic 2.1):** In the 18th century, Shah Waliullah diagnosed the moral and sectarian roots of decline, translating the Quran into Persian to make scripture broadly accessible and formulating *tatbiq* to reconcile legal schools. In the post-1857 era, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan spearheaded the Aligarh Movement, arguing that survival required modern scientific education, critical rationalism, and institutional self-reliance. Through the Scientific Society, *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq*, and the MAO College at Aligarh, Sir Syed fostered an educated Muslim intelligentsia that transitioned from cultural self-defense to proactive civic leadership.
2. **The Pakistan Movement and Statehood (Topic 2.2):** Catalyzed by the 1905 Partition of Bengal, the Simla Deputation secured separate electorates in 1906, and the All-India Muslim League was established at Dhaka to defend Muslim political rights. While Muslim leaders under Quaid-e-Azam pursued constitutional cooperation through the 1916 Lucknow Pact, the majoritarian centralization of the 1928 Nehru Report and the exclusionary governance of Congress ministries (1937–1939) demonstrated that minority rights could not survive in an undivided unitary state. Guided by Allama Iqbal's 1930 philosophical vision, the Muslim League adopted the Lahore Resolution on 23 March 1940, demanding independent sovereign Muslim states. The decisive 1945–46 general elections provided an undisputed democratic mandate, leading to the partition of British India and the birth of Pakistan in August 1947.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Shah Waliullah translated the Holy Quran into which language to make divine guidance directly accessible to educated South Asians?
   - A) Urdu
   - B) Persian
   - C) English
   - D) Punjabi

2. *(Remember)* In which year was the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College founded by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan at Aligarh?
   - A) 1857
   - B) 1867
   - C) 1875
   - D) 1906

3. *(Understand)* What was the primary purpose of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's Scientific Society, established in 1864?
   - A) To recruit Muslim youth into the British armed forces
   - B) To translate modern European scientific, historical, and agricultural works into Urdu
   - C) To organize political protests against colonial taxation
   - D) To print traditional theological treatises exclusively

4. *(Understand)* What was the core constitutional demand presented by the Simla Deputation to Viceroy Lord Minto in October 1906?
   - A) An immediate British departure from South Asia
   - B) Complete nationalization of land ownership
   - C) Separate electorates and proportional representation for Muslims
   - D) The cancellation of the Partition of Bengal

5. *(Remember)* In which city was the All-India Muslim League officially founded on 30 December 1906?
   - A) Aligarh
   - B) Lahore
   - C) Simla
   - D) Dhaka

6. *(Understand)* Why was the Lucknow Pact of 1916 regarded as a landmark constitutional milestone for South Asian Muslims?
   - A) The British government agreed to divide India into two dominions immediately
   - B) The Indian National Congress formally accepted separate electorates for Muslims
   - C) Urdu was officially adopted as the sole national language of British India
   - D) The Muslim League agreed to merge permanently into the Congress party

7. *(Apply)* A student claims that Quaid-e-Azam's Fourteen Points (1929) were a demand for immediate partition. How does historical evidence refute this claim?
   - A) The points proposed a unitary government centered in New Delhi
   - B) The points sought constitutional minority safeguards and provincial autonomy within a united federal India
   - C) The points demanded the resignation of all elected Muslim legislators
   - D) The points suggested British rule should continue indefinitely without reforms

8. *(Understand)* In his historic 1930 Allahabad Presidential Address, what political solution did Allama Muhammad Iqbal propose?
   - A) A consolidated Muslim state in northwestern South Asia
   - B) An immediate launch of armed resistance against the British Empire
   - C) The complete dissolution of all regional political parties
   - D) The adoption of a unitary parliamentary system under Congress leadership

9. *(Apply)* In the 1945–46 general elections, the Muslim League won all 30 Central Assembly Muslim seats and 87% of provincial Muslim seats. What was the direct political consequence of this mandate?
   - A) It established Congress as the sole spokesman for all Indian communities
   - B) It proved that the Muslim League possessed undisputed democratic legitimacy to demand a separate homeland
   - C) It convinced the British government to cancel upcoming constitutional negotiations
   - D) It led to the immediate revocation of provincial autonomy

10. *(Apply)* A high school teacher explains that the 1940 Lahore Resolution was an abrupt and unprovoked decision. Which historical evidence best corrects this misconception?
    - A) The resolution was drafted by British officials overnight
    - B) The resolution followed twenty-five years of failed constitutional negotiations and the exclusionary governance of Congress ministries (1937–1939)
    - C) The Muslim League had never participated in provincial elections prior to 1940
    - D) All communities in British India had already voted in favor of partition in 1935

1. *(Understand)* State two key measures undertaken by Shah Waliullah to counter moral and sectarian decline in 18th-century South Asia.
2. *(Understand)* Describe the primary editorial and social objectives of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's journal *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq*.
3. *(Understand)* State two reasons why Sir Syed Ahmad Khan advised Indian Muslims to prioritize modern education rather than joining active political agitation in the 1880s.
4. *(Understand)* Explain the significance of the 1867 Hindi-Urdu controversy in shifting Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's views on joint Hindu-Muslim political cooperation.
5. *(Analyze)* Explain why the demand for separate electorates was considered essential by Muslim leaders under a majoritarian electoral system in British India.
6. *(Understand)* Identify two key constitutional provisions agreed upon by the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League in the 1916 Lucknow Pact.
7. *(Analyze)* Analyze how the provisions of the 1928 Nehru Report directly led to Quaid-e-Azam's formulation of his Fourteen Points in 1929.
8. *(Understand)* Summarize the central concept proposed by Allama Muhammad Iqbal in his 1930 Allahabad Presidential Address regarding the territorial future of South Asian Muslims.
9. *(Analyze)* Why did Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah and the Muslim League observe the "Day of Deliverance" on 22 December 1939?
10. *(Analyze)* Explain how the 1940 Lahore Resolution redefined the political objective of the Muslim League from constitutional safeguards to national self-determination.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* Trace the development of 19th-century Muslim educational reform from the aftermath of the 1857 War of Independence to the expansion of the Aligarh Movement. Analyze how the institutions founded by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan prepared a new generation of educated professionals capable of articulating Muslim interests within modern constitutional frameworks.
2. *(Evaluate)* Compare and evaluate the reform philosophies of Shah Waliullah and Sir Syed Ahmad Khan. In your response, examine their diagnoses of South Asian Muslim decline, their reform methods (traditional/spiritual vs. modern/scientific), and their enduring impacts on South Asian Muslim identity.
3. *(Analyze)* Analyze the constitutional evolution of the Pakistan Movement between the 1916 Lucknow Pact and the 1940 Lahore Resolution. Explain how the shift from cooperative federalism to the demand for sovereign statehood was driven by the breakdown of constitutional negotiations and the experience of the 1937–1939 Congress ministries.
4. *(Evaluate)* Assess the decisive role played by the 1945–46 general elections and the Cabinet Mission Plan in the establishment of Pakistan. Evaluate whether the creation of Pakistan in August 1947 was the product of a democratic and constitutional mandate or sudden colonial partition.
5. *(Create)* Design a comprehensive three-part instructional unit outline for Grade 9 Pakistan Studies that connects 18th-century reform movements (Shah Waliullah and Sir Syed) to the achievement of independence in 1947. Formulate learning objectives, classroom inquiry tasks, and primary source discussion questions that demonstrate historical continuity.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Shah Waliullah translated the Holy Quran into Persian, the lingua franca of educated South Asians, breaking clerical monopolies and enabling direct comprehension.
2. C - Sir Syed Ahmad Khan established MAO College at Aligarh in 1875 (upgraded from school to college in 1877) to impart modern scientific education alongside Islamic studies.
3. B - The Scientific Society, founded at Ghazipur in 1864, translated major European scientific, historical, and agricultural works into Urdu to make modern knowledge accessible.
4. C - The Simla Deputation of October 1906 sought separate electorates and proportional weightage to ensure that Muslims would not be permanently outvoted in joint constituencies.
5. D - The All-India Muslim League was officially founded at Dhaka on 30 December 1906 during the annual session of the Muhammadan Educational Conference.
6. B - In the 1916 Lucknow Pact, the Congress formally accepted separate electorates for Muslims, conceding their distinct constitutional identity.
7. B - Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929) sought a federal constitution with residuary powers in provinces and minority protections within a united Indian federation, not partition.
8. A - In his 1930 Allahabad Address, Iqbal proposed the creation of a consolidated Muslim state in northwestern South Asia comprising Punjab, NWFP, Sindh, and Balochistan.
9. B - The sweeping victory in 1945–46 (winning 100% of central Muslim seats and 87% of provincial Muslim seats) provided an undeniable democratic mandate for the Pakistan demand.
10. B - The Lahore Resolution of 1940 was the culmination of two decades of rejected constitutional safeguards and the discriminatory governance of Congress ministries (1937–1939).

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

#### RRQ 1
- **Model Answer:** Shah Waliullah translated the Holy Quran into Persian to make divine revelation directly accessible to educated Muslims beyond specialized Arabic clerics. He also introduced the principle of *tatbiq* (reconciliation) to bridge theological and juristic disputes among differing schools of Islamic jurisprudence (*fiqh*).
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the translation of the Quran into Persian and its purpose (accessibility).
  - 1 Mark: Identifies *tatbiq* or the reconciliation of juristic/sectarian divisions.
  - 1 Mark: Explains the broader social or moral intent (countering intellectual stagnation and disunity).

#### RRQ 2
- **Model Answer:** The primary objective of *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq* (The Social Reformer), launched in 1870, was to stimulate intellectual awakening, eliminate outdated prejudices and fatalistic attitudes, and encourage Muslims to embrace modern rationalism, scientific inquiry, and ethical self-discipline.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the journal as a vehicle for social and cultural reform.
  - 1 Mark: Mentions the rejection of fatalism/dogma and promotion of rational inquiry.
  - 1 Mark: Connects this reform to enabling Muslims to progress in modern society.

#### RRQ 3
- **Model Answer:** Sir Syed advised Muslims to avoid active political agitation because, following the 1857 trauma, Muslims lacked modern education, economic strength, and technical training. He recognized that joining political agitation without educational parity would leave Muslims vulnerable to colonial retaliation and dominated by numerically superior, better-educated communities.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the educational deficit of Muslims in the 1880s compared to other groups.
  - 1 Mark: Cites the risk of colonial suspicion or retaliation following the 1857 uprising.
  - 1 Mark: Explains that educational empowerment was a necessary prerequisite before entering competitive politics.

#### RRQ 4
- **Model Answer:** The 1867 Hindi-Urdu controversy in Benares, where Hindu leaders campaigned to replace Urdu (written in Persian script) with Hindi (written in Devanagari script), convinced Sir Syed that cultural cooperation was precarious. He realized that language was an essential marker of cultural identity, leading him to conclude that the two communities had distinct cultural futures.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: States the dispute over language and script in 1867.
  - 1 Mark: Explains Sir Syed's realization that language was an inseparable component of cultural survival.
  - 1 Mark: Notes the shift from advocating joint Hindu-Muslim political action to distinct community protection.

#### RRQ 5
- **Model Answer:** Under a first-past-the-post joint electorate system, a 25% minority community would consistently be outvoted in mixed constituencies by the 75% majority, effectively denying them authentic representation. Separate electorates ensured that Muslims elected representatives who genuinely championed their community's constitutional and cultural interests.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the numerical vulnerability of a minority under simple majority voting.
  - 1 Mark: Explains how separate electorates guaranteed authentic community representation.
  - 1 Mark: **Analyze**: Connects the electoral mechanism to preventing perpetual political disenfranchisement.

#### RRQ 6
- **Model Answer:** In the 1916 Lucknow Pact, the Congress formally agreed to separate electorates for Muslims in provincial legislatures. In return, the Muslim League agreed to accept reduced representation in Muslim-majority provinces (Punjab and Bengal) in exchange for weightage (extra seats) in Muslim-minority provinces.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Names Congress acceptance of separate electorates.
  - 1 Mark: Names the system of weightage (representation adjustments across provinces).
  - 1 Mark: Recognizes the pact as a mutual constitutional compromise between League and Congress.

#### RRQ 7
- **Model Answer:** The 1928 Nehru Report rejected separate electorates, refused guaranteed Muslim representation in the central legislature, and proposed a centralized unitary government with residuary powers at the center. In response, Quaid-e-Azam issued his Fourteen Points in 1929 to articulate the non-negotiable minimum safeguards needed to protect federal autonomy and minority rights.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the unitary/majoritarian provisions of the Nehru Report.
  - 1 Mark: Identifies key counter-proposals in the Fourteen Points (provincial autonomy, residuary powers, 1/3 central seats).
  - 1 Mark: **Analyze**: Explains how the rejection of federal power-sharing precipitated Jinnah's "parting of the ways."

#### RRQ 8
- **Model Answer:** In his 1930 Allahabad Address, Allama Muhammad Iqbal proposed that the consolidation of the contiguous Muslim-majority areas of northwestern India (Punjab, NWFP, Sindh, and Balochistan) into a self-governing state was the final destiny of South Asian Muslims, establishing that Islam's ethical and social principles required an autonomous territorial expression.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the geographical units proposed (northwestern provinces).
  - 1 Mark: States the proposal for an autonomous/self-governing Muslim political unit.
  - 1 Mark: Explains Iqbal's ideological rationale (Islam as a comprehensive social and moral order).

#### RRQ 9
- **Model Answer:** The Muslim League observed the Day of Deliverance on 22 December 1939 to mark relief from the governance of Congress ministries (1937–1939). During their rule, Congress ministries enforced policies that discriminated against Muslim culture, including the mandatory recitation of *Bande Mataram*, the *Vidya Mandir* educational scheme, and the exclusion of the League from government.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Names the resignation of Congress ministries in late 1939.
  - 1 Mark: Cites specific discriminatory policies (*Bande Mataram*, *Vidya Mandir*, educational/cultural exclusion).
  - 1 Mark: **Analyze**: Explains how this period convinced Muslims that majoritarian rule would erode their rights.

#### RRQ 10
- **Model Answer:** Before 1940, the Muslim League sought constitutional safeguards, separate electorates, and provincial autonomy within a united Indian federation. The 1940 Lahore Resolution abandoned the framework of minority safeguards within an undivided India, defining Muslims as a distinct nation entitled to complete sovereign statehood in their majority zones.
- **Mark Scheme (3 Marks):**
  - 1 Mark: Identifies the pre-1940 framework of minority safeguards within a federation.
  - 1 Mark: States the 1940 transition to demanding independent sovereign states.
  - 1 Mark: **Analyze**: Explains the shift from viewing Muslims as a political minority to defining them as a sovereign nation.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Educational Reform and the Aligarh Movement
- **Task:** Trace the development of 19th-century Muslim educational reform from post-1857 to the Aligarh Movement, analyzing how it prepared leaders for modern constitutional governance.
- **Analytic Rubric (10 Marks):**
  - *Historical Context & Diagnosis (0–2 Marks):* Accurately details the post-1857 political repression, cultural isolationism, and economic marginalization of South Asian Muslims.
  - *Key Institutions & Reform Steps (0–3 Marks):* Describes the Scientific Society (1864), *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq* (1870), MAO College (1875), and the Muhammadan Educational Conference (1886) with precise historical facts.
  - *Analytical Depth - Institutional Impact (0–3 Marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze**: Evaluates how residential collegiate education and modern curriculum fostered an articulate middle class of lawyers, civil servants, and journalists who became the organizational backbone of the Muslim League.
  - *Clarity, Organization & Style (0–2 Marks):* Employs clear academic register, cohesive paragraph transitions, and precise terminology.

#### ERQ 2: Comparative Reform Philosophies (Shah Waliullah vs. Sir Syed)
- **Task:** Compare and evaluate the reform philosophies of Shah Waliullah and Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, examining their diagnoses of decline, reform methods, and enduring impacts.
- **Analytic Rubric (10 Marks):**
  - *Diagnosis of Decline (0–2 Marks):* Compares Shah Waliullah's focus on moral, spiritual, and sectarian decay with Sir Syed's focus on scientific and educational obsolescence post-1857.
  - *Methodologies & Interventions (0–3 Marks):* Analyzes Quranic translation and *tatbiq* versus modern institutional schooling, scientific translation, and cultural modernization.
  - *Comparative Evaluation (0–3 Marks, **Evaluate**):* **Evaluate**: Justifies an assessment of how both thinkers contributed complementary religious-intellectual and socio-political foundations for South Asian Muslim identity.
  - *Clarity & Synthesis (0–2 Marks):* Demonstrates exceptional comparative organization and balanced synthesis.

#### ERQ 3: Constitutional Evolution (1916 to 1940)
- **Task:** Analyze the constitutional evolution of the Pakistan Movement between the 1916 Lucknow Pact and the 1940 Lahore Resolution, focusing on failed federal negotiations and Congress rule.
- **Analytic Rubric (10 Marks):**
  - *Historical Progression (0–2 Marks):* Outlines the sequence from the 1916 Lucknow Pact to the 1928 Nehru Report, 1929 Fourteen Points, 1930 Allahabad Address, and 1937–1939 ministries.
  - *Constitutional Analysis of Rupture (0–3 Marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze**: Critically evaluates why the Nehru Report's unitary bias and the exclusionary practices of Congress ministries made power-sharing within an undivided India impossible.
  - *Significance of the 1940 Resolution (0–3 Marks):* Explains how the Lahore Resolution synthesized Iqbal's vision into a non-negotiable territorial demand for independent sovereign statehood.
  - *Structure & Coherence (0–2 Marks):* Well-argued, logically organized, and historically precise.

#### ERQ 4: The 1945–46 Mandate and Independence
- **Task:** Assess the role of the 1945–46 elections and the Cabinet Mission Plan in the establishment of Pakistan, evaluating whether independence resulted from democratic mandate or sudden colonial partition.
- **Analytic Rubric (10 Marks):**
  - *Electoral Evidence (0–3 Marks):* Cites specific data from the 1945–46 elections (100% central Muslim seats, 87% provincial Muslim seats) and explains their democratic significance.
  - *Negotiation Dynamics (0–2 Marks):* Analyzes the Cabinet Mission Plan's three-tier grouping, League acceptance, Congress reservations, and the 3 June Partition Plan.
  - *Critical Historical Evaluation (0–3 Marks, **Evaluate**):* **Evaluate**: Evaluates competing historical perspectives, providing evidence-based justification that Pakistan was created through a democratic and constitutional mandate.
  - *Clarity & Logic (0–2 Marks):* Sophisticated historical writing, clear reasoning, and flawless organization.

#### ERQ 5: Instructional Unit Design (Integrative)
- **Task:** Design a comprehensive three-part instructional unit outline for Grade 9 Pakistan Studies connecting 18th-century reform to 1947 independence.
- **Analytic Rubric (10 Marks):**
  - *Curricular Structure & Continuity (0–3 Marks, **Create**):* **Create**: Structures a cohesive unit plan linking Shah Waliullah, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, the Muslim League, and 1947 independence as a continuous historical continuum.
  - *Pedagogical Activities & Primary Sources (0–3 Marks):* Proposes engaging classroom tasks, including primary source excerpts (e.g. *Musaddas-e-Hali*, Fourteen Points, Lahore Resolution) and collaborative inquiry questions.
  - *Assessment Alignment (0–2 Marks):* Integrates formative checks and summative assessment tasks that measure higher-order thinking (Understand, Analyze).
  - *Professional Classroom Utility (0–2 Marks):* Directly applicable to secondary classrooms in Pakistan, with realistic time allocations and clear teacher guidance.
