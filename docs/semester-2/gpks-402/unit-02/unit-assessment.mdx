## Unit summary

This unit traced the groundwork of Pakistan across two topics:

- Topic 2.1 named the reformers: Shah Waliullah revived Muslim identity; Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founded
  the Aligarh Movement; the Muslim intelligentsia turned educational awakening into political
  consciousness.
- Topic 2.2 traced the Pakistan Movement: the Muslim League (1906), Iqbal's address (1930), the
  Lahore Resolution (1940), elections (1945-46), the Cabinet Mission (1946) and the creation of
  Pakistan (1947).

Together these show that Pakistan was not created in a single moment but grew from decades of
reform and political struggle.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Who founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh in 1875?
   - A) Shah Waliullah
   - B) Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
   - C) Allama Iqbal
   - D) Liaquat Ali Khan

2. *(Remember)* In which year was the All-India Muslim League founded?
   - A) 1857
   - B) 1898
   - C) 1906
   - D) 1940

3. *(Understand)* What was the main purpose of the Aligarh Movement?
   - A) To demand a separate Muslim state
   - B) To provide Muslims with modern education combined with Islamic studies
   - C) To fight the War of Independence
   - D) To oppose the British government

4. *(Understand)* What did the Lahore Resolution of 1940 demand?
   - A) Complete independence for a united India
   - B) Separate electorates for Muslims
   - C) Independent states for Muslims in the northwest and east of India
   - D) The end of British rule

5. *(Apply)* A teacher says, "Sir Syed Ahmad Khan wanted Pakistan." How would you correct this?
   - A) Sir Syed wanted a separate state, so the statement is correct
   - B) Sir Syed argued for Hindu-Muslim distinction and Muslim political representation, but worked for a united India with a Muslim voice
   - C) Sir Syed had no political views at all
   - D) Sir Syed wanted Hindus and Muslims to merge into one nation

6. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "Pakistan was created on 23 March 1940." What is the significance of that date?
   - A) It is the date of the creation of Pakistan
   - B) It is the date of the Lahore Resolution, which demanded independent states for Muslims
   - C) It is the date of the Muslim League's founding
   - D) It is the date of Iqbal's Allahabad address

7. *(Understand)* Which event demonstrated that the Muslim League had the support of the Muslim masses?
   - A) The War of Independence in 1857
   - B) The founding of Aligarh College in 1875
   - C) The 1945-46 provincial elections, in which the Muslim League won most Muslim seats
   - D) The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946

8. *(Apply)* What was the human cost of the creation of Pakistan?
   - A) There was no cost; the transition was peaceful
   - B) Millions of people migrated between India and Pakistan, accompanied by widespread violence
   - C) Only political leaders were affected
   - D) Only the British suffered losses

9. *(Apply)* A textbook jumps from 1857 to 1906 without explaining what happened in between. What does Topic 2.1 suggest is missing?
   - A) The geography of Pakistan
   - B) The reform movements: Shah Waliullah, Sir Syed and the Aligarh Movement
   - C) The Constitution of 1973
   - D) Nothing; the gap is correct

10. *(Apply)* Which of the following best describes the link between the Aligarh Movement and the Muslim League?
    - A) There was no link; they were unrelated
    - B) The Aligarh Movement produced educated Muslims who later led the Muslim League
    - C) The Muslim League founded the Aligarh College
    - D) Both were founded in 1906

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's political position and the demand of the Lahore Resolution in 1940.

2. *(Understand)* Explain three milestones of the Pakistan Movement between 1906 and 1947 and why each mattered.

3. *(Apply)* A colleague says, "The Pakistan Movement started in 1940." Using what you learned in Topic 2.1, give two reasons that statement is incomplete.

4. *(Understand)* What was the role of Muslim intelligentsia in the Pakistan Movement? Give one sentence.

5. *(Apply)* Take the statement: "The Muslim League was founded to demand Pakistan." State whether this is accurate for 1906 or 1940, and give one reason.

6. *(Analyze)* A teacher says, "Shah Waliullah directly proposed the creation of Pakistan." Explain in two or three sentences why this statement is not accurate.

7. *(Understand)* What was the significance of the 1945-46 elections for the Pakistan Movement?

8. *(Apply)* A Grade 8 textbook lists the Lahore Resolution as the "day Pakistan was created." Using Topic 2.2, explain in one or two sentences why this is misleading.

9. *(Analyze)* Why did the Muslim League reject the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946? Give one reason.

10. *(Apply)* A pupil confuses the Lahore Resolution with the actual independence of Pakistan. Explain the difference in one or two sentences.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (80-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit presented the Aligarh Movement as a foundation for the Pakistan Movement. Compare these two positions: (a) "The Aligarh Movement was only about education, not politics" and (b) "The Aligarh Movement was the foundation of the Pakistan Movement." Which is more accurate, and why?

2. *(Evaluate)* A textbook presents the Pakistan Movement as a simple sequence: Muslim League founded (1906), Lahore Resolution (1940), Pakistan created (1947). Evaluate this approach using what you learned in Topic 2.2. What does it capture, and what does it miss?

3. *(Analyze)* A pupil says, "Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and Jinnah wanted the same thing." Analyse this statement: explain the difference between Sir Syed's position and Jinnah's demand, and give one piece of evidence from the unit for each.

4. *(Evaluate)* The creation of Pakistan in 1947 was accompanied by mass migration and violence. Evaluate whether a Pakistan Studies teacher should include this human dimension in a lesson on the Pakistan Movement. Give reasons.

5. *(Create/Evaluate)* A new teacher asks you how to explain "why Pakistan took 41 years from the Muslim League's founding to independence" to a Grade 8 class. Using the milestones from Topic 2.2, write a short explanation (100-150 words) suitable for pupils. Draw on both topics from the unit.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh in 1875. A lived in the 1700s, C was a poet-philosopher, D was a later political leader.
2. **C** - 1906. A is the War of Independence, B is the year of Sir Syed's death, D is the Lahore Resolution.
3. **B** - The Aligarh Movement provided modern education combined with Islamic studies. A is wrong (it was not a demand for a state), C is wrong (it came after 1857), D is wrong (Sir Syed worked with the British).
4. **C** - Independent states for Muslims. A is wrong (it was not for a united India), B is wrong (separate electorates were an earlier demand), D is wrong (it was more specific than just ending British rule).
5. **B** - Sir Syed argued for distinction and representation but worked for a united India. A is wrong, C is wrong (he had clear political views), D is wrong (he argued for distinction, not merger).
6. **B** - 23 March 1940 is the date of the Lahore Resolution. A is wrong (Pakistan was created in August 1947), C is wrong (1906), D is wrong (1930).
7. **C** - The 1945-46 elections. A, B and D are wrong (they predate the electoral mandate).
8. **B** - Mass migration and violence. A is wrong (it was not peaceful), C is wrong (millions of ordinary people were affected), D is wrong (the British were not the main sufferers).
9. **B** - The reform movements. A is wrong (geography is not the gap), C is wrong (1973 is much later), D is wrong (the gap is real).
10. **B** - The Aligarh Movement produced educated Muslims who later led the Muslim League. A is wrong, C is wrong (the League did not found Aligarh), D is wrong (Aligarh was 1875).

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Sir Syed argued for Hindu-Muslim distinction and Muslim political representation within a united India. The Lahore Resolution demanded independent states for Muslims, a demand for separation. **Marks (2):** 1 - Sir Syed's position (united India with Muslim voice); 1 - Lahore Resolution's demand (separate states).

2. **Model:** 1906 (Muslim League founded), 1930 (Iqbal's address), 1940 (Lahore Resolution), 1947 (independence). Any three. **Marks (2):** 1 for two correct, 2 for three.

3. **Model:** The statement is incomplete because (a) the reform movements (Shah Waliullah, Sir Syed, Aligarh) prepared the ground before 1940, and (b) the Muslim League was founded in 1906, showing political organisation began earlier. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the reform movements; 1 - names the Muslim League's earlier founding.

4. **Model:** The Muslim intelligentsia turned the educational awakening of the Aligarh period into political consciousness, providing the leaders and thinkers of the Pakistan Movement. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the intelligentsia's role; 1 - links it to the Movement.

5. **Model:** It is accurate for 1940 but not for 1906. In 1906 the League's purpose was to safeguard Muslim interests within a united India; the demand for Pakistan came in 1940. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies 1940 as the turning point; 1 - explains the difference.

6. **Model:** Shah Waliullah revived the idea of a distinct Muslim identity in the 1700s, long before the demand for a separate state. He did not propose Pakistan; that demand came more than a century later. **Marks (3):** 1 - Shah Waliullah's period; 1 - his contribution (identity revival); 1 - the gap between his work and the Pakistan demand.

7. **Model:** The 1945-46 elections demonstrated that the Muslim League had the support of the Muslim masses, giving it an electoral mandate to speak for Muslims. **Marks (2):** 1 - states the election outcome; 1 - explains its significance (mandate).

8. **Model:** The Lahore Resolution was a demand for independent states, not the actual creation of Pakistan. Pakistan was created seven years later, in August 1947, after elections, the Cabinet Mission and the partition plan. **Marks (2):** 1 - distinguishes demand from reality; 1 - names the actual date.

9. **Model:** The Muslim League rejected the Cabinet Mission Plan because the grouping of provinces did not give Muslims the autonomy they sought, and it became clear that the plan would not lead to a separate state. **Marks (2):** 1 - states the reason (insufficient autonomy); 1 - links it to the demand for separation.

10. **Model:** The Lahore Resolution (1940) was a political demand for independent states. Pakistan's actual independence came on 14-15 August 1947, after seven years of political struggle. **Marks (2):** 1 - states what the Resolution was; 1 - states when Pakistan was actually created.
