## A real classroom situation

A teacher in a government school in Rawalpindi is teaching a lesson on the Pakistan Movement. A
pupil asks, "Sir, did the idea of Pakistan start only in 1940 with the Lahore Resolution? Or were
people thinking about it earlier?" The teacher realises that the textbook jumps from the War of
Independence in 1857 straight to the Muslim League in 1906, without explaining what happened in
between. The pupil's question is a good one: the Pakistan Movement did not come from nowhere. It
was prepared by decades of reform.

That gap between 1857 and 1906 is exactly what this topic fills. The reformers who worked during
those decades prepared the ground on which the Pakistan Movement later stood.

## Explanation

### Shah Waliullah: reform and revival

Shah Waliullah (1703-1762) was a Islamic scholar and reformer who lived during the decline of the
Mughal Empire. He saw that Muslim society in India was weakened by internal divisions, a departure
from Islamic principles, and political fragmentation. His response was a programme of religious
and social reform: he translated the Quran into Persian (the learned language of the time) so that
more Muslims could access it directly, he wrote works explaining Islamic jurisprudence in clear
terms, and he called for a return to the core principles of Islam as a basis for social renewal
(Rabbani, n.d.).

Shah Waliullah's significance for the later Pakistan Movement is indirect but real. He was among
the first thinkers to argue that Muslim identity in India rested on a distinct religious and
cultural foundation, not only on political power. That idea, developed by later thinkers, became
part of the intellectual soil from which the Two-Nation Theory grew.

### Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement

Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817-1898) was the most influential Muslim reformer of the 19th century. After
the War of Independence in 1857, he concluded that Muslims in India were falling behind because
they had rejected modern education. His solution was the Aligarh Movement: a programme of
educational reform that founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh in 1875 (later
Aligarh University). The college taught both modern Western subjects and Islamic studies, producing
a generation of Muslims who were comfortable in both worlds (Rabbani, n.d.).

Sir Syed also articulated a political idea that mattered for the future. He argued that Hindus and
Muslims were two distinct communities with different interests, and that Muslims needed their own
political voice to protect those ideas. This was not yet the demand for a separate state, but it was
the intellectual starting point from which that demand later grew.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees arrive believing that "Sir Syed Ahmad Khan directly
proposed the Two-Nation Theory or the creation of Pakistan." He did not. Sir Syed argued for
Hindu-Muslim distinction and for Muslim political representation, but he worked for a united India
in which Muslims had their own voice. The demand for a separate state came later, built on the
foundation he laid. A teacher who conflates Sir Syed with Jinnah will mislead pupils about the
chronology of ideas.

### The role of Muslim intelligentsia

The Aligarh Movement produced a generation of Muslim intellectuals and professionals who went on
to lead the political movement. These included poets and thinkers like Allama Iqbal, who gave the
Two-Nation Theory its philosophical depth, and political leaders like Liaquat Ali Khan. The
intelligentsia played a crucial role: they took the educational and cultural awakening of the
Aligarh period and turned it into a political programme. Without this class of educated Muslims,
the Pakistan Movement would have had no leaders, no thinkers and no organisers.

## Activity: Reform to political movement

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Draw a simple chain on a sheet of paper with four links. Label the links: (1) Shah Waliullah's
revival, (2) Sir Syed and Aligarh, (3) Muslim intelligentsia, (4) All-India Muslim League. For
each arrow between the links, write one sentence explaining how the first led to the second. Then
answer: which link do you think was the most important, and why?

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name two contributions of Shah Waliullah to Muslim reform in India.
2. *(Understand)* Explain the link between the Aligarh Movement and the founding of the All-India Muslim League in one or two sentences.
3. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "Sir Syed wanted Pakistan." Using what you learned in this topic, give two reasons that statement is not accurate.

## Summary

Shah Waliullah revived the idea of a distinct Muslim identity. Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founded the
Aligarh Movement, which produced a generation of educated Muslims. The Muslim intelligentsia turned
that educational awakening into political consciousness. Together, these reformers prepared the
ground for the All-India Muslim League and the Pakistan Movement.
