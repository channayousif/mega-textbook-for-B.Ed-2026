## Unit summary

This unit built a clear picture of what Pakistan Studies is and why Pakistan exists as a nation.

- Topic 1.1 gave the scope of Pakistan Studies: it is an interdisciplinary subject drawing on
  history, geography, economy, civics and culture. National ideology is the set of core ideas
  that explain why Pakistan exists; national identity is the sense of belonging those ideas create.
  The subject matters in schools because education is the main way a nation passes its identity
  to the next generation.
- Topic 1.2 separated the two foundational ideas: the Two-Nation Theory (Muslims are a distinct
  nation) and the Islamic concept of state (governance by Islamic principles of justice and
  equal rights). The vision of Quaid-e-Azam combined both into a modern, democratic state where
  citizenship determines rights, not religion.

Together these give you, as a future teacher, the conceptual tools to teach Pakistan Studies
with clarity and to answer the harder questions pupils will ask.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following best describes the scope of Pakistan Studies as set out by the HEC curriculum?
   - A) It is a history subject covering events from 1857 to the present
   - B) It is an interdisciplinary subject drawing on history, geography, economy, civics and culture
   - C) It is the same as Islamiat, focused on religious instruction
   - D) It is a geography subject focused on Pakistan's physical features

2. *(Remember)* What does the Two-Nation Theory hold?
   - A) That Muslims and Hindus share one Indian nation
   - B) That Muslims of the subcontinent are a distinct nation separate from Hindus
   - C) That Pakistan should be governed only by Islamic law
   - D) That all religions should be treated equally in the state

3. *(Understand)* A teacher says, "National ideology is the same as patriotism." How would you correct that statement?
   - A) National ideology is about loving the country; patriotism is about governing it
   - B) National ideology is a set of ideas that explain why the nation exists; patriotism is an emotion of love for one's country
   - C) National ideology and patriotism are the same thing
   - D) National ideology is only about religion; patriotism is about culture

4. *(Understand)* Which event formally demanded independent states for Muslims in 1940?
   - A) The Allahabad Address
   - B) The Lahore Resolution
   - C) The Objectives Resolution
   - D) The Constituent Assembly Address

5. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "Pakistan was made because Islam says Muslims need their own state." Which idea does that statement most reflect?
   - A) The Two-Nation Theory
   - B) The Islamic concept of state
   - C) National identity
   - D) The HEC curriculum

6. *(Apply)* A textbook chapter covers the Indus River system, wheat production and the textile industry. Which subject area of Pakistan Studies does this chapter belong to?
   - A) History
   - B) Civics
   - C) Geography and economy
   - D) Culture

7. *(Understand)* What is the key difference between the Two-Nation Theory and the Islamic concept of state?
   - A) There is no difference; they are the same idea
   - B) The Two-Nation Theory explains why a separate nation was needed; the Islamic concept of state explains how it should be governed
   - C) The Two-Nation Theory is about governance; the Islamic concept of state is about identity
   - D) The Two-Nation Theory is about culture; the Islamic concept of state is about economy

8. *(Apply)* In his 11 August 1947 address, Jinnah said that a citizen's religion "has nothing to do with the business of the State." What does this statement best reflect?
   - A) The Islamic concept of state as a theocracy
   - B) The Two-Nation Theory as a demand for a separate nation
   - C) The vision of a modern, democratic state where citizenship determines rights
   - D) The HEC curriculum's emphasis on history

9. *(Apply)* A teacher reduces Pakistan Studies to memorising dates and events. What does Topic 1.1 suggest is missing from that approach?
   - A) The geography of Pakistan only
   - B) The interdisciplinary scope: geography, economy, civics and culture, not only history
   - C) The Islamic concept of state only
   - D) Nothing; memorising dates is the correct approach

10. *(Apply)* A pupil asks, "Are the Two-Nation Theory and the Islamic concept of state the same thing?" Which answer is most accurate?
    - A) Yes, both say Muslims need a separate state
    - B) No; the first explains why a separate nation was needed, the second explains how it should be governed
    - C) Yes, both were stated in the Lahore Resolution
    - D) No; the first is about geography, the second is about history

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between national ideology and general patriotism.

2. *(Understand)* Explain how Pakistan Studies draws on three subject areas other than history, and why a teacher needs each one.

3. *(Apply)* A colleague says, "Teaching Pakistan Studies is just teaching the history of 1947." Using what you learned in Topic 1.1, give two reasons that statement is incomplete.

4. *(Understand)* What question does the Two-Nation Theory answer, and what question does the Islamic concept of state answer? Give one sentence for each.

5. *(Apply)* Take the statement: "Pakistan was created because Muslims and Hindus are two separate nations." State whether this reflects the Two-Nation Theory or the Islamic concept of state, and give one reason.

6. *(Analyze)* A teacher says, "Jinnah wanted Pakistan to be a religious state where only Muslims have full rights." Using Jinnah's 11 August 1947 address, explain in two or three sentences why this statement does not match his vision.

7. *(Understand)* What was the Lahore Resolution of 1940, and why was it significant for the Two-Nation Theory?

8. *(Apply)* A Grade 5 textbook chapter is titled "Our Country: Rivers, Crops and Industries." To which subject area of Pakistan Studies does this chapter contribute? Give one reason.

9. *(Analyze)* Why is Pakistan Studies a compulsory subject in Pakistani schools? Give one educational reason from Topic 1.1.

10. *(Apply)* A pupil confuses national ideology with national identity. Explain the difference in one or two sentences, using a Pakistani example.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (80-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit defined Pakistan Studies as an interdisciplinary subject, not only a history subject. Compare these two views and explain, with a classroom example, why the interdisciplinary view matters for a teacher.

2. *(Evaluate)* A textbook presents Pakistan Studies as a chronological list of events from 1857 to 1947. Evaluate this approach using what you learned in Topic 1.1. What does it capture, and what does it miss?

3. *(Analyze)* A pupil says, "The Two-Nation Theory and the Islamic concept of state are the same thing." Analyse this statement: explain the difference between the two ideas and give one piece of evidence from Topic 1.2 for each.

4. *(Evaluate)* Jinnah's 11 August 1947 address stated that a citizen's religion "has nothing to do with the business of the State." Evaluate this statement against the Islamic concept of state. Does it contradict the idea of an Islamic state? Explain your reasoning.

5. *(Create/Evaluate)* A new teacher asks you how to explain "why Pakistan exists" to a Grade 8 class. Using the Two-Nation Theory, the Islamic concept of state and Jinnah's vision, write a short explanation (100-150 words) suitable for pupils. Draw on both topics from the unit.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Pakistan Studies is interdisciplinary (history, geography, economy, civics, culture). A is too narrow, C confuses it with Islamiat, D is only one component.
2. **B** - The Two-Nation Theory holds that Muslims are a distinct nation. A contradicts the theory, C describes the Islamic concept of state (partially), D is closer to Jinnah's vision.
3. **B** - National ideology is a set of ideas explaining why the nation exists; patriotism is an emotion. A reverses the meanings, C is wrong, D is wrong.
4. **B** - The Lahore Resolution (1940) demanded independent states for Muslims. A was 1930, C was 1949, D was 1947.
5. **A** - The statement reflects the Two-Nation Theory (Muslims as a distinct nation needing a homeland). B would focus on governance, C and D are not the best fit.
6. **C** - Rivers, crops and industries fall under geography and economy. A is history, B is civics, D is culture.
7. **B** - The Two-Nation Theory answers "why a separate nation?"; the Islamic concept of state answers "how should it be governed?" A is wrong, C reverses the roles, D is wrong.
8. **C** - Jinnah's statement reflects a modern, democratic state where citizenship determines rights. A is the opposite, B is about identity not governance, D is not specific enough.
9. **B** - Topic 1.1 says Pakistan Studies draws on geography, economy, civics and culture, not only history. A is incomplete, C is wrong, D is wrong.
10. **B** - The Two-Nation Theory explains why a separate nation was needed; the Islamic concept of state explains governance. A is wrong, C is wrong (Lahore Resolution only), D is wrong.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** National ideology is a set of core ideas that explain why a nation exists (for Pakistan, the distinct nationhood of Muslims). General patriotism is the emotion of loving one's country. **Marks (2):** 1 - defines ideology as ideas; 1 - defines patriotism as emotion.

2. **Model:** Geography, economy, civics, culture. **Marks (2):** 1 for naming two correctly, 2 for three.

3. **Model:** The statement is incomplete because (a) Pakistan Studies draws on geography, economy, civics and culture, not only history, and (b) the subject's purpose is to develop informed citizens, not only to transmit historical facts. **Marks (2):** 1 - names the interdisciplinary scope; 1 - names the citizen-development purpose.

4. **Model:** The Two-Nation Theory answers "why do Muslims need a separate nation?" The Islamic concept of state answers "how should that nation be governed?" **Marks (2):** 1 per correct pairing.

5. **Model:** It reflects the Two-Nation Theory. The statement asserts that Muslims and Hindus are two separate nations, which is the core claim of the Two-Nation Theory. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the Two-Nation Theory; 1 - gives the reason.

6. **Model:** Jinnah's address stated that religion "has nothing to do with the business of the State" and that all citizens are free to practise their religion. This reflects a modern, democratic state where citizenship, not religion, determines rights, not a religious state where only Muslims have full rights. **Marks (3):** 1 - cites the address; 1 - explains the democratic-citizenship idea; 1 - contrasts with the claim.

7. **Model:** The Lahore Resolution (1940) formally demanded independent states for Muslims in the northwest and east of India. It was significant because it turned the Two-Nation Theory from an idea into a concrete political demand. **Marks (2):** 1 - states what the Resolution demanded; 1 - explains its significance for the Two-Nation Theory.

8. **Model:** It contributes to geography and economy. Rivers (geography), crops and industries (economy) are key components of the subject's scope. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies geography and/or economy; 1 - links to the content.

9. **Model:** Pakistan Studies is compulsory because education is the main way a nation passes its identity and civic values to the next generation. **Marks (2):** 1 - gives the educational reason; 1 - links to identity/citizenship.

10. **Model:** National ideology is the set of ideas explaining why Pakistan exists (Two-Nation Theory); national identity is the sense of belonging that those ideas create (a Sindhi pupil and a Punjabi pupil both feeling Pakistani). **Marks (2):** 1 - defines ideology; 1 - defines identity with an example.
