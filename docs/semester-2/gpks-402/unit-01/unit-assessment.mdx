## Unit summary

This unit built one idea across two topics: **Pakistan Studies is the study of the whole country, and that country rests on ideological foundations that every teacher must be able to explain accurately and fairly.**

- **Topic 1.1** explained what Pakistan Studies is. It covers history and ideology, constitution and civics, geography, economy, society and culture, foreign relations and contemporary issues, so it is far more than a history subject. A national ideology is a set of ideas about why a nation exists and what it stands for; a national identity is a shared sense of belonging. People hold layered identities, and the Constitution protects both national and regional layers (Articles 33 and 251). The Ideology of Pakistan draws on Islam but is not the same as religion. In the classroom, the subject asks every teacher for accuracy, fairness, connection to pupils' lives, and thinking rather than memorising.
- **Topic 1.2** explained Pakistan's three ideological foundations. The Two-Nation Theory, which grew from the reform era after 1857 through 1906, 1930 and 1940, held that South Asia's Muslims were a separate nation; it explains why Pakistan exists, and it does not mean that people of different faiths cannot live together. The Islamic concept of state holds that sovereignty belongs to Allah and that authority is a trust exercised by the people's chosen representatives with justice, consultation, equality and protection for non-Muslims; it entered the Constitution through the Objectives Resolution of 1949, and it is not a theocracy. Quaid-e-Azam's vision joined these: a homeland for a nation, governed democratically under the rule of law, guided by Islamic values, with equal citizenship for all.

The enduring understandings to carry into Units 2 to 6 are these. First, every topic in this course connects to the others. Second, the Two-Nation Theory answers *why* Pakistan exists, and the Islamic concept of state answers *how* it should be governed. Third, a teacher of these ideas must use evidence and include every pupil.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which statement best describes the meaning of Pakistan Studies?
   - A) The study of the events of the Pakistan Movement from 1906 to 1947
   - B) The study of Pakistan as a whole: its history, ideology, government, land, economy, society and culture
   - C) The study of Islamic history in South Asia
   - D) The study of Pakistan's relations with other countries

2. *(Understand)* What is the main difference between a national ideology and a national identity?
   - A) Ideology is about shared ideas on why a nation exists; identity is about a shared sense of belonging
   - B) Ideology belongs to the government; identity belongs to the provinces
   - C) Ideology is about religion; identity is about language
   - D) Ideology is learned in school; identity is decided at birth

3. *(Apply)* A pupil in Hyderabad says, "I am Sindhi, so I cannot also be fully Pakistani." Which idea from this unit best answers her?
   - A) The Two-Nation Theory
   - B) Sovereignty
   - C) Layered identity
   - D) Separate electorates

4. *(Apply)* A teacher starts a lesson at the canal outside the school and asks pupils where its water comes from and which crops it feeds. Which two areas of Pakistan Studies is she linking most directly?
   - A) Foreign policy and history
   - B) Constitution and foreign policy
   - C) Society and constitution
   - D) Geography and economy

5. *(Remember)* Which article of the Constitution asks the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices?
   - A) Article 2
   - B) Article 33
   - C) Article 227
   - D) Article 251

6. *(Remember)* In which year did Allama Muhammad Iqbal deliver his Allahabad address to the All-India Muslim League?
   - A) 1906
   - B) 1930
   - C) 1940
   - D) 1949

7. *(Understand)* The Two-Nation Theory is best described as a claim about:
   - A) personal friendship between Hindus and Muslims
   - B) which religion is closer to the truth
   - C) political nationhood and the right of Muslims to govern themselves
   - D) the borders of the Mughal Empire

8. *(Remember)* The Objectives Resolution, which brought the principles of the Islamic concept of state into Pakistan's constitutional order, was passed by the Constituent Assembly in:
   - A) 1940
   - B) 1947
   - C) 1949
   - D) 1973

9. *(Understand)* In the Islamic concept of state as expressed in Pakistan's Constitution, who exercises authority on behalf of the people?
   - A) The ulema, as a separate ruling class
   - B) The Governor-General alone
   - C) The army, as guardian of the ideology
   - D) The chosen representatives of the people, as a sacred trust

10. *(Apply)* A pupil reads this sentence from a speech: "we are all citizens, and equal citizens, of one State". Which part of Quaid-e-Azam's vision does it show most clearly?
    - A) Equal citizenship for people of every faith
    - B) The Muslims of South Asia as a separate nation
    - C) The rule of law and the fight against corruption
    - D) The demand for a homeland in the north-west

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question within the limit stated.

1. *(Understand)* In three or four sentences, explain why Pakistan Studies is not only a history subject. Use one real place in Pakistan as your example.

2. *(Understand)* In two or three sentences, explain the difference between ideology and religion, using the Ideology of Pakistan as your example.

3. *(Apply)* Name **two** of the four responsibilities of a Pakistan Studies teacher described in Topic 1.1, and give one concrete classroom action for each.

4. *(Analyze)* A Class 9 textbook chapter on national identity mentions only Punjab and Sindh. In three or four sentences, analyse what problem this creates for pupils in Balochistan or Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and what a teacher could add.

5. *(Understand)* Explain, in three or four sentences, the role of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and of the founding of the Muslim League in 1906 in the early growth of the Two-Nation Theory.

6. *(Understand)* List **three** reasons why the Two-Nation Theory was significant for the creation of Pakistan.

7. *(Analyze)* A pupil says, "The Two-Nation Theory proves that Hindus and Muslims hate each other." In four or five sentences, explain what is wrong with this claim, using two pieces of evidence from the unit.

8. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between an Islamic state in the sense of Pakistan's Constitution and a theocracy. Answer in three sentences.

9. *(Apply)* Choose **three** features of the Islamic concept of state and, for each, name the part of the Constitution where it appears.

10. *(Analyze)* Read this description: *"On 11 August 1947, speaking to the members who would write Pakistan's constitution, Quaid-e-Azam said every citizen was free to go to their temples or mosques, and that all were equal citizens of one State."* Apply the four source-reading steps to this description, one sentence per step.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a structured answer of 250-400 words unless stated otherwise.

1. *(Evaluate)* "Pakistan Studies is a subject for exam memorisation; it has nothing to do with being a good teacher." Evaluate this statement. Use the scope of the subject, the teacher's responsibilities, and at least one example from a real Pakistani classroom.

2. *(Create)* Design a 35-minute Class 9 lesson on "national identity" for a mixed class in which pupils come from different provinces, speak different home languages, and include Muslim and non-Muslim children. Give the learning outcome, the main activity, the questions you will ask, and how you will make sure every pupil is included. Justify two of your choices using ideas from Topic 1.1.

3. *(Analyze)* Trace the growth of the Two-Nation Theory from 1857 to 1947. For each stage, explain what changed in the idea, and analyse which stage you consider most important in turning the idea into a political demand. Justify your choice.

4. *(Evaluate)* A newspaper letter says: "Quaid-e-Azam wanted Pakistan to be ruled by religious scholars." Evaluate this claim using at least two of his speeches and the difference between the Islamic concept of state and a theocracy.

5. *(Analyze)* Distinguish between the Two-Nation Theory and the Islamic concept of state, and explain how both shaped the Ideology of Pakistan. In your answer, show what question each idea answers, use evidence from at least two sources (speeches, resolutions or the Constitution), and explain how Quaid-e-Azam's vision links the two. (400-500 words)

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Attempt every question above before you read the guidance below. Model answers show one good response; other well-reasoned answers that use accurate evidence should receive credit.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Pakistan Studies covers the whole country. A is only one part of the history area; C and D are each one narrow area.
2. **A** - Ideology concerns ideas about why a nation exists and what it stands for; identity concerns belonging. C repeats the misconception that ideology and religion are the same; B and D are not distinctions the unit makes.
3. **C** - Layered identity explains that a person can be Sindhi and Pakistani at the same time. The other three are real terms but do not answer her worry.
4. **D** - Water sources are geography; crops and livelihoods are economy. None of the other pairs fits the canal example.
5. **B** - Article 33 discourages parochial and similar prejudices. Article 2 makes Islam the State religion, Article 227 concerns conformity of laws with Islam, and Article 251 concerns the national language.
6. **B** - Iqbal spoke at Allahabad in December 1930. 1906 is the founding of the Muslim League, 1940 the Lahore Resolution, 1949 the Objectives Resolution.
7. **C** - The theory is about political nationhood and self-government. A is the misconception corrected in Topic 1.2; B and D are not what the theory claims.
8. **C** - The Objectives Resolution was passed on 12 March 1949. 1973 is the year of the present Constitution, which carries it as the Preamble and, since 1985, as Article 2A.
9. **D** - The Preamble says the State acts through the chosen representatives of the people and that authority is a sacred trust. A describes a theocracy, which the unit distinguishes from the Islamic concept of state; B and C have no basis in the Constitution.
10. **A** - The quotation is about equal citizenship. B and D describe the 1940 demand, and C is a different point from the same 11 August address.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Pakistan Studies covers history, geography, economy, constitution and civics, society and culture, foreign relations and current issues. A real place connects several areas at once. For example, the Sukkur Barrage is on the Indus (geography), waters farmland for wheat and cotton (economy), was built in the British period (history), raises questions of water-sharing between provinces (civics), and supports village livelihoods (society). **Marks (4):** 1 - names at least three non-history areas; 1 - names a real place; 2 - links the place to at least three areas accurately (1 mark for two areas).

2. **Model:** Religion is a faith about God, worship and moral life; an ideology is a set of ideas about how a society should be organised and governed. The Ideology of Pakistan draws on Islamic principles such as justice and consultation, but it also includes democracy, the separate nationhood of South Asian Muslims and equal citizenship for minorities. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines religion; 1 - defines ideology; 1 - uses the Ideology of Pakistan to show both the link and the difference.

3. **Model:** Any two of accuracy, fairness, connection and thinking, each with an action. For example: *fairness* - when teaching national identity, ask pupils of every faith and province to share what they contribute to Pakistan; *connection* - begin a lesson on rivers at the local canal. **Marks (4):** 1 for each responsibility named; 1 for each concrete and matching action.

4. **Model:** Pupils from Balochistan or Khyber Pakhtunkhwa may feel that their province is not part of the national story, which weakens the shared layer of identity the lesson is meant to build. It also gives everyone an incomplete picture of Pakistan. The teacher could add examples from every province, such as languages, festivals or places, and invite pupils to add their own. **Marks (4):** 1 - names the exclusion problem; 1 - links it to national identity or integration (**Analyze**: explains the effect, not just the omission); 1 - notes the accuracy problem; 1 - proposes a concrete addition.

5. **Model:** After 1857, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan urged Muslims to take up modern education and spoke of Hindus and Muslims as distinct communities with different interests, though he did not demand a separate state. In 1906 the All-India Muslim League was founded at Dhaka to protect Muslims' political rights, and the British soon accepted separate electorates. Together these steps established Muslims as a distinct political community. **Marks (4):** 2 - Sir Syed's role, including that he did not demand a separate state; 2 - the 1906 League and separate electorates.

6. **Model:** Any three of: it was the basis of the demand for Pakistan; it united Muslims of many regions and languages in one political identity; it explained why majority rule alone could not protect a large minority; it remains the answer to why Pakistan is a separate country. **Marks (3):** 1 for each accurate reason.

7. **Model:** The claim confuses a political argument with personal hostility. The Two-Nation Theory said Muslims were a separate nation entitled to self-government; it did not say people of different faiths must hate each other. Evidence: Quaid-e-Azam told the Constituent Assembly on 11 August 1947 that all were equal citizens of one State; the Constitution protects freedom of religion (Article 20) and minorities' rights (Article 36). Hindus and Muslims had lived as neighbours for centuries, and non-Muslim Pakistanis serve the country as full citizens. **Marks (5):** 1 - identifies the confusion (**Analyze**: separates the political claim from hostility); 2 - two accurate pieces of evidence (1 each); 1 - explains how the evidence answers the claim; 1 - clear, respectful expression.

8. **Model:** A theocracy is ruled by a class of religious leaders who claim to govern in God's name without the people's consent. In Pakistan's constitutional sense, sovereignty belongs to Allah, but authority is a trust exercised by the people's elected representatives. So Islam supplies the principles, while parliament, elections and courts govern. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines theocracy; 1 - states the role of elected representatives; 1 - draws the contrast clearly.

9. **Model:** Any three accurate pairs, for example: sovereignty belongs to Allah - the Preamble; authority as a trust exercised through chosen representatives - the Preamble; equality - Article 25; Islamic principles in public life - Articles 2, 31 or 227; rights of non-Muslims - Articles 20 and 36. **Marks (6):** 1 for each feature correctly named; 1 for each matching constitutional location.

10. **Model:** *Who and when:* Quaid-e-Azam, President of the Constituent Assembly, on 11 August 1947, three days before independence. *Audience:* the members who would write the constitution and, through the press, the whole new nation, including non-Muslims. *What it says:* every citizen is free to worship as they choose, and all are equal citizens of one State. *What it tells us:* he wanted a state in which a person's faith would not decide their rights as a citizen. **Marks (4):** 1 for each step answered accurately; the fourth mark requires an inference supported by the words (**Analyze**), not a slogan.
