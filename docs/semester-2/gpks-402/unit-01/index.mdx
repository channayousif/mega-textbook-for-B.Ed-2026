# Introduction and Ideological Foundations

Before you can teach Pakistan Studies to your pupils, you need to be clear about what the
subject is, why it exists, and what ideas hold the country together. This unit gives you that
foundation. It answers three questions a new teacher commonly faces: What exactly is Pakistan
Studies? Why does it matter in a classroom? And what are the core ideas (the Two-Nation Theory,
the Islamic concept of state, the vision of Quaid-e-Azam) that form the ideological basis of
Pakistan?

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain what Pakistan Studies is, describe its scope and importance, and say why it matters for a future teacher.
2. Distinguish national ideology from general patriotism and explain what "national identity" means in the Pakistani context.
3. Describe the Two-Nation Theory, trace its origins and explain its significance for the creation of Pakistan.
4. Explain the Islamic concept of state and distinguish it from the Two-Nation Theory.
5. Summarise the vision of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah and connect it to the idea of a modern democratic Pakistan.

## Prerequisite knowledge

None beyond HSC-level general knowledge. This is the opening unit of the course. No prior
study of Pakistan Studies or political science is assumed.

## In this unit

1. [What Pakistan Studies is, and why it matters](./topic-01) - the scope, importance and educational context of Pakistan Studies.
2. [The ideological foundations of Pakistan](./topic-02) - the Two-Nation Theory, the Islamic concept of state and the vision of Quaid-e-Azam.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through the explanation,
and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your understanding" items ask you to retrieve
what you have learned - have a go without looking back, then check. The unit ends with a summative
assessment that draws both topics together.
