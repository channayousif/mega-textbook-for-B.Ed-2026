## Unit summary

This unit joined two subjects that are often taught apart: **how Pakistan is governed, and the land it governs.** The enduring idea is that a constitution and a geography together set the problems a country must solve, and that citizens and teachers need to understand both.

- **Topic 4.1** explained how Pakistan is governed. Pakistan is a federation of four provinces and the Islamabad Capital Territory (Article 1). Article 142 gives Parliament the subjects on the Federal Legislative List and the provinces the rest; the Eighteenth Amendment of 2010 abolished the Concurrent List, so school education is now mainly provincial. The legislature (National Assembly and Senate) makes laws and controls the government; the executive (Prime Minister and Cabinet, with the President as Head of State acting on advice) carries them out; the judiciary (Supreme Court, High Courts and subordinate courts) interprets the law and protects rights. Each branch checks the others. The Constitution places the Armed Forces under the direction of the Federal Government (Article 245), yet Pakistan has had about 32 years of military rule. Weak civilian institutions and a strong sense of insecurity help explain why, and broken constitutions and weakened institutions were among the results.
- **Topic 4.2** explained rights and duties. Articles 8 to 28 guarantee fundamental rights, including security of person, fair trial, freedom of speech and religion, equality and, since 2010, free and compulsory education for children aged five to sixteen (Article 25A). Article 8 makes conflicting laws void, and a citizen can seek a remedy through a writ petition in a High Court (Article 199) or the Supreme Court (Article 184(3)). These rights are Pakistan's own, not copies of another country's. The principles of policy (Articles 29 to 40) guide the State but cannot be enforced in court (Article 30(2)); they can, however, grow into rights, as education did. Citizens owe loyalty and obedience to law (Article 5), and in a democracy they also vote, take part, seek information and respect others' rights.
- **Topic 4.3** explained the land. Pakistan has five physical regions (mountains, plateaus, the Indus Plain, deserts and the coast), linked by the Indus River system, which supplies the largest connected irrigation system in the world. The climate is mostly dry, with most rain from the summer monsoon; the 2022 floods showed how climate and weak preparation together create disasters. Many mineral and energy resources lie in less developed areas. Pakistan's location between China, Central Asia, West Asia, India and the Arabian Sea gives it strategic importance, which brings opportunities such as CPEC and challenges for security, federal relations and civil-military balance.

Three understandings to carry into Units 5 and 6: power in Pakistan is divided and checked, and knowing who is responsible is the first step to solving a problem; a right is only as strong as the remedy behind it and the citizens who use it; and geography does not decide a country's future, but it sets many of the problems its government must solve.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Under Article 50 of the Constitution, the Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament) consists of:
   - A) the National Assembly and the four Provincial Assemblies
   - B) the President, the National Assembly and the Senate
   - C) the Prime Minister, the Cabinet and the National Assembly
   - D) the National Assembly and the Supreme Court

2. *(Understand)* Why does each province elect the same number of members to the Senate?
   - A) Because the Senate controls the budget and must be small
   - B) To give every federating unit an equal voice, balancing the population-based National Assembly
   - C) Because the provinces have equal populations
   - D) To make sure the Senate always agrees with the National Assembly

3. *(Apply)* After the Eighteenth Amendment, a citizen wants to know which government is mainly responsible for the school curriculum in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Which answer is correct?
   - A) The federal government, because education is on the Federal Legislative List
   - B) The Supreme Court, because it interprets the Constitution
   - C) The provincial government, because education is not on the Federal Legislative List and the Concurrent List was abolished
   - D) The Council of Common Interests, because it decides all shared matters

4. *(Remember)* Which period was a time of direct military rule in Pakistan?
   - A) 1971-1977
   - B) 1977-1985
   - C) 1988-1999
   - D) 2008-2013

5. *(Remember)* Which article of the Constitution gives every child aged five to sixteen the right to free and compulsory education?
   - A) Article 25A
   - B) Article 37
   - C) Article 19A
   - D) Article 5

6. *(Understand)* Article 30(2) says that an action cannot be challenged in court for not following the Principles of Policy. What does this show?
   - A) The Principles of Policy have been repealed
   - B) The Principles of Policy are goals that guide the State but are not enforced by the courts
   - C) The courts may ignore the fundamental rights
   - D) Only the President must follow the Principles of Policy

7. *(Apply)* A district officer refuses to release information on how a public school's repair funds were spent. A parent wants to rely on the Constitution. Which right applies most directly?
   - A) Freedom of movement (Article 15)
   - B) Freedom of association (Article 17)
   - C) Right to information (Article 19A)
   - D) Protection of property rights (Article 24)

8. *(Remember)* Which three mountain ranges meet in the far north of Pakistan?
   - A) Sulaiman, Kirthar and Safed Koh
   - B) Himalaya, Karakoram and Hindu Kush
   - C) Himalaya, Sulaiman and Kirthar
   - D) Karakoram, Kirthar and Hindu Kush

9. *(Understand)* Why does most of Pakistan's population live on the Indus Plain?
   - A) Because it has the coolest climate in the country
   - B) Because it has the most mineral resources
   - C) Because its fertile river soil and canal water support farming
   - D) Because it is closest to the Chinese border

10. *(Apply)* A teacher in Gwadar wants to give pupils one reason why their port matters to China. Which reason is best supported by this unit?
    - A) Gwadar is the coldest port in Asia
    - B) Gwadar offers western China a short route to the Arabian Sea through Pakistan
    - C) Gwadar is the capital of Balochistan
    - D) Gwadar lies on the border with India

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the length stated.

1. *(Understand)* Explain, in three or four sentences, what federalism means and why it suits a country as diverse as Pakistan.
2. *(Understand)* Describe two powers of the National Assembly that the Senate does not have.
3. *(Apply)* For each of the following, state which branch of government acts and why: (a) passing the annual budget; (b) removing an unlawful order of a district officer; (c) running government hospitals in Punjab.
4. *(Analyze)* Identify two causes of military intervention in Pakistan, and explain in four or five sentences how they worked together.
5. *(Understand)* Explain, in three sentences, why Article 8 is called the key to the fundamental rights.
6. *(Analyze)* Compare the fundamental rights in Pakistan's Constitution with the US Bill of Rights. Give one similarity and two differences.
7. *(Apply)* A Christian pupil is told that she must sit the Islamiat examination. Name the relevant right and article, and describe two steps her family could take, in order.
8. *(Understand)* Describe Pakistan's climate in four sentences, mentioning temperature, the monsoon and one regional difference.
9. *(Analyze)* Using evidence from this unit, explain in four or five sentences why the 2022 floods caused so much damage. Separate the climate factors from the human and governance factors.
10. *(Analyze)* Explain, in four or five sentences, how sharing the water of the Indus creates a question for Pakistan's federal system, and name the constitutional body that deals with it.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer in 300-450 words each.

1. *(Evaluate)* "Pakistan has always been governed by the military." Evaluate this claim. Use the periods of civilian and military rule, at least two causes and two consequences of military intervention, and the constitutional position of the Armed Forces.
2. *(Analyze)* Analyse how the right to education moved from a principle of policy in 1973 to an enforceable fundamental right in 2010. Explain what changed for a child out of school, and what still depends on governments and citizens.
3. *(Evaluate)* "Pakistan's land is its greatest wealth." Evaluate this statement with reference to physical features, water, climate and natural resources. Include at least one pressure on resources.
4. *(Analyze)* Explain how Pakistan's geography shapes the governance challenges it faces. Use at least two geographic factors (for example the Indus system, the location of resources, or the country's borders and strategic position) and link each to a specific institution or issue from Topic 4.1.
5. *(Create)* Design a 40-minute Class 9 lesson called "Who governs, and what are my rights?" for a school in your district. Include a learning objective, an opening hook from pupils' own lives, one activity that uses a constitutional article, one activity about citizens' role, and a short check of learning. Justify how your lesson addresses one misconception from this unit.

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting every question. The keys below give one sound answer; other answers that are accurate and supported by evidence from the unit should receive credit.

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Article 50: the Majlis-e-Shoora consists of the President and two Houses, the National Assembly and the Senate. Provincial Assemblies are provincial legislatures; the Cabinet is part of the executive.
2. B - Equal provincial seats in the Senate balance the population-based National Assembly, so smaller provinces have an equal voice. Provinces do not have equal populations (C).
3. C - School education is not on the Federal Legislative List, and the Eighteenth Amendment abolished the Concurrent List, so under Article 142 it is mainly provincial.
4. B - General Zia ul Haq ruled under martial law from 1977 to 1985. The other periods were civilian.
5. A - Article 25A, added in 2010. Article 37 is the principle of policy on illiteracy and secondary education; 19A is information; 5 is loyalty and obedience.
6. B - Article 30(2) makes the Principles of Policy non-enforceable in court; they remain the responsibility of every organ of the State under Article 29.
7. C - Article 19A gives every citizen access to information in matters of public importance, subject to law.
8. B - The Himalaya, Karakoram and Hindu Kush meet in the north. The Sulaiman and Kirthar are western ranges.
9. C - The Indus Plain's alluvial soil and canal irrigation support most of the country's farming, and so its people and cities.
10. B - Gwadar is on the CPEC route linking western China to the Arabian Sea. Quetta is the provincial capital; Gwadar is not near India.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: federalism divides power by the constitution between a central government and provincial governments, each with its own powers; neither can abolish the other. It suits Pakistan because the provinces differ in language, history, population and needs, so each can manage local matters such as schools and police while the centre manages defence, foreign affairs and currency. **Marks (4):** 1 - definition with division of power; 1 - each level has its own powers; 1 - reason linked to diversity; 1 - example of a provincial and a federal subject.
2. Model: a Money Bill, including the budget, must begin in the National Assembly and the Senate can only recommend changes (Article 73); and the National Assembly elects the Prime Minister and can remove the Prime Minister by a vote of no-confidence. **Marks (4):** 2 for each power correctly described (1 for naming, 1 for explanation or article).
3. Model: (a) legislature, because a budget is a Money Bill passed by the National Assembly; (b) judiciary, because a High Court can issue a writ under Article 199 against an unlawful action; (c) provincial executive, because health is a provincial subject run by the Punjab government. **Marks (6):** 1 for each branch, 1 for each reason.
4. Model: weak political institutions (the early deaths of Quaid-e-Azam and Liaquat Ali Khan, a divided Muslim League, frequent changes of government) and a sense of insecurity (tension with India, later the war in Afghanistan) that made defence the top priority and gave the military a large budget and voice. Together they created a gap between a strong, organised military and weak civilian institutions, so takeovers in 1958, 1969 and 1977 met little resistance (Rizvi, 2004). **Marks (5):** 1 for each cause with evidence (2); 2 for explaining how they combined (**Analyze**); 1 for a specific date or event.
5. Model: Article 8 makes any law, custom or usage that is inconsistent with the fundamental rights void, and forbids the State from making a law that takes them away. This means the rights stand above ordinary law. Without Article 8, a later law could quietly cancel a right. **Marks (3):** 1 - void if inconsistent; 1 - State cannot make such laws; 1 - explains why this makes rights "fundamental".
6. Model: similarity - both protect liberty, speech, religion and a fair trial. Differences - Pakistan writes "reasonable restrictions imposed by law" into the text, while US limits were largely developed by courts; Pakistan includes a right to education (25A) and to language and culture (28), which the US federal Constitution does not; Pakistan also has non-enforceable Principles of Policy and makes Islam the State religion with freedom of religion for all. **Marks (5):** 1 for the similarity; 2 for each well-explained difference (**Analyze**).
7. Model: Article 22(1): no person attending an educational institution shall be required to receive religious instruction in a religion other than their own (Article 20 on freedom of religion also applies). Steps: first, a polite written request to the head teacher citing Article 22; if refused, a complaint to the district education office or the ombudsman; as a last resort, a writ petition in the High Court under Article 199. **Marks (5):** 2 - right and article; 2 - two steps in a sensible order; 1 - names the court route correctly.
8. Model: summers on the plains are very hot (Jacobabad is among the hottest places in Asia) and winters in the northern mountains are freezing. Most of the country is dry. The summer monsoon, about July to September, brings most of the rain, mainly to the north-east. Sindh and Balochistan receive the least rain, while winter rain and snow come to the north and west. **Marks (4):** 1 each for temperature, monsoon, a regional difference, and a second accurate detail.
9. Model: climate factors - extreme monsoon rainfall, which Otto et al. (2023) found has become heavier as Pakistan has warmed. Human and governance factors - settlements and farms close to flood plains, weak infrastructure, an outdated river management system, poverty and limited preparation. The damage came from both together: the rain was the hazard, and vulnerability turned it into a disaster. **Marks (5):** 1 for climate factor; 2 for two human or governance factors; 2 for explaining how they combined (**Analyze**).
10. Model: the Indus and its tributaries flow through several provinces, so water used upstream in one province affects farmers downstream in another, as in disputes between Punjab and Sindh. Because water crosses provincial boundaries, no single province can decide it alone; it is a federal question. Article 155 lets a government complain to the Council of Common Interests, chaired by the Prime Minister with the Chief Ministers, which decides the matter. **Marks (5):** 2 - explains the upstream-downstream link; 1 - explains why it is federal; 1 - names the Council of Common Interests; 1 - cites Article 155 or a real example.

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 12 with an analytic rubric. Award each criterion separately.

1. **Civil-military relations.** *Knowledge of periods* (0-3): accurate periods of military and civilian rule, including about 32 years of military rule. *Causes and consequences* (0-3): at least two of each, with evidence. *Constitutional position* (0-2): Article 245, Article 6 or civilian supremacy explained. *Judgement* (0-4, **Evaluate**): reaches a clear verdict (for example, "partly true: long military rule and influence, but not always") and justifies it by weighing the evidence on both sides.
2. **Education from principle to right.** *Knowledge* (0-3): Article 37(b) in 1973, Article 25A in 2010, the provincial law. *Analysis of enforceability* (0-4, **Analyze**): explains, using Articles 8 and 30(2), exactly what changed for a child out of school, and why a principle could not be enforced. *What still depends on others* (0-3): school places, provincial laws, teachers, families, citizens' action. *Clarity and use of evidence* (0-2).
3. **Land as wealth.** *Physical and climatic knowledge* (0-3): accurate regions, the Indus system and climate. *Resources* (0-3): named resources with locations. *Pressures* (0-2): at least one pressure, such as water storage, salinity, groundwater, deforestation or floods, with evidence (for example Qureshi, 2011). *Judgement* (0-4, **Evaluate**): a balanced verdict that weighs wealth against pressures and recognises the role of management.
4. **Geography and governance.** *Geographic factors* (0-3): two factors described accurately with places. *Links to governance* (0-4, **Analyze**): each factor linked to a specific institution or issue (for example Indus water and the Council of Common Interests under Article 155; gas and Article 161; borders and insecurity in civil-military relations, Rizvi, 2004; CPEC and security, Wolf, 2017), explaining *how* the geography creates the challenge. *Use of evidence* (0-3): articles or sources cited correctly. *Conclusion* (0-2): states what good governance must do to turn geography into an advantage.
5. **Lesson design.** *Objective and hook* (0-2): a clear, measurable objective and a hook from pupils' lives. *Constitutional activity* (0-3): an accurate article used in an active task. *Citizenship activity* (0-2): pupils practise a lawful role of citizens. *Check of learning* (0-2): a short retrieval check linked to the objective. *Justification* (0-3, **Evaluate/Create**): names a misconception from the unit and explains how the lesson design corrects it.
