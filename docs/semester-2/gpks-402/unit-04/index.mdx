# Governance, Fundamental Rights and Geography

Unit 3 traced how Pakistan arrived at the Constitution of 1973. This unit asks what that Constitution does in daily life. Who makes the laws that govern your school? Who runs the government, and who checks it? What rights does every pupil in your class hold, and what can a family do if a right is denied? And what is the land itself like: its mountains, rivers, deserts and coast, its climate and resources, and its place on the map of Asia?

These questions belong together. A government is always the government of a particular land and a particular people. Floods in Sindh, water shared between provinces, a long border with four neighbours: all of these are questions of geography and questions of governance at the same time. By the end of the unit you should be able to see both sides of such questions and explain them to a class.

The unit has three topics. Topic 4.1 explains how Pakistan is governed: the federal system, the legislature, the executive and the judiciary, and the relationship between civilian governments and the armed forces. Topic 4.2 explains the fundamental rights in the Constitution, the principles of policy that guide the State, and the role of citizens. Topic 4.3 turns to the land: physical features, climate, water and natural resources, and the strategic importance of Pakistan's location.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain how power is divided between the federation and the provinces, and name the main federal institutions that manage that division.
2. Describe the composition and main functions of the National Assembly, the Senate, the Prime Minister and Cabinet, the President, and the courts, and explain how each branch checks the others.
3. Describe the main periods of civilian and military rule since 1947, and analyse the causes and consequences of military intervention using evidence.
4. Identify the main fundamental rights in Articles 8 to 28 of the Constitution, and explain how a citizen can seek a remedy when a right is violated.
5. Explain the difference between a fundamental right and a principle of policy, using the right to education as an example.
6. Describe the role and duties of citizens in a democratic Pakistan, and plan classroom activities that build them.
7. Describe Pakistan's main physical regions, its climate and its water and natural resources, and explain the strategic importance of its location.
8. Analyse how Pakistan's geography shapes the challenges its government faces.

Unit 3 of this course, especially the salient features of the 1973 Constitution: its parliamentary and federal form, and its Islamic provisions. ## How to use this unit

Each topic is one complete learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation and decide how you would respond. Then read the explanation, which answers that situation step by step. Keep a copy of the Constitution open while you read Topics 4.1 and 4.2; the article numbers are given so that you can check every claim yourself. Keep a map of Pakistan open for Topic 4.3.

Do each activity with a partner or a small group. The "Check your understanding" questions are retrieval practice, so answer them from memory first and then look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and return to any part you cannot tick. The practicum task takes each idea into a real school.

When you have finished all three topics, attempt the unit assessment. It has 10 multiple-choice questions, 10 short-answer questions and 5 extended questions. Answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question before you look.

A note on dates: the Constitution has been amended many times. This unit uses the official text as amended to 2012, which includes the Eighteenth Amendment of 2010. Some details, such as the number of seats in each House, have changed through later amendments. When you teach a number, check the current text on the National Assembly website first.
