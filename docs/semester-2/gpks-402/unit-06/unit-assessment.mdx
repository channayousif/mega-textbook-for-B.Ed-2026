## Unit summary

This unit synthesized Pakistan's external relations, internal socioeconomic challenges, and the transformative power of education. In Topic 6.1, you examined how Pakistan's foreign policy is anchored in constitutional principles (specifically Article 40) that mandate international peace, sovereign equality, and solidarity with the Muslim world. You evaluated key bilateral ties with China, Afghanistan, Iran, and India, and surveyed Pakistan's distinguished record in multilateral forums such as the United Nations, OIC, and SAARC.

In Topic 6.2, you explored the interconnected matrix of contemporary domestic challenges: rapid population growth (2.55 percent annually), persistent poverty and youth unemployment, the heavy human and economic costs of terrorism and violent extremism, and escalating climate vulnerability characterized by acute water stress, super-floods, and deforestation. You learned that single-cause explanations fail: these crises feed into one another and demand integrated policy solutions.

In Topic 6.3, you discovered that true nation building happens within the educational sphere. You distinguished forced assimilation from democratic national integration, showing how Pakistan's diverse federation is strengthened when cultural and linguistic heritages are valued under shared constitutional rights (Article 33 and Article 25A). You examined active civic education across its three dimensions (knowledge, skills, and values) and explored how teachers act as nation builders across their classrooms, school environments, and local communities.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Which Article of the 1973 Constitution specifically directs the state of Pakistan to promote international peace, foster goodwill, and strengthen fraternal relations among Muslim countries?
   - A) Article 25A
   - B) Article 33
   - C) Article 38
   - D) Article 40

2. *(Understand)* Which of the following best reflects the core guiding principle of Pakistan's foreign policy as articulated by Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah in 1948?
   - A) Aggressive regional expansion to secure strategic depth
   - B) Friendliness and goodwill towards all nations based on honesty and fair play
   - C) Alignment exclusively with Western military alliances
   - D) Complete economic and political isolation from global institutions

3. *(Understand)* What makes the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) a transformative dimension of Pakistan's bilateral relationship with China?
   - A) It replaces all domestic agricultural production with Chinese imports
   - B) It shifts the partnership from traditional diplomatic ties toward deep economic and infrastructure connectivity
   - C) It eliminates the need for Pakistan to maintain relations with any other neighbouring state
   - D) It establishes a single joint currency between Pakistan and China

4. *(Apply)* A Pakistani military officer is deployed to an international conflict zone in Africa under the auspices of the United Nations. This mission reflects which of Pakistan's major global contributions?
   - A) Membership in regional bilateral defense pacts
   - B) Decades-long commitment as a leading troop contributor to UN peacekeeping operations
   - C) Enforcement of unilateral regional sanctions
   - D) Sole financial underwriting of all international refugee operations

5. *(Remember)* According to the 2023 Digital Census, what is Pakistan's approximate total population and annual intercensal growth rate?
   - A) 180 million and 1.2 percent
   - B) 207 million and 1.8 percent
   - C) 241.5 million and 2.55 percent
   - D) 280 million and 3.4 percent

6. *(Understand)* Why does a high youth dependency ratio caused by rapid population growth intensify household poverty in rural Pakistan?
   - A) It instantly raises the market value of rural agricultural land
   - B) A single working wage must be divided among many non-earning family members, reducing spending on nutrition and schooling
   - C) It eliminates the need for public schooling investments by the state
   - D) It guarantees immediate formal employment for all teenage children

7. *(Understand)* Which statement best corrects the common misconception that terrorism in Pakistan is caused solely by poverty?
   - A) Poverty never influences any social or political behaviour in developing nations
   - B) Most poor citizens remain peaceful and law-abiding, while violent extremism is driven by radical ideology, geopolitical factors, and organized networks
   - C) Extremism only occurs in highly industrialized, affluent societies
   - D) Educational attainment has no documented relationship with social tolerance

8. *(Apply)* A government primary school in Sindh has its building flooded for the second time in three years. Which combination of national environmental factors explains this recurring catastrophe?
   - A) Excessive forest cover in the northern mountains and low sea levels
   - B) Severe deforestation accelerating catchment runoff combined with climate-induced erratic monsoon downpours
   - C) Strict adherence to urban zoning laws that restrict river development
   - D) Depletion of all industrial greenhouse emissions within the province

9. *(Understand)* In the context of Pakistan's federal system, what is the crucial distinction between cultural assimilation and national integration?
   - A) Assimilation celebrates regional languages, whereas integration forces all citizens to speak a single dialect
   - B) Assimilation demands the erasure of regional identities, whereas integration builds national unity upon equal rights while respecting cultural diversity
   - C) Integration requires the dissolution of all provincial boundaries into a single unitary province
   - D) Assimilation is a constitutional requirement under Article 33 of the 1973 Constitution

10. *(Apply)* In a Pakistan Studies class, a teacher moves away from traditional rote civics by having pupils investigate local municipal water contamination and draft a formal petition to the local council. According to Dean (2005), which dimension of active civic education is primarily developed here?
   - A) Passive compliance with authoritarian school regulations
   - B) Purely theoretical memorisation of constitutional clauses
   - C) Practical civic skills in collaborative problem-solving and institutional engagement
   - D) Preparation solely for casting ballots in general parliamentary elections

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* State three enduring principles that guide Pakistan's foreign policy under the 1973 Constitution and international law. Answer in three complete sentences.
2. *(Understand)* Explain the primary historical dispute that has impeded the normalisation of bilateral relations between Pakistan and India since 1947. Answer in two to three sentences.
3. *(Understand)* Describe the strategic significance of the border between Pakistan and Afghanistan, identifying one major mutual interest and one persistent challenge. Answer in three to four sentences.
4. *(Understand)* Name two major international organisations of which Pakistan is a founding member, and explain one key diplomatic contribution made within each. Answer in two to three sentences.
5. *(Understand)* Explain how low rates of female secondary education directly contribute to high population growth in rural Pakistan. Answer in two to three sentences.
6. *(Apply)* A district education officer notes high dropout rates among 14-year-old boys in an impoverished rural union council. Explain how household poverty leads to this outcome, citing the economic concept of opportunity cost. Answer in three to four sentences.
7. *(Understand)* Explain two major objectives of the National Action Plan (NAP) adopted by Pakistan following the 2014 Army Public School tragedy. Answer in two to three sentences.
8. *(Apply)* Pakistan's per capita water availability has dropped below 1,000 cubic metres annually. Provide two distinct policy or agricultural measures required to manage this acute water stress. Answer in two to three sentences.
9. *(Understand)* How does Article 33 of the 1973 Constitution guide teachers in handling provincial, ethnic, and sectarian differences among pupils in school? Answer in two to three sentences.
10. *(Apply)* Contrast a traditional, passive civics classroom with an active, inquiry-based civic education classroom using a concrete example of how students learn about local governance. Answer in three to four sentences.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* Analyze how Pakistan's foreign policy seeks to maintain a strategic balance between its close partnership with China and its bilateral relationships with neighboring states (Afghanistan, Iran, and India). Evaluate the tensions between national security priorities and regional economic connectivity.
2. *(Analyze)* Analyze the systemic interactions between rapid population growth, elevated poverty rates, youth unemployment, and environmental vulnerability in modern Pakistan. Explain why treating any one of these issues in isolation fails to achieve sustainable national development.
3. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the argument that forced cultural assimilation weakens national unity, whereas democratic national integration based on equal constitutional rights strengthens it. Discuss the role of provincial languages, inclusive curricula, and Article 33 in building a resilient Pakistani federation.
4. *(Create)* Propose a comprehensive, school-based intervention strategy titled "Education for Peace and Resilience" designed to counter violent extremism and sectarian intolerance among secondary school students. Your proposal must include curricular initiatives, school cultural reforms, and community outreach.
5. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the statement: "The school teacher is the ultimate nation builder in Pakistan, bridging international obligations, domestic challenges, and civic cohesion." Assess how a classroom teacher translates constitutional ideals into daily educational practice to address Pakistan's contemporary crises.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. D - Article 40 of the 1973 Constitution specifically directs the state to foster international peace and strengthen fraternal ties among Muslim nations.
2. B - Quaid-e-Azam declared in February 1948 that Pakistan's foreign policy is founded on friendliness, goodwill, honesty, and fair play towards all nations.
3. B - CPEC transformed the bilateral relationship with China by adding multi-billion-dollar energy, highway, and port infrastructure to traditional diplomatic ties.
4. B - Pakistan has consistently served as one of the world's leading contributors of military and police personnel to United Nations peacekeeping operations.
5. C - The 2023 Digital Census officially recorded Pakistan's population at approximately 241.5 million with an annual intercensal growth rate of 2.55 percent.
6. B - High dependency ratios mean that a single breadwinner's earnings must feed and clothe many non-working dependents, leaving no surplus for schooling or health.
7. B - Terrorism is driven primarily by violent ideologies, geopolitical conflicts, and organized militant networks, while the vast majority of poor citizens remain peaceful.
8. B - Catchment deforestation strips mountain hillsides of soil protection, accelerating rainwater runoff that combines with climate-induced monsoons to produce catastrophic riverine flooding.
9. B - Assimilation attempts to erase provincial cultural differences, whereas integration unites diverse groups around shared democratic citizenship while respecting their distinct heritages.
10. C - Engaging local municipal officials on community sanitation teaches active civic skills in collective problem-solving, moving beyond passive memorisation.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model answer:** First, Pakistan's foreign policy is committed to safeguarding national sovereignty and territorial integrity against external aggression. Second, Pakistan upholds the peaceful settlement of international disputes in accordance with the United Nations Charter. Third, Article 40 of the Constitution mandates the preservation and strengthening of fraternal relations among Muslim countries.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for sovereignty/territorial integrity; 1 mark for peaceful dispute settlement/UN Charter; 1 mark for Muslim solidarity/Article 40.

2. **Model answer:** The primary historical impediment has been the unresolved dispute over the territory of Jammu and Kashmir since the partition of 1947. This dispute triggered major wars and ongoing military skirmishes, stalling trade and diplomatic normalisation despite peace initiatives such as the Indus Waters Treaty.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for identifying the Jammu and Kashmir dispute; 1 mark for noting the resulting military conflict and border tensions; 1 mark for explaining the impact on trade and normalisation.

3. **Model answer:** The 2,600 km Durand Line represents a vital frontier connecting Pakistan with Central Asian transit corridors. A major cooperative interest is bilateral trade and transit commerce for landlocked Afghanistan. A persistent challenge is border security, including unregulated crossings and cross-border militant sanctuaries that disrupt regional stability.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for noting geographical/transit significance; 1 mark for identifying trade/transit as mutual interest; 1 mark for identifying cross-border security/militant movement as the core challenge.

4. **Model answer:** Pakistan is a founding member of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) and the South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC). In the OIC, Pakistan hosted the landmark 1974 Lahore Summit to unify Muslim diplomacy; in SAARC, Pakistan championed regional commerce through the South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA).
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for naming OIC and SAARC; 1 mark for contribution to OIC (1974 summit or Muslim advocacy); 1 mark for contribution to SAARC (SAFTA or hosting summits).

5. **Model answer:** When girls complete secondary education, they typically marry later in life, acquire knowledge regarding health and birth spacing, and participate more actively in household decision-making. Conversely, low secondary schooling in rural areas leads to early marriages, extended reproductive periods, and limited access to family planning information.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for linking education to delayed marriage; 1 mark for linking education to health/family planning awareness; 1 mark for contrasting with rural early marriage outcomes.

6. **Model answer:** In impoverished rural households, the opportunity cost of schooling is the immediate daily wage a child forgives by sitting in class. When a family cannot afford food, withdrawing a 14-year-old boy from school allows him to earn casual agricultural wages, meeting short-term survival needs at the cost of long-term human development.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for defining opportunity cost as foregone daily earnings; 1 mark for linking family survival to child labour; 1 mark for concluding that poverty forces immediate work over future education.

7. **Model answer:** First, the National Action Plan sought to eliminate militant sanctuaries through decisive, targeted security and military operations across the country. Second, it aimed to dismantle ideological and financial support for violent extremism by banning hate speech, regulating madrasa funding, and setting up counter-terrorism intelligence bodies.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1.5 marks for military/counter-militancy operations; 1.5 marks for ideological/financial choking of extremist networks and hate speech.

8. **Model answer:** First, the agricultural sector must transition from obsolete flood-irrigation methods to water-efficient techniques such as drip and sprinkler irrigation, alongside canal lining to reduce seepage losses. Second, the government must implement the National Climate Change Policy by constructing rainwater storage reservoirs and enforcing watershed catchment protection.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1.5 marks for agricultural efficiency (drip irrigation, canal lining); 1.5 marks for policy/catchment management (storage, water policy enforcement).

9. **Model answer:** Article 33 directs the state to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian, and provincial prejudices among citizens. For teachers, this means actively eliminating ethnic favouritism in the classroom, ensuring that pupils from all regional and linguistic backgrounds receive equal respect, and forbidding derogatory stereotyping among students.
   * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1 mark for identifying Article 33 mandate against parochial prejudices; 1 mark for classroom equity regardless of background; 1 mark for actively preventing ethnic/sectarian stereotyping.

10. **Model answer:** In a traditional classroom, students passively memorize the names of municipal tiers from a textbook without understanding their function. In an active civic classroom, pupils investigate a real neighbourhood sanitation problem, identify the legally responsible local council official, and collaboratively draft an official petition, thereby practicing democratic participation and institutional accountability.
    * **Mark scheme (3 marks):** 1.5 marks for describing passive textbook memorisation; 1.5 marks for describing the active problem-solving and petition-writing approach.
