## A real classroom situation

Tariq is completing his final B.Ed practicum at a government high school in Sukkur. His Class 9 Pakistan Studies class includes pupils from diverse backgrounds: Sindhi-speaking boys from agricultural families, Urdu-speaking pupils whose parents are city shopkeepers, and children of Pashtun and Punjabi transport workers who settled in the district.

During a discussion on national identity, a disagreement breaks out. One student remarks, "Sindhis, Pashtuns, Baloch, and Punjabis have different languages, poetry, and traditions. How can we talk about a single national identity when everyone looks out only for their own province?" Another student responds, "Our textbook says national unity means everyone should speak only Urdu and forget regional differences." A third pupil mutters, "Why are we debating this? Teachers cannot change the country; a teacher's only job is to finish the textbook chapters so we pass our board examinations."

Tariq listens to these heartfelt frustrations. He recognizes two profound errors. First, his pupils confuse integration with assimilation: they assume that to be a united nation, citizens must erase their rich regional and cultural heritages. Second, they view the teacher as a mere technician of exam preparation, completely unaware of the teacher's historic and constitutional role in building a democratic, unified society.

## Explanation

Nation building is not achieved solely through military defense, central legislation, or large physical infrastructure. True nationhood is forged in the minds of young citizens. Education is the primary social institution where diverse individuals encounter one another as equal members of a shared democratic community.

### Education and national integration

<Glossary term="National integration">National integration</Glossary> is the process of bringing diverse regional, cultural, linguistic, and religious groups together into a cohesive, harmonious nation based on equal citizenship, mutual trust, and shared democratic values.

To understand national integration, prospective teachers must distinguish between two contrasting concepts:

* **Assimilation (erasing diversity):** An authoritarian approach that demands all citizens discard their provincial languages, local customs, and cultural heritages to adopt a single uniform identity. History shows that forced assimilation creates resentment, alienation, and regional conflict.
* **Integration (unity in diversity):** A democratic approach that celebrates cultural and linguistic diversity while uniting all citizens around common constitutional principles, equal rights, and shared national goals.

Pakistan is a multi-ethnic, multilingual federation. The Constitution of 1973 explicitly embraces unity in diversity. Article 33 directs the state to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian, and provincial prejudices among citizens. Furthermore, Article 251 protects regional languages, affirming that provincial assemblies may prescribe measures for the teaching, promotion, and use of provincial languages alongside the national language, Urdu.

```
       +-------------------------------------------------------+
       |               APPROACHES TO NATIONHOOD                |
       +-------------------------------------------------------+
                |                                      |
                v                                      v
    [ Assimilation: Rigid ]                [ Integration: Plural ]
   - Erases local languages               - Values mother tongues
   - Views diversity as threat            - Views diversity as strength
   - Imposes central uniformity           - Unites through equal rights
   - Breeds regional resentment           - Builds durable cohesion
```

#### The role of schools in fostering national integration

Schools promote national integration through deliberate structural and pedagogical choices:

1. **Equal access under Article 25A:** By providing free, compulsory, and high-quality education to all children aged 5 to 16, the public school acts as a great social equalizer, bridging economic and social divides.
2. **Balanced, inclusive curricula:** History and social studies must present the contributions of all provinces - Sindh, Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan, Gilgit-Baltistan, and Azad Jammu and Kashmir - with dignity, balance, and evidence.
3. **Linguistic respect:** Schools build national cohesion by teaching Urdu as a shared national medium of communication while respecting and valuing students' mother tongues (such as Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, Punjabi, and Saraiki).
4. **Inter-cultural exposure:** Organizing sports competitions, arts exhibitions, and cultural exchanges that celebrate regional folk arts, literature, and festivals teaches children to appreciate the rich mosaic of Pakistani society.

### Civic education: what it is and why it matters

<Glossary term="Civic education">Civic education</Glossary> is the preparation of young people to understand their constitutional rights and duties, engage constructively in community life, and participate responsibly in democratic governance.

In an influential study of Pakistani schooling, Dean (2005) argued that civics instruction has historically failed because it treated students as passive vessels. Traditional teaching focused on the rote memorisation of government department names, parliamentary seats, and historical dates. This approach produced passive citizens who know facts for examinations but lack the ethical commitment, critical thinking, or practical agency required to build a democratic society.

Dean (2005) emphasized that meaningful civic education requires three interlinked dimensions:

#### 1. Civic knowledge

Learners must understand how their country is governed and how their rights are protected. This includes:
* Understanding the 1973 Constitution, especially the Fundamental Rights guaranteed in Articles 8 to 28 (equality, freedom of speech, fair trial, religious freedom, right to education).
* Grasping the separation of powers between the legislature, executive, and independent judiciary.
* Understanding the federal structure and provincial autonomy established by the Eighteenth Constitutional Amendment.

#### 2. Civic skills

Knowledge alone is insufficient; citizens must develop active democratic capacities:
* **Critical media literacy:** The ability to evaluate news, identify misinformation, and reject inflammatory sectarian or extremist propaganda.
* **Constructive deliberation:** The skill of debating controversial public issues calmly, listening to opposing views with respect, and seeking common ground.
* **Collaborative problem-solving:** Working with neighbours and classmates to identify community problems and organize collective solutions.

#### 3. Civic values

The ethical foundation of citizenship:
* Unwavering commitment to social justice and human dignity for all persons, regardless of religion, gender, ethnicity, or social class.
* Respect for the rule of law and rejection of corruption and vigilantism.
* Care for public property, civic spaces, and the natural environment.

**Correcting a common misconception: "Civic education is only about voting."** Voting during elections is an essential civic duty, but it occurs once every several years. Real civic life happens every single day. Civic education teaches young people how to treat their neighbours, how to resolve disputes without violence, how to conserve community water and electricity, how to demand accountability from local officials, and how to defend the constitutional rights of vulnerable fellow citizens.

A worked example illustrates the shift from passive memorisation to active civic learning:

* **Traditional passive lesson:** The teacher writes the three tiers of local government on the chalkboard. Pupils copy the notes silently into their exercise books and memorize them for a weekly quiz.
* **Active civic learning lesson:** The teacher presents a real local problem: standing drainage water outside the school gate that breeds mosquitoes and prevents children from walking to school safely.
* **Student task:** Pupils work in groups to examine municipal responsibilities. They investigate which local government official (the Union Council secretary or municipal officer) is legally responsible for sanitation.
* **Action:** The class collaboratively drafts a respectful, structured letter describing the health hazard and requesting municipal action.
* **Pedagogical outcome:** Pupils learn local government structure by using it; they experience civic agency and understand that citizens have the right and responsibility to engage public institutions.

### Responsibilities of teachers in nation building

The classroom teacher is the most important civic actor in the school system. Long after pupils forget specific textbook details, they remember how their teacher treated them, how disputes were handled, and whether every child felt valued.

<Figure id="fig-U6-6" src="/img/figures/gpks-402/unit-06/fig-U6-6.svg" alt="Diagram of concentric circles illustrating the teacher's role as a nation builder radiating from classroom practice to school culture and community leadership." />

As illustrated in the figure, the teacher's role as a nation builder radiates outward across three distinct spheres:

#### 1. The classroom sphere: Modeling democratic practice

Within the four walls of the classroom, the teacher holds sovereign moral authority:
* **Uncompromising equity:** The teacher ensures that every pupil - whether male or female, affluent or poor, Muslim or non-Muslim, Urdu-speaker or Sindhi-speaker - receives equal attention, dignity, and encouragement.
* **Eradicating corporal punishment:** Violence in the classroom teaches children that might makes right. A teacher who manages discipline through fair, clear rules, mutual respect, and patient explanation models the constitutional rule of law.
* **Encouraging questioning and critical thinking:** Despotic regimes rely on unthinking obedience; a democratic nation requires thoughtful, discerning citizens. A teacher who welcomes honest student questions helps immunize youth against extremist manipulation.

#### 2. The school sphere: Cultivating an inclusive culture

Beyond individual lessons, teachers shape the institutional climate of the school:
* Fostering student councils and collaborative clubs where pupils learn leadership, team collaboration, and democratic decision-making.
* Organizing national day celebrations (such as 14 August Independence Day and 23 March Pakistan Day) that highlight the heroic contributions of all provinces, religious minorities, and women in the creation and defense of Pakistan.
* Actively intervening against bullying, ethnic teasing, and sectarian exclusion among pupils.

#### 3. The community sphere: Civic leadership

In many towns and villages across Pakistan, the school teacher is one of the most respected and educated figures in the community:
* Engaging parents to support girls' education and prevent early school dropout.
* Serving as an impartial, trusted mediator in local neighborhood disputes, modeling peaceful conflict resolution.
* Mobilising young citizens for local community service: tree-planting drives, village clean-up campaigns, and adult literacy circles.

**Correcting a common misconception: "Teachers have no role in nation building beyond the classroom."** Some view teaching as a routine clerical job restricted to delivering textbook chapters. In reality, teachers are the foundational architects of the nation. By shaping the values, reasoning habits, and social attitudes of future parents, judges, police officers, civil servants, and workers, teachers hold the future of Pakistan in their hands.

## Activity: Designing an Inclusive National Integration Lesson

* **Format:** Work in pairs.
* **Time:** Approximately 20 minutes.
* **Context:** A B.Ed curriculum design workshop.

**Instructions:**
1. Choose a national celebration event (such as Pakistan Independence Day on 14 August) or a standard Pakistan Studies curriculum unit.
2. Design a 40-minute interactive lesson plan that explicitly promotes national integration while celebrating diversity:
   - **Hook (5 mins):** A brief video, song, or image showing cultural artefacts (shawls, musical instruments, architecture) from Sindh, Punjab, Balochistan, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
   - **Group Inquiry (20 mins):** Assign each small group of pupils one province or territory. Pupils research and share one unique cultural contribution and one major national economic contribution made by that province to the federation.
   - **Synthesis (10 mins):** Guide pupils to construct a shared class poster titled: "Four Provinces, One Nation: United in Equal Citizenship."
   - **Reflection (5 mins):** Pupils write one sentence in their notebooks answering: "How does respecting my neighbour's mother tongue make Pakistan stronger?"
3. Exchange your plan with another pair and provide constructive feedback on whether the activities actively engage all learners.

## Summary

National integration in Pakistan cannot be built through forced assimilation; it requires an inclusive federation that values cultural and linguistic diversity while uniting all citizens under equal constitutional rights and Article 33 directives. Meaningful civic education moves beyond rote memorisation to cultivate active civic knowledge, critical deliberation skills, and democratic values. Within this framework, the teacher acts as a primary nation builder across three radiating spheres: establishing an equitable and non-violent classroom, shaping an inclusive school climate, and providing civic leadership in the broader community.
