## A real classroom situation

Hina is a B.Ed trainee teaching Pakistan Studies at a government girls' higher secondary school in Larkana. During a discussion on modern Pakistan, she asks her Class 10 pupils to name the greatest challenge facing their community. The answers come rapidly. One student mentions that her family's small farm was ruined by the 2022 monsoon floods, forcing her elder brother to abandon his college studies to look for casual labour. Another pupil argues, "My father says unemployment and poverty are the only reason why young men join extremist groups." A third pupil sighs and remarks, "Our country has too many people and too few resources. Floods and poverty are our fate, and there is nothing anyone can do."

Hina listens attentively. She realizes that her pupils see the symptoms of crisis all around them, but they struggle to connect the underlying causes. They view environmental catastrophes as unpreventable natural fates and explain complex social phenomena such as extremism through single, oversimplified explanations.

## Explanation

No national challenge exists in isolation. Rapid population growth strains public services, which deepens poverty; poverty and lack of productive employment leave young people vulnerable to alienation; environmental degradation and climate disasters wipe out family livelihoods in a single season. Understanding modern Pakistan requires examining how these pressures interact.

### Population growth: causes, consequences and policy responses

According to the 2023 Population and Housing Census, Pakistan's population was approximately 241.5 million, with an annual growth rate of 2.55 percent between censuses (its intercensal growth rate, measuring the average yearly increase from the 2017 count to the 2023 count). The Pakistan Demographic and Health Survey (PDHS 2017-18) reported a total fertility rate (TFR) of 3.6 children per woman. At this pace, the population is projected to double in less than three decades.

#### Causes of high population growth

Demographic research and survey evidence identify several root causes for this rapid expansion:

* **Low female educational attainment:** In districts where girls complete secondary education, family sizes consistently decline. Where female literacy remains low, fertility rates remain high.
* **Early marriage:** In many rural communities, early marriage extends the reproductive span of women.
* **Unmet need for family planning:** Many families express a desire to space births for maternal and child health but lack reliable access to healthcare facilities, advice, or supplies.
* **Cultural norms:** Traditional attitudes often associate a large family - especially having multiple sons - with family security, social status, and agricultural labour support.

#### Consequences for human development

The consequences of rapid population growth touch every sector of society:

* **Schooling deficits:** The school-age population grows faster than new classrooms and teachers can be funded. Pakistan faces a crisis of an estimated 25 to 26 million out-of-school children aged 5 to 16 (UNICEF, 2024; Pakistan Institute of Education, 2024), representing a massive loss of human potential.
* **Health and nutrition burdens:** Rapidly expanding families often struggle to provide balanced nutrition, contributing to high rates of childhood stunting. The 2018 National Nutrition Survey found stunting in 40.2 percent of children under five.
* **Urban and water strain:** Cities such as Karachi, Lahore, and Faisalabad experience rapid unplanned growth, resulting in informal squatter settlements, acute drinking water shortages, and severe waste management crises.

#### Policy responses

National responses focus on expanding the Lady Health Workers (LHW) programme, integrating reproductive health into primary healthcare centres, and launching public awareness campaigns through community and religious leaders. Crucially, evidence demonstrates that expanding girls' access to safe, high-quality secondary education is the single most effective long-term policy for stabilizing population growth.

### Poverty and unemployment

<Glossary term="Poverty">Poverty</Glossary> and joblessness represent fundamental hurdles to social stability and economic progress. In economic analysis, poverty is measured either as absolute poverty (the inability to afford basic nutritional and non-food necessities) or relative poverty (inequality relative to general societal standards).

The World Bank's *Pakistan Poverty and Equity Brief* (October 2024) estimates that 40.5 percent of the population lived below the lower-middle-income poverty line of US$3.65 per day (2017 PPP) in FY24. This is a different measure from Pakistan's national poverty line, which the World Bank's October 2025 *Development Update* puts at 22.2 percent in FY25. Recent macroeconomic shocks, sharp food and fuel inflation, and the devastation of agricultural crops by floods have pushed millions of vulnerable households into acute financial distress.

#### Unemployment and the youth bulge

Census and survey data indicate that over 60 percent of Pakistan's population is under 30 years of age. While this "youth bulge" has the potential to drive economic growth if young citizens are educated and productively employed, it becomes a severe liability if the economy cannot absorb them.

Every year, labour-force estimates suggest that between two and three million young people enter the domestic labour market. The formal economic sector generates only a fraction of the necessary jobs, leaving millions to work in the informal economy - in low-wage agriculture, petty retail, or unregistered workshops without contracts, health benefits, or pensions. Furthermore, a high proportion of youth, especially young women, remain not in education, employment, or training (NEET).

#### Constitutional mandates and social safety nets

Article 38 of the 1973 Constitution directs the state to secure the social and economic well-being of the people, reduce extreme inequalities in wealth, and provide basic necessities such as food, clothing, housing, and healthcare for all citizens unable to earn their livelihood.

The primary state initiative addressing acute poverty is the Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP), established in 2008. BISP provides unconditional cash transfers to millions of poor women across Pakistan, as well as conditional educational stipends (Taleemi Wazaif) that require children to maintain a minimum attendance record in primary and secondary schools. Complementary programs by the National Vocational and Technical Training Commission (NAVTTC) offer technical skills training to help young workers secure productive livelihoods.

A worked example demonstrates the link between family size and poverty:

* **Scenario:** A rural farming family in upper Sindh consists of two parents and six school-age children. The family relies on a single daily-wage earner earning Rs. 25,000 per month.
* **Analysis:** With eight family members, per-capita monthly income is approximately Rs. 3,125 (under Rs. 105 per day per person).
* **Consequence:** After spending on flour, cooking oil, and shelter, nothing remains for school transport, books, or uniforms. The elder children are withdrawn from school to work as agricultural labourers, perpetuating low educational attainment and intergenerational poverty.

### Terrorism and extremism: origins, impact and responses

Over the past two decades, violent <Glossary term="Terrorism">terrorism</Glossary> and ideological <Glossary term="Extremism">extremism</Glossary> have inflicted profound suffering on Pakistani society. Terrorism is the unlawful use of violence or intimidation against civilians, state institutions, or minority communities to advance political, ideological, or sectarian agendas. Extremism refers to holding rigid, intolerant beliefs that reject democratic constitutional law and pluralism.

#### Origins and drivers

The roots of modern extremism in Pakistan are complex and multi-faceted:

* **Geopolitical fallout:** The Soviet-Afghan war in the 1980s and the subsequent conflicts following the September 11, 2001 events in the United States militarised Pakistan's north-western border regions. Millions of firearms and militant networks entered the regional theatre.
* **Sectarian divisions and hate propaganda:** Unregulated educational materials, radical printing presses, and foreign funding in earlier decades fostered sectarian intolerance, turning community differences into violent rivalries.
* **Governance vacuums:** Peripheral border regions historically lacked effective administrative, judicial, and police infrastructure, enabling militant groups to establish sanctuaries.
* **Social and economic alienation:** While poverty alone does not generate violent ideology, economic exclusion and disenfranchisement (being shut out of productive jobs, public services, and legal justice) together with perceived social marginalisation makes young people susceptible to recruitment by extremist groups.

**Correcting a common misconception: "Terrorism is caused only by poverty."** This is a widespread mistake. Millions of impoverished Pakistanis lead peaceful, law-abiding lives, while many terrorist leaders have come from educated or wealthy backgrounds. Terrorism is driven primarily by violent political ideologies, organized networks, distorted religious interpretations, and strategic rivalries. Poverty may enable recruitment, but ideology and weak rule of law are its real drivers.

#### Impact on society and economy

The toll of terrorism on Pakistan has been immense. Estimates of the death toll from terrorist violence since 2002 vary by source, but widely cited research databases (such as the South Asia Terrorism Portal) and national economic surveys place the cumulative casualties of citizens and security personnel at over 70,000. Tragic attacks, such as the December 2014 massacre at the Army Public School in Peshawar where 132 school children were killed, left deep psychological scars on the national conscience.

The economic costs are estimated at over US$120 billion, covering damaged infrastructure, lost foreign direct investment, disrupted commercial trade, and public expenditure diverted from education and healthcare into counter-terrorism operations.

#### National responses

In response to the APS Peshawar tragedy, the federal and provincial leadership adopted the National Action Plan (NAP) in December 2014. Key measures included:

1. Targeted combat operations (direct military actions and counter-insurgency operations, such as Operation Zarb-e-Azb and Operation Radd-ul-Fasaad) to clear militant strongholds.
2. Establishment of the National Counter Terrorism Authority (NACTA) to coordinate intelligence.
3. Choking financial flows to banned organisations and regulating digital hate speech.
4. The *Paigham-e-Pakistan* national consensus declaration issued in 2018, endorsed by Islamic scholars of all schools of thought, which declared suicide bombings, sectarian violence, and rebellion against the state to be un-Islamic and unlawful.
5. School safety protocols and curriculum revisions to eliminate intolerant portrayals of minority groups.

### Environment: climate change, deforestation, water scarcity

Pakistan faces an escalating ecological crisis. Pakistan contributes a very small share of global greenhouse gas emissions, yet it is among the countries most vulnerable to climate change.

<Figure id="fig-U6-4" src="/img/figures/gpks-402/unit-06/fig-U6-4.svg" alt="Table summarizing four contemporary national issues in Pakistan with their indicators, affected areas, and policy responses." />

#### 1. Climate vulnerability and extreme events

Pakistan is situated in a geographic zone where warming trends exceed the global average. Extreme weather events have become frequent and severe:

* **Super-floods:** The 2022 monsoon floods submerged one-third of the country, affected an estimated 33 million people (with approximately 8 million displaced), damaged or destroyed over two million homes, ruined millions of acres of standing crops, and caused damages and economic losses estimated at over US$30 billion (Post-Disaster Needs Assessment, 2022).
* **Heatwaves and droughts:** Cities such as Jacobabad, Sibi, and Nawabshah routinely experience summer temperatures exceeding 50 degrees Celsius, threatening human survival, outdoor farm labour, and livestock.

#### 2. Water scarcity and the Indus basin

Pakistan's agriculture and economy depend almost entirely on the Indus River Basin, fed by glaciers in the Karakoram, Hindukush, and Himalayan mountain ranges. However, Pakistan has transitioned from being a water-abundant country at independence to a water-stressed country today.

Per capita surface water availability has declined from 5,260 cubic metres in 1951 to around 1,000 cubic metres in 2016, and to below 1,000 cubic metres in recent estimates (National Water Policy, 2018; PCRWR) - the international Falkenmark benchmark for acute water scarcity. Accelerated melting of northern glaciers threatens temporary river flooding followed by long-term flow depletion, while obsolete flood-irrigation techniques in agriculture waste substantial volumes of fresh water.

#### 3. Deforestation and land degradation

Forests cover only 5.45 percent of Pakistan's total land area. The Updated National Climate Change Policy (2021) treats this as a serious deficit and prioritises afforestation and catchment protection. Uncontrolled timber harvesting, clearing land for housing schemes, and dependence on firewood in rural areas have stripped mountainsides in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Gilgit-Baltistan and degraded riverine forests in Sindh. Deforestation accelerates topsoil erosion, silts up major reservoirs such as Tarbela and Mangla, and drastically increases the speed and violence of downstream floodwaters.

#### Policy initiatives

The Government of Pakistan approved the Updated National Climate Change Policy in 2021 (MOCC, 2021), focusing on climate adaptation, drought-resistant agriculture, and early warning systems. Key initiatives include the Ten Billion Tree Tsunami afforestation drive, the Living Indus Initiative to restore the health of the river basin, and new building codes for climate-resilient schools and public infrastructure.

## Activity: Problem-Tree Analysis of a Contemporary Challenge

* **Format:** Work in pairs.
* **Time:** Approximately 20 minutes.
* **Context:** A teacher education seminar on social issues in education.

**Instructions:**
1. Choose one of the four contemporary issues discussed in this topic:
   - Option A: Rapid population growth
   - Option B: Youth unemployment
   - Option C: Violent extremism
   - Option D: Water scarcity and climate stress
2. Draw a simple "Problem Tree" on a sheet of paper:
   - **Roots:** Identify at least three distinct root causes (political, cultural, economic, or geographic).
   - **Trunk:** Name the specific core issue.
   - **Branches:** Identify three major downstream consequences for children and school classrooms.
3. Underneath your tree, write down two concrete actions a school teacher could take (such as promoting water conservation habits or establishing an inclusive classroom debate club) to help address the problem.
4. Share your problem tree with another pair and discuss how the chosen challenges reinforce each other.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* State Pakistan's current approximate population from the 2023 Digital Census and its annual intercensal growth rate.
2. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between absolute poverty and relative poverty, and give an example of how poverty affects school attendance in rural Pakistan.
3. *(Apply)* A local elder in your school's village remarks: "Floods are purely an act of nature, and human planning has nothing to do with them." Using the concepts of climate vulnerability and deforestation, provide two reasoned points explaining how human actions worsen flood severity.

## Summary

Pakistan faces four deeply interconnected contemporary challenges: rapid population growth (2.55 percent annually), which strains schools, hospitals, and infrastructure; persistent poverty and youth unemployment, which affect approximately 40 percent of households; terrorism and extremism, which demand both security enforcement and ideological deradicalisation through initiatives like *Paigham-e-Pakistan*; and acute environmental vulnerability, highlighted by devastating floods, water scarcity, and deforestation. Addressing these challenges requires coordinated public policy, constitutional commitment, and the active engagement of educational institutions.
