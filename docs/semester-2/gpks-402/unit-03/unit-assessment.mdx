# Constitutional Development - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Pakistan's constitutional development involves both ideas about legitimate authority and institutions intended to give those ideas effect. The Objectives Resolution of 1949 set guiding commitments, including Islamic principles, chosen representatives, rights, minority safeguards, an independent judiciary and a federation with autonomous units. It was not a complete constitution. The first full constitution came into force in 1956. A parliamentary design placed the Prime Minister at the head of government, with a President as head of state and one central legislative chamber.

The 1962 Constitution adopted a presidential design while retaining a federal form and one central chamber. Executive authority was concentrated in the presidency, and the Basic Democracies electoral college provided an indirect route to national offices. Strong central power did not make the framework simply unitary. Its later amendments also mean that statements about its rights safeguards need a date or a version qualification. The abrogations of 1958 and 1969 differed from amendments: they interrupted constitutional frameworks instead of changing their text through the ordinary constitutional route.

Representation, provincial autonomy and acceptance of institutional limits remained difficult questions. The 1970 election, the failure to agree on transfer of power and the 1971 crisis formed the background to a new settlement in the remaining federation. An Interim Constitution and committee work in 1972 preceded adoption of the permanent Constitution in April 1973. It came into force on 14 August. Formation involved political agreement and institutional work, not only the action of a single person.

The 1973 framework combines parliamentary government, federalism, two federal chambers, rights and Islamic provisions. The National Assembly and Senate represent population and provinces on different bases. Council of Islamic Ideology advice is distinct from legislation. Articles concerning Islam must be read with their institutional functions and related protections, including the safeguard for non-Muslim personal laws and citizenship in Article 227(3). A careful reader does not infer the entire constitutional system from its title alone.

Continuity since 1973 has included interruptions and amendments. The Objectives Resolution was already in the original Preamble; Article 2A gave it substantive status in 1985. Article 25A and the removal of the Concurrent Legislative List belong to the Eighteenth Amendment of 2010. The reading edition used here incorporates amendments to 28 February 2012. It supports historical study at that date, not an undated claim about today's law. When explaining a provision, keep article, institution, scope and date together.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose A, B, C or D. Each item is worth 1 mark.

1. What was the principal function of the Objectives Resolution when adopted in 1949? *(Remember)*
   - A) To establish the two chambers of the 1973 Parliament.
   - B) To state guiding principles for making a constitution.
   - C) To introduce the presidential cabinet of the 1962 system.
   - D) To replace the Interim Constitution adopted in 1972.

2. Which distinction between a government and a constitution is most accurate? *(Understand)*
   - A) A government sets permanent rules; a constitution administers daily policy.
   - B) A government and a constitution are two names for the cabinet.
   - C) A government holds office; a constitution establishes governing rules.
   - D) A government records rights; a constitution records election results.

3. A pupil finds that the 1956 framework had a President and concludes that it was presidential. Which further feature best helps classify the system? *(Apply)*
   - A) The Prime Minister headed government in a parliamentary arrangement.
   - B) The state used a constitutional title identifying it as a republic.
   - C) The country maintained provincial as well as national institutions.
   - D) The constitutional document took effect after formal approval.

4. What role did Basic Democrats perform in the 1962 framework? *(Remember)*
   - A) They acted as the permanent judges of provincial courts.
   - B) They formed the second chamber of the federal legislature.
   - C) They drafted the Interim Constitution after the 1971 crisis.
   - D) They served as an electoral college for national institutions.

5. A timeline groups the 1958 abrogation with changes passed within a continuing constitutional framework. Which label best distinguishes the 1958 event? *(Apply)*
   - A) A transfer of office under the same parliamentary rules.
   - B) A replacement of one policy through ordinary legislation.
   - C) A setting aside of the constitutional framework itself.
   - D) A clarification of an existing clause through amendment.

6. Which event occurred on 14 August 1973? *(Remember)*
   - A) The Objectives Resolution was adopted by the first Assembly.
   - B) The permanent 1973 Constitution came into force.
   - C) The Constitution Committee presented its first draft.
   - D) The Assembly first adopted the Interim Constitution.

7. What is the main representative purpose of combining the National Assembly and Senate? *(Understand)*
   - A) To give the federal executive two separate cabinets.
   - B) To provide identical institutions for the same electorate.
   - C) To make provincial governments branches of one House.
   - D) To combine population-based and provincial representation.

8. A trainee sees Article 25A in the official 2012 edition and wants to describe its historical origin. What should the trainee do next? *(Apply)*
   - A) Check the amendment note identifying when it was inserted.
   - B) Use the Constitution's title year as the article's entry date.
   - C) Treat the article number as a year in the timeline.
   - D) Use the PDF download date as the provision's adoption date.

9. Which action best describes the Council of Islamic Ideology's constitutional role? *(Understand)*
   - A) Selecting the cabinet that heads the federal executive.
   - B) Replacing both Houses when a law concerns religion.
   - C) Advising on laws' conformity with Islamic injunctions.
   - D) Deciding the population basis for provincial representation.

10. A trainee prepares a card on the safeguard within Article 227 itself for non-Muslim citizens. Which provision directly addresses the card's subject? *(Apply)*
   - A) Article 227(1), concerning conformity of laws.
   - B) Article 227(3), concerning personal laws and citizenship status.
   - C) Article 228, concerning the Council's composition.
   - D) Article 230, concerning the Council's advisory functions.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Each response is worth 4 marks. Keep to the requested scope; a longer answer does not earn extra marks.

1. In **three sentences**, explain why the Objectives Resolution was significant but was not itself a complete constitution. Include one commitment concerning representation and one concerning protection of people. *(Understand)*

2. In **three or four sentences**, compare the executive and central legislature under the 1956 and 1962 frameworks. Explain one similarity and one difference. *(Analyze)*

3. Use an **invented example of two regions** to explain how equal regional seats can differ from representation by population. Give no historical population figures. Explain why the distinction mattered to constitution-making. *(Apply)*

4. A revision chart says, "The 1962 Constitution was unitary, so provincial institutions did not exist." In **three sentences**, identify the mistaken inference and replace it with a more accurate description. *(Analyze)*

5. In **four sentences**, connect one constitutional interruption before 1973 to one challenge of representation or accountability. Distinguish the interruption from an amendment. Do not claim that one factor explains the whole period. *(Analyze)*

6. In **three sentences**, distinguish passage, authentication and commencement using the three principal 1973 dates. *(Understand)*

7. In **four sentences**, explain how the two federal Houses address different representative concerns. Include why the President's inclusion in Article 50 does not make a third chamber. *(Analyze)*

8. A pupil writes, "The Objectives Resolution was added as the Preamble in 1985." In **three sentences**, correct the sequence using 1949, 1973 and Article 2A. *(Apply)*

9. In **four sentences**, distinguish the Council's recommendation from enactment of a law. Identify the relevant article range and one reason the difference matters for explaining the constitutional system. *(Analyze)*

10. In **four sentences**, explain why Article 25A in a 2012 copy cannot be used as proof of its presence in the original 1973 text. Name the relevant amendment and explain one limit of the reading edition for a present-day legal claim. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Each task is worth 12 marks. Write **500-650 words** per task. Use a short plan before drafting, and identify sources in the response.

1. A school display labels 1949 "first constitution", 1956 "presidential government" and 1962 "no provinces". Analyse the three errors and explain the distinction each correction requires. Propose a replacement display with a short note showing how learners could test it against official sources. Address document type, executive responsibility and federal form. *(Analyze)*

2. Evaluate the claim: "The only problem before 1973 was choosing the wrong executive system." Use the 1956 and 1962 frameworks, at least one interruption, and the representation or autonomy question to defend a qualified judgement. Explain what the evidence supports and what it cannot establish. *(Evaluate)*

3. Design a source-reading lesson for a Sindh secondary class on the formation and main features of the 1973 Constitution. Connect a challenge from the preceding period to one institutional response. Include a worked article-reading example, an individual retrieval question and a way to detect confusion between original and later provisions. *(Create)*

4. Evaluate this claim using constitutional institutions and safeguards: "Islamic provisions mean the Council passes laws and non-Muslim citizenship has no protection in the text." Use Article 227(3), the Council's article range and at least one related rights protection. Explain how a teacher could discuss the issue without requiring pupils to disclose beliefs. *(Evaluate)*

5. Compare the **1956, 1962 and 1973 frameworks on executive responsibility**, then connect that comparison to the Objectives Resolution's commitment to chosen representatives. Explain one contribution of the 1973 federal design and one later amendment that must be dated separately. Judge whether constitutional continuity can be established merely by showing that the same document title remains in use. *(Analyze / Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

This final section is intentionally public self-study guidance. It is distinct from any protected LMS quiz bank. Several restricted and extended tasks permit different examples and judgements. Credit a well-supported alternative where it meets the question's scope and the constitutional evidence. Do not award marks for agreement with a preferred political opinion or for a learner's religious identity.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** The Resolution set guiding constitutional commitments in 1949. It did not create the later Parliament or replace the 1972 interim framework.
2. **C.** A government occupies offices within a framework; a constitution establishes the rules and institutions under which it should govern. The distinction allows a change of government without a replacement constitution.
3. **A.** Classification depends on executive responsibility. The existence of a President, a republican title or provincial institutions does not by itself distinguish parliamentary from presidential government.
4. **D.** Basic Democrats provided the electoral college in the indirect system. They were not a second federal chamber or a body of judges.
5. **C.** Abrogation sets aside the framework. An amendment changes constitutional text through a constitutional procedure; an ordinary policy change is a different action again.
6. **B.** The Constitution came into force on 14 August 1973, following passage on 10 April and authentication on 12 April. The other options concern different documents or stages.
7. **D.** The design combines representation mainly by population in the National Assembly with equal provincial representation in the Senate. The Houses are not duplicate cabinets or identical bodies.
8. **A.** An amendment note helps establish when a provision entered the framework. A consolidated edition includes later changes, so its title year cannot date every article within it.
9. **C.** The Council advises and recommends under Articles 228-230. Its constitutional importance does not turn it into Parliament or the federal executive.
10. **B.** Article 227(3) explicitly safeguards non-Muslim personal laws and citizenship status within that Part. The other listed provisions address different functions.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**General marking:** award each named point once. Accept equivalent plain-English wording. An article number without an explanation does not earn a point intended for understanding. Conversely, do not penalise a minor wording difference where the constitutional meaning remains accurate. Each model shows the required content; learners may organise it differently within the stated sentence bound.

1. **Model:** The Objectives Resolution set principles to guide constitution-making after independence. It called for authority through chosen representatives and included rights or minority safeguards. It was not a complete constitution because it did not provide the full institutional structure and detailed procedures of government. **Marks (4):** 1 for guiding purpose; 1 for representation; 1 for a valid protective commitment; 1 for explaining the missing institutional detail. Merely saying "it was important" earns no purpose mark. Accept independent judiciary as the protective commitment if its role is explained.

2. **Model:** Both frameworks had one central legislative chamber and a federal form. In 1956 the Prime Minister headed government in a parliamentary arrangement. In 1962 executive authority lay with the President and the cabinet was responsible to that office. The similarity in having a President did not erase the difference in executive responsibility. **Marks (4):** 1 for the accurate legislative similarity; 1 for the 1956 executive; 1 for the 1962 executive; 1 for explicitly analysing responsibility as the distinguishing relationship. A list of office titles without the relationship earns at most 2.

3. **Model:** Suppose Region A has 60 voters and Region B has 40, but each receives ten seats. The regions have equal seats, while each A seat represents six voters and each B seat four. Population-based allocation would give the larger population more seats if the ratio were the same. Constitution-makers had to address the different claims of territorial balance and the weight of population. **Marks (4):** 1 for a clearly invented workable example; 1 for explaining equal seats; 1 for showing the population difference; 1 for connecting it to constitutional representation. No actual historical figure is required or rewarded.

4. **Model:** The chart mistakes concentrated central power for the absence of a federal form. The 1962 Constitution retained national and provincial institutions but strengthened the presidency, including presidential appointment of governors. It is more accurate to describe a formally federal framework with strong central control than to infer that provinces did not exist. **Marks (4):** 1 for identifying the inference; 1 for federal form; 1 for an accurate concentration example; 1 for a corrected statement that keeps form and degree of centralisation separate. An unsupported use of "unitary" receives no correction mark.

5. **Model:** The 1956 Constitution was abrogated in October 1958, interrupting a parliamentary framework before a general election under it. This deprived the constitutional system of a completed national electoral cycle through which authority could be renewed. The event illustrates a problem of securing observance of representative rules by powerful officeholders. It differed from an amendment because the framework was set aside rather than altered through its recognised process. **Marks (4):** 1 for an accurate interruption; 1 for a relevant institutional challenge; 1 for an explained connection; 1 for the amendment distinction. Accept a carefully supported alternative based on 1969. Do not require a single-cause account.

6. **Model:** Passage occurred on 10 April 1973 when the Assembly approved the constitutional text. Authentication followed on 12 April as formal confirmation. Commencement on 14 August meant the new framework came into force. **Marks (4):** 1 for each correct date-event pairing, plus 1 for distinguishing approval stages from operation. A bare list of three dates earns no more than 3. Do not substitute the 1972 Interim Constitution's adoption date for a stage in the permanent Constitution's formation.

7. **Model:** The National Assembly represents the population through its constitutional allocation of seats. The Senate gives provinces equal representation as provinces, helping ensure a voice for smaller units. The different bases of representation address concerns that one population-based chamber alone cannot address in the same way. Article 50 includes the President as a component of Parliament, but the chambers are the National Assembly and Senate. **Marks (4):** 1 for each House's representative basis; 1 for analysing why the combination matters; 1 for distinguishing the President from a chamber. Do not award the analytical point for merely repeating "bicameral".

8. **Model:** The Objectives Resolution was adopted in 1949 as a guide for constitution-making. Its commitments were included in the Preamble when the 1973 Constitution was adopted. Article 2A, inserted in 1985, made the Resolution reproduced in the Annex a substantive part of the Constitution; that was not its first appearance as a preamble. **Marks (4):** 1 for 1949 adoption; 1 for the original 1973 Preamble; 1 for dating Article 2A to 1985; 1 for distinguishing preambular inclusion from substantive status. Treating both events as the same change loses the final point.

9. **Model:** The Council of Islamic Ideology gives advice and recommendations on conformity with Islamic injunctions under Articles 228-230. Parliament and the relevant assemblies remain the law-making institutions within the constitutional process. A recommendation is therefore not itself the enactment of a law, even though constitutional duties concerning advice and reconsideration can matter. The distinction prevents an account that confuses a religious advisory institution with the legislature. **Marks (4):** 1 for the advisory function; 1 for the article range; 1 for distinguishing the legislative act; 1 for explaining the institutional significance. Accept Article 231 as additional procedural context, but do not require it.

10. **Model:** A consolidated 2012 copy combines the original framework with amendments made before its stated cutoff. Article 25A was added by the Eighteenth Amendment of 2010. Its presence therefore establishes the provision in that edition, not its presence in 1973. The edition does not include subsequent changes and cannot alone establish the law as amended today. **Marks (4):** 1 for explaining consolidation; 1 for naming and dating the amendment; 1 for correcting the historical inference; 1 for the present-day limit. Do not require an account of later amendments outside the reading edition.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Use the task-specific descriptors below. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation or evidence; 0 means absent, materially incorrect or unrelated. These levels apply to each criterion separately. A fluent essay can receive low marks where its institutional reasoning is wrong; short plain sentences can receive full marks.

**ERQ 1: Repairing the display**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Document-type analysis | Distinguishes guiding Resolution from full constitution and explains the 1949/1956 sequence | Gives accurate sequence but limited explanation of document type | Names an early document without explaining its function | Repeats the first-constitution error |
| Executive comparison | Explains parliamentary responsibility in 1956 and why a President does not prove presidential government | Classifies 1956 accurately but develops responsibility weakly | Names Prime Minister without connecting the role | Repeats presidential classification |
| Federal-form analysis | Corrects the 1962 claim by separating provincial institutions from central concentration | States federal form with limited explanation of concentration | Mentions provinces without analysing the mistaken inference | Repeats absence-of-provinces claim |
| Replacement and verification | Proposes clear replacement labels with a usable official checking method | Proposes accurate labels but a weak checking method | Offers one usable correction without a coherent display | Offers no workable correction |

A strong response repairs the underlying categories, not only the date labels. The third criterion explicitly requires analysis. A different display format can earn full marks if it makes the same institutional distinctions and can be checked against the official history and historical constitutional texts.

**ERQ 2: Evaluating an executive-only explanation**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accurate design contrast | Compares 1956 parliamentary and 1962 presidential responsibility accurately | Gives accurate classifications with one incomplete relationship | Names systems without explaining responsibility | Confuses the systems |
| Use of interruption evidence | Uses a dated interruption to test the executive-only claim | Gives a relevant event but makes a weak analytical link | Lists a date without using it as evidence | Gives no relevant evidence |
| Alternative challenge | Explains representation or autonomy as a distinct challenge and connects it to stability | Explains the challenge but its relevance is partly implicit | Names a challenge without developing it | Treats design as the only possible factor |
| Qualified judgement | Weighs evidence, states a defensible conclusion and identifies what it cannot prove | Gives a supported conclusion but a weak limitation | States an opinion with little support | Gives no defensible judgement |

Do not require a claim that executive design was irrelevant. A nuanced response can judge that design mattered while rejecting it as a complete explanation. Evidence of two interrupted designs supports a challenge to the claim; it does not prove that all forms of government have identical consequences.

**ERQ 3: Creating a source-reading lesson**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formation and design accuracy | Uses accurate formation stages and an accurately explained main feature | Gives stages and feature with one minor omission | Names dates or a feature without explanation | Presents material historical or institutional errors |
| Challenge-response analysis | Explicitly connects an earlier challenge to an institutional response and explains the limits of that response | Makes a plausible connection but leaves its limit implicit | Names challenge and feature without a clear relationship | Gives no relevant relationship |
| Worked source use | Models locating, paraphrasing and dating a provision with a clear edition distinction | Models article use but omits one reading step | Asks pupils to find an article without modelling | Treats all edition contents as original |
| Learning evidence | Includes feasible retrieval and a diagnostic check whose result informs revision | Includes both checks but weakly connects them to revision | Includes an activity without individual evidence | Includes no workable learning check |

The lesson can use printed extracts and local examples. Technology is not required. A strong design checks understanding before asking for an unsupported opinion. For example, a pupil's dating error should lead the teacher back to an amendment note, not merely to another memorisation exercise.

**ERQ 4: Evaluating the Council and citizenship claim**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional analysis | Distinguishes advice, reconsideration where applicable and enactment using relevant articles | Distinguishes advice and legislation with incomplete textual support | Calls the Council advisory without explaining the consequence | Gives law-making power to the Council |
| Article 227 safeguard | Explains clause (3)'s personal-law and citizenship protection with correct scope | Identifies safeguard but develops only one element | Refers generally to protection without locating or explaining it | Denies the textual safeguard |
| Related protection | Uses Article 20 or 22 accurately and relates it to the claim | Uses a relevant article with a weak connection | Names a right without explaining its relevance | Gives no relevant protection |
| Evidence-based and respectful judgement | Evaluates both parts of the claim and designs discussion that avoids compelled belief disclosure | Evaluates the claim but discussion safeguards are incomplete | Offers an opinion or a general conduct rule with little evidence | Relies on stereotypes or demands belief disclosure |

Full marks do not depend on a preferred label for the political system. They depend on explaining the actual constitutional relationships and protections. The learner may acknowledge debate about implementation while distinguishing that debate from what the cited text provides.

**ERQ 5: Integrating constitutional development**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three-framework comparison | Compares executive responsibility across all three accurately on the same dimension | Accurately compares two and partly explains the third | Lists three labels with little explanation | Gives a materially wrong comparison |
| Resolution and representation | Connects chosen representatives to the different institutional routes without treating guiding principles as identical mechanisms | Gives a plausible connection with a partly developed distinction | Names the Resolution without using it analytically | Gives no relevant link |
| Federal design and amendment | Explains one contribution of the 1973 design and dates a genuine later amendment separately | Explains one element fully and the other partly | Names a feature or amendment without explaining it | Treats a later addition as original or gives no relevant content |
| Judgement on continuity | Distinguishes title, operation and textual change, using interruption and amendment evidence | Distinguishes continuity and change but evidence is incomplete | Gives a general opinion with little supporting evidence | Equates an unchanged title with uninterrupted operation |

A defensible conclusion is that the continuing title alone is insufficient evidence of uninterrupted constitutional government. Accept another formulation if it distinguishes a continuing framework from the history of its operation and supports the judgement with specific evidence. Do not reward additional dates unless the writer uses them to develop the comparison.
