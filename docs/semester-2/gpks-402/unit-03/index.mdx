# Constitutional Development

A date tells us when a constitution appeared. It does not tell us who could govern, how leaders answered to representatives, or why the rules survived or broke down. This unit connects the dates to those questions. It prepares you to explain constitutional history through evidence rather than a list learned by heart.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the content and significance of the Objectives Resolution of 1949. *(Understand; SLO:GPKS-402-3-1)*
- Compare the 1956 and 1962 constitutions and explain the challenges that interrupted constitutional government before 1973. *(Analyze; SLO:GPKS-402-3-1)*
- Explain the background and formation of the 1973 Constitution. *(Understand; SLO:GPKS-402-3-2)*
- Use constitutional articles to explain its parliamentary, federal and rights-based features and its Islamic provisions. *(Apply; SLO:GPKS-402-3-2)*
- Distinguish the original framework from later amendments and support a comparison with dated evidence. *(Evaluate; both outcomes)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 2: the Pakistan Movement and independence in 1947. You should be able to explain why gaining a state creates a need to agree on rules for governing it. No previous study of law is required. Keep a notebook for dates, comparisons and unfamiliar words.

## How to use this unit

Read the topics in order. Each begins with a school situation, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before asking you to use them. Complete the pair or group activity and answer the retrieval questions without looking back. Revisit the explanation when your answer lacks a reason or a source.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) contains practice questions and a final public self-study guidance section. Attempt the questions before reading that guidance. In formal course assessment, the default weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative; your teacher will explain which work is collected.

**Source edition:** article references use the National Assembly's Constitution text updated to 28 February 2012, the edition specified in the approved reading list. It is a historical reading edition, not a statement of the law as amended today. The unit identifies selected later changes to the original 1973 framework through that edition. For a present-day legal question, consult the latest official text. Urdu translation and independent expert review remain pending.
