# Unit 5: Economy and Society - Assessment

## Unit summary

Pakistan's economy connects agriculture, industry and services. A cotton uniform, for example, can connect farming, processing, stitching, transport and selling. Agriculture's importance to food and raw materials does not mean it is the only sector. A sector's GDP share measures production value, not the percentage of people employed in it. Use dated sources when making numerical comparisons, and distinguish shares, growth rates and household conditions.

Poverty concerns resources for basic needs and participation. Inequality concerns differences between people or groups. Debt concerns a repayment obligation. The same case may involve all three, but evidence for one does not establish the others. A loan may fund a useful activity while creating future repayment pressure. National growth also does not prove that every household has more secure work or that every child can reach school.

Education can build literacy, numeracy and other capabilities that support productive activity. The pathway depends on meaningful learning, an opportunity to use it and wider conditions such as reliable infrastructure. A sound proposal explains the barrier, the teaching response and an observable learning check. It also states what the response cannot solve. Training in measurement cannot by itself repair power supply or create buyers.

Social structure concerns relationships, roles and institutions. Rural and urban refer to settlement settings; tribal relationships are an overlapping dimension of social organisation. Family support, livelihoods, access and authority vary within communities. A pupil's address or affiliation is not evidence of ability or willingness to learn.

Culture includes learned meanings and practices. Languages, traditions, arts and food practices vary within and across provinces and regions. People can hold several identities and share national belonging. Inclusive teaching provides ways to understand a lesson and contribute without being required to represent a whole community. A shared goal, a language bridge and an independent check make inclusion visible in learning rather than only in a display.

## Summative assessment

This is a public self-study bank. It is separate from any protected LMS assessment. Attempt the items before reading the final guidance. MCQs carry 1 mark each, RRQs carry 4 marks each and ERQs carry 12 marks each. These are practice-bank marks, not a change to the course's 60% summative and 40% formative split. For extended answers, use the stated fictional facts and explain any assumptions. Do not collect household financial or identity information.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Select one best option for each item.

1. Which activity belongs most directly to agricultural production? *(Remember)*
   - A) Driving milk to a shop
   - B) Keeping cattle for milk
   - C) Packaging milk in a factory
   - D) Selling milk at a shop

2. What does a sector's share of GDP describe? *(Understand)*
   - A) Its share of all employed people
   - B) Its share of all registered firms
   - C) Its share of all household income
   - D) Its share of measured production value

3. A Pakistani mill buys equipment from another country and sells cloth to a shop in Lahore. How should these two transactions be classified? *(Apply)*
   - A) Equipment import and domestic cloth trade
   - B) Equipment export and domestic cloth trade
   - C) Domestic equipment trade and cloth export
   - D) Domestic equipment trade and cloth import

4. Two fictional households can meet their stated basic needs, but one has a much larger income. What is directly illustrated by the difference? *(Apply)*
   - A) An agricultural output share
   - B) An international trade deficit
   - C) An inequality in income
   - D) A debt repayment obligation

5. A workshop has functioning machines and orders, but workers repeatedly misread a length scale. Which response best addresses the stated immediate barrier? *(Apply)*
   - A) Seek buyers in another district
   - B) Practise measurement with feedback
   - C) Add a second electricity connection
   - D) Reduce the number of cloth suppliers

6. What does social structure refer to? *(Remember)*
   - A) The patterned roles, relationships and institutions of society
   - B) The monthly balance between exports and imports
   - C) The annual value of goods produced within a country
   - D) The sequence of words used in an official document

7. A city resident maintains tribal kinship ties and works in a software enterprise. What does this case show? *(Understand)*
   - A) Employment replaces family relationships
   - B) Tribal ties describe a rural address
   - C) Urban residence implies one family form
   - D) Settlement and social affiliations can overlap

8. A pupil speaks Sindhi at home and also uses Urdu at school. Which classroom record best reflects the evidence? *(Apply)*
   - A) The pupil lacks a stable regional identity
   - B) The pupil represents every Sindhi household
   - C) The pupil uses more than one language
   - D) The pupil has identical skill in both languages

9. A class plans a cultural display. Which instruction best recognises variation within a region? *(Apply)*
   - A) Choose the single costume that every resident wears
   - B) Present examples and explain whose experiences they show
   - C) Assign each child one province based on a family surname
   - D) Rank each region by how closely its practices resemble ours

10. A pupil demonstrates a process accurately but cannot yet say its English label. Which first teaching response best matches the evidence? *(Apply)*
   - A) Supply the label and connect it to the demonstrated meaning
   - B) Repeat the same question more loudly without changing support
   - C) Record the process itself as misunderstood and move on
   - D) Replace the science task with memorisation of unrelated words

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer in the stated scope. Each response carries 4 marks.

1. In three or four sentences, explain why describing agriculture as a backbone does not establish that it is the largest GDP sector. State what additional evidence would identify the largest sector. *(Understand)*

2. A fictional shirt route contains farming, stitching and selling. In up to four sentences, classify these activities and explain two links between them. *(Understand)*

3. In a fictional monthly budget, a household has Rs 25,000 and listed basic costs of Rs 28,000. A second has Rs 55,000 with the same listed costs. In four sentences, distinguish the first household's shortfall from the income gap, and state one limit of the example. *(Analyze)*

4. A fictional borrower receives Rs 12,000 for a productive tool and agrees to repay Rs 13,200. In up to four sentences, calculate the stated extra payment, explain a possible benefit of the loan and identify a condition that could make repayment difficult. Do not treat this as a complete public-debt model. *(Apply)*

5. A fictional school gives price-comparison practice and all pupils attend. In up to four sentences, explain why attendance alone cannot show useful learning. Propose one independent check and one condition affecting later economic use. *(Analyze)*

6. In three or four sentences, explain why rural and urban settings cannot be used as exclusive alternatives to tribal relationships. Include one coherent possible example. *(Understand)*

7. A teacher says, “A pupil lives in a city, so school access must be easy.” In up to four sentences, identify the unsupported inference, give one possible urban access barrier and state what relevant evidence the school could examine without asking for private household finances. *(Analyze)*

8. In three or four sentences, explain how a living tradition can continue while changing. Give a fictional craft example and distinguish continuity from complete sameness. *(Understand)*

9. A pupil can explain a science idea through a diagram but pauses over a new classroom term. In up to four sentences, propose a language bridge and an independent check. Explain what the two forms of evidence help distinguish. *(Apply)*

10. In up to four sentences, analyze a display that gives each province exactly one language and requires every child to represent their family's culture. Identify two problems and propose one revision that addresses both. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Write about 450–600 words for each selected task. Each carries 12 marks. Use paragraphs, a labelled sketch or a small table where useful. A list of benefits without a causal explanation is insufficient.

1. **An interrupted production route.** A fictional Sindh enterprise buys farm produce, processes it and sells through town shops. Some produce spoils during transport. Workers also misread storage instructions. The owner proposes one short literacy lesson as the complete solution. Analyze the agriculture, industry and service links. Evaluate the proposal, distinguishing the problem that teaching could address from a problem requiring another response. Specify a learning check and one limit of your judgment. *(Analyze / Evaluate)*

2. **Education and economic pressure.** Two fictional learners complete a measurement course. One can use reliable equipment at a workplace with orders. The other has the same demonstrated skill but cannot access functioning equipment. Compare their possible pathways from learning to productive activity. Explain why the course can be valuable without guaranteeing equal earnings. Propose a bounded education response and a complementary response outside teaching, and explain how each would be checked. *(Analyze / Evaluate)*

3. **Compare social conditions without stereotyping.** In a fictional village, a learner has relatives willing to help but faces a long journey to school. In a fictional city, a learner lives near a school but faces an unsafe route and crowded learning space. A third learner lives in a town, maintains tribal kinship ties and receives help with study materials from relatives elsewhere. Analyze settlement, relationships and access separately. Recommend fair school responses and explain why no case supports a claim about every member of a community. *(Analyze / Evaluate)*

4. **Design a lesson on living culture.** Create a 20-minute lesson using a short story or craft example that the school has permission to share. The fictional class contains several home languages, and some pupils cannot purchase materials. Show how the lesson recognises regional diversity, avoids making a child a community spokesperson and connects familiar explanations to shared course terms. Specify an independent check of understanding and explain how you would challenge a stereotype if one arose. *(Create / Evaluate)*

5. **An integrated school proposal.** A fictional district team plans a “skills and culture day.” It proposes an expensive craft kit, English-only instructions and a promise that attendance will improve every learner's future income. The team has paper, local examples it may share and teachers able to organise paired work. Analyze two economic and two social or language barriers. Redesign the event as an accessible learning activity. Explain a conditional pathway to useful capability, choose two observable checks and state what those checks cannot establish about livelihoods or national unity. *(Analyze / Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

These are intentionally public self-study responses. Accept equivalent sound examples. Do not reward a stereotype, unsupported statistic or claim of a guaranteed economic return merely because it uses confident language. In fictional cases, assess how well the response follows the stated facts. Do not require information that the question does not supply.

### MCQ answer key

1. B - cattle keeping produces milk; transport and selling are services, while factory packaging belongs to processing.
2. D - a GDP share concerns production value, not a direct count of workers, firms or household incomes.
3. A - equipment entering Pakistan is an import; selling cloth within Pakistan is domestic trade.
4. C - the stated income difference illustrates inequality without establishing a basic-needs shortfall or debt.
5. B - the case identifies measurement skill as the immediate barrier and states that equipment and orders are available.
6. A - social structure concerns organised relationships, roles and institutions through which social life operates.
7. D - urban residence, tribal affiliations and a service occupation can coexist in one person's life.
8. C - using two languages supports a multilingual description; it does not prove equal proficiency or a fixed identity.
9. B - specific examples with limits recognise diversity within regions and avoid assigning every resident one practice.
10. A - the successful demonstration provides evidence of meaning that can be connected to the unfamiliar label.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. “Backbone” describes agriculture's supporting links to food, raw materials and livelihoods. It is not a statistical ranking. A dated, comparable table of sectoral GDP shares is needed to identify the largest measured sector. **Marks (4):** 1 for explaining the supporting role; 1 for separating importance from largest share; 1 for specifying sectoral GDP evidence; 1 for requiring a date and comparable basis. Naming a largest sector without identifying the needed evidence earns no final evidence mark.

2. Farming belongs to agriculture, stitching to manufacturing within industry and selling to services. Farming supplies material used in the product, while selling connects the finished product to buyers. **Marks (4):** 1 for correct classification of farming and stitching together; 1 for classifying selling; 1 each for two explained links. Accept a cotton-to-cloth step inserted to make the material route clear. Simply listing three occupations without explaining a link does not earn a link mark.

3. The first household has a Rs 3,000 shortfall against the listed costs. The incomes differ by Rs 30,000, illustrating inequality in income. The shortfall and gap answer different questions. The exercise is not an official poverty estimate because it does not supply a validated poverty method or complete household circumstances. **Marks (4):** 1 for each correct calculation; 1 for the conceptual distinction; 1 for a relevant limit. A response may name omitted household size or needs instead of the official-method limit.

4. The stated extra payment is Rs 1,200. A useful tool may support production or income, which can help finance repayment. Damage to the tool, weak demand or another income interruption could make repayment difficult. **Marks (4):** 1 for the calculation; 1 for a plausible use-related benefit; 1 for a relevant condition; 1 for explaining how that condition affects repayment. Do not require an interest-rate calculation or assume that the example includes all possible charges.

5. Attendance shows presence, not that each pupil can compare prices independently. A new pair of equal-quality packets with different quantities and prices can test the comparison, with the pupil explaining the choice. Later use also requires relevant opportunities and suitable products or work conditions. **Marks (4):** 1 for the attendance-learning distinction; 1 for a genuinely new individual task; 1 for requiring evidence of the comparison rather than repetition; 1 for a relevant condition on later use. A copied group answer is not independent evidence.

6. Rural and urban refer to settlement settings, while tribal ties concern kinship and community relationships. Such ties can continue across settings. A person may live in a town and receive study support from relatives linked through a tribal network. **Marks (4):** 1 for rural/urban as settlement; 1 for tribal as relationships; 1 for explaining overlap; 1 for a coherent example. Do not reward an example that defines tribal identity by poverty or lack of schooling.

7. The teacher infers easy access from a city address without evidence about the route or school. An unsafe crossing, transport cost or overcrowded school could create difficulty. The school could inspect route conditions or review its own attendance and space records. **Marks (4):** 1 for identifying the inference; 1 for a plausible barrier; 1 for relevant non-intrusive evidence; 1 for explaining how that evidence tests the barrier. Merely restating that cities have schools does not test access for this pupil.

8. A living tradition carries knowledge and meaning through continued practice, but practitioners may adapt its tools or uses. A fictional embroiderer might teach a familiar stitch while using a new frame or selling through a different route. The learned skill can continue without every detail staying identical. **Marks (4):** 1 for transmission; 1 for adaptation; 1 for a coherent example; 1 for explaining continuity without complete sameness. Accept other practices if the same distinction is clear.

9. The teacher can name the term while pointing to the pupil's diagram, allow a brief familiar-language explanation and ask the pupil to apply the term to a new diagram independently. This separates knowledge of the process from vocabulary difficulty. **Marks (4):** 1 for a bridge connected to meaning; 1 for a new individual check; 1 for identifying conceptual understanding; 1 for distinguishing it from language familiarity. Do not require the teacher to know every home language or make another child a permanent interpreter.

10. The display hides variation within provinces and forces pupils to disclose or perform a single identity. Replace closed boxes with overlapping, sourced examples that pupils may research rather than personally represent. **Marks (4):** 1 for each of the two problems; 1 for a revision showing internal variation; 1 for a revision allowing choice without personal representation. A response can earn both revision marks through one well-explained connected design.

### ERQ rubrics

Each item has four criteria worth 3 marks each. Use the descriptors for that item, then apply the common score scale: **3** means the stated demand is met accurately with specific case reasoning; **2** means mostly sound reasoning with one significant omission; **1** means a relevant idea is named but not explained or applied; **0** means absent, irrelevant or fundamentally incorrect. Award criteria independently. A weak language form should not remove marks for a clearly demonstrated concept; use an accessible response mode where the assessment setting permits it.

1. **Production route, 12 marks.**
   - **Sector links (3):** explains farm supply, processing and transport or retail as connected agricultural, industrial and service activities. Full credit requires relationships, not only sector names.
   - **Barrier analysis, Analyze (3):** distinguishes literacy-related instruction errors from spoilage during transport and explains a plausible mechanism for each. Does not assume that every spoilage problem has one proven cause.
   - **Proposal evaluation, Evaluate (3):** supports targeted teaching while rejecting the claim that it is a complete solution; proposes investigation or a practical transport/storage response linked to the non-learning barrier.
   - **Evidence and limits (3):** proposes independent reading and application of a new instruction, alongside a relevant limit such as untested storage or transport conditions. Does not equate a short learning check with increased national output.

2. **Education pathways, 12 marks.**
   - **Conditional pathway, Analyze (3):** follows skill to task performance, opportunity and possible livelihood benefit, identifying equipment access as the relevant difference.
   - **Comparison and judgment, Evaluate (3):** explains why equal demonstrated learning can coexist with unequal opportunities and why training can retain value without guaranteed earnings.
   - **Matched responses (3):** offers a bounded teaching improvement and an equipment-access or other non-teaching response, stating which barrier each addresses.
   - **Checks and limits (3):** includes a new independent measurement task and a relevant check of usable equipment or access; explains that neither alone proves a sustained earnings gain. Do not require a forecast of exact wages.

3. **Social conditions, 12 marks.**
   - **Conceptual comparison (3):** keeps settlement setting, family or kinship support and service access distinct across all three cases.
   - **Evidence-based analysis, Analyze (3):** explains how distance, route safety, crowding and support can affect participation without assigning causes to a whole community.
   - **Fair responses (3):** links practical school actions to the actual barriers, recognising helpful networks while preserving equal access for learners without them.
   - **Limits and judgment, Evaluate (3):** identifies what each case leaves unknown and explains why three fictional learners cannot establish population-wide traits. A polished description of “rural character” cannot replace case evidence.

4. **Living-culture lesson, 12 marks.**
   - **Representation (3):** presents the example as one living practice, credits a permitted source and recognises internal variation rather than one culture per region.
   - **Workable design, Create (3):** gives a sequence achievable in twenty minutes with available low-cost materials and a route that avoids personal disclosure or compulsory cultural performance.
   - **Language and evidence (3):** connects familiar meaning to shared terms and checks each learner's understanding independently through a new explanation, demonstration or application.
   - **Reasoned response, Evaluate (3):** explains how to challenge an unsupported group claim while protecting dignity and avoiding the demand that a child defend a community. Enjoyment alone is not a learning check.

5. **Integrated redesign, 12 marks.**
   - **Economic and social analysis, Analyze (3):** identifies two economic barriers or unsupported economic assumptions and two social/language barriers, explaining how each could affect access or the event's claims.
   - **Accessible redesign, Create (3):** replaces costly required kits with available resources, includes a practical language bridge and gives each pupil a meaningful role without assigning identity by background.
   - **Conditional capability pathway (3):** explains a specific learned capability, its possible use and at least one condition on any later economic benefit. Does not treat attendance as proof of improved income.
   - **Checks and limits, Evaluate (3):** specifies two observable checks, including an independent learning performance and a participation/access check; explains why these cannot establish future livelihoods or lasting national unity. Accept a well-justified alternative participation measure.
