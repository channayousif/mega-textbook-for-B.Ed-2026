# Unit 5: Economy and Society

A school is part of the economy and society around it. The price of transport can affect attendance. The language used for a lesson can affect who joins the discussion. A useful explanation connects these experiences to wider patterns without assuming that every household has the same needs.

This unit covers Weeks 11 and 12 of the course guide. It helps you explain economic links and social differences with evidence, and turn that understanding into fair classroom choices. Its numerical examples are teaching cases unless a dated source is named. They describe possible situations, not survey findings about a whole district or community.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of the unit, you should be able to:

- Explain how agriculture, industry, services and trade depend on one another in Pakistan, using a local production example. This develops `SLO:GPKS-402-5-1`.
- Distinguish poverty, inequality and debt, and analyze how one of these challenges affects educational opportunity. This develops `SLO:GPKS-402-5-1`.
- Explain a pathway from education to economic development, including a condition that could interrupt it. This develops `SLO:GPKS-402-5-1`.
- Compare rural, urban and tribal dimensions of social life without treating them as fixed or exclusive types. This develops `SLO:GPKS-402-5-2`.
- Use examples of languages and living traditions to explain cultural diversity and propose an inclusive classroom response. This develops `SLO:GPKS-402-5-2`.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 4's discussion of governance, resources and location. Explain how water availability can affect farming and how a public decision can affect school access. Recall that citizens share rights even when their languages, livelihoods and family arrangements differ.

You need basic percentage and subtraction skills for the economic examples. A percentage is a number out of one hundred. Keep the date and unit beside any figure you copy. You do not need to memorise a changing set of national statistics.

## How to use this unit

Read each classroom situation before its explanation. Study the worked cases, then attempt the group activity and retrieval questions without copying the text. Check the statements you can demonstrate with an example. Use the practicum task to test a teaching choice during one school visit.

Complete the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) after both topics. Attempt the questions before consulting the final self-study guidance. The course uses the default 60% summative and 40% formative weighting. Your teacher selects and schedules work within that split; the bank is a resource rather than an instruction to complete every item in one sitting.

Talk about patterns without asking classmates or children to reveal household income, debt, religion or family difficulties. Use the fictional cases when a question needs such information.
