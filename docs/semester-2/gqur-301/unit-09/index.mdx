# Introduction to Probability

A school plans under uncertainty every day. How many pupils might attend an outdoor event? How can a lucky draw give each pupil the same chance? Probability helps us make a careful statement about chance without pretending to know the next result. This unit follows guide Chapter 9, Week 9. All numerical school examples are invented teaching data, not reports about real pupils or schools.

## Unit learning outcomes

1. Describe experiments, outcomes, sample spaces and events; calculate and interpret probability from equally likely outcomes and observed records. (SLO:GQUR-301-9-1; course outcomes 1 and 2.)
2. Apply complement, addition and multiplication rules; distinguish mutually exclusive events from independent events and evaluate probability claims about school situations. (SLO:GQUR-301-9-2; course outcomes 1, 2 and 3.)

## Prerequisite knowledge

Review Units 7 and 8 on counting. You should be able to multiply the numbers of choices at successive stages, list combinations without duplication, simplify fractions, and change a fraction into a decimal or percentage. You will use counting to identify possible results before calculating their chances.

## In this unit

1. [Basic probability concepts](./topic-01): define the chance process, identify its possible results, and explain what a probability means.
2. [Probability rules](./topic-02): combine events carefully and check the assumptions behind a calculation.

## How to use this unit

Work through each explanation and its worked examples before the pair activity. Attempt the retrieval questions with the page closed. If you cannot explain a step, revisit the worked example and then try again with different numbers. Complete the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) before reading its final self-study guidance.
