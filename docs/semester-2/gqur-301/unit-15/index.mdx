# Hypothesis Testing

Unit 14 showed you how to estimate an unknown population mean from a sample
and how confident you can be in that estimate. This unit, which follows guide
Chapter 15, Week 15, takes the next step. Instead of estimating, you ask a
yes/no question about a population: is the average test score in this school
really different from the district average? The tool that answers such a
question is a **hypothesis test**. You state a claim, collect sample data,
and decide whether the data support the claim or contradict it.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain the logic of hypothesis testing, state null and alternative hypotheses, and interpret a p-value in plain language. (SLO:GQUR-301-15-1; course outcomes 1 and 3.)
2. Carry out a z-test for a sample mean when the population spread is known, compute the test statistic, find the p-value, and make a decision at a stated significance level. (SLO:GQUR-301-15-2; course outcomes 1, 2 and 3.)
3. Carry out a t-test for a small sample when the population spread is unknown, distinguish when each test is appropriate, and evaluate what the result means in a school-evaluation context. (SLO:GQUR-301-15-3; course outcomes 1, 2 and 3.)

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 14 (confidence intervals and estimation) and Unit 11 (the normal model).
You should be able to compute a mean and a standard deviation from a sample,
read probabilities from the standard normal table, and explain what a confidence
interval means.

## In this unit

1. [Concept of hypothesis testing](./topic-01) - the logic of testing a claim, stating hypotheses, and the meaning of a p-value.
2. [The z-test](./topic-02) - testing a mean when the population standard deviation is known.
3. [The t-test](./topic-03) - testing a mean when the spread is unknown and the sample is small.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Work through the classroom situation and the
explanation before attempting the activity. The "Check your understanding" items
are retrieval practice: try them without looking back, then check. Keep a
calculator and a standard normal table (or t-table) nearby for Topics 15.2 and
15.3. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws all three topics
together.
