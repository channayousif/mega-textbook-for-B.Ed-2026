# Probabilistic Models

How can a teacher plan when attendance and test results vary? This unit uses
simple probability models to describe possible outcomes and check predictions.
The classroom situations and numerical data are invented teaching examples,
not reports of actual schools or provincial assessments.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Distinguish deterministic from probabilistic models, state the defining
   characteristics of binomial and normal models, and explain how uncertainty is
   represented mathematically.
2. Apply probabilistic models to real educational and social situations,
   calculate expected outcomes, and evaluate whether a given model's assumptions
   are valid for real-world decision-making.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Before starting this unit, you should be familiar with basic probability concepts
from Unit 9 (outcomes, sample spaces, independent events, and the multiplication
rule) and probability distributions from Unit 10 (discrete versus continuous
random variables, probability distributions, and expected value).

## In this unit

1. [Introduction to probabilistic models](./topic-01) - deterministic versus
   probabilistic thinking, and the binomial and normal models.
2. [Applications in real life](./topic-02) - multiple-choice guessing,
   diagnostic thresholds with the empirical rule, and checking model assumptions.

## How to use this unit

Each topic follows a nine-part cycle. Begin with the classroom situation to see
the practical problem, study the explanation and worked examples, and complete
the collaborative activity with a peer. Use the "Check your understanding" items
to test your recall and application before consulting your notes. The unit
concludes with a comprehensive summative assessment (MCQs, RRQs, and ERQs) with
marking guidance.
