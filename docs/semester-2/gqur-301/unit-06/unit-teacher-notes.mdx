# Unit 6 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 6, which follows
Chapter 6 of the course guide (Week 6). They complement the student-facing
topics with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and practical work, in line
with the guide's teaching strategy of lectures, discussion, question-answer,
brainstorming and practical group and individual work.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 6 (first session) | 6.1 Range | Why the centre is not enough; the range; what it hides |
| 6 (second session) | 6.2 Variance and standard deviation | Deviations, squaring, the five-step table; N and n - 1 |
| 6 (third session) | 6.3 Data interpretation using dispersion | Reading the standard deviation; distance in SDs; purpose decides |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 6.1 Range

**Opening.** Write two lists of marks with the same mean on the board and ask:
"Are these two classes the same?" Let students argue before anyone computes
anything. The need for a measure of spread comes from the discussion.

**Key move.** After the range is computed, show Section C (same mean, same
range as Section B, but bunched). Ask: "Does the range describe Section C
fairly?" Students should see for themselves that a measure using every value is
needed.

### Topic 6.2 Variance and standard deviation

**Opening.** Ask students to propose their own "average distance from the
mean". Most will add the deviations; let them find that the total is always
zero. This makes the squaring step a solution to a problem they have met,
rather than a rule to memorise.

**Key move.** Insist on the full computation table for every exercise, with the
check that the deviations add to zero. Most calculation errors happen in the
signs of the deviations or in forgetting the square root. Keep data sets small
(five to eight values) so that the reasoning, not the arithmetic, takes the
time. Mention the n - 1 divisor and the σ and *s* calculator keys, but use N
for whole groups in this course.

### Topic 6.3 Data interpretation using dispersion

**Opening.** Present the two Class 6 sections (mean 12, SDs about 1.1 and 6.0)
and the head teacher's question "which section is better?" A likely wrong
answer is "Section A, because its SD is smaller." Work with that answer: ask
what each SD says about the pupils, and whether "smaller" is always "better".

**Key move.** Have students locate the same raw mark in two classes using
distance in SDs, then list situations where large spread is useful, where small
spread is a warning, and where spread is simply a fact to plan for.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "Range tells you everything about spread" | Show two data sets with the same mean and range but different arrangements (Sections B and C), drawn as dot plots. |
| "Variance and standard deviation are the same thing" | Compute both for one data set and write the units beside each (days squared, days). Ask which one a parent would understand. |
| "A larger standard deviation is always bad" | Use a placement test, a multi-grade class and a test everyone found easy to show that the purpose decides whether spread is welcome. |

## Practical work

1. **Same range, different story (Topic 6.1).** Pairs build data sets with equal ranges and different spreads, and draw them as dot plots.
2. **Pass the table (Topic 6.2).** Groups of four complete one computation table, one column each, and check each other's work.
3. **Two sections, one staff meeting (Topic 6.3).** Role-play in which teachers explain their section's standard deviation to a head teacher.
4. **Group assignment.** Each group analyses an anonymised class marksheet (mean, range and standard deviation) and presents one teaching recommendation that follows from the spread.

## Assessment guidance

The unit assessment (10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ) is summative. Use each topic's
"Check your understanding" items for formative assessment during teaching,
especially the computation items in Topic 6.2. The practicum tasks link the
college classroom to the placement school.

## Materials needed

- Calculators with a square-root key (a mobile phone calculator is enough)
- Blank computation tables (value, deviation, squared deviation)
- Two or three anonymised class marksheets from a local school
