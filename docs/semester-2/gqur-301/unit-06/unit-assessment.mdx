## Unit summary

This unit, which follows Chapter 6 of the course guide, added the second half of
the description of a data set: its dispersion, or spread.

- Topic 6.1 introduced the range, the largest value minus the smallest. It is
  quick and in the units of the data, but it uses only the two extreme values,
  so it ignores how the rest are arranged and can be changed sharply by one
  unusual value. Two data sets with the same mean and the same range can be
  spread very differently.
- Topic 6.2 built the variance and the standard deviation from the deviations
  of every value from the mean. Deviations add to zero, so they are squared;
  the variance is the mean of the squared deviations, in squared units, and the
  standard deviation is its square root, back in the units of the data. Divide
  by N for a whole group and by n - 1 for a sample. One extreme value can raise
  the standard deviation sharply.
- Topic 6.3 interpreted the standard deviation beside the mean. A small value
  means a consistent group and a large value a varied one, which often matters
  more for teaching than the mean. Distance in standard deviations locates one
  value within its group. A larger spread is not automatically bad: its meaning
  depends on the purpose of the data.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* The range of a data set is:
   - A) The most frequent value
   - B) The largest value minus the smallest value
   - C) The middle value of the sorted data
   - D) The sum of the values divided by their number

2. *(Apply)* What is the range of the marks 15, 8, 22, 11, 19?
   - A) 7
   - B) 14
   - C) 15
   - D) 22

3. *(Understand)* The main weakness of the range as a measure of spread is that it:
   - A) Can be negative
   - B) Is in squared units
   - C) Uses only the largest and the smallest values
   - D) Cannot be used with test marks

4. *(Remember)* For any data set, the deviations from the mean always add to:
   - A) The mean
   - B) The range
   - C) The number of values
   - D) Zero

5. *(Apply)* A data set has a variance of 16 marks squared. Its standard deviation is:
   - A) 4 marks
   - B) 8 marks
   - C) 16 marks
   - D) 256 marks

6. *(Understand)* Why is the standard deviation usually reported instead of the variance?
   - A) It is always larger than the variance
   - B) It is in the same units as the data
   - C) It ignores extreme values
   - D) It uses only two values

7. *(Apply)* What is the standard deviation of the attendance figures 5, 5, 5, 5, 5?
   - A) 0
   - B) 1
   - C) 5
   - D) 25

8. *(Understand)* Two classes both have a mean mark of 30. Class X has a standard
   deviation of 2 and Class Y has a standard deviation of 8. Which statement is
   best supported?
   - A) Class X did better than Class Y
   - B) Class Y did better than Class X
   - C) Class X's marks are more consistent than Class Y's
   - D) Class Y's marks are more consistent than Class X's

9. *(Apply)* In a class with mean 50 and standard deviation 4, a pupil scored 58.
   How far is this mark from the mean?
   - A) 0.5 standard deviations above
   - B) 2 standard deviations above
   - C) 4 standard deviations above
   - D) 8 standard deviations above

10. *(Understand)* In which situation is a large standard deviation most useful
    to a teacher?
    - A) Daily attendance of a class
    - B) A placement test used to form reading groups
    - C) The number of textbooks issued to each pupil
    - D) The time each pupil spends in assembly

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in a few sentences or a short paragraph.

1. *(Understand)* Explain, in two or three sentences, why the mean alone is not
   enough to describe the marks of a class.

2. *(Apply)* The days absent last term of six pupils were 3, 7, 0, 12, 5, 4. Find
   the range, then state one thing it shows and one thing it hides.

3. *(Analyze)* Data set X is 1, 9, 9, 9, 9, 17 and data set Y is
   1, 4, 7, 11, 14, 17. Show that they have the same mean and the same range,
   and analyze how their spreads differ.

4. *(Understand)* Explain why the deviations are squared before they are
   averaged in the computation of the variance.

5. *(Apply)* Find the variance and the standard deviation of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11,
   treating the values as the whole group. Show your computation table.

6. *(Analyze)* A trainee writes: "Variance and standard deviation are the same
   thing." Analyze what is wrong with this statement, referring to how the two
   are related and to their units.

7. *(Apply)* For the values 2, 4, 6, find the variance dividing by N and dividing
   by n - 1. State when each divisor should be used.

8. *(Analyze)* Saleem scored 70 in a class with mean 60 and standard
   deviation 5. Rani scored 70 in another class with mean 60 and standard deviation 20.
   Analyze whose result is more unusual within their own class, showing the
   distance in standard deviations.

9. *(Analyze)* A teacher in a multi-grade school says, "My class has a large
   standard deviation in reading scores, so my teaching must be poor." Analyze
   this claim in three or four sentences.

10. *(Analyze)* Two schools both have a mean daily attendance of 92 percent over
    a term. School M has a standard deviation of 2 percentage points across its
    classes and School N a standard deviation of 15. Analyze which school should
    worry the district office more, and why.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a structured paragraph or set of paragraphs.

1. *(Analyze)* Two Class 6 sections took the same test out of 25. Section Saba:
   5, 12, 13, 13, 14, 14, 15, 22. Section Noor: 5, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18, 20, 22. Find
   the mean and the range of each section, draw both as dot plots, and analyze
   whether the range gives the head teacher a fair comparison of their spreads.
   (Write 100-150 words after your working.)

2. *(Analyze)* The marks out of 20 of nine pupils were
   6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 10, 11, 11, 18. Compute the standard deviation, treating the pupils as the whole group.
   Then remove the 18 and recompute it for the remaining eight. Analyze what the
   two results show about how the standard deviation responds to one extreme
   value, and how a teacher should report the spread of this class. (Write
   120-160 words after your working.)

3. *(Evaluate)* Two Class 10 sections in a Hyderabad school both have a mean of
   30 out of 50 on the pre-board mathematics test. Section 1 has a standard
   deviation of 3 and Section 2 a standard deviation of 12. The head teacher
   plans to give both sections the same revision programme. Evaluate this plan,
   using what the means and standard deviations reveal, and propose a better
   plan for each section. (Write 150-200 words.)

4. *(Evaluate)* A district officer proposes to rank schools by the standard
   deviation of their test marks, saying "the schools with the largest spread
   are the worst taught". Evaluate this proposal. Use at least two school
   situations in which a large or a small standard deviation means something
   other than good or poor teaching. (Write 150-200 words.)

5. *(Evaluate)* Integrative task. The scores out of 20 on a weekly quiz were:
   Section Kiran 8, 9, 10, 10, 11, 12; Section Sitara 2, 6, 9, 11, 14, 18. Compute
   the mean, the range and the standard deviation of each section (whole group).
   Then write a report of 150-200 words for the School Management Committee
   that compares the sections fairly, explains what the mean alone would have
   hidden, and recommends one teaching action for each section.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

| Item | Answer | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | The range is the largest value minus the smallest. |
| 2 | B | 22 - 8 = 14. Option C is the mean, not the range. |
| 3 | C | The range ignores every value between the two extremes. |
| 4 | D | The mean balances the values above and below it, so the deviations add to zero. |
| 5 | A | The standard deviation is the square root of the variance: √16 = 4 marks. |
| 6 | B | Taking the square root returns the measure to the units of the data. |
| 7 | A | Every value equals the mean, so every deviation is 0. |
| 8 | C | A smaller standard deviation means the marks sit closer to the mean; equal means say nothing about which class did better. |
| 9 | B | (58 - 50) ÷ 4 = 2. |
| 10 | B | A placement test should spread pupils out so that groups can be formed. |

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. The mean only says where the centre is. Two classes can have the same mean while one has all pupils close to it and the other has pupils far below and far above it, which calls for different teaching. (2 marks: 1 for the centre-only point, 1 for the same-mean, different-spread point.)

2. Range = 12 - 0 = 12 days. It shows the distance between the best and the worst attendance. It hides how the other four pupils are spread (most were absent 3-7 days) and depends heavily on the single pupil absent 12 days. (3 marks: 1 for the range, 1 for what it shows, 1 for what it hides.)

3. X: sum 54, mean 9, range 17 - 1 = 16. Y: sum 54, mean 9, range 16. In X four of the six values sit exactly at the mean, with one far value at each end; in Y the values are spread evenly across the range. Y is more spread out overall, though the range cannot show it. (3 marks: 1 for both means and ranges, 1 for describing each arrangement, 1 for concluding that the range misses the difference.)

4. The deviations always add to zero, because values above the mean balance those below it, so their plain average is always zero. Squaring makes every deviation positive, so they no longer cancel, and it gives more weight to values far from the mean. (2 marks: 1 for the cancelling reason, 1 for the effect of squaring.)

5. Mean = 35 ÷ 5 = 7. Deviations -4, -2, 0, 2, 4; squares 16, 4, 0, 4, 16, total 40. Variance = 40 ÷ 5 = 8; standard deviation = √8 ≈ 2.83. (3 marks: 1 for the table, 1 for the variance, 1 for the standard deviation.)

6. They are related but not the same: the standard deviation is the square root of the variance. They differ in value (a variance of 25 gives a standard deviation of 5) and in unit (the variance is in squared units, such as marks squared; the standard deviation is in marks). (2 marks: 1 for the square-root relation, 1 for the units.)

7. Mean 4; squared deviations 4, 0, 4, total 8. Dividing by N: 8 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.67. Dividing by n - 1: 8 ÷ 2 = 4. Use N when the values are the whole group of interest; use n - 1 when they are a sample used to estimate the spread of a larger group. (3 marks: 1 for each variance, 1 for when to use each.)

8. Saleem: (70 - 60) ÷ 5 = 2 standard deviations above the mean. Rani: (70 - 60) ÷ 20 = 0.5 standard deviations above. Saleem's result is more unusual in his class, although the two raw marks are equal. (3 marks: 1 for each distance, 1 for the conclusion.)

9. The claim does not follow. A multi-grade class contains pupils of different ages and grades, so a wide spread of reading scores is expected whatever the teaching. The standard deviation describes the class; it does not grade the teacher. A better use is to plan reading groups at different levels. (3 marks: 1 for rejecting the inference, 1 for the multi-grade reason, 1 for a constructive use.)

10. School N. Both have the same mean, but N's large standard deviation shows that some of its classes have much lower attendance than 92 percent, hidden by others that are higher. School M's classes are all close to 92 percent. (2 marks: 1 for choosing N, 1 for the reason based on spread.)
