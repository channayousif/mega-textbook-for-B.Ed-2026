# Measures of Dispersion

Units 4 and 5 gave you three ways to describe the centre of a data set: the
mean, the median and the mode. But two classes can have exactly the same mean
and still be very different classes. This unit, which follows Chapter 6 of the
course guide, adds the second half of the picture: how spread out the values
are. Statisticians call this spread **dispersion**.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Compute the range of a data set and explain what it shows and what it hides.
2. Compute the variance and standard deviation of a small data set step by step, and explain how the two are related.
3. Interpret the standard deviation in a school context, and use dispersion together with the mean to compare groups and make teaching decisions.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 4 and 5 (mean and median, and choosing a measure of centre). You also
need to be comfortable squaring a number and finding a square root with a
calculator.

## In this unit

1. [Range](./topic-01) - the simplest measure of spread, and its limits.
2. [Variance and standard deviation](./topic-02) - measuring how far values sit from the mean, on average.
3. [Data interpretation using dispersion](./topic-03) - what small and large spread mean for a teacher.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through
the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. Topic 6.2 has a full
worked computation: follow it line by line with a pencil and a calculator before
you try the exercises. The "Check your understanding" items are there to help you
retrieve what you have learned: have a go at them without looking back, then
check your answer. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws the three
topics together.
