# Correlation and Regression

In Unit 12 you learned to plot two variables together on a scatter plot and to describe the
direction, strength and form of the pattern you saw. That description was visual and
qualitative. This unit, which follows Chapter 13 of the course guide, makes it
quantitative. You will learn to put a number on the strength and direction of a
linear relationship (the correlation coefficient) and to draw the straight line that
best fits the cloud (linear regression), then to use that line to predict.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Distinguish positive, negative and zero correlation from a scatter plot and describe what each means.
2. Compute and interpret a Pearson correlation coefficient for a small bivariate data set.
3. Fit a least-squares regression line, interpret its slope and intercept, and use it to make a prediction.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 12 (scatter plots), Unit 4 (mean, median) and Unit 6 (standard deviation). You
should be comfortable plotting paired data and computing means and standard
deviations before starting this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Correlation](./topic-01) - types of correlation and the correlation coefficient.
2. [Regression analysis (linear regression)](./topic-02) - fitting and using the least-squares line.

## How to use this unit

Topic 13.1 quantifies what you saw in a scatter plot: how strong is the linear
pattern, and in which direction? Topic 13.2 turns that pattern into an equation you
can use to predict. Together they close the bivariate analysis sequence (Units 12-13)
and prepare you for the inference ideas in Units 14-15.
