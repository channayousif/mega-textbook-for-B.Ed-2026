## Unit summary

This unit, which follows Chapter 13 of the course guide, made the visual
assessment of a scatter plot quantitative.

- Topic 13.1 introduced correlation. The correlation coefficient r measures the
  direction and strength of a linear relationship on a scale from -1 (perfect
  negative) through 0 (no linear correlation) to +1 (positive). r captures
  straight-line association only; a non-linear pattern can exist even when r is
  near 0, and r never implies causation.
- Topic 13.2 introduced linear regression. The least-squares line ŷ = a + bx
  summarises the linear pattern with an equation. The slope b gives the predicted
  change in y per unit of x; the intercept a gives the predicted y when x = 0.
  Use the line to predict within the data range (interpolation) and compute
  residuals (y - ŷ) to check the fit. Regression gives prediction, not a causal
  explanation.

Together, correlation and regression complete the bivariate analysis sequence
that began in Unit 12 and prepare you for the inference methods in Units 14-15.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* The correlation coefficient r measures:
   - A) The strength of any relationship between two variables
   - B) The direction and strength of the linear relationship between two variables
   - C) Whether one variable causes the other
   - D) The slope of the regression line

2. *(Remember)* A scatter plot shows a cloud that falls from left to right. This indicates:
   - A) Positive correlation
   - B) Negative correlation
   - C) Zero correlation
   - D) Non-correlation only if r is exactly 0

3. *(Understand)* A correlation coefficient of r = 0 means:
   - A) There is no relationship of any kind between the variables
   - B) There is no linear relationship between the variables
   - C) One variable causes the other to be zero
   - D) The regression line has a slope of 1

4. *(Apply)* A trainee computes r = 0.85 between weekly study hours and test score for 12 pupils. Using the rule-of-thumb scale, how would you describe this correlation?
   - A) Weak positive
   - B) Moderate positive
   - C) Strong positive
   - D) No correlation

5. *(Apply)* A regression line is given by ŷ = 20 + 3x. What is the predicted value of y when x = 5?
   - A) 23
   - B) 35
   - C) 25
   - D) 100

6. *(Understand)* The slope of a regression line tells you:
   - A) The value of y when x = 0
   - B) The predicted change in y when x increases by one unit
   - C) The correlation between x and y
   - D) The residual for each point

7. *(Apply)* A regression line for predicting test score (y) from study hours (x) is ŷ = 40 + 6x. A pupil who studies 3 hours is predicted to score 58. The pupil's actual score is 61. What is the residual?
   - A) -3
   - B) +3
   - C) +61
   - D) Cannot be determined

8. *(Remember)* Which of the following is the correct interpretation of a correlation coefficient of r = -0.60?
   - A) Strong positive correlation
   - B) Moderate negative correlation
   - C) Weak positive correlation
   - D) No correlation

9. *(Understand)* Prediction using an x value that lies outside the range of the original data is called:
   - A) Interpolation
   - B) Extrapolation
   - C) Correlation
   - D) Residual analysis

10. *(Apply)* A researcher finds r = 0.90 between ice cream sales and the number of sunburns at a beach town. She concludes that ice cream causes sunburns. What is the most careful response?
    - A) The data prove she is right because r is so high
    - B) The correlation shows a strong linear association, but does not prove causation; a third variable (hot, sunny weather) could explain both
    - C) The correlation is meaningless because r is not exactly 1
    - D) She should reverse the variables and recompute r

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in a few sentences or a short paragraph.

1. *(Understand)* Explain how r = -0.8 and r = +0.8 differ and what they have in common. Include the possible range of r.

2. *(Understand)* Explain why r = 0 does not necessarily mean two variables are unrelated.

3. *(Apply)* A trainee computes r = -0.40 between the number of days absent and the final grade for 15 pupils. State the direction and strength using the rule-of-thumb scale, and interpret what this means in plain language.

4. *(Apply)* A regression line is ŷ = 50 + 4x. State the slope and intercept, interpret the slope in plain language, and predict y when x = 6.

5. *(Analyze)* A teacher computes r = 0.02 between pupils' shoe size and their mathematics test score. She concludes there is no link between physical size and maths ability. Evaluate this conclusion: is it supported by the correlation, and what else should she consider? (Answer in 4-6 sentences.)

6. *(Analyze)* A regression line for predicting monthly electricity bill (y, in rupees) from number of fans in a household (x) is ŷ = 800 + 200x. A household with 4 fans is billed 1700 rupees. Compute the residual and explain what it means in this context. Then explain why the slope alone does not prove that adding a fan causes the bill to rise by exactly 200 rupees. (Answer in 5-7 sentences.)

7. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between interpolation and extrapolation. Why is extrapolation riskier?

8. *(Analyze)* Two scatter plots display exactly the same paired observations, but different axis aspect ratios make Plot A look tighter than Plot B. A trainee claims Plot A has a larger absolute r. Analyze the claim and explain whether changing the display can change the computed correlation.

9. *(Analyze)* A researcher claims that because r = 0.80 between weekly exercise time and a health score, "exercise causes better health". Critically evaluate this claim by identifying what r actually shows and what additional evidence would be needed to support a causal conclusion. (Answer in 5-7 sentences.)

10. *(Analyze)* A school head teacher reports: "We found r = 0.65 between the number of textbooks per pupil and average test scores across 20 schools. Providing more textbooks will raise our scores." Break the claim into an observed association and a proposed causal policy effect. Explain how school funding could affect both textbook provision and scores, and describe a comparison between schools with similar funding that would help separate these explanations. (Answer in 6-8 sentences.)

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a structured paragraph or set of paragraphs.

1. *(Analyze)* The table below gives weekly study hours (x) and mathematics test scores (y) for 6 pupils in an illustrative government-school example set in Sanghar. These are invented teaching data, not collected observations.

   | Pupil | x (hours) | y (score/100) |
   |---|---|---|
   | A | 3 | 45 |
   | B | 5 | 55 |
   | C | 7 | 70 |
   | D | 9 | 75 |
   | E | 4 | 50 |
   | F | 6 | 62 |

   (a) Compute the correlation coefficient r for this data set. Show your working.
   (b) State the direction and strength of the correlation using the rule-of-thumb scale.
   (c) Interpret r in the context of this question. A teacher attributes the association entirely to study time. Explain how prior attainment could affect both variables and propose a comparison using prior scores to investigate that alternative.
   (Write 120-180 words for parts (b) and (c) together.)

2. *(Analyze)* Using the data from ERQ 1, fit the least-squares regression line ŷ = a + bx. Show the steps: compute b, then a, then write the line. Interpret the slope in plain language. Then predict the test score for a pupil who studies 8 hours per week and compute the residual for Pupil D (9 hours, score 75). Diagnose whether this line justifies predicting the score of a pupil from a different school. Compare interpolation within these six cases with transfer to a new population, identifying two checks needed before transfer. (Write 150-200 words.)

3. *(Evaluate)* A newspaper headline reads: "Study proves that eating breakfast raises children's test scores: researchers found a strong correlation." The article reports r = 0.72 between a survey question about breakfast habits and standardised test scores in a sample of 200 children. Evaluate this claim by discussing: (a) what the correlation coefficient actually shows; (b) at least one third variable that could explain the correlation; (c) why the word "proves" is misleading; and (d) what additional evidence would be needed to support a causal conclusion. (Write 150-200 words.)

4. *(Evaluate)* A trainee teacher collects two variables from 8 pupils: hours of television watched per week and a reading test score. She computes r = -0.78. She then fits a regression line and uses it to predict the reading score for a pupil who watches 15 hours of television per week, even though her original data range was 2 to 10 hours. Evaluate her analysis by discussing: (a) what r = -0.78 means; (b) the problem with her prediction; and (c) what she should have done instead. (Write 120-180 words.)

5. *(Evaluate)* Collect and analyze real data from at least 10 consenting adult B.Ed colleagues in Pakistan: hours spent studying last week and their most recent comparable course-test percentage. Do not record names or publish individual records; participation is voluntary. Report the college/district, collection date, sample size and measurement method. If collection is impractical, use an anonymised paired dataset supplied with permission by your lecturer from a Pakistani college and identify its provenance. Plot the pairs, compute r and fit a line if appropriate. Evaluate whether the plot and residuals support the line, explain a specific alternative explanation, and justify the limits of any prediction. Submit the anonymous data table and calculations with a 150-200 word analysis. Do not substitute invented data.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

| Item | Answer | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | r measures the direction and strength of the *linear* relationship. |
| 2 | B | A cloud falling left to right indicates negative correlation. |
| 3 | B | r = 0 means no linear relationship; a non-linear pattern may still exist. |
| 4 | C | \|r\| = 0.85 is at least 0.70, which falls in the strong range. |
| 5 | B | ŷ = 20 + 3(5) = 35. |
| 6 | B | The slope gives the predicted change in y per one-unit increase in x. |
| 7 | B | Predicted ŷ = 40 + 6(3) = 58; residual = 61 - 58 = +3. |
| 8 | B | \|r\| = 0.60 is moderate; the sign is negative. |
| 9 | B | Prediction outside the data range is extrapolation. |
| 10 | B | A high r shows strong association, not proven causation; a third variable (weather) could drive both. |

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Both values describe equally strong sample linear associations; -0.8 is negative and +0.8 positive. The possible range is -1 to +1. (3 marks: 1 for direction, 1 for equal strength, 1 for range.)

2. r = 0 means no *linear* correlation, but the two variables could still follow a non-linear pattern (for example, a U-shaped curve), so they are not necessarily unrelated. (2 marks: 1 for the linear-only point, 1 for the non-linear example.)

3. r = -0.40 indicates a moderate negative correlation (negative sign, magnitude 0.40 falls in the 0.30-0.70 range). This means that, for these 15 pupils, higher absence tends to go with a lower final grade, with a moderate linear pattern. (3 marks: 1 for direction, 1 for strength, 1 for plain-language interpretation.)

4. Slope = 4, intercept = 50. The slope means that for each one-unit increase in x, the predicted y rises by 4. Predicted y when x = 6: ŷ = 50 + 4(6) = 74. (3 marks: 1 for slope and intercept, 1 for slope interpretation, 1 for prediction.)

5. r = 0.02 is near 0, so there is negligible observed linear association between shoe size and maths score. The sample supports only a negligible linear association, not a population-wide absence of a link; sampling uncertainty and a non-linear pattern remain possible, and "physical size and maths ability" is a broader claim than the data test. (3 marks: 1 for interpreting r near 0, 1 for the linear-only caveat, 1 for the scope-of-claim point.)

6. Residual = 1700 - (800 + 200 × 4) = 1700 - 1600 = +100 rupees. This household's bill is 100 rupees above the line's prediction. The slope shows an association (each additional fan goes with 200 rupees more on average), but it does not prove causation: other factors (house size, appliance use, tariff changes) could also explain the difference, and households that buy more fans may differ in other ways. (3 marks: 1 for correct residual, 1 for interpretation, 1 for the causation caveat.)

7. Interpolation is predicting for an x value that lies *within* the range of the original data; extrapolation is predicting for an x value *outside* that range. Extrapolation is riskier because the linear pattern observed in the data may not hold beyond it. (2 marks: 1 for each part.)

8. Both plots have identical r because they contain the same paired observations. Axis aspect ratio changes appearance, not the values entering the formula. (2 marks: 1 for invariant r, 1 for distinguishing display from data.)

9. r = 0.80 shows a strong positive linear association between exercise time and health score, not a proven causal link. To support causation, one would need to rule out third variables (for example, diet, socioeconomic status, pre-existing health), show a plausible mechanism, and ideally test the relationship in a controlled study. (3 marks: 1 for what r shows, 1 for a third variable or mechanism, 1 for additional evidence needed.)

10. (a) r = 0.65 tells us there is a moderate positive linear association between textbooks per pupil and test scores across the 20 schools. (b) A plausible third variable is school funding: better-funded schools have more textbooks and also better teachers, facilities or pupil intake, any of which could drive higher scores. (c) Compare schools with similar funding but different textbook provision, checking whether the association remains. This helps investigate the funding explanation but still cannot rule out every other difference. (3 marks: 1 for separating observed association from policy effect, 1 for explaining both funding pathways, 1 for the comparison with similar funding.)
