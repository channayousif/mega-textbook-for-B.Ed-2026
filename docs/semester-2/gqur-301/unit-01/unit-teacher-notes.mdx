# Unit 1 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 1, which follows
Chapter 1 of the course guide (Week 1). They complement the student-facing
topics with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and practical work, in line
with the guide's teaching strategy of lectures, discussion, question-answer,
brainstorming and practical group and individual work.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (first half) | 1.1 Types of data | Classification and the arithmetic test |
| 1 (second half) | 1.2 Sources of data | Primary vs secondary, the three checks |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 1.1 Types of data

**Opening.** Bring a real register or class notebook and ask students to
classify each column by type. Let them argue about borderline cases such as
roll numbers or "class 7": they look like numbers but are labels. The
disagreement is the teaching point: the type depends on whether arithmetic is
meaningful, not on appearance.

**Key move.** Before giving any definition, have pairs apply two questions to a
list of ten mixed items: is it a number that supports arithmetic? If so, is it
counted or measured? Let them build the categories from the examples.

### Topic 1.2 Sources of data

**Opening.** Ask: "How would you find out the average household size in this
area?" List the students' suggestions on the board, then sort them into primary
and secondary. The sorting is the lesson.

**Key move.** Work through three scenarios in pairs with the three checks (does
suitable data exist? does it match my group and question? do I have the time and
resources?). Show a real Pakistan Bureau of Statistics table so that
"secondary source" is concrete.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "Qualitative data is less important" | Show a decision that depends entirely on qualitative data, such as which language to use for a notice to parents. Importance depends on the question, not the type. |
| "Continuous data must have decimals" | A height recorded as 145 cm is still continuous; a count of 30 is still discrete. The underlying variable matters, not the precision of the recording. |
| "Anything printed by the government is primary" | Primary or secondary describes the relationship between the data and the user: a census table is secondary for a teacher who did not collect it. |

## Practical work

1. **Data scavenger hunt (Topics 1.1-1.2).** Each student finds five pieces of data in the college or a nearby school and records the item, its type, and whether it is a primary or secondary source. Discuss in the next session.

## Assessment guidance

The unit assessment (10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ) is summative. Use each topic's
"Check your understanding" items for formative assessment during teaching. The
practicum tasks link the college classroom to the placement school.
