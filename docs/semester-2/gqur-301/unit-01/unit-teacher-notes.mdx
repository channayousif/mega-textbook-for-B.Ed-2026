# Unit 1 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 1. They complement
the student-facing materials with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and
practical work suggestions.

## Unit overview

Unit 1 covers the foundation of statistical work: recognizing data types,
understanding where data come from, organizing data into frequency tables, and
choosing and producing appropriate charts. The unit assumes no prior statistics
knowledge beyond everyday numeracy. The four topics are designed to be taught
in order, each building on the previous one.

| Week | Topic | Focus |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 1.1 - Types of data

**Opening.** Bring a real notebook or register to class and ask students to
classify each entry by type. Let them argue about borderline cases (for example,
"class number" looks like a number but is really a label). The disagreement is
the teaching point: the classification depends on whether arithmetic is
meaningful, not on appearance.

**Key move.** Emphasize the two-question test (is it a number that supports
arithmetic? is it a count or a measurement?). Have students apply it to a list
of 10 mixed items before you give any definition. Let them discover the
categories from the examples.

### Topic 1.2 - Sources of data

**Opening.** Pose a concrete question: "How would you find out the average
household size in this area?" List the options on the board as students suggest
them, then sort them into primary and secondary. The sorting is the lesson.

**Key move.** Use the three-question rule (does the data exist? do they match
your question? do you have the resources?). Have students work through three
scenarios in pairs, deciding primary or secondary each time.

### Topic 1.3 - Frequency distributions

**Opening.** Give each student a small data set (15-20 values) on a slip of
paper. Ask them to find the highest and lowest, then to sort the slips by
value. The physical sorting makes the need for grouping obvious.

**Key move.** Build one frequency table on the board together, step by step,
with the class suggesting each decision (number of intervals, width, boundaries).
Then add the cumulative frequency column and ask what it answers that the plain
frequency column does not.

### Topic 1.4 - Graphical representation

**Opening.** Show the same data set as a bar chart, a histogram and a pie chart
(you can prepare these in advance). Ask: "Which one is wrong, and which one is
right?" The discussion leads naturally to the rules for choosing.

**Key move.** Emphasize the one structural difference that carries meaning: the
gaps between bars in a bar chart (categories are separate) vs the touching bars
in a histogram (intervals are continuous). Have students draw all three charts
for one data set to feel the difference.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "Qualitative data is less important" | Show a decision that depends entirely on qualitative data (for example, which subject has the most failurs). Importance depends on the question, not the type. |
| "Continuous data must have decimals" | Point out that a count of 30 is discrete even though it is a large number, and a height rounded to the nearest cm is still continuous even though it has no decimal shown. The underlying variable matters, not the precision of the recording. |
| "A histogram and a bar chart are the same" | Draw both from the same data. The gaps in the bar chart communicate that the categories are separate; the touching bars in the histogram communicate continuity. The visual grammar carries meaning. |
| "Frequency tables are only for small data sets" | Point forward to Unit 4: probability distributions are frequency tables extended to theoretical settings. The skill scales. |
| "Pie charts are always the best choice" | Show a pie chart with eight slices and ask what it communicates. The clutter makes the point: pie charts fail when there are too many categories. |

## Practical work

1. **Data scavenger hunt (Topic 1.1-1.2).** Ask each student to find five
   pieces of data in the college or a nearby school. For each, they record the
   item, its type, and whether it is a primary or secondary source. Share and
   discuss in the next session.

2. **Build a frequency table together (Topic 1.3).** Give a raw data set of
   25-30 values. As a class, sort the values, agree on the number of intervals
   and width, then tally and count. Project or draw the table on the board as
   you go.

3. **Chart station rotation (Topic 1.4).** Set up three stations, one for each
   chart type. Students rotate in small groups, spending 10 minutes at each
   station drawing the chart from a provided data set. Debrief by comparing what
   each chart emphasized.

## Assessment guidance

The unit assessment (10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ) is summative. Use the "Check your
understanding" items in each topic for formative assessment during teaching.
The practicum tasks ("Try this at your practicum school") bridge the classroom
and the placement school.

## Materials needed

- Slips of paper with small data sets for sorting (one set per student)
- Blank graph paper or chart templates for the chart-drawing activity
- A real school register or notebook for the Topic 1.1 opening
- Prepared versions of the same data as a bar chart, histogram and pie chart
  for the Topic 1.4 opening

## Connections to other units

- Unit 2 builds on Topic 1.1: the data type determines whether you can compute
  a mean, median or mode.
- Unit 2 builds on Topic 1.3: frequency distributions are the basis for
  histograms and for the probability distributions in Unit 4.
- Unit 5 builds on Topic 1.4: scatter plots (Topic 5.1) are a new chart type
  that, like histograms, require quantitative data.
