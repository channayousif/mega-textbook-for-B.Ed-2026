# Random Variables and Distributions

Unit 9 gave you the probability of a single event, such as "a pupil is absent
today". This unit, which follows Chapter 10 of the course guide, takes the next
step. Instead of one event, we look at a number that changes by chance, such as
*how many* pupils are absent today, and we describe every value that number can
take together with its probability. That description is called a probability
distribution, and it is the bridge between probability and the statistics you
will meet in the rest of the course.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain what a random variable is, and classify a random variable as discrete or continuous with a reason.
2. Build the probability distribution of a discrete random variable from records or from equally likely outcomes, and check whether a table is a valid probability distribution.
3. Compute the expected value of a discrete random variable and interpret it as a long-run average, not as the most likely value.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 9 (basic probability and the probability rules). Unit 1 (discrete and
continuous data). Unit 4 (the mean), because the expected value is a mean.

## In this unit

1. [Random variables](./topic-01) - numbers that come from chance, and the difference between counting and measuring.
2. [Probability distributions](./topic-02) - listing every value with its probability, checking the table, and finding the expected value.
