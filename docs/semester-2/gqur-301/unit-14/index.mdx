# Estimation and Confidence Interval

So far in this course you have learned how to describe a single data set: its
type, its centre and its spread. But statistics becomes most powerful when you
use a sample to say something about a larger population. That is what this unit,
which follows Chapter 14 of the course guide, is about. Sometimes a single
number from the sample is enough to start with. Often you want a range that
captures the uncertainty that comes from observing only part of the population.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Compute a point estimate of a population mean or proportion from sample data and explain what it tells you and what it leaves uncertain.
2. Construct and interpret a confidence interval for a population mean, explaining what the confidence level means in repeated sampling.
3. Explain how sample size and confidence level affect the width of a confidence interval and evaluate a statistical claim that reports an estimate.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 3 (population and sampling), Units 4-6 (mean and standard deviation), and
Unit 11 (the normal model). You need to be comfortable computing a sample mean,
a sample standard deviation and a z-score before starting this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Point estimation](./topic-01) - a single best guess from sample data, and its limits.
2. [Confidence intervals](./topic-02) - a range that captures the uncertainty around that guess.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Work through the classroom situation and
explanation first, then try the activity and the retrieval items. The unit ends
with a summative assessment that combines both topics. When you reach the
confidence-interval formula, have a calculator or spreadsheet handy:
the steps are simple but you should see them work on real numbers.
