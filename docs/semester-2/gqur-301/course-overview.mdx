# Quantitative Reasoning-II (Statistics) - Course Overview

This course introduces prospective teachers to the fundamental concepts of
statistics and develops their ability to analyze and interpret quantitative
information. Across six units you will learn how data is classified, organized
and displayed; how samples are drawn and summarized; how variation is measured;
how probability models uncertainty; how relationships between variables are
described; and how conclusions are drawn from samples through estimation and
hypothesis testing.

## Course outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. Demonstrate understanding of fundamental statistical concepts.
2. Interpret data presented in tables, graphs and charts.
3. Critically evaluate quantitative information and make evidence-based
   decisions using appropriate computational tools.

## Syllabus

The course follows the University of Sindh course guide for GQUR-301
(Quantitative Reasoning-II, Semester 2, 3 credit hours). The guide's 16 chapters
are organized into six units:

1. **[Introduction to Data and Data Organization](./unit-01/)** - types of
   data, sources of data, frequency distributions, bar charts, histograms and
   pie charts.
2. **[Sampling and Measures of Central Tendency](./unit-02/)** - population
   concepts, sampling techniques, mean, median, mode and their interpretation.
3. **[Dispersion and Counting Techniques](./unit-03/)** - range, variance,
   standard deviation, counting principles, permutations and combinations.
4. **[Probability](./unit-04/)** - probability concepts and rules, random
   variables, probability distributions and probabilistic models.
5. **[Bivariate Analysis and Correlation](./unit-05/)** - scatter plots,
   correlation and linear regression.
6. **[Estimation and Hypothesis Testing](./unit-06/)** - point and interval
   estimation, z-test, t-test and statistical modeling.

## Teaching and learning

The course is delivered through lectures, class discussions, question-answer
sessions and brainstorming activities. Practical work includes group work,
group assignments, individual assignments and presentations.

## Recommended reading

The course guide lists the following sources. They are cited for scope and
further reading only; they are never reproduced as content.

- Mann, P. S. (2001). *Introductory Statistics.* Wiley.
- Baboons, S. *Applied Statistical Modeling.*
- Zaslow, E. *Quantitative Reasoning: Thinking in Numbers.*
- Chatfield, C. (1983). *Statistics for Technology: A Course in Applied Statistics.* Chapman & Hall.
- Lock, R. H., Lock, P. F., Lock Morgan, K., & Lock, E. F. (2008). *Statistics: Unlocking the Power of Data.* Wiley.
- Chaudhry, S. M., & Kamal, S. (2008). *Introduction to Statistical Theory* (Parts I & II, 8th ed.). Ilmi Kitab Khana, Lahore.
- Haq, M. (1984). *Foundation of Probability and Statistics.* Tahir Sons, Urdu Bazar, Karachi.

Open-access resources used for worked examples and activities in the units
include the OpenStax *Introductory Statistics* textbook, the NIST/SEMATECH
e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, and the *Online Statistics Education* project
(Rice University).
