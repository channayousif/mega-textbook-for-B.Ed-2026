# Quantitative Reasoning-II (Statistics) - Course Overview

This course introduces prospective teachers to the fundamental concepts of
statistics and develops their ability to analyze and interpret quantitative
information. Across 16 units you will learn how data is classified, organized
and displayed; how samples are drawn and summarized; how variation is measured;
how probability models uncertainty; how relationships between variables are
described; and how conclusions are drawn from samples through estimation and
hypothesis testing.

## Course outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. Demonstrate understanding of fundamental statistical concepts.
2. Interpret data presented in tables, graphs and charts.
3. Critically evaluate quantitative information and make evidence-based
   decisions using appropriate computational tools.

## Syllabus

The course follows the University of Sindh course guide for GQUR-301
(Quantitative Reasoning-II, Semester 2, 3 credit hours) chapter by chapter: the
guide's 16 chapters, one per week, are the course's 16 units, and each topic is
one of the chapter's numbered sections.

1. **Unit 1 (Week 1): [Introduction to Data](./unit-01/index.mdx)** - 1.1 types of data; 1.2 sources of data.
2. **Unit 2 (Week 2): [Data Organization and Presentation](./unit-02/index.mdx)** - 2.1 tabular presentation of data; 2.2 graphical representation.
3. **Unit 3 (Week 3): [Population and Sampling](./unit-03/index.mdx)** - 3.1 population concepts; 3.2 sample and sampling techniques.
4. **Unit 4 (Week 4): [Measures of Central Tendency - I](./unit-04/index.mdx)** - 4.1 mean (arithmetic average); 4.2 median.
5. **Unit 5 (Week 5): [Measures of Central Tendency - II](./unit-05/index.mdx)** - 5.1 mode; 5.2 comparison of mean, median and mode; 5.3 applications and interpretation.
6. **Unit 6 (Week 6): Measures of Dispersion (coming soon)** - 6.1 range; 6.2 variance and standard deviation; 6.3 data interpretation using dispersion.
7. **Unit 7 (Week 7): Counting Techniques (coming soon)** - 7.1 basic counting principles; 7.2 multiplicative rule.
8. **Unit 8 (Week 8): Permutations and Combinations (coming soon)** - 8.1 permutations; 8.2 combinations; 8.3 applications in problem solving.
9. **Unit 9 (Week 9): Introduction to Probability (coming soon)** - 9.1 basic probability concepts; 9.2 probability rules.
10. **Unit 10 (Week 10): Random Variables and Distributions (coming soon)** - 10.1 random variables; 10.2 probability distributions.
11. **Unit 11 (Week 11): Probabilistic Models (coming soon)** - 11.1 introduction to probabilistic models; 11.2 applications in real life.
12. **Unit 12 (Week 12): Bivariate Analysis (coming soon)** - 12.1 scatter plots; 12.2 interpretation of relationships.
13. **Unit 13 (Week 13): Correlation and Regression (coming soon)** - 13.1 correlation; 13.2 regression analysis (linear regression).
14. **Unit 14 (Week 14): Estimation and Confidence Interval (coming soon)** - 14.1 point estimation; 14.2 confidence intervals.
15. **Unit 15 (Week 15): Hypothesis Testing (coming soon)** - 15.1 concept of hypothesis testing; 15.2 z-test; 15.3 t-test.
16. **Unit 16 (Week 16): Quantitative Reasoning Applications (coming soon)** - 16.1 statistical modeling exercises; 16.2 problem solving using statistical concepts; 16.3 review and integration of concepts.

## Teaching and learning

The course is delivered through lectures, class discussions, question-answer
sessions and brainstorming activities. Practical work includes group work,
group assignments, individual assignments and presentations.

## Recommended reading

The course guide lists the following sources. They are cited for scope and
further reading only; they are never reproduced as content.

- Mann, P. S. (2001). *Introductory Statistics.* Wiley.
- Baboons, S. *Applied Statistical Modeling.*
- Zaslow, E. *Quantitative Reasoning: Thinking in Numbers.*
- Chatfield, C. (1983). *Statistics for Technology: A Course in Applied Statistics.* Chapman & Hall.
- Lock, R. H., Lock, P. F., Lock Morgan, K., & Lock, E. F. (2008). *Statistics: Unlocking the Power of Data.* Wiley.
- Chaudhry, S. M., & Kamal, S. (2008). *Introduction to Statistical Theory* (Parts I & II, 8th ed.). Ilmi Kitab Khana, Lahore.
- Haq, M. (1984). *Foundation of Probability and Statistics.* Tahir Sons, Urdu Bazar, Karachi.

Open-access resources used for worked examples and activities in the units
include the OpenStax *Introductory Statistics* textbook, the NIST/SEMATECH
e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, and the *Online Statistics Education* project
(Rice University).
