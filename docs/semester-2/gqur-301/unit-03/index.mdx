# Population and Sampling

Most questions a teacher or an education officer asks are about a whole group:
all pupils in a school, all schools in a district. It is rarely possible to
measure the whole group, so we measure part of it and reason from that part to
the whole. This unit, which follows Chapter 3 of the course guide, gives you the
language of that reasoning and the methods for choosing the part well.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Distinguish a population from a sample and a parameter from a statistic, and explain why sampling is necessary.
2. Distinguish random from non-random sampling techniques and choose an appropriate method for a given study.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-2 (data types and organizing data). HSC-level arithmetic is sufficient.

## In this unit

1. [Population concepts](./topic-01) - population, sample, parameter and statistic, and why we sample.
2. [Sample and sampling techniques](./topic-02) - random and non-random methods and how to choose between them.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through
the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your
understanding" items are there to help you retrieve what you have learned: have
a go at them without looking back, then check your answer. The unit ends with a
summative assessment that draws the two topics together.
