# Unit 3 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 3, which follows
Chapter 3 of the course guide (Week 3). They complement the student-facing
topics with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and practical work, in line
with the guide's teaching strategy of lectures, discussion, question-answer,
brainstorming and practical group and individual work.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 3 (first half) | 3.1 Population concepts | Population, sample, parameter, statistic |
| 3 (second half) | 3.2 Sample and sampling techniques | Random and non-random methods |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 3.1 Population concepts

**Opening.** Ask: "How would you find the average class size in all government
schools in this district?" Most students suggest visiting every school;
challenge them with the time and cost. The tension motivates sampling.

**Key move.** Have students name the population, sample, parameter and statistic
in three short scenarios before giving formal definitions, and draw the
inference arrow from statistic to parameter each time.

### Topic 3.2 Sample and sampling techniques

**Opening.** Describe three ways to choose 30 pupils for a survey (the first
30 at the gate, 30 chosen by the teacher, 30 drawn from the register by lot) and
ask which group is most likely to represent the school.

**Key move.** Draw a real simple random sample from a class register in front of
the class with numbered slips. Then use the three questions (sampling frame?
practical? subgroups?) as a decision tool on new scenarios.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "A bigger sample is always better however it is chosen" | A large biased sample stays biased. Show a large city-only sample that cannot represent rural pupils. |
| "Random means haphazard" | Random means a defined chance mechanism, such as drawing numbered slips, not casual selection. |
| "The sample must be a fixed percentage of the population" | The selection method matters more than the fraction; a well-chosen sample of 400 can serve a large or a small population. |

## Practical work

1. **Sampling frame exercise (Topics 3.1-3.2).** Each student names a population at their practicum school, finds or describes its sampling frame, and plans how to draw a sample of 20. Critique the plans in the next session.

## Assessment guidance

The unit assessment (10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ) is summative. Use each topic's
"Check your understanding" items for formative assessment during teaching. The
practicum tasks link the college classroom to the placement school.

## Materials needed

- A real class register for drawing a random sample
- Numbered slips and a box
