# Unit 5 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 5, which follows
Chapter 5 of the course guide (Week 5). They complement the student-facing
topics with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and practical work, in line
with the guide's teaching strategy of lectures, discussion, question-answer,
brainstorming and practical group and individual work.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (first session) | 5.1 Mode | Most frequent value; bimodal and no-mode cases; modal class |
| 5 (second session) | 5.2 Comparison of mean, median and mode | Properties of each; skew; the limits of the rule |
| 5 (third session) | 5.3 Applications and interpretation | Choosing and reporting a measure |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 5.1 Mode

**Opening.** Ask: "What is the most popular subject in this class?" Neither the
mean nor the median can answer. The need for a third measure is immediate.

**Key move.** Tally a quick class survey (home language, number of people at
home) and find each mode. Include a set with no mode and one that is bimodal, and
ask what two peaks might mean.

### Topic 5.2 Comparison of mean, median and mode

**Opening.** Present the workshop wages and the owner's claim, and ask whether
the owner is lying.

**Key move.** Compute all three measures for a symmetric, a right-skewed and a
left-skewed set, and let the usual pattern emerge. Then show 0, 0, 2, 2 and a
small bimodal set so that students learn the pattern as a rule of thumb, not a
law.

### Topic 5.3 Applications and interpretation

**Opening.** Give the head teacher's three questions (walking distance, most
common class size, stationery budget) and ask which "average" each needs.

**Key move.** Use the three checks (data type, outliers or skew, the question)
and have students rewrite a vague report ("the average is 4 km") into an honest
one that names the measure and its context.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "Mode only matters for qualitative data" | The mode also answers "most common" questions about numbers, such as the shoe size a shop should stock most of. |
| "Skewed data always give mean > median > mode in the textbook order" | Show 0, 0, 2, 2 and a small bimodal set; the order is typical for single-peaked data, not guaranteed. |
| "If the data are numbers, report the mean" | For skewed numbers like distances or incomes the mean can describe almost nobody; choose by the question and name the measure. |

## Practical work

1. **Survey and tally (Topic 5.1).** Groups tally class survey data and find each mode.
2. **Same mean, different stories (Topic 5.2).** Pairs compare two groups with equal means but different shapes.
3. **Three questions, three measures (Topic 5.3).** Groups write questions each measure answers best and draft a report sentence.

## Assessment guidance
