# Measures of Central Tendency - II

Unit 4 gave you the mean and the median. This unit, which follows Chapter 5 of
the course guide, adds the third measure of central tendency, the mode, and then
does the more important job: comparing the three, and deciding which one to
report in a real school situation. Choosing well is what turns calculation into
quantitative reasoning.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Find the mode of qualitative, ungrouped and grouped data, and recognise bimodal data and data with no mode.
2. Compare the mean, median and mode of a data set and explain what the comparison suggests about its shape, including the limits of that rule.
3. Choose and justify the measure of central tendency to report for a given data set and question.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 4 (mean and median). Unit 2 (frequency tables) for the grouped-data mode.

## In this unit

1. [Mode](./topic-01) - the most frequent value, for every kind of data.
2. [Comparison of mean, median and mode](./topic-02) - what each measure does and how skew separates them.
3. [Applications and interpretation](./topic-03) - choosing and reporting a measure in real school situations.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through
the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your
understanding" items are there to help you retrieve what you have learned: have
a go at them without looking back, then check your answer. The unit ends with a
summative assessment that draws the three topics together.
