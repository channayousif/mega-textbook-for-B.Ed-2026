## Unit summary

This unit, which follows Chapter 7 of the course guide, introduced the
counting techniques on which probability is built.

- Topic 7.1 defined an outcome as one possible result of a choice, and
  showed how to list outcomes systematically (fix the first choice, run
  through the second, then move on) so that none is missed or repeated. When
  one option is chosen from separate groups that share nothing, the addition
  rule gives the total. When groups overlap, the shared outcomes must be
  subtracted once. Listing is the foundation and the check, but it becomes
  impractical for large problems.
- Topic 7.2 gave the multiplicative rule: when a decision is made in stages
  and every outcome needs a choice at each stage, multiply the number of ways
  at each stage. A tree diagram shows why, because every path is one outcome.
  The deciding questions are: does each outcome need a choice from every
  group (multiply), or does it use only one option in total (add)? Many real
  problems need both: multiply along a path, add across separate branches.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* In counting, an outcome is:
   - A) The total number of choices available
   - B) One possible result of a choice or an event
   - C) The most frequent result of a choice
   - D) A choice that has already been made

2. *(Understand)* The main reason for listing outcomes in a fixed order is to:
   - A) Make the list shorter
   - B) Avoid having to multiply
   - C) Make sure no outcome is missed or repeated
   - D) Put the most likely outcome first

3. *(Apply)* A pupil may choose one prize book from 6 Urdu novels or 5 English
   novels. How many different choices are there?
   - A) 1
   - B) 6
   - C) 30
   - D) 11

4. *(Apply)* In a class, 15 pupils are in the debate club, 10 are in the quiz
   club and 4 are in both. How many pupils are in at least one of the clubs?
   - A) 21
   - B) 25
   - C) 29
   - D) 19

5. *(Understand)* The addition rule of counting can be applied directly when:
   - A) Every outcome needs one choice from each group
   - B) The groups share no option and one option is chosen in total
   - C) The groups have the same number of options
   - D) The outcomes are listed in a fixed order

6. *(Remember)* By the multiplicative rule, if a first choice can be made in
   *m* ways and then a second in *n* ways, both can be made in:
   - A) *m* + *n* ways
   - B) *m* - *n* ways
   - C) *m* x *n* ways
   - D) *n* ways

7. *(Apply)* A school uniform has 4 shirt colours and 3 trouser colours. How
   many different uniforms are possible?
   - A) 12
   - B) 7
   - C) 1
   - D) 64

8. *(Apply)* A locker label is one letter from A to E followed by one digit
   from 0 to 9. How many different labels are possible?
   - A) 15
   - B) 10
   - C) 5
   - D) 50

9. *(Apply)* A tree diagram has 2 branches at the first stage, each splitting
   into 3 at the second stage, each of those splitting into 2 at the third.
   How many end points does it have?
   - A) 7
   - B) 12
   - C) 6
   - D) 8

10. *(Understand)* Which of these is counted by adding rather than
    multiplying?
    - A) Choosing one shirt and one pair of trousers
    - B) Filling Period 1 and then Period 2 of a timetable
    - C) Choosing one sweet from 3 kinds or one fruit from 4 kinds
    - D) Making a code of a letter followed by a digit

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in a few sentences or a short calculation.

1. *(Understand)* Explain how the method "fix the first choice, run through
   the second, then move on" makes sure a list of outcomes is complete.

2. *(Apply)* On a test day, the morning paper is Mathematics or English and
   the afternoon paper is Science, Urdu or Sindhi. List every possible
   morning-and-afternoon pair systematically and state the count.

3. *(Apply)* A Class 9 section has 45 pupils. Twenty are scouts, 15 are in the
   dramatics society and 5 are in both. How many pupils are in neither? Show
   your working.

4. *(Analyze)* A prospectus says: "We offer 9 electives: 5 in the arts group
   and 4 in the science group." Computer Studies is listed in both groups.
   Analyze whether the claim of 9 electives is accurate, and give the correct
   number.

5. *(Understand)* A trainee counts uniforms with 2 shirts, 2 trousers and the
   sweater choice "none, grey or maroon" as 2 x 2 x 2 = 8. Explain the error.

6. *(Apply)* A locker code is a letter from A to F followed by a digit from 1
   to 9. How many codes are possible? Which rule did you use?

7. *(Apply)* Four pupils are to sit in the four seats of the front row. Use
   the multiplicative rule to find the number of seating orders, and explain
   why the number of ways falls at each stage.

8. *(Analyze)* Each pupil chooses one club, either the science club (which
   meets on 2 possible days) or the sports club (which meets on 3 possible
   days), and then one meeting day. Count the outcomes and explain why the
   multiplicative rule cannot be used for the whole problem.

9. *(Analyze)* For each situation, decide whether to add or multiply, give
   the count and a reason: (a) a pupil borrows one item, a storybook from 5 or
   a magazine from 3; (b) a pupil borrows one storybook from 5 and one
   magazine from 3.

10. *(Analyze)* A school with 300 pupils plans an ID code of one section
    letter (A, B or C) followed by one digit. Analyze whether this format is
    enough, and suggest the smallest change that would give every pupil a
    code.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a structured paragraph or set of paragraphs.

1. *(Analyze)* A school has three clubs: debate (18 members), science
   (22 members) and art (15 members). Four pupils belong to both debate and
   science; no other pupil belongs to more than one club. The principal
   reports that "55 pupils take part in clubs". Analyze the principal's
   figure: explain how it was obtained, why it is wrong and what the correct
   number is. Then suggest one routine the school could use to avoid this
   error. (Write 120-160 words after your working.)

2. *(Analyze)* A head teacher plans the first four periods of a timetable:
   Period 1 is one of 3 languages, Period 2 is Mathematics or Science,
   Period 3 is one of 2 activity periods and Period 4 is one of 3 other
   subjects. Use the multiplicative rule to count the possible timetables,
   draw the tree for the first two stages, and analyze whether the school
   could run a different timetable on every one of 40 school days. (Write
   100-150 words after your working.)

3. *(Evaluate)* A trainee's worksheet shows four answers: (a) uniforms from
   3 shirts and 2 trousers, she wrote 5; (b) one prize chosen from 4 books or
   3 pens, she wrote 12; (c) a journey made by one of 2 van routes, or by one
   of 3 rickshaw stands and then one of 2 walking paths, she wrote 7; (d)
   codes of a letter A to D followed by one digit, she wrote 40. Evaluate
   each answer, correct those that are wrong, and write feedback that would
   help her decide between adding and multiplying in future. (Write 150-200
   words.)

4. *(Evaluate)* A school expects to enrol up to 1,200 pupils over the next
   ten years and needs an admission-number format. Proposal X is one letter
   from A to Z followed by one digit. Proposal Y is a section letter A to D
   followed by three digits. Count the codes each proposal allows, evaluate
   which proposal the school should adopt, and suggest one further feature a
   good format should have beyond its size. (Write 150-200 words.)

5. *(Evaluate)* A school canteen advertises "more than 20 different meal
   deals every day". A deal is rice or roti, then one of 3 curries (daal,
   chicken, sabzi), then water or lassi, then kheer or no dessert. On Fridays
   chicken is not served. Count the deals on an ordinary day and on a
   Friday, and evaluate the canteen's claim. Rewrite the advertisement so
   that it is accurate. (Write 150-200 words.)

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

| Item | Answer | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | An outcome is one possible result of a choice. |
| 2 | C | A fixed order prevents missed and repeated outcomes. |
| 3 | D | One book from either group, which share nothing: 6 + 5 = 11. |
| 4 | A | 15 + 10 - 4 = 21; the 4 pupils in both are subtracted once. |
| 5 | B | The addition rule needs non-overlapping groups and one option in total. |
| 6 | C | Staged choices multiply. |
| 7 | A | 4 x 3 = 12. |
| 8 | D | 5 x 10 = 50. |
| 9 | B | 2 x 3 x 2 = 12 end points. |
| 10 | C | One option in total from separate groups is counted by adding: 3 + 4 = 7. |

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Holding the first choice fixed and running through every option of the second guarantees that every pairing with that first option appears once. Moving to each first option in turn covers all of them, so nothing is missed and nothing is repeated. (2 marks: 1 for "every pairing with the fixed option", 1 for "each first option in turn".)

2. Maths-Science, Maths-Urdu, Maths-Sindhi, English-Science, English-Urdu, English-Sindhi: 6 pairs. (2 marks: 1 for a complete systematic list, 1 for the count.)

3. At least one group: 20 + 15 - 5 = 30. Neither: 45 - 30 = 15. (2 marks: 1 for subtracting the overlap, 1 for 15.)

4. The claim is not accurate. Adding 5 + 4 counts Computer Studies twice because the groups overlap, so the addition rule cannot be applied directly. There are 5 + 4 - 1 = 8 different electives. (3 marks: 1 for identifying the overlap, 1 for the reason the rule fails, 1 for 8.)

5. The sweater stage has 3 options, because "none" is one of the choices. The correct count is 2 x 2 x 3 = 12, so the trainee missed 4 uniforms. (2 marks: 1 for "none counts as an option", 1 for 12.)

6. 6 x 9 = 54 codes, by the multiplicative rule, because each code needs a letter and then a digit. (2 marks: 1 for 54, 1 for naming the rule with a reason.)

7. 4 x 3 x 2 x 1 = 24 orders. Each pupil seated cannot sit again, so one fewer pupil is available for each next seat. (2 marks: 1 for 24, 1 for the explanation.)

8. Science gives 2 outcomes and sports gives 3, so there are 2 + 3 = 5. The number of days depends on the club chosen, so there is no single count to multiply by at the second stage; the club choices are alternatives and are added. (3 marks: 1 for 5, 1 for "number of days differs", 1 for "add across branches".)

9. (a) Add: one item in total from separate groups, 5 + 3 = 8. (b) Multiply: each outcome needs one item from each group, 5 x 3 = 15. (3 marks: 1.5 for each correct decision with count and reason; round down.)

10. The format gives 3 x 10 = 30 codes, far fewer than 300, so it is not enough. Adding a second digit gives 3 x 10 x 10 = 300 codes, which is just enough; a third digit (3,000 codes) would leave room for growth. (3 marks: 1 for 30, 1 for the judgement, 1 for a correct change with count.)
