# Measures of Central Tendency - I

When a head teacher asks "how did the class do?", the answer is usually one
number that stands for the whole set of marks. This unit, which follows Chapter
4 of the course guide, introduces the first two such numbers: the mean, which
shares the total out equally, and the median, which finds the middle. Unit 5
adds the mode and shows how to choose between all three.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Compute the arithmetic mean of a raw data set and of a frequency table, and explain what it represents.
2. Compute the median of a data set with an odd or an even number of values, and explain why it is robust to outliers.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1-2 (data types and frequency tables). HSC-level arithmetic is sufficient;
no advanced mathematics is assumed.

## In this unit

1. [Mean (arithmetic average)](./topic-01) - computing the mean from a list and from a frequency table.
2. [Median](./topic-02) - finding the middle value, and how it differs from the mean.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through
the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your
understanding" items are there to help you retrieve what you have learned: have
a go at them without looking back, then check your answer. The unit ends with a
summative assessment that draws the two topics together.
