## Explanation

### The median: the middle value

The <Glossary term="Median" /> is the middle value when the data are arranged in
order. Half of the values lie at or below it and half lie at or above it. To
find the median:

1. **Sort** the data from smallest to largest. This step is not optional.
2. **Count** the number of values, n.
3. If n is **odd**, the median is the value in position (n + 1) ÷ 2.
4. If n is **even**, there are two middle values, in positions n ÷ 2 and
   n ÷ 2 + 1, and the median is their average.

**Odd example.** The ages of 7 teachers are 41, 29, 35, 52, 33, 38, 30.
Sorted: 29, 30, 33, 35, 38, 41, 52. With n = 7, the median is in position
(7 + 1) ÷ 2 = 4, so the median age is **35**.

**Even example.** The quiz marks sorted are 3, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 17, 18, 18,
19. With n = 10, the middle positions are 5 and 6, which hold 16 and 17, so the
median is (16 + 17) ÷ 2 = **16.5**. Notice that 16.5 is not one of the marks:
with an even number of values the median can fall between two of them.

### Why the median resists outliers

The median depends only on which values sit in the middle positions, not on how
far away the extreme values are. If the pupil who scored 3 had scored 0, or 11,
the sorted list would still have 16 and 17 in positions 5 and 6, and the median
would still be 16.5. The mean, by contrast, would change every time.

This is why the median is called **robust**. In the quiz, the mean (14.9) is
lower than seven of the ten marks, while the median (16.5) sits
with the cluster where most pupils actually scored (the figures above show both
on the same number line). Lane (2023, Chapter 3) makes the same comparison and
notes that the two measures agree closely only when the data are roughly
symmetric.

### Worked example: mean and median together

**Data:** test marks (out of 50) of 8 pupils: 28, 35, 42, 38, 45, 31, 40, 36.

**Step 1: sort.** 28, 31, 35, 36, 38, 40, 42, 45.

**Step 2: mean.** Sum = 295, n = 8, mean = 295 ÷ 8 = 36.875, about 36.9.

**Step 3: median.** n = 8 is even, so average the 4th and 5th values:
(36 + 38) ÷ 2 = 37.

**Result:** mean 36.9, median 37. Here the two are almost equal because the
marks are spread evenly with no extreme value. A large gap between the mean and
the median is a signal to look for outliers or a long tail in the data.

### Choosing between mean and median

- **Use the mean** when the data are roughly symmetric with no extreme values.
  It uses all the information and later techniques build on it.
- **Use the median** when the data contain outliers or a long tail, such as
  family incomes or the quiz with one very low mark.
- **Report both** when they differ, because the gap itself tells the reader
  something about the data.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think "the median is just the middle
number in the list". Taking the middle of the list *as it was written*, without
sorting, gives a meaningless answer: in the unsorted quiz list the 5th and 6th
entries are 17 and 17, which happens to look reasonable but is luck. A second
mistake is to think the median must be one of the data values; with an even
number of values it is the average of the two middle ones.

## Activity: Move one mark

Work in groups of three. About 15 minutes.

Start from the sorted quiz marks: 3, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 17, 18, 18, 19.

1. Compute the mean and the median.
2. Change the 3 to 0. Recompute both. Then change it to 15. Recompute both.
3. Change the 19 to 50 instead. Recompute both.
4. Record your results in a table. As a group, write two sentences: one
   describing what happened to the mean, and one describing what happened to
   the median, and why.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* List the steps for finding the median of a data set with an
   even number of values.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why changing the largest value in a data set from 19
   to 50 does not change the median.
3. *(Apply)* Find the mean and the median of these monthly salaries (thousands
   of rupees) of 9 school staff: 25, 28, 30, 32, 35, 38, 40, 45, 200. Which
   better describes a typical salary, and why?

## Summary

The median is the middle value of sorted data: the value in position
(n + 1) ÷ 2 when n is odd, or the average of the two middle values when n is
even. It depends only on the middle positions, so outliers barely move it,
while the mean moves with every value. When the data are roughly symmetric the
two agree; when they differ, the median usually describes the typical value
better, and reporting both tells the reader about the shape of the data.

## Try this at your practicum school

Obtain 10 to 15 marks from one class test (with names removed). Compute the
mean and the median. If they differ by more than a mark or two, find the values
responsible. Write a short note for your cooperating teacher saying which figure

## Summative task

The monthly incomes (thousands of rupees) of 11 families in a neighbourhood are
18, 22, 25, 28, 30, 32, 35, 38, 40, 45, 120.

a) Compute the mean income.
b) Compute the median income.
c) In 80-120 words, explain which measure better represents the typical family
   income and why. Identify the outlier and describe its effect on each
   measure.
