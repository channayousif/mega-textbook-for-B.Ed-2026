## Unit summary

This unit, which follows Chapter 4 of the course guide, introduced the first
two measures of central tendency.

- Topic 4.1 defined the mean as the sum of the values divided by their number,
  readable as an equal share or as the point where the data balance. From a
  frequency table it is Σfx ÷ Σf. Because every value enters the sum, one
  outlier can pull the mean away from most of the data.
- Topic 4.2 defined the median as the middle value of sorted data: the value in
  position (n + 1) ÷ 2 when n is odd, or the average of the two middle values
  when n is even. It depends only on the middle positions, so it is robust to
  outliers.

When the data are roughly symmetric the two measures agree; a large gap between
them is a signal to look for outliers or a long tail. Unit 5 adds the mode and
the full comparison.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* The arithmetic mean of a data set is:
   - A) The value that occurs most often
   - B) The sum of the values divided by the number of values
   - C) The middle value of the sorted data
   - D) The difference between the largest and smallest values

2. *(Apply)* Five pupils scored 6, 8, 9, 7 and 10 on a quiz. What is the mean?
   - A) 7
   - B) 8
   - C) 9
   - D) 40

3. *(Apply)* In a frequency table, marks 4, 5 and 6 were obtained by 3, 5 and 2
   pupils. What is the mean mark?
   - A) 4.9
   - B) 5.0
   - C) 15.0
   - D) 16.3

4. *(Understand)* Adding one very high value to a data set will usually:
   - A) Leave the mean unchanged
   - B) Raise the mean
   - C) Lower the mean
   - D) Make the mean equal to the median

5. *(Understand)* "The mean is the balance point of the data" means that:
   - A) The mean is always the middle value
   - B) The distances of values below the mean and above it add to the same total
   - C) Half the values are equal to the mean
   - D) The mean is the most common value

6. *(Remember)* Before finding the median, you must first:
   - A) Add all the values
   - B) Sort the values in order
   - C) Remove the largest value
   - D) Find the mode

7. *(Apply)* What is the median of 41, 29, 35, 52, 33, 38, 30?
   - A) 35
   - B) 36.9
   - C) 33
   - D) 38

8. *(Apply)* What is the median of 3, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 17, 18, 18, 19?
   - A) 16
   - B) 16.5
   - C) 17
   - D) 14.9

9. *(Understand)* A teacher finds that her class's mean mark is much lower than
   the median. This most likely suggests that:
   - A) The data are perfectly symmetric
   - B) A few very low marks are pulling the mean down
   - C) A few very high marks are pulling the mean up
   - D) The median was calculated incorrectly

10. *(Apply)* For the values 5, 7, 8, 8, 9, 10, 45, which measure better describes
    the typical value?
    - A) The mean, because it uses every value
    - B) The median, because it is not pulled by 45
    - C) The mean, because 45 is the largest value
    - D) Neither, because the data have an outlier

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in a few sentences or a short paragraph.

1. *(Understand)* Explain the mean in two ways: as a fair share and as a balance
   point.

2. *(Apply)* The marks (out of 30) of 8 pupils are 18, 22, 25, 19, 21, 23, 20, 4.
   Compute the mean, showing your working.

3. *(Apply)* A survey of 20 households records 2 children in 4 households, 3 in
   9, 4 in 5 and 6 in 2. Compute the mean number of children per household.

4. *(Analyze)* A trainee computes the mean of a frequency table by adding the
   products f × x and dividing by the number of rows. Explain the error and its
   effect, using a small example.

5. *(Analyze)* Two teachers compute the mean of the same class's test marks.
   Teacher A gets 38 and Teacher B gets 42, and both added correctly. Teacher A
   included two pupils who were absent and recorded as 0; Teacher B left them
   out. Explain why their means differ in this direction, and which figure is
   more appropriate for reporting the class's performance on the test.

6. *(Understand)* Explain why the median is called "robust" while the mean is
   "sensitive to outliers".

7. *(Apply)* Using the marks in RRQ 2 (18, 22, 25, 19, 21, 23, 20, 4), find the
   median, showing your working. Compare it with the mean.

8. *(Apply)* Use a small example of five or six values to show that changing the
   largest value changes the mean but not the median.

9. *(Analyze)* A newspaper reports that the "average household income in Sindh
   is Rs 45,000 per month". Explain why this figure might overstate a typical
   household's income and what you would want to know before accepting it.

10. *(Analyze)* For a data set, the mean and the median are almost equal.
    Explain what this suggests about the data, and why you should still look at
    the data before concluding that it has no outliers.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a structured paragraph or set of paragraphs.

1. *(Analyze)* The number of days absent in a term for 12 pupils are 0, 1, 1, 2,
   2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 29. (a) Compute the mean using a frequency table.
   (b) Compute the mean without the value 29. (c) Analyze what the difference
   shows about the mean as a summary of this class's attendance. (Write 100-150
   words for part (c).)

2. *(Evaluate)* A head teacher must report "the average class size" for six
   sections of sizes 32, 38, 41, 35, 44 and 40 to support a request for a new
   teacher. Compute the mean and the median, then evaluate which figure she
   should report and how she should word it. (Write 100-150 words after your
   working.)

3. *(Analyze)* The monthly incomes (thousands of rupees) of 11 families are 18,
   22, 25, 28, 30, 32, 35, 38, 40, 45, 120. (a) Compute the mean and the median.
   (b) Analyze the effect of the outlier on each, and state which better
   represents the typical family. (Write 100-150 words for part (b).)

4. *(Evaluate)* Two schools report their grade 5 mathematics results. School P:
   mean 61, median 70. School Q: mean 64, median 63. A district officer says
   "School Q did better because its average is higher". Evaluate this
   conclusion: explain what the gap between mean and median suggests about each
   school's marks, and what further information you would need. (Write 150-200
   words.)

5. *(Evaluate)* Design a short activity for your practicum class (grade 6 or 7)
   that teaches pupils why the mean and the median can differ. Describe the data
   pupils would use, the steps they would follow, and how you would judge
   whether they had understood. Evaluate one risk in your design and how you
   would handle it. (Write 150-200 words.)

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

| Item | Answer | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | The mean is the sum divided by the count. |
| 2 | B | (6 + 8 + 9 + 7 + 10) ÷ 5 = 40 ÷ 5 = 8. |
| 3 | A | (4×3 + 5×5 + 6×2) ÷ 10 = 49 ÷ 10 = 4.9. |
| 4 | B | A high value adds a large amount to the sum, raising the mean. |
| 5 | B | Distances below and above the mean cancel exactly. |
| 6 | B | The median is the middle of sorted data. |
| 7 | A | Sorted: 29, 30, 33, 35, 38, 41, 52; the 4th value is 35. |
| 8 | B | n = 10; the 5th and 6th values are 16 and 17; (16 + 17) ÷ 2 = 16.5. |
| 9 | B | Low outliers pull the mean below the median. |
| 10 | B | The median ignores how far away 45 is. |

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Fair share: if all the values were pooled and shared equally, each member would get the mean. Balance point: on a number line the values balance at the mean, because the distances below it and above it add to the same total. (2 marks: 1 for each.)

2. Sum = 18 + 22 + 25 + 19 + 21 + 23 + 20 + 4 = 152; n = 8; mean = 152 ÷ 8 = 19. (2 marks: 1 for the sum, 1 for the mean.)

3. Σfx = 2×4 + 3×9 + 4×5 + 6×2 = 8 + 27 + 20 + 12 = 67; Σf = 20; mean = 67 ÷ 20 = 3.35 children. (3 marks: 1 for products, 1 for Σf, 1 for the mean.)

4. The divisor must be the total frequency (number of values), not the number of rows. Example: marks 5, 6, 7, 8 with frequencies 2, 3, 4, 1 give Σfx = 64; 64 ÷ 10 = 6.4 is correct, but 64 ÷ 4 = 16 is impossible on a 10-mark test. Dividing by rows inflates the mean whenever frequencies exceed 1. (3 marks: 1 for the error, 1 for the example, 1 for the effect.)

5. Teacher A's two zeros add nothing to the sum but increase the count, so her mean is lower. The zeros record absence, not performance, so Teacher B's 42 is more appropriate for reporting performance on the test; absences should be reported separately. (3 marks: 1 for the direction, 1 for the reason, 1 for the justified choice.)

6. The median depends only on which values are in the middle positions, so moving an extreme value does not change it. The mean adds every value, so an extreme value changes the sum and the mean. (2 marks: 1 for each.)

7. Sorted: 4, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 25. n = 8, so the median is the average of the 4th and 5th values: (20 + 21) ÷ 2 = 20.5. The mean (19) is lower because the mark of 4 pulls it down. (3 marks: 1 for sorting, 1 for 20.5, 1 for the comparison.)

8. Example: 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 has mean 14 and median 14. Change 18 to 100: mean = 152 ÷ 5 = 30.4, median still 14. (2 marks: 1 for a correct example, 1 for the explanation.)

9. Income data are usually skewed right: a few very high incomes raise the mean above what a typical household earns. I would want to know whether the figure is a mean or a median, how the data were collected and from which households, and the median for comparison. (3 marks: 1 for skew, 1 for suggesting the median, 1 for further information.)

10. Close mean and median suggest roughly symmetric data without a strong pull in one direction. But extreme values on both sides can balance out, so a symmetric pair of outliers would leave the two close; a dot plot or sorted list should be checked. (2 marks: 1 for the suggestion, 1 for the caution.)
