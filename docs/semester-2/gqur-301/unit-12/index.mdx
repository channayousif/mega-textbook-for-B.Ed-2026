# Bivariate Analysis

So far in this course you have learned to describe one variable at a time: its type,
its source, its centre and its spread. Much of statistics, however, is about *pairs*
of variables: does a pupil's attendance relate to their test score? Does the time a
learner studies relate to their grade? This unit, which follows Chapter 12 of the
course guide, introduces the simplest tool for exploring such questions, the scatter
plot, and shows how to read the relationship it reveals without jumping past what the
data actually say.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Construct a scatter plot from a small bivariate data set, labelling the axes correctly.
2. Describe the direction, strength and form of the relationship shown in a scatter plot.
3. Identify an outlier and explain how it affects the interpretation of a relationship.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 (data types) and Unit 2 (graphs). You should be comfortable plotting points on
axes and reading bar charts and histograms before starting this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Scatter plots](./topic-01) - plotting bivariate data and the parts of a scatter plot.
2. [Interpretation of relationships](./topic-02) - direction, strength, form, outliers and the limits of interpretation.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Topic 12.1 teaches you to plot; Topic 12.2 teaches
you to read and to hold back from reading too much in. Together they prepare you for
the correlation and regression ideas in Unit 13.
