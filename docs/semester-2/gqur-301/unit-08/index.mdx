# Permutations and Combinations

Unit 7 gave you two counting rules: add when you choose one option *or*
another, and multiply when you make one choice *and then* another. This unit,
which follows Chapter 8 of the course guide, uses the multiplicative rule to
build two powerful shortcuts. A **permutation** counts arrangements, where
order matters. A **combination** counts selections, where it does not. Most
of the unit is about one question you will ask again and again: does the
order matter here?

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Use factorial notation and the permutation formula to count arrangements of pupils, prizes and posts.
2. Use the combination formula to count selections such as committees and teams, and explain how it is linked to the permutation formula.
3. Decide, with reasons, which counting method a school problem needs, solve it, and check that the answer is sensible.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 7 (counting principles), especially the multiplicative rule and tree
diagrams. HSC-level arithmetic with multiplication and division.

## In this unit

1. [Permutations](./topic-01) - arrangements in which order matters, and factorial notation.
2. [Combinations](./topic-02) - selections in which order does not matter.
3. [Applications in problem solving](./topic-03) - choosing the right method for a real school problem.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work
through the explanation with a pencil in hand, and try the activity before you
move on. Keep a calculator nearby, but do every small case by listing first:
the formulas only make sense once you have seen what they count. The "Check
your understanding" items help you retrieve what you have learned, so try them
without looking back. The unit ends with a summative assessment that draws the
three topics together.
