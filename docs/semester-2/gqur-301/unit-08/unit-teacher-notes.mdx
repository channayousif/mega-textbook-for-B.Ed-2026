# Unit 8 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 8, which follows
Chapter 8 of the course guide (Week 8). They complement the student-facing
topics with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and practical work, in line
with the guide's teaching strategy of lectures, discussion, question-answer,
brainstorming and practical group and individual work.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (first session) | 8.1 Permutations | Arrangements, factorial notation, P(n, r), alike items |
| 8 (second session) | 8.2 Combinations | Selections, the swap test, C(n, r) as P(n, r) ÷ r! |
| 8 (third session) | 8.3 Applications in problem solving | Choosing the method; stages, restrictions, "at least one"; checking answers |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 8.1 Permutations

**Opening.** Call three students to the front and ask the class to direct them
into every possible line. Record each order on the board. Students usually
find six only after a few repeats and misses, which motivates a systematic
method.

**Key move.** Link each factor of n! to a physical place in the line: "how many
people could stand here?" Only then introduce the notation. Show P(n, r) as
"the first r factors of n!", and write n! ÷ (n - r)! afterwards as a compact
form, not the starting point.

### Topic 8.2 Combinations

**Opening.** Reuse the three students from the first session: "Now they are a
committee, not a line. How many committees?" The answer, one, is surprising
and memorable.

**Key move.** Teach the swap test as the decision rule, and derive C(n, r) by
dividing the permutation count by r!. Have students check C(4, 2) = 6 by
listing pairs before using the formula on larger numbers.

### Topic 8.3 Applications in problem solving

**Opening.** Present the trainees' four problems from the classroom situation
and take a quick vote on "permutation or combination?" for each. The split
vote shows why a decision method is needed.

**Key move.** Walk through the flowchart for each problem aloud, then let
groups do the card sort. Insist that every answer comes with a check: a small
listed case, a comparison with the unrestricted count, or a size check.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "n! means multiplying a number by itself" | Write 5! next to 5 × 5 and 5⁵ and compute all three. Tie each factor of 5! to a place in a line. |
| "A committee where members have no roles is a permutation" | Use the swap test: if swapping two members gives the same committee, order does not matter. Contrast with a committee that has a chair and a secretary. |
| "Permutations and combinations are interchangeable" | Compute 1,000, 720 and 120 for three questions about the same ten digits, and ask what each number counts. |

## Practical work

1. **Human arrangements (Topic 8.1).** Groups physically form every line of three and then four members, predicting the count before listing.
2. **Committee or line-up? (Topic 8.2).** Pairs write and swap selection situations and justify the method with the swap test.
3. **Sort, solve and swap (Topic 8.3).** Groups sort problem cards with the flowchart, solve them, check them, and mark another group's work.
4. **Group assignment.** Each group writes three counting problems from a real school (timetable, sports, committees), solves them, and presents one to the class with its check.

## Assessment guidance

The unit assessment (10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ) is summative. Use each topic's
"Check your understanding" items for formative assessment during teaching. The
practicum tasks link the college classroom to the placement school.
