## A real classroom situation

A teacher has recorded the marks (out of 20) of 10 pupils on a short quiz:
12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 17, 18, 18, 19, 3. One pupil scored only 3. The teacher
wants to report a single number that represents how the class did. She adds the
marks, divides by 10, and gets 14.9. But she knows that 14.9 does not feel right
- most pupils scored 15 or above. The one very low mark is pulling the average
down. She wonders if there is a better way to describe the class's centre.

Her instinct is correct. This topic gives you two measures of central tendency
- the mean and the median - and the tools to decide which one to report.

## Explanation

### The mean: the arithmetic average

The <Glossary term="Mean" /> (often called the arithmetic average) is the sum
of all values divided by the number of values.

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}$$

For the quiz marks:

$$\bar{x} = \frac{12 + 14 + 15 + 16 + 17 + 17 + 18 + 18 + 19 + 3}{10} = \frac{149}{10} = 14.9$$

The mean uses every value in the data set. This is both its strength and its
weakness: the mean uses all the information, but it is sensitive to extreme
values. The mark of 3 pulls the mean down by about 1.2 points compared to what
it would be without that one value.

### The median: the middle value

The <Glossary term="Median" /> is the middle value when the data are arranged
in order. To find the median:

1. **Sort** the data from smallest to largest.
2. **Count** the number of values, *n*.
3. If *n* is **odd**, the median is the value at position (*n* + 1) / 2.
4. If *n* is **even**, the median is the average of the two middle values
   (positions *n* / 2 and *n* / 2 + 1).

For the quiz marks (sorted): 3, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 17, 18, 18, 19.

*n* = 10 (even), so the median is the average of the 5th and 6th values:
(16 + 17) / 2 = 16.5.

The median is 16.5, much closer to where most of the marks sit. The one low
mark of 3 barely affects the median because the median only cares about the
middle position, not the actual values at the extremes.

### Worked example: computing mean and median together

**Data:** test marks (out of 50) of 8 pupils: 28, 35, 42, 38, 45, 31, 40, 36.

**Step 1: Sort the data.** 28, 31, 35, 36, 38, 40, 42, 45.

**Step 2: Compute the mean.** Sum = 28 + 31 + 35 + 36 + 38 + 40 + 42 + 45 = 295.
*n* = 8. Mean = 295 / 8 = 36.875.

**Step 3: Find the median.** *n* = 8 (even), so the median is the average of
the 4th and 5th values: (36 + 38) / 2 = 37.

**Result:** Mean = 36.875, Median = 37. In this data set the two measures are
close because the data are roughly symmetric with no extreme outliers.

### When to use which

- **Use the mean** when the data are roughly symmetric with no extreme
  outliers. The mean uses all the information and is the basis for many later
  statistical techniques.
- **Use the median** when the data are skewed or contain outliers. The median
  is robust - it is not pulled by extreme values.
- **Report both** when they differ, because the gap between them tells the
  reader about the shape of the distribution.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think "the mean is always the right
answer for the average". The mean is the default, but when a distribution is
skewed (for example, income data, where a few very high incomes pull the mean
up), the median gives a more honest picture of the typical value. There is no
single "correct" measure - the right choice depends on the shape of the data
and the question you are answering.

## Activity: Compute mean and median from your own data

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Collect a small data set of your choice from your college or practicum school.
For example: the ages of 10 pupils in a class, the number of books read in the
last month by 10 pupils, or the heights (in cm) of 10 classmates.

1. Record the raw data.
2. Sort the data and compute the mean.
3. Sort the data and compute the median.
4. Compare the two. If they differ, explain why (look for outliers or skewness).
5. Write one sentence saying which measure better represents the centre of
   your data and why.

Share your findings with another pair. Discuss whether the mean or median was
more representative and why.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* State the formula for the mean in words and in symbols.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why the median is described as "robust" while the mean is "sensitive to outliers".
3. *(Apply)* The following salaries (in thousands of rupees per month) are recorded for 9 employees: 25, 28, 30, 32, 35, 38, 40, 45, 200. Compute both the mean and the median. Which measure better represents the typical salary and why?

## Summary

The mean is the arithmetic average (sum divided by count) and uses every value.
The median is the middle value when data are sorted and is robust to outliers.
For symmetric data without outliers the two are similar; for skewed data or
data with outliers the median is often more representative. Reporting both
gives the reader the fullest picture of the centre.

## Summative task

The following are the monthly incomes (in thousands of rupees) of 11 families
in a neighbourhood: 18, 22, 25, 28, 30, 32, 35, 38, 40, 45, 120.

a) Compute the mean income.
b) Compute the median income.
c) Write a short paragraph (80-120 words) explaining which measure better
   represents the typical family income in this neighbourhood and why. In your
   paragraph, identify the outlier and describe its effect on the mean.
