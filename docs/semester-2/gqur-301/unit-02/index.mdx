# Sampling and Measures of Central Tendency

Unit 1 taught you how to classify, organize and display data. This unit takes the
next step: summarizing a data set with a single number that represents its
"centre". But before you can summarize, you need to know where the data came
from. The first half of this unit covers populations and the samples drawn from
them. The second half covers the three most common measures of central
tendency - mean, median and mode - and when each one tells the most honest
story about your data.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Distinguish a population from a sample and explain why sampling is necessary.
2. Distinguish random from non-random sampling techniques and describe when each is appropriate.
3. Compute the arithmetic mean and median of a raw data set.
4. Compute the mode of a data set and identify the modality of a distribution.
5. Compare mean, median and mode for a given data set and explain which measure best represents the centre, including the effect of skewness and outliers.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 (data types, frequency distributions). You should be comfortable building
a frequency table and computing a mean from simple numbers. HSC-level arithmetic
is sufficient; no advanced mathematics is assumed.

## In this unit

1. [Population concepts](./topic-01) - what a population is and why we sample.
2. [Sampling techniques](./topic-02) - random and non-random methods.
3. [Mean and median](./topic-03) - the arithmetic average and the middle value.
4. [Mode, comparison and interpretation](./topic-04) - the most frequent value and choosing the right measure.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is one learning cycle. Start with the classroom situation, work through
the explanation, and try the activity before you move on. The "Check your
understanding" items are there to help you retrieve what you have learned - have
a go at them without looking back, then check your answer. The unit ends with a
summative assessment that draws the four topics together.
