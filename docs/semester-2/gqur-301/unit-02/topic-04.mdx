## A real classroom situation

A teacher records the favourite subject of each pupil in her class of 30: Urdu
8, Mathematics 12, Science 6, English 3, Social Studies 1. She also has the
test marks from the last exam and has already computed the mean (38.2) and the
median (37.5). Now the head teacher asks her: "What is the most popular
subject?" That is a different question, and neither the mean nor the median
can answer it. She needs a third measure.

## Explanation

### The mode: the most frequent value

The <Glossary term="Mode" /> is the value that occurs most frequently in a
data set. To find the mode:

1. **Tally** the frequency of each value (or each interval, for grouped data).
2. **Identify** the value with the highest frequency.

For the favourite subjects data: Mathematics occurs 12 times, more than any
other subject. The mode is Mathematics.

A data set can have one mode (unimodal), two modes (bimodal), or more
(multimodal). If every value occurs the same number of times, there is no
mode. The mode is the only measure of central tendency that can be used with
qualitative data - you cannot compute a mean or median for "Urdu" and
"Mathematics", but you can say which subject occurs most often.

### Mean, median and mode: the symmetry story

In a perfectly symmetric distribution, the mean, median and mode are all
equal. As a distribution becomes skewed, they diverge in a predictable way:

- **Right-skewed** (long tail to the right): mean > median > mode.
  The mean is pulled toward the high outliers.
- **Left-skewed** (long tail to the left): mean < median < mode.
  The mean is pulled toward the low outliers.

This relationship is useful because it gives you a quick check: if you compute
the three measures and find mean = 45, median = 38, mode = 32, you know the
distribution is right-skewed without drawing a graph.

### Choosing the right measure: a decision guide

| Situation | Best measure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quantitative data, symmetric, no outliers | Mean | Uses all information; basis for later inference |
| Quantitative data, skewed or with outliers | Median | Robust to extremes |
| Qualitative (categorical) data | Mode | Only measure that applies |
| You want the most typical category | Mode | Tells you what is most common |
| Income, house prices, test marks with a few extremes | Median | Prevents outliers from distorting the picture |

### Worked example: comparing all three

**Data:** test marks (out of 10) of 12 pupils: 4, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 4.

**Mode:** 7 occurs three times (most frequent). Mode = 7.

**Median:** *n* = 12 (even). Average of 6th and 7th values: (7 + 7) / 2 = 7.

**Mean:** Sum = 4 + 5 + 6 + 6 + 7 + 7 + 7 + 8 + 8 + 9 + 9 + 4 = 80. Mean = 80 / 12 = 6.67.

Here the mean (6.67) is slightly below the median and mode (both 7) because the
two low values (4, 4) pull the mean down. The distribution is slightly
left-skewed.

**Common misconception.** Many trainees think "if the data are numbers, you
should always report the mean". This is not true. For qualitative data the mode
is the only option. For skewed quantitative data the median is often more
honest. The best practice is to compute all three when possible and let the
comparison tell you about the shape of the distribution.

## Activity: Find all three measures

Work in pairs. About 15 minutes.

Use the following data set: the number of siblings of 15 pupils in a class:
0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 6, 8.

1. Compute the mean (show your work).
2. Compute the median (show your work).
3. Find the mode.
4. Compare the three values. Is the distribution roughly symmetric,
   right-skewed or left-skewed? Write one sentence explaining your reasoning.

Compare your results with another pair. If your answers differ, re-check the
calculations together.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Define the mode in your own words and explain when it is the only applicable measure of central tendency.
2. *(Understand)* A data set has mean = 25, median = 28, mode = 30. Is the distribution left-skewed or right-skewed? Explain your reasoning.
3. *(Apply)* A shopkeeper records the shoe size of the last 20 customers: 7, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10. Which measure of central tendency best represents the typical shoe size and why?

## Summary

The mode is the most frequent value and is the only measure that works for
qualitative data. In symmetric distributions mean, median and mode are equal; in
skewed distributions they diverge predictably. The best measure depends on the
data type and the shape of the distribution: mean for symmetric quantitative
data, median for skewed data, mode for categorical data or when you want the
most common value.

## Summative task

The following are the ages (in years) of 10 teachers at a school: 28, 35, 42,
31, 38, 45, 29, 33, 40, 32.

a) Compute the mean, median and mode.
b) Write a short paragraph (80-120 words) describing what the comparison of
   the three measures tells you about the shape of this distribution. Based on
   your analysis, which measure would you report as the "typical" age and why?
