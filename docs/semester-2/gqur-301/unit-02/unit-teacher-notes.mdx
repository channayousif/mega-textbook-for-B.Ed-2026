# Unit 2 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 2, which follows
Chapter 2 of the course guide (Week 2). They complement the student-facing
topics with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and practical work, in line
with the guide's teaching strategy of lectures, discussion, question-answer,
brainstorming and practical group and individual work.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 2 (first half) | 2.1 Tabular presentation of data | Building tables, cumulative frequency |
| 2 (second half) | 2.2 Graphical representation | Bar charts, histograms, pie charts and choosing between them |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 2.1 Tabular presentation of data

**Opening.** Give each student a slip with one quiz mark (use the 25 marks in
the topic). Ask the class to stand in order of their marks, then to group
themselves into intervals of 5. The physical grouping makes the need for
intervals obvious, and the count of each group is the frequency.

**Key move.** Build one table on the board with the class making each decision
(number of intervals, width, boundaries), then add the cumulative frequency
column and ask what it answers that the frequency column does not. Check every
count against the raw list: the worked example deliberately has two tied
intervals.

### Topic 2.2 Graphical representation

**Opening.** Show the same data as a bar chart, a histogram and a pie chart and
ask which one is wrong and which ones are right. The discussion leads to the
rules for choosing.

**Key move.** Emphasize the one structural difference that carries meaning: gaps
between bars for separate categories, touching bars for continuous intervals.
Have students draw all three charts for one data set.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "Frequency tables are only for small data sets" | Point forward to Unit 10: probability distributions are frequency tables extended to theoretical settings. The skill scales. |
| "A histogram and a bar chart are the same" | Draw both from the same data. The gaps in the bar chart say the categories are separate; the touching bars of the histogram say the scale is continuous. |
| "A pie chart suits any data" | Show a pie chart with eight or more slices and ask what it communicates. Pie charts work only for a few parts of one whole. |

## Practical work

1. **Build a frequency table together (Topic 2.1).** Give a raw data set of 25-30 values; as a class, sort, agree on intervals, tally and count, recording the table on the board.
2. **Chart station rotation (Topic 2.2).** Three stations, one per chart type; groups spend 10 minutes at each drawing the chart from a provided data set, then compare what each chart emphasized.

## Assessment guidance

"Check your understanding" items for formative assessment during teaching. The
