# Unit 2 teacher notes

These notes are for the instructor delivering GQUR-301 Unit 2. They complement
the student-facing materials with teaching strategies, likely difficulties and
practical work suggestions.

## Unit overview

Unit 2 covers the foundation of statistical inference: where data come from
(populations and samples) and how to summarise a data set with a single number
(mean, median, mode). The four topics are designed to be taught in order, each
building on the previous one.

## Sequencing suggestion

| Week(s) | Topic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3.1 Population concepts | Population, sample, parameter, statistic |
| 4 | 3.2 Sampling techniques | Random and non-random methods |
| 5 (first half) | 4.1 Mean and median | Computation and comparison |
| 5 (second half) | 5.1 Mode, comparison and interpretation | Mode and choosing the right measure |

## Teaching strategies

### Topic 3.1 - Population concepts

**Opening.** Pose a concrete question: "How would you find out the average
class size in all government schools in this district?" Let students discuss
the options. Most will suggest visiting all schools; challenge them with the
cost and time. The tension motivates sampling.

**Key move.** Emphasise the four terms (population, sample, parameter,
statistic) and the direction of inference. Have students apply the terms to
three short scenarios before giving any definition.

### Topic 3.2 - Sampling techniques

**Opening.** Describe the three colleagues from the opening situation and ask:
"Which colleague's method would give the most representative group?" The
discussion leads naturally to the distinction between random and non-random.

**Key move.** Use the three-question rule (sampling frame? practical? subgroups?)
as a decision tool. Have students work through three scenarios, choosing a
method each time.

### Topic 4.1 - Mean and median

**Opening.** Give the data set from the opening situation and ask students to
compute the "average". Most will compute the mean (14.9). Then ask: "Does 14.9
represent how the class did?" The discomfort motivates the median.

**Key move.** Build one worked example on the board together, computing both
mean and median. Then change one value to an extreme outlier and recompute.
The contrast makes the robustness of the median memorable.

### Topic 5.1 - Mode, comparison and interpretation

**Opening.** Ask: "What is the most popular subject in this class?" This
cannot be answered with the mean or median. The need for a third measure is
immediate.

**Key move.** Show three distributions (symmetric, right-skewed, left-skewed)
and have students compute all three measures for each. The pattern
(mean = median = mode in symmetric; diverging in skewed) emerges from the data.

## Common misconceptions and how to address them

| Misconception | How to address |
|---|---|
| "A bigger population needs a bigger sample" | Show that a sample of 400 gives similar precision for populations of 10,000 and 10 million. |
| "The mean is always the best average" | Use a skewed income data set to show the median is more representative. |
| "Random sampling means haphazard or informal" | Emphasise that random means a defined probabilistic procedure, not casual selection. |
| "The median is just the middle number without calculation" | Show the full procedure: sort, count, find position(s), average if even. |
| "Mode only matters for qualitative data" | Point out that mode is also useful for identifying the most frequent quantitative value. |

## Practical work

1. **Sampling frame exercise (Topic 3.1-3.2).** Ask each student to identify a
   population they could study at their practicum school, then describe how
   they would draw a sample of 20. Share and critique in the next session.

2. **Compute mean and median from real marks (Topic 4.1).** Give each pair a
   small data set of 10-15 test marks. Have them compute both measures and
   explain any difference.

3. **Three-measures comparison (Topic 5.1).** Give three data sets
   (symmetric, right-skewed, left-skewed). Each group computes all three
   measures for one data set, then the class compares results.

## Assessment guidance

The unit assessment (10 MCQ / 10 RRQ / 5 ERQ) is summative. Use the "Check your
understanding" items in each topic for formative assessment during teaching.
The practicum tasks ("Try this at your practicum school") bridge the classroom
and the placement school.

## Materials needed

- Small data sets on slips of paper for the mean/median activity
- Three prepared data sets (different shapes) for the three-measures comparison
- A real school list or register for the sampling frame exercise
