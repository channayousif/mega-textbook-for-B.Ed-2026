## Unit summary

This unit gave you the language and tools to describe the centre of a data
set and to understand where data come from.

- Topic 3.1 defined population, sample, parameter and statistic, and explained
  why sampling is necessary.
- Topic 3.2 covered random sampling (simple, systematic, stratified) and
  non-random sampling (convenience, purposive, quota), with the three-question
  rule for choosing a method.
- Topic 3.3 showed how to compute the mean and median, and explained when the
  median is preferred because it is robust to outliers.
- Topic 3.4 introduced the mode and showed how the relationship between mean,
  median and mode reveals the shape of a distribution.

Together these give you the foundation for inference: you can now draw a sample,
compute a statistic, and choose the measure that most honestly represents your
data.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following is a parameter?
   - A) The mean of a sample of 30 pupils
   - B) The true average height of all pupils in the school
   - C) The median of a data set
   - D) The mode of a sample

2. *(Remember)* In which sampling method does every member of the population
   have an equal chance of selection?
   - A) Convenience sampling
   - B) Purposive sampling
   - C) Simple random sampling
   - D) Quota sampling

3. *(Understand)* A teacher computes the mean and median of her class's test
   marks. The mean is much lower than the median. What does this most
   suggest about the data?
   - A) The data are symmetric
   - B) There are some very low marks pulling the mean down
   - C) There are some very high marks pulling the mean up
   - D) The median was calculated incorrectly

4. *(Understand)* Why is the median described as "robust" while the mean is
   "sensitive to outliers"?
   - A) The median uses more data points than the mean
   - B) The median is not affected by extreme values because it depends only on the middle position
   - C) The mean is always wrong when there are outliers
   - D) The median is easier to calculate

5. *(Apply)* A researcher wants to estimate the average family size in a
   district with both urban and rural areas. She believes family size differs
   by area. Which sampling method is most appropriate?
   - A) Convenience sampling
   - B) Simple random sampling
   - C) Stratified sampling (stratum = urban / rural)
   - D) Purposive sampling

6. *(Apply)* The following data set is recorded: 5, 7, 8, 8, 9, 10, 45.
   Which measure of central tendency best represents the typical value?
   - A) Mean
   - B) Median
   - C) Mode
   - D) All three are equally representative

7. *(Apply)* A data set has mean = 42, median = 38, mode = 35. What can you
   conclude about the shape of the distribution?
   - A) It is symmetric
   - B) It is right-skewed (tail to the right)
   - C) It is left-skewed (tail to the left)
   - D) It is bimodal

8. *(Apply)* Which measure of central tendency can be used with qualitative
   (categorical) data?
   - A) Mean
   - B) Median
   - C) Mode
   - D) Mean and median

9. *(Apply)* A quality inspector tests 20 light bulbs from a batch of 2,000
   to estimate the percentage that fail. The 20 bulbs are the:
   - A) Population
   - B) Parameter
   - C) Sample
   - D) Statistic

10. *(Apply)* A teacher selects every 5th pupil from an alphabetical list of
    100 pupils. This is an example of:
    - A) Simple random sampling
    - B) Systematic sampling
    - C) Stratified sampling
    - D) Convenience sampling

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in a few sentences or a short paragraph.

1. *(Remember)* State the difference between a population and a sample in your
   own words, and give one example of each from a school context.

2. *(Remember)* Define parameter and statistic. Explain how they are related.

3. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between stratified sampling and quota
   sampling. Which one is random and why?

4. *(Understand)* A data set has mean = 50 and median = 45. Explain what this
   tells you about the shape of the distribution.

5. *(Apply)* The following marks (out of 30) were obtained by 8 pupils: 18, 22,
   25, 19, 21, 23, 20, 4. Compute the mean and median. Show your working.

6. *(Apply)* A researcher wants to study the study habits of university
   students in Sindh. She has no complete list of all students. Explain which
   sampling method she can and cannot use, and why.

7. *(Apply)* Find the mode of the following data set: 7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10,
   10, 10, 11. What is the modality of this distribution?

8. *(Apply)* Explain why the mean is sensitive to outliers but the median is
   not. Use a small numerical example (5-6 values) to illustrate.

9. *(Analyze)* A newspaper reports that the "average household income in Sindh
   is Rs 45,000 per month". Explain why this figure might overstate the
   typical income and what additional information you would want before
   accepting the claim.

10. *(Analyze)* Two teachers compute the mean of the same class's test marks.
    Teacher A gets 42. Teacher B gets 38. Both are correct. Explain one
    possible reason for the difference (consider what each teacher might have
    included or excluded from the data).

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a structured paragraph or set of paragraphs.

1. *(Analyze)* A researcher surveys 100 households in a village of 500 to
   estimate the average number of children per household. She uses convenience
   sampling (interviewing households near the main road).
   a) Identify the population, sample, parameter and statistic.
   b) Evaluate the sampling method: one strength and one limitation.
   c) Recommend a better method and explain why it would be better.
   (Write 120-180 words.)

2. *(Analyze)* The following are the weekly wages (in thousands of rupees) of
   12 workers at a small factory: 18, 20, 22, 24, 25, 26, 28, 30, 32, 35,
   40, 85.
   a) Compute the mean, median and mode.
   b) Write one paragraph (80-120 words) explaining which measure best
      represents the typical worker's wage and why. Identify the outlier and
      describe its effect on the mean.

3. *(Evaluate)* A head teacher claims that "our school's average test score is
   75 out of 100, which is the highest in the district." On investigation, you
   find that the school has 200 pupils, but the "average" was computed from
   only the top 20 pupils' marks. Evaluate this claim by identifying the
   sampling problem, explaining how it distorts the result, and stating what
   the honest figure would require. (Write 120-180 words.)

4. *(Evaluate)* Consider the following statement from a research report: "We
   used a sample of 30 schools, which is 10 per cent of the 300 schools in
   the district, so our results are representative." Evaluate this claim by
   explaining whether the fraction of schools sampled determines
   representativeness, what matters more, and what additional information
   you would need to judge the claim. (Write 120-180 words.)

5. *(Create)* You are asked to conduct a small study at your practicum school
   to find out how many hours pupils spend on homework each week.
   a) Describe the population and the parameter of interest.
   b) Recommend a sampling method and explain your choice.
   c) Describe how you would collect the data and which measure of central
      tendency you would use to summarise it and why. (Write 150-200 words.)

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

| Item | Answer | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | The true average of all pupils is a population parameter. |
| 2 | C | Simple random sampling gives every member an equal chance. |
| 3 | B | The mean is pulled toward low outliers, making it lower than the median. |
| 4 | B | The median depends only on the middle position, not the extreme values. |
| 5 | C | Stratified sampling by urban/rural captures the known subgroup difference. |
| 6 | B | The median is robust to the outlier (45). |
| 7 | B | Mean > median > mode indicates a right-skewed distribution. |
| 8 | C | The mode is the only measure applicable to qualitative data. |
| 9 | C | The 20 bulbs are the subset tested; they constitute the sample. |
| 10 | B | Selecting every kth member from a list is systematic sampling. |

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Population is the complete group of interest; sample is the subset actually measured. Example of population: all pupils in a school. Example of sample: 30 pupils selected for a survey. (2 marks: 1 for the distinction, 1 for valid examples.)

2. A parameter is a numerical summary of a population (usually unknown); a statistic is a numerical summary of a sample (used to estimate the parameter). (2 marks: 1 for each definition.)

3. Stratified sampling divides the population into strata and draws a random sample from each; quota sampling fills predetermined quotas by any means. Stratified sampling is random because the selection within each stratum is probabilistic; quota sampling is not. (2 marks: 1 for the distinction, 1 for identifying which is random.)

4. The mean (50) is higher than the median (45), suggesting a right-skewed distribution: some high values are pulling the mean up. (2 marks: 1 for right-skewed, 1 for the reasoning.)

5. Sorted: 4, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 25. Sum = 152. Mean = 152 / 8 = 19. Median = (20 + 21) / 2 = 20.5. (3 marks: 1 for correct mean, 1 for correct median, 1 for showing working.)

6. She cannot use simple random or stratified sampling because she has no sampling frame (complete list of students). She could use convenience sampling (easiest but least representative) or purposive sampling (selecting specific types of students relevant to her question). (3 marks: 1 for why random methods are unavailable, 1 for naming a feasible method, 1 for the reason.)

7. Both 9 and 10 occur three times. The data set is bimodal (modes: 9 and 10). (2 marks: 1 for identifying both modes, 1 for bimodal.)

8. Example: data set 10, 12, 14, 16, 18. Mean = 14, median = 14. Add an outlier: 10, 12, 14, 16, 100. Mean = 30.4, median = 14. The mean is pulled up by the outlier; the median stays at the middle position. (2 marks: 1 for the correct example, 1 for the correct explanation.)

9. The figure might overstate the typical income because the mean is sensitive to high outliers (a few very high incomes pull the mean up). The median would be more representative. I would want to know whether the report used the mean or median, the distribution shape, and the sample size. (3 marks: 1 for the outlier explanation, 1 for suggesting the median, 1 for naming additional information needed.)

10. One possible reason: Teacher A included all pupils (including absentees who scored 0), lowering the mean. Teacher B excluded absentees. Alternatively, Teacher A included an outlier (a very low score) that Teacher B excluded. (2 marks: 1 for a plausible explanation, 1 for the reasoning.)
