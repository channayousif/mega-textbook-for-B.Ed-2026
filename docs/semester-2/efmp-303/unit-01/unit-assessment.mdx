## Unit summary

This unit built a picture of Pakistan's education system through four topics.

- Topic 1.1 defined educational policy as a statement of intent and educational planning as the process
  of working out how to achieve it. Policy is the "what"; planning is the "how."
- Topic 1.2 showed the triangle between education, society and development: each shapes and is shaped
  by the others.
- Topic 1.3 traced education from the colonial period through the early decades after independence,
  showing what changed and what persisted.
- Topic 1.4 placed the key milestones in chronological order, revealing patterns of policy response
  and structural persistence.

Together these give you a foundation: you can now explain where Pakistan's education system came
from, what its key turning points were and why policy and planning matter.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following best describes educational policy?
   - A) A step-by-step plan for building schools
   - B) A statement of what the education system aims to achieve
   - C) A list of teacher salaries
   - D) A record of examination results

2. *(Remember)* What was the literacy rate in the territory that became Pakistan at independence?
   - A) About 50 per cent
   - B) About 35 per cent
   - C) About 15 per cent
   - D) About 5 per cent

3. *(Understand)* What is the main difference between educational policy and educational planning?
   - A) Policy is written by teachers; planning is written by government
   - B) Policy sets out what is to be done; planning sets out how, when and by whom
   - C) Policy is about schools; planning is about universities
   - D) Policy and planning are the same thing

4. *(Understand)* How does society shape education, as described in this unit?
   - A) Society has no influence on education
   - B) Through language norms, gender expectations and economic conditions that affect who goes to school
   - C) Only through government funding
   - D) Only through examination systems

5. *(Apply)* A new policy says all primary schools must teach a second language. Which is a planning question that must be answered?
   - A) What colour should the school building be
   - B) How many additional language teachers must be recruited and trained
   - C) What the school's motto should say
   - D) Who designed the policy document

6. *(Apply)* Which milestone introduced the grade structure (primary, middle, secondary, higher secondary) that still shapes Pakistan's schools?
   - A) The 1947 Education Conference
   - B) The 1959 Sharif Commission
   - C) The 2009 National Education Policy
   - D) The 1992 Education Policy

7. *(Apply)* A father says, "My daughter will not benefit from school because no educated woman from
   our village has found work." Using the education-society-development triangle, what is the father
   questioning?
   - A) The cost of school uniforms
   - B) Whether education actually delivers the development benefits it promises
   - C) Whether the school building is safe
   - D) Whether the teacher is qualified

8. *(Apply)* Which of the following was a feature of colonial education that Pakistan inherited?
   - A) A system designed for mass literacy
   - B) Urdu as the medium of all education
   - C) An examination system modelled on the British system
   - D) Free and compulsory education for all

9. *(Apply)* The timeline of milestones shows that many policies name the same problems. What does this pattern suggest?
   - A) Policies are always successful
   - B) Naming a problem in a policy does not guarantee it is solved
   - C) Education policy is not important
   - D) Problems disappear on their own

10. *(Apply)* Which milestone nationalised private schools and colleges, expanding government control over education?
    - A) 1970 National Education Policy
    - B) 1972 Education Policy
    - C) 1998 National Education Policy
    - D) 2017 National Education Policy

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between educational policy and educational planning, using the idea of "what" and "how".

2. *(Understand)* Name three milestones from Pakistan's education history between 1947 and 2000, with their years, and explain why one of them was significant.

3. *(Apply)* Give one example of how society shapes education in Pakistan and one example of how education shapes society.

4. *(Apply)* The Sharif Commission recommended a specific grade structure. What was it, and why does it still matter today?

5. *(Analyze)* Explain one reason why many Pakistan education policies have named the same problems (low literacy, gender gaps) without solving them. Give one specific example.

6. *(Understand)* What was the main purpose of colonial education, and how did the 1947 Education Conference try to change that purpose?

7. *(Apply)* A policy says "all children will complete primary school by 2030." List three planning questions that must be answered to make this policy work.

8. *(Understand)* Name two features of the colonial education system that persisted after independence.

9. *(Analyze)* Compare the 1972 and 1979 education policies. What was different about their main focus?

10. *(Apply)* Why does the unit describe the education-society-development relationship as a "triangle"? Use one example in your answer.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (100-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit defined educational policy as a "statement of intent" and educational planning
   as the "how." Using a real or imagined Pakistani example, explain why policy without planning is
   unlikely to produce results. Give a specific example in your answer.

2. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the statement: "Pakistan built an entirely new education system after
   independence." Using evidence from Topics 1.3 and 1.4, explain what changed and what stayed the
   same. State whether the statement is accurate and why.

3. *(Analyze)* A rural district in Sindh has low girls' enrolment. Using the
   education-society-development triangle, analyse two social factors that may be contributing to
   this, and explain one way education could help address the situation.

4. *(Analyze)* The timeline of milestones shows that Pakistan has produced many education policies.
   Analyse one pattern this timeline reveals and explain what it tells us about the relationship
   between policy announcements and actual change in the education system.

5. *(Create/Evaluate)* Choose one milestone from Pakistan's education history. Evaluate its
   significance: what it changed, what it failed to change, and how it connects to the unit's idea
   that policy and planning must work together. Draw on at least two topics from the unit.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - A statement of what the education system aims to achieve. A describes planning, C and D are unrelated.
2. **C** - About 15 per cent. The unit states literacy in 1947 was roughly 15 per cent across the territory that became Pakistan.
3. **B** - Policy sets out what is to be done; planning sets out how, when and by whom. A, C and D misrepresent the distinction.
4. **B** - Through language norms, gender expectations and economic conditions. A, C and D are incorrect or incomplete.
5. **B** - How many additional language teachers must be recruited and trained. This is a planning question about resources and implementation.
6. **B** - The 1959 Sharif Commission. It recommended the 5-3-2-2 grade structure that still exists in modified form.
7. **B** - Whether education actually delivers the development benefits it promises. The father is questioning the education-development link.
8. **C** - An examination system modelled on the British system. A is wrong (colonial education was not for mass literacy); B is wrong (English was the higher-level medium); D is wrong (free and compulsory was a goal, not a colonial feature).
9. **B** - Naming a problem in a policy does not guarantee it is solved. This is the pattern the timeline reveals.
10. **B** - 1972 Education Policy. Under Bhutto, private schools and colleges were nationalised, expanding government control.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Educational policy states what the system aims to achieve (the "what"). Educational planning sets out how to achieve it - the actions, timeline, budget and responsibilities (the "how"). **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies policy as "what"; 1 - identifies planning as "how".

2. **Model:** Any three of: 1947 Education Conference, 1959 Sharif Commission, 1970 National Education Policy, 1972 Education Policy, 1979 Education Policy, 1992 Education Policy, 1998 National Education Policy. Each with correct year. **Marks (3):** 1 per milestone with correct year (max 3).

3. **Model:** Society shapes education example: gender norms determining whether girls attend school. Education shapes society example: educated mothers having healthier children. **Marks (2):** 1 per relevant example.

4. **Model:** The Sharif Commission recommended 5 years primary, 3 years middle, 2 years secondary, 2 years higher secondary. It still matters because this structure is the basis of the current school system. **Marks (2):** 1 - states the structure; 1 - explains its present significance.

5. **Model:** Policies name problems but planning and implementation often fail. Example: multiple policies have named low literacy, yet literacy rates remain low in many areas because of insufficient schools, teacher shortages or social barriers. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the pattern; 1 - explains why; 1 - gives a specific example.

6. **Model:** Colonial education aimed to produce clerks and administrators for the colonial government. The 1947 Conference called for free and compulsory primary education, a shift to national languages and an Islamic orientation. **Marks (2):** 1 - colonial purpose; 1 - 1947 Conference change.

7. **Model:** Any three planning questions: How many schools are needed? How many teachers must be trained? What will it cost? Where will the schools be built? What is the timeline? **Marks (3):** 1 per relevant planning question (max 3).

8. **Model:** Any two of: the examination system, the hierarchical school structure, the language hierarchy privileging English. **Marks (2):** 1 per correct feature.

9. **Model:** The 1972 policy focused on nationalisation and equitable access. The 1979 policy focused on Islamization of the curriculum and Islamic values. **Marks (2):** 1 - 1972 focus; 1 - 1979 focus.

10. **Model:** A triangle has three connected sides. Education, society and development each influence the other two. Example: society's language norms shape the medium of instruction (society affects education), and education builds human capital that drives economic growth (education affects development). **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the triangle; 1 - gives an example.
