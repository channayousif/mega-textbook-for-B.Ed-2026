## Unit summary

This unit looked outward and forward across three topics.

- Topic 6.1 compared Pakistan's education indicators with those of Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Nepal, showing that specific access policies, gender-focused interventions and efficient spending produce results. It introduced SDG4 and its relevance to Pakistan's policy framework.
- Topic 6.2 examined how ICT in education management (especially EMIS), data-driven planning and digital transformation are changing the tools available to planners, while emphasising that technology supports but does not replace planning.
- Topic 6.3 explored innovations in policy design (evidence-based, participatory, iterative, cross-sector), strategic planning for quality and equity, and the role of teachers and administrators as policy actors who interpret, feedback and adapt.

Together these topics close the course by connecting Pakistan's education planning to global evidence, new tools and the people who make policies work in practice.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which country achieved near-universal primary enrolment through community schools and female teacher recruitment?
   - A) Sri Lanka
   - B) Bangladesh
   - C) Nepal
   - D) India

2. *(Remember)* What does SDG4 aim to achieve by 2030?
   - A) Universal military service
   - B) Inclusive and equitable quality education for all
   - C) Free university education only
   - D) A single global curriculum

3. *(Understand)* What is the primary function of an Education Management Information System (EMIS)?
   - A) To replace teachers with computers
   - B) To collect, store and report education data for planning
   - C) To write education policies
   - D) To train teachers in ICT skills

4. *(Understand)* Why does the topic describe Sri Lanka's education system as efficient?
   - A) It spends the most money per pupil in South Asia
   - B) It achieves strong outcomes through efficient teacher deployment and free education despite modest spending
   - C) It has the smallest population
   - D) It does not collect education data

5. *(Apply)* A district's EMIS shows that girls' enrolment drops by 30 per cent after class 5. Using data-driven planning, what is the most appropriate first step?
   - A) Ignore the data because enrolment is a provincial matter
   - B) Investigate the specific transition point and plan a targeted intervention
   - C) Build a new university in the district
   - D) Reduce the number of male teachers

6. *(Apply)* A teacher receives a new curriculum policy and realises it does not work for her multi-grade classroom. She adjusts the grouping strategy while keeping the learning objectives. Which role from Topic 6.3 is she demonstrating?
   - A) Interpreter
   - B) Adapter
   - C) Feedback provider
   - D) Policy writer

7. *(Apply)* Which innovation in policy design means setting shorter planning cycles and adjusting based on evidence?
   - A) Top-down design
   - B) Iterative planning
   - C) Political planning
   - D) Static planning

8. *(Apply)* A developing country succeeds with a school feeding programme. A Pakistani planner wants to copy it exactly. What principle from Topic 6.1 should guide the planner?
   - A) Copy exactly; what works there will work here
   - B) Adapt, not transplant; policies must fit local conditions
   - C) Wait until the policy has worked for 50 years
   - D) Reject all international ideas

9. *(Apply)* Which of the following best describes the relationship between technology and planning, as discussed in Topic 6.2?
   - A) Technology replaces the need for planning
   - B) Technology supports planning but does not replace human decisions
   - C) Technology is irrelevant to planning
   - D) Technology makes planning slower

10. *(Apply)* Strategic planning for equity requires disaggregated data. What does "disaggregated" mean in this context?
     - A) Data broken down by district, gender or school type rather than national totals only
     - B) Data that has been discarded
     - C) Data collected only from urban schools
     - D) Data that has not been checked for errors

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* Explain one education policy result achieved by each of two developing countries discussed in Topic 6.1.

2. *(Understand)* Explain three management functions that ICT supports in education planning and why each matters.

3. *(Understand)* Explain why the topic says policies must be "adapted, not transplanted." Give one example.

4. *(Understand)* What is the difference between data-driven planning and planning based on assumption? Use one example.

5. *(Apply)* A province wants to improve girls' secondary enrolment. Drawing on one lesson from Topic 6.1, suggest one policy action and explain why it might work.

6. *(Apply)* Describe two ways a teacher acts as a policy actor (not just an implementer), using ideas from Topic 6.3.

7. *(Analyze)* Explain why technology is described as "a tool, not a substitute" for planning. Use one example from Topic 6.2.

8. *(Understand)* What does SDG4 target, and how does it connect to Pakistan's National Education Policy 2017?

9. *(Analyze)* Compare evidence-based policy design with traditional top-down design. What is the key difference?

10. *(Apply)* A school's EMIS data shows that teacher absenteeism is highest on Fridays. Using data-driven planning, suggest one planning action this data could support.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (100-140 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit argued that teachers are policy actors, not just implementers. Analyse two ways teachers influence policy outcomes, using examples from Topic 6.3 or your own experience. Explain why treating teachers only as implementers limits policy success.

2. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the statement: "Technology will solve Pakistan's education planning problems." Using evidence from Topic 6.2, explain what technology can and cannot do for planning. State whether the statement is accurate and why.

3. *(Analyze)* A Pakistani district wants to learn from Bangladesh's community school model. Analyse two conditions that would need to be in place for this policy transfer to succeed, drawing on the "adapted, not transplanted" principle from Topic 6.1.

4. *(Create/Evaluate)* Propose one innovation in education policy design for Pakistan (evidence-based, participative, iterative or cross-sector). Explain what it would involve, why it is needed, and one challenge in implementing it. Draw on at least two ideas from this unit.

5. *(Evaluate)* This course covered policy (Unit 2), planning (Unit 3), monitoring (Unit 4), quality (Unit 5) and international perspectives (Unit 6). Evaluate which of these themes is most important for improving Pakistan's education system in the next decade. Give two reasons for your choice and one reason against. Draw on at least three units in your answer (200-250 words).

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Bangladesh. It invested in community schools and female teacher recruitment, raising girls' enrolment.
2. **B** - Inclusive and equitable quality education for all. SDG4 commits countries to this by 2030.
3. **B** - To collect, store and report education data for planning. EMIS is a data system, not a teaching tool or policy-writing body.
4. **B** - It achieves strong outcomes through efficient teacher deployment and free education despite modest spending. Efficiency, not spending level, is the point.
5. **B** - Investigate the specific transition point and plan a targeted intervention. Data-driven planning targets the specific problem the data reveals.
6. **B** - Adapter. The teacher adjusts the policy to her multi-grade context while keeping the learning objectives.
7. **B** - Iterative planning. Shorter cycles with evidence-based adjustment are the definition.
8. **B** - Adapt, not transplant; policies must fit local conditions. This is the principle Topic 6.1 emphasises.
9. **B** - Technology supports planning but does not replace human decisions. This is the "tool, not a substitute" principle.
10. **A** - Data broken down by district, gender or school type rather than national totals only. Disaggregation reveals variation that national averages hide.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Bangladesh: near-universal primary enrolment through community schools and female teachers. Sri Lanka: high literacy through free education and efficient teacher deployment. Nepal: improved remote enrolment through cluster schools and girls' scholarships. **Marks (2):** 1 per country with correct result (max 2).

2. **Model:** Any three of: enrolment tracking, teacher deployment, budget allocation, monitoring and evaluation, policy feedback. **Marks (2):** 1 per correct function (max 2).

3. **Model:** Policies must be adapted to local social, cultural and institutional conditions rather than copied exactly. Example: Bangladesh's community school model worked because of dense populations, a strong NGO sector and cultural acceptance of female teachers; these conditions may not exist everywhere in Pakistan. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the principle; 1 - gives an example.

4. **Model:** Data-driven planning uses evidence (EMIS data, survey results) to identify problems and set targets, whereas planning based on assumption relies on habit, political pressure or guesswork. Example: a district using EMIS data to target falling enrolment in specific union councils versus a district that allocates funds based on political influence. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the difference; 1 - gives an example.

5. **Model:** Drawing on Bangladesh's lesson: recruit female teachers from local communities and establish community-based schools or satellite campuses near where girls live. This works because distance and lack of female staff are key barriers to girls' secondary attendance. **Marks (2):** 1 - suggests a relevant action; 1 - explains why it might work.

6. **Model:** Two of: interpretation (teacher understands and explains the policy's purpose), feedback (teacher reports what works and what fails), adaptation (teacher adjusts the policy to fit the local classroom context). **Marks (2):** 1 per role correctly described (max 2).

7. **Model:** Technology provides faster, more detailed data and supports management functions, but it does not make planning decisions. Example: an EMIS dashboard shows falling enrolment, but a human planner must decide what to do about it. Technology is the tool; planning is the human work. **Marks (2):** 1 - explains the principle; 1 - gives an example.

8. **Model:** SDG4 targets inclusive and equitable quality education and lifelong learning for all by 2030. It connects to Pakistan's NEP 2017 through shared commitments: free and compulsory education, improved quality, gender equity and expanded technical education. **Marks (2):** 1 - states SDG4 target; 1 - links to NEP 2017.

9. **Model:** Evidence-based design uses local data to identify problems and test solutions, whereas traditional top-down design relies on central ministry decisions without systematic local evidence. The key difference is the role of data in shaping the policy. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the key difference; 1 - explains it.

10. **Model:** The data could support a planning action such as adjusting the school timetable (moving key classes to other days), increasing monitoring on Fridays, or providing incentives for Friday attendance. The point is that the data identifies a pattern that a plan can address. **Marks (2):** 1 - suggests a relevant action; 1 - links it to the data.
