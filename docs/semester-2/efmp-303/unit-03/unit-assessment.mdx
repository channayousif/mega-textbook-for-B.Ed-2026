## Unit summary

This unit examined how development plans connect to education and how the planning process works.

- Topic 3.1 showed that development plans are multi-sector documents with education chapters that
  set targets and budgets. Provinces lead education development with federal support, and provincial roles also existed before 2010. Education links to economic growth through human capital,
  but the link depends on quality, not just spending.
- Topic 3.2 defined educational planning as the process of turning goals into actions with resources
  and timelines. Planning operates at two levels of scale: micro and macro. Strategic planning is a
  focused, time-bound, outcome-oriented approach that can operate at either level. It follows six
  stages from needs assessment through monitoring and evaluation.
- Topic 3.3 examined the planning process in detail: needs assessment as the foundation, resource
  allocation as the concrete step, and planning models (rational, iterative, participatory,
  strategic) as the frameworks that guide the process.

Together these give you the vocabulary and tools to understand how Pakistan's education system is
planned, why some plans succeed and others fail, and what role different levels of planning play.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* What is a development plan?
   - A) A document that states only education policy goals
   - B) A multi-sector document setting economic and social goals with budgets for a fixed period
   - C) A school-level timetable for the academic year
   - D) A report on past education performance

2. *(Remember)* What does educational planning involve?
   - A) Only writing policy documents
   - B) Diagnosing conditions, setting goals, identifying resources and designing actions to achieve education outcomes
   - C) Only building school infrastructure
   - D) Only recruiting teachers

3. *(Understand)* What is the main difference between a development plan and an education policy?
   - A) There is no difference; they are the same
   - B) A policy states what the system should achieve; a plan states how much will be spent, on what, and by when
   - C) A policy is only for schools; a plan is only for universities
   - D) A policy is written by teachers; a plan is written by government

4. *(Understand)* What is the key difference between micro planning and macro planning?
   - A) Micro planning is about money; macro planning is about people
   - B) Micro planning operates at the school level; macro planning operates at the district, provincial or national level
   - C) Micro planning is done by the federal government; macro planning is done by schools
   - D) There is no difference

5. *(Apply)* A province wants to improve literacy. Which is the first stage of the planning process it should follow?
   - A) Build 500 new schools immediately
   - B) Conduct a needs assessment to understand current literacy levels and gaps
   - C) Recruit 10,000 new teachers without knowing where they are needed
   - D) Request funding from the federal government

6. *(Apply)* A five-year plan must implement a longer-term Vision literacy target. Which action best connects the two?
   - A) Copy the target without a deadline or costs
   - B) Set costed annual literacy milestones within the five-year period
   - C) Treat the Vision target as proof of achievement
   - D) Replace the budget with a slogan

7. *(Apply)* A head teacher prepares a plan to improve science results in her school. What type of planning is this?
   - A) Macro planning
   - B) Micro planning
   - C) Federal planning
   - D) National planning

8. *(Apply)* A school has an approved building allocation but procurement has stalled. Which problem should the planner address first?
   - A) Excess funding in every school
   - B) The barrier preventing utilisation of the allocation
   - C) The absence of any competing need
   - D) A surplus of completed classrooms

9. *(Understand)* The rational planning model is best described as:
   - A) A cyclical process with continuous feedback
   - B) A linear sequence from problem analysis through implementation to evaluation
   - C) A model that involves only community members
   - D) A model that ignores data

10. *(Remember)* Which Constitutional Amendment strengthened provincial responsibility for education through devolution?
    - A) 16th Amendment
    - B) 17th Amendment
    - C) 18th Amendment
    - D) 19th Amendment

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between a development plan and an education policy, using the idea of "what" and "how much/when."

2. *(Understand)* Name the six stages of the educational planning process in order, and explain why needs assessment must come first.

3. *(Apply)* A district education authority has data showing low girls' enrolment. Describe the first three stages of the planning process the authority should follow.

4. *(Apply)* For each scenario, identify the level of planning (micro or macro) and state whether
   it also shows strategic planning (focused, time-bound, outcome-oriented). Explain your reasoning in
   one sentence each.
   - A provincial plan to recruit 30,000 teachers over five years.
   - A school's plan to improve attendance by building a boundary wall.
   - A district's two-year strategy to improve learning outcomes in the ten lowest-performing schools.

5. *(Analyze)* Explain why increased education spending does not automatically produce better education outcomes in Pakistan. Give one specific example.

6. *(Understand)* What does resource allocation involve, and name two challenges it faces in Pakistan's education sector.

7. *(Apply)* After the 18th Amendment, who is responsible for education planning in each province? How did this change from the pre-2010 system?

8. *(Understand)* What is the difference between the rational planning model and the iterative planning model?

9. *(Analyze)* Compare micro planning and strategic planning. What makes them different, and when would each be appropriate?

10. *(Apply)* Give one example of how education spending in Pakistan links to economic growth. Use a specific type of education in your answer.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (120-160 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* A development plan promises to build 1,000 new schools in Balochistan within five years.
   After three years, only 400 have been completed. Analyse two reasons why the plan may be falling
   short, using what you have learned about development plans and resource allocation.

2. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the statement: "Macro planning is only the federal government's job." Using
   evidence from Topic 3.1 and 3.2, explain how planning responsibilities changed after the 18th
   Amendment. State whether the statement is accurate and why.

3. *(Analyze)* A rural district in Punjab has low learning outcomes in primary schools. Using one
   of the planning models from Topic 3.3, walk the district through the first four stages of the
   planning process. Explain why the model you chose suits this scenario.

4. *(Analyze)* Analyse how Pakistan's strengthened provincial planning alongside national development
   plans has affected education planning. What are the advantages and one challenge of this shift?
   Use specific examples.

5. *(Create/Evaluate)* You are asked to design a school improvement plan for a government school with
   low enrolment, poor learning outcomes and teacher absenteeism. Drawing on all three topics in this
   unit, outline: the type of planning you would use, what a needs assessment would involve, how
   resources should be allocated, and which planning model would guide your approach. Write 150-200
   words.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - A multi-sector document setting economic and social goals with budgets for a fixed period. A is wrong (development plans are multi-sector, not only education). C is wrong (timetables are not development plans). D is wrong (development plans are forward-looking, not reports on the past).
2. **B** - Diagnosing conditions, setting goals, identifying resources and designing actions. A, C and D describe isolated activities, not the full planning process.
3. **B** - A policy states what the system should achieve; a plan states how much will be spent, on what, and by when. A is wrong (they are different). C and D are wrong (both are misleading distinctions).
4. **B** - Micro planning operates at the school level; macro planning at the district, provincial or national level. A, C and D are incorrect.
5. **B** - Conduct a needs assessment. This is the first stage. A, C and D skip needs assessment and jump to implementation without data.
6. **B** - Costed annual milestones turn a longer-term aspiration into a five-year implementation programme. A omits feasibility; C confuses intent with achievement; D supplies no actions or resources.
7. **B** - Micro planning. A head teacher planning for her own school is doing micro planning.
8. **B** - The stated problem is utilisation: procurement blocks use of the allocation. A, C and D are unsupported by the scenario.
9. **B** - A linear sequence from problem analysis through implementation to evaluation. A describes the iterative model. C and D are incorrect.
10. **C** - 18th Amendment. The 18th Constitutional Amendment (2010) strengthened provincial responsibility through devolution; provinces already had education roles before 2010.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** A development plan states how much money will be spent on education, on what priorities, and by when (the "how much" and "when"). An education policy states what the education system should achieve and the principles it will follow (the "what"). **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies development plan as how much/when; 1 - identifies policy as what.

2. **Model:** (1) Needs assessment, (2) Goal setting, (3) Resource planning, (4) Action design, (5) Implementation, (6) Monitoring and evaluation. **Needs assessment comes first because evidence of gaps and causes is needed to choose suitable goals and resources. Marks (3):** 1 for all six stages; 1 for correct sequence; 1 for explaining this evidence-to-decision link. Do not award the reasoning mark merely for listing stages.

3. **Model:** Stage 1 - Needs assessment: collect data on current girls' enrolment, identify which areas have the lowest enrolment and why (distance, poverty, social norms). Stage 2 - Goal setting: set a measurable target, for example "increase girls' enrolment by 15 per cent within three years." Stage 3 - Resource planning: identify the funds, staff and materials needed. **Marks (3):** 1 per stage correctly described.

4. **Model:** Provincial plan to recruit 30,000 teachers = macro level (system-wide, provincial scale); strategic approach (specific target of 30,000 teachers over a five-year time frame). School plan to improve attendance = micro level (single school); not strategic as stated (no measurable goal or fixed time frame given). District two-year strategy = macro level (district-wide, ten schools); strategic approach (focused on the lowest-performing schools, two-year time frame, outcome-oriented). **Marks (3):** 1 per scenario for correct level (micro or macro) and a defensible strategic judgement.

5. **Model:** More spending does not automatically produce better outcomes because money must be spent well - on trained teachers, relevant curricula and functional schools. Example: a province may allocate funds for new schools but fail to recruit teachers, resulting in empty buildings. **Marks (3):** 1 - explains why spending alone is insufficient; 1 - identifies a specific condition (quality/utilisation); 1 - gives a concrete example.

6. **Model:** Resource allocation involves deciding how funds, people, materials and time are distributed to achieve goals. Challenges in Pakistan: (1) limited budgets (low GDP share for education); (2) funds released late or not fully spent; (3) equity gaps between urban and rural areas. **Marks (2):** 1 - defines resource allocation; 1 - names two challenges.

7. **Model:** After the 18th Amendment, each province is responsible for its own education planning through its provincial education department and provincial development plan. Before 2010, federal and provincial governments shared responsibilities with federal coordination (NEP 2009, paragraphs 62–66). Vision 2025 pp. 33–34 describes provincial leadership with federal support after devolution. **Marks (2):** 1 - provincial leadership after devolution; 1 - accurately contrasts earlier shared roles without claiming exclusive federal control.

8. **Model:** The rational model follows a linear sequence: analyse the problem, set goals, select the best option, implement, evaluate. The iterative model is a continuous cycle with feedback: after implementation, the plan is reviewed and adjusted, then repeated. **Marks (2):** 1 - describes rational model; 1 - describes iterative model.

9. **Model:** Micro planning operates at the school level and focuses on detailed school-level decisions (timetables, teacher deployment, resources). Strategic planning focuses on a specific problem within a defined scope and time frame. Micro planning is appropriate for school-level decisions. Strategic planning can address a specific issue at school, district, provincial or national scale. A school’s two-year attendance target can be both micro and strategic. Accept justified overlap. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the key difference (scope and focus); 1 - explains when each is appropriate.

10. **Model:** Example: technical and vocational training funded through a development plan can produce graduates with skills matched to industry needs, increasing employment and productivity. Or: girls’ access to relevant learning could expand employment opportunities where suitable jobs exist. These are mechanisms, not measured returns supplied by this unit. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies a specific type of education; 1 - explains the economic link.

merely named idea; 0.5 for a relevant but partly explained response; 1 for an accurate, scenario-linked
