## Unit 3 teacher notes: Development Plans and Educational Planning

These notes are for teacher educators using this unit with B.Ed trainees. They suggest sequencing,
teaching strategies and ways to handle likely misconceptions.

### Sequencing

The three topics are designed to build in order. Topic 3.1 (development plans and education) gives
trainees the big picture: how education fits into national and provincial planning. Topic 3.2
(concepts and types) introduces the vocabulary of planning. Topic 3.3 (process and models) applies
that vocabulary to the actual planning process. Reordering is possible but not recommended: Topic
3.3 assumes the vocabulary from 3.2, which in turn assumes the institutional context from 3.1.

If you have limited time, Topics 3.1 and 3.3 carry the most examination weight. Topic 3.2 can be
assigned as reading with a focused discussion rather than a full session.

### Teaching strategies

1. **Plan document analysis.** Bring a real development plan excerpt (a chapter from a five-year plan,
   a Vision document summary, a provincial education sector plan) and ask trainees to identify: what
   are the education targets? What budget is allocated? Are the targets measurable? This makes
   Topic 3.1 concrete and shows trainees how plans differ from policies.

2. **Level-sorting exercise.** Give trainees a set of planning scenarios (school timetable, district
   enrolment target, provincial teacher recruitment, national curriculum revision). Ask them to sort
   each into micro, macro or strategic planning. This reinforces Topic 3.2 and surfaces the
   misconception about macro planning being only federal.

3. **Walk-through activity.** For Topic 3.3, give trainees a simple education problem (for example,
   a school with high dropout rates) and ask them to walk it through the six stages of planning.
   Then ask them to repeat the walk-through using a different planning model. This shows that the
   same problem can be approached in different ways.

4. **Compare-and-contrast table.** Ask trainees to create a table comparing the four planning models
   (rational, iterative, participatory, strategic) across criteria: who is involved, how data is
   used, how flexible it is, and when it is most appropriate. This addresses the misconception that
   planning models are only theoretical.

### Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"Development plans and education policies are the same."** Use the "what vs. how much/when"
  distinction. Have trainees classify real statements as plan or policy until the distinction is
  automatic.
- **"Macro planning is only the federal government's job."** Use the 18th Amendment as the turning
  point. Ask trainees to identify which level of government is responsible for their own school's
  planning. Show that macro planning happens at provincial and district levels too.
- **"More resources automatically mean better education."** Use the quality argument. Ask: if you
  build a school but do not train the teacher, what happens? This makes the point that spending
  must be accompanied by quality-focused planning.
- **"Planning models are theoretical and not used in practice."** Walk through a real Pakistani
  education plan (a provincial education sector plan) and identify which model it follows. Show
  that every plan follows a model, whether explicitly or not.

### Practical work

- **Plan comparison.** Ask trainees to find two Pakistani education plans (for example, a federal
  five-year plan education chapter and a provincial education sector plan). Compare their targets,
  budgets and time frames. Write a short analysis (100-140 words) of the similarities and
  differences.
- **Mini planning exercise.** In groups, trainees design a simple school improvement plan for a
  described scenario. They must include: a needs assessment, one measurable goal, resource
  decisions and a named planning model. Groups present and compare their choices.
- **Planning model poster.** Trainees create a poster showing one planning model's stages and apply
  it to a Pakistani education example. Display and compare the models.

### Assessment notes

The unit assessment is designed for self-study. If you use it for formal assessment, the ERQs are
the most discriminating items. ERQ 1 (why a plan falls short) and ERQ 5 (designing a school
improvement plan) reward trainees who can connect ideas across topics rather than recall isolated
facts.
