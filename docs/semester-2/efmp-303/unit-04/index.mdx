# Monitoring, Evaluation and Challenges

Policies and plans do not implement themselves. Somewhere between a policy announcement in Islamabad and a changed classroom in a district school, a chain of decisions, resources and actions must hold together. When that chain breaks, the policy fails. This unit examines two sides of that chain. First, how do we track whether a policy is being carried out as intended and judge whether it is working? Second, what are the forces in Pakistan's context that most often break the chain?

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

1. Explain what monitoring and evaluation mean in education and distinguish one from the other.
2. Describe the process of monitoring and evaluation and identify appropriate tools for each stage.
3. Identify the main challenges (financial, political and bureaucratic) that block policy implementation in Pakistan.
4. Analyse how these challenges interact to produce implementation failure in a described case.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 3 introduced the planning process: needs assessment, goal setting, resource allocation and implementation. This unit picks up where that process ends. Monitoring and evaluation are the stages that tell you whether the plan is working. Before starting this unit, you should be comfortable with the idea that planning is a staged process with a beginning, a middle and an end.
