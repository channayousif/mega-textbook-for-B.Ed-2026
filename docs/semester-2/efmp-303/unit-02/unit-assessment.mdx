## Unit summary

This unit examined Pakistan's national education policies from 1947 to 2017 through three topics.

- Topic 2.1 traced the major policies from independence to 2000: the 1947 Education Conference, the 1959 Sharif Commission, the 1970, 1972, 1979, 1992 and 1998 policies. It showed how each policy reflected the political priorities of its time and why many policy goals were repeated across decades without being fully achieved.
- Topic 2.2 analysed the National Education Policy 2009 in detail: its objectives (free and compulsory education, quality improvement, curriculum reform, teacher quality, governance devolution), its implementation strategies, and its mixed outcomes (Article 25-A, increased enrolment, but missed spending targets and persistent quality gaps).
- Topic 2.3 examined the National Education Policy 2017: its vision, its five pillars (access and equity, quality, governance, relevance, partnership), its curriculum reform and quality assurance measures, and its priority areas (literacy, higher education, technical education). It showed how the 2017 policy built on the 2009 policy while placing greater emphasis on quality and accountability.

Together these topics reveal a pattern: Pakistan has produced many education policies, but the gap between policy intent on paper and classroom reality remains the central challenge of educational governance.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. *(Remember)* Which body recommended the 5-3-2-2 grade structure that still shapes Pakistan's schools?
   - A) The 1947 Education Conference
   - B) The 1959 Sharif Commission
   - C) The 1972 Education Policy
   - D) The 2009 National Education Policy

2. *(Remember)* What did the 1972 Education Policy under Zulfikar Ali Bhutto do?
   - A) Introduced Islamization of the curriculum
   - B) Closed all private schools permanently
   - C) Nationalised private schools and colleges
   - D) Transferred education to the provinces

3. *(Understand)* What was the main reason many education policies between 1947 and 2000 produced limited classroom change?
   - A) Teachers refused to read policy documents
   - B) Policies were written in English rather than Urdu
   - C) Planning, funding and administrative follow-through were often weak
   - D) Parents did not want their children to go to school

4. *(Understand)* What constitutional change gave legal force to the right to education in Pakistan?
   - A) The 1973 Constitution's original text
   - B) Article 25-A, added by the 18th Amendment
   - C) The 1959 Sharif Commission
   - D) The 1998 National Education Policy

5. *(Apply)* A new policy promises to raise education spending to seven per cent of GDP. Based on the unit's evidence, what is the most likely outcome?
   - A) The target is met within two years
   - B) The target is announced but not achieved, as happened after 2009
   - C) The target causes inflation
   - D) The target is rejected by teachers

6. *(Apply)* Which of the following best describes the effect of the 18th Amendment on education governance?
   - A) The federal government took direct control of all schools
   - B) Education management was devolved to provincial governments
   - C) All private schools were closed
   - D) The curriculum became uniform across all provinces

7. *(Apply)* A province wants to improve teacher quality. Following the NEP 2009 approach, which action would be most consistent with the policy?
   - A) Announcing a new salary scale without providing training
   - B) Offering in-service training programmes and strengthening pre-service teacher education
   - C) Reducing the number of teachers hired
   - D) Transferring all teaching to private schools

8. *(Apply)* A policy that emphasises activity-based learning, critical thinking and reduced rote memorisation reflects the approach of which policy?
   - A) The 1947 Education Conference
   - B) The 1972 Education Policy
   - C) The 2017 National Education Policy
   - D) The 1959 Sharif Commission

9. *(Apply)* The National Education Policy 2017 placed greater emphasis on quality assurance than the 2009 policy. What does this suggest about how policy thinking evolved?
   - A) Policymakers gave up on expanding access
   - B) Policymakers recognised that access alone was not enough without quality
   - C) Policymakers decided to close government schools
   - D) Policymakers wanted to copy another country's system exactly

10. *(Apply)* Which of the following is a priority area specifically highlighted in the National Education Policy 2017?
     - A) Nationalising all private universities
     - B) Technical and vocational education and training
     - C) Abolishing the Higher Education Commission
     - D) Returning to the pre-2009 curriculum

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question in the space indicated.

1. *(Understand)* Name three education policies introduced in Pakistan between 1947 and 2000, with their years, and explain the main focus of one of them.

2. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between a policy on paper and a policy in practice, using one example from this unit.

3. *(Apply)* The 1972 Education Policy nationalised private schools. Give one positive effect and one negative effect of this decision.

4. *(Understand)* What role did the 18th Amendment play in education governance? Explain in one or two sentences.

5. *(Analyze)* The National Education Policy 2009 set a spending target of seven per cent of GDP. Explain two reasons why such targets are often missed in Pakistan's education system.

6. *(Apply)* Name two quality assurance mechanisms proposed by the National Education Policy 2017.

7. *(Understand)* What is the difference between the 2009 and 2017 policies in their approach to quality? Answer in two to three sentences.

8. *(Apply)* A provincial government wants to improve literacy among adults. Following the 2017 policy, list two actions it could take.

9. *(Analyze)* Compare the 1972 and 1979 education policies. What was different about their main focus?

10. *(Apply)* Why does the unit describe the period 1947-2000 as showing a "pattern" of policy repetition? Use one example in your answer.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer each question in a short paragraph (120-160 words unless stated otherwise).

1. *(Analyze)* This unit argues that "a policy on paper is not a policy in practice." Using evidence from at least two policies discussed in this unit, analyse three factors that explain the gap between policy intentions and classroom outcomes.

2. *(Evaluate)* Evaluate the statement: "The National Education Policy 2009 was a failure because it did not meet all its objectives." Using evidence from the unit, explain what the policy achieved and what it did not, and state whether you agree with the statement.

3. *(Analyze)* The 18th Amendment devolved education to the provinces. Analyse two opportunities and two challenges this created for education governance in Pakistan. Use specific examples from the unit.

4. *(Evaluate)* Compare the National Education Policy 2009 and the National Education Policy 2017. Evaluate which policy offers a more realistic approach to improving Pakistan's education system, drawing on at least three points of comparison from the unit.

5. *(Create/Evaluate)* A new national education policy is being drafted in 2026. Drawing on the lessons of the 2009 and 2017 policies, propose two priorities you would include in the new policy and explain why each one addresses a persistent problem identified in this unit.

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Try every question above before you look back at the keys below.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - The 1959 Sharif Commission recommended the 5-3-2-2 grade structure. The 1947 Conference set broader goals; the 1972 and 2009 policies did not introduce this structure.
2. **C** - The 1972 Education Policy nationalised private schools and colleges. Islamization came in 1979; devolution came with the 18th Amendment in 2010.
3. **C** - Planning, funding and administrative follow-through were often weak. This is the pattern the unit identifies across multiple policies.
4. **B** - Article 25-A, added by the 18th Amendment in 2010, gave constitutional force to the right to education.
5. **B** - The target is announced but not achieved, as happened after 2009. The unit notes that spending targets were repeatedly missed.
6. **B** - Education management was devolved to provincial governments. This was a consequence of the 18th Amendment.
7. **B** - In-service training and strengthened pre-service education are the actions consistent with NEP 2009's teacher quality strategy.
8. **C** - The 2017 National Education Policy emphasised activity-based learning, critical thinking and reduced memorisation.
9. **B** - Policymakers recognised that access alone was not enough without quality. The 2017 policy shifted emphasis accordingly.
10. **B** - Technical and vocational education and training is a priority area in the 2017 policy. The other options are not mentioned as priorities.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Any three of: 1947 Education Conference, 1959 Sharif Commission, 1970 National Education Policy, 1972 Education Policy, 1979 Education Policy, 1992 Education Policy, 1998 National Education Policy. Each with correct year. **Marks (3):** 1 per policy with correct year (max 3).

2. **Model:** A policy on paper is the document that states goals and intentions. A policy in practice is what actually changes in schools and classrooms. Example: the 1998 policy promised four per cent of GDP for education, but this target was not met, so the policy did not change classroom conditions. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines policy on paper; 1 - defines policy in practice; 1 - gives a specific example from the unit.

3. **Model:** Positive effect: expanded access to education for children from poorer families by bringing private schools under government control. Negative effect: government schools had to absorb more pupils without matching increases in teachers, buildings or budgets, which reduced quality in many places. **Marks (2):** 1 - one positive effect; 1 - one negative effect.

4. **Model:** The 18th Amendment (2010) transferred education from the federal to the provincial level. Provincial governments now manage schools, teachers and budgets. This was intended to allow more local decision-making but also required provinces to build new management capacity. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the devolution; 1 - explains one consequence (opportunity or challenge).

5. **Model:** Two reasons: (1) administrative and planning capacity is weak - targets are set but detailed implementation plans are not developed or funded; (2) political priorities change - a new government may abandon the previous government's targets; (3) revenue constraints - government income may be lower than projected, making spending targets unaffordable. **Marks (3):** 1 per valid reason (max 3).

6. **Model:** Two of: national standards for education, accreditation systems, regular national learning assessments, accountability mechanisms for schools and education managers. **Marks (2):** 1 per correct mechanism.

7. **Model:** The 2009 policy emphasised inputs - more teachers, more buildings, more budgets. The 2017 policy placed more emphasis on outputs - what pupils actually learn, quality assurance, and accountability for results. The 2017 policy introduced national standards, accreditation and regular assessments to measure learning outcomes. **Marks (3):** 1 - 2009 emphasis; 1 - 2017 emphasis; 1 - specific example of a quality assurance mechanism.

8. **Model:** Two of: adult literacy programmes, non-formal schools for out-of-school children, second-chance education for dropouts, community-based literacy centres. **Marks (2):** 1 per valid action.

9. **Model:** The 1972 policy focused on nationalisation and equitable access (Bhutto's socialist agenda). The 1979 policy focused on Islamization of the curriculum and Islamic values (Zia's programme). **Marks (2):** 1 - 1972 focus; 1 - 1979 focus.

10. **Model:** The "pattern" refers to the same problems being named in policy after policy without being solved. Example: low literacy was identified in 1947, 1959, 1970, 1992 and 1998, yet literacy rates remained low for decades. This shows that announcing a problem in a policy does not automatically solve it. **Marks (2):** 1 - identifies the pattern; 1 - gives a specific example.
