## Unit 2 teacher notes: National Education Policies

These notes are for teacher educators using this unit with B.Ed trainees. They suggest sequencing, teaching strategies and ways to handle likely misconceptions.

### Sequencing

The three topics build in chronological and conceptual order. Topic 2.1 (policies 1947-2000) gives trainees the historical foundation. Topic 2.2 (NEP 2009) and Topic 2.3 (NEP 2017) build on that foundation by examining the two most recent policies in detail. Reordering is possible: Topic 2.3 can be taught before Topic 2.2, since the 2017 policy is more current. However, teaching 2.1 first is strongly recommended, because the later policies make more sense when trainees understand what came before.

If time is limited, Topics 2.2 and 2.3 carry the most direct relevance to current policy debates. Topic 2.1 can be assigned as guided reading with a focused discussion on the policy-practice gap.

### Teaching strategies

1. **Policy document comparison.** Bring (or project) excerpts from the NEP 2009 and NEP 2017 documents. Ask trainees to identify one objective that appears in both and one that is different. This makes Topic 2.3's comparison concrete and trains close reading of policy language.

2. **Timeline on the wall.** Extend the timeline from Unit 1 (Topic 1.4) by adding the 2009 and 2017 policies. Trainees see the full arc from 1947 to 2017. Ask them to identify which problems appear repeatedly and discuss why they persist.

3. **Case study discussion.** For Topic 2.2, present a short case: a province that received devolved education authority after the 18th Amendment but had limited capacity to manage it. Ask trainees to identify the challenges and propose solutions. This connects governance theory to practical reality.

4. **Role-play: policy announcement.** For Topic 2.1, assign each group a different policy (1972, 1979, 1992). Each group announces the policy to the class as if they are the government of the time. The rest of the class plays the role of teachers reacting to the announcement. This helps trainees understand the gap between policy announcement and teacher response.

### Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"A policy on paper is a policy in practice."** Use the timeline from Topic 2.1 and ask trainees to name problems that were identified in multiple policies but never solved. The repetition itself is the evidence.
- **"The 2009 and 2017 policies are identical."** Use the comparison table (Topic 2.3) and ask trainees to fill in the differences. The shift from input-focused (2009) to output-focused (2017) is the key distinction.
- **"Policies fail only because of money."** Use the NEP 2009 case to show that funding is one factor among many: administrative capacity, political will, coordination between federal and provincial governments, and implementation planning all matter.
- **"Every new government writes a new policy."** Use the timeline to show that some policies were genuinely new responses to changed circumstances (1972 nationalization, 1979 Islamization), while others repeated earlier goals without new thinking. Help trainees distinguish between the two.

### Practical work

- **Policy document analysis.** Ask trainees to obtain a copy of either the NEP 2009 or NEP 2017 summary from the Ministry of Education website. They should identify three objectives and three implementation strategies, then write one paragraph assessing whether each strategy was carried out.
- **Practicum observation.** During practicum, trainees should ask their cooperating head teacher: "What is one national education policy that has affected this school?" They write a short note on what they learn, connecting it to the unit's content.
- **Policy comparison poster.** In groups, trainees create a poster comparing the 2009 and 2017 policies across four dimensions: access, quality, curriculum and governance. Display and compare.

### Assessment notes

The unit assessment is designed for self-study. If you use it for formal assessment, the ERQs are the most discriminating items. ERQ 1 (the policy-practice gap) and ERQ 4 (compare the two policies) reward trainees who can analyse and evaluate, not just recall facts. The MCQs test breadth across all three topics; ensure trainees have studied all of Topic 2.1, not only the 2009 and 2017 policies.
