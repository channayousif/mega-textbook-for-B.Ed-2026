# Contemporary Forms of Literacy

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain critical and digital literacy, showing how each extends reading and writing (SLO:EFMP-406-2-1).
- Apply critical questions and purposeful digital choices to a familiar message (SLO:EFMP-406-2-1).
- Explain media and information literacy, and examine how words, images, layout, colour and sound work together (SLO:EFMP-406-2-2).
- Analyze an everyday text using more than one form of literacy, with reasons drawn from the text (SLO:EFMP-406-2-2).

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 1: literacy involves making meaning in social situations. Explain one difference between a narrow reading-and-writing view and a contemporary view. Bring an example of reading outside school, such as a bus notice or a shop sign. You do not need your own phone or internet account.

## How to use this unit

Read each worked example before doing the paired activity. Cover the page when answering the retrieval questions. Then check whether you can explain your reasoning, rather than repeat a definition. Collect the two topic tasks in a small portfolio. Use the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) after both topics; attempt the questions before opening its public self-study guidance.

All classroom cases, messages, prices and counts in this unit are invented teaching examples. They are not reports about named schools or current public announcements. Use paper copies where devices are unavailable. The [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes) offer ways to organize discussion and group presentations. Unit work uses the default 40% formative and 60% summative balance; the course guide's separate course assessment weights remain unchanged.
