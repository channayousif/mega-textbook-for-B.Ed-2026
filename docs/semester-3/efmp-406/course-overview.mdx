# Contemporary Literacies - Course Overview

This course looks at literacy in a time of fast social, cultural and technological change. It treats literacy as something people do in real places for real purposes, not only as the skill of reading and writing. Beyond reading and writing, it covers critical, digital, media, visual and information literacies. It prepares you, as a future teacher, to plan, teach and evaluate literacy work for pupils with different needs and backgrounds.

## Learning outcomes

The course guide states that by the end of the course you will be able to:

1. Define and explain the concept and scope of contemporary literacy.
2. Differentiate between traditional and modern perspectives of literacy.
3. Analyze the role of literacy in social, cultural, and digital contexts.
4. Apply contemporary literacy teaching strategies in classroom settings.
5. Design literacy activities that promote critical thinking and learner engagement.

## Units

The guide divides the course into six units and gives no week allocation for them.

| Unit | Title | Sub-topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concept and Evolution of Literacy | meaning and nature of literacy; traditional and contemporary views; historical development of literacy practices; literacy as a lifelong learning process |
| 2 | Contemporary Forms of Literacy | critical literacy; digital literacy; media and information literacy; visual and multimodal literacy |
| 3 | Literacy, Language, and Society | literacy as a social and cultural practice; language, identity and power relations; literacy for citizenship and democratic participation; multilingualism and literacy development |
| 4 | Literacy in the Digital Era | technology and literacy development; digital reading and writing practices; online communication and social media; ethical, legal and responsible use of digital resources |
| 5 | Pedagogical Approaches to Contemporary Literacy | learner-centred literacy instruction; inquiry-based and project-based learning; integration of ICT in literacy teaching; assessment and evaluation of literacy skills |
| 6 | Issues, Challenges, and Future Directions | literacy challenges in developing contexts; equity, access and inclusion; role of teachers; future trends |

Units are published as they are written. Unit 1 is available now.

## Assessment

The course guide gives these course-level weights:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Class participation and reflective journals | 10% |
| Mid-term written test | 20% |
| Project: developing an inclusive lesson plan | 30% |
| Final exam (written and presentation) | 40% |

The guide does not say which components are formative and which are summative. Each unit's own practice questions follow the default split of 40% formative and 60% summative. Your teacher will explain which work is collected.

## Teaching approach and practical work

The guide names group discussions and seminars, role-plays and simulations, case study analysis, visits to inclusive schools, reflection journals and portfolios, lectures, discussion, question-answer and brainstorming. Practical work includes group work, group assignments, individual assignments and presentations. Each unit's teacher notes show how these are used.

## Reading

Each unit lists the open-access sources it draws on in its "Further reading" sections. Sources are cited by reference only and are never reproduced.
