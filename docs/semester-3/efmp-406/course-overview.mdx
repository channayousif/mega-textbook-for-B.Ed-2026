# Contemporary Literacies - Course Overview

This course equips prospective teachers with a deep understanding of contemporary perspectives
on literacy in an era of rapid social, cultural and technological change. It treats literacy
not merely as an isolated technical skill, but as a socially situated practice embedded in
relationships, cultural identities, power dynamics and digital environments.

## Course learning outcomes

From the course guide (`3rd 2026.txt:240-246`), by the end of this course prospective teachers will be able to:

1. Define and explain the concept and scope of contemporary literacy.
2. Differentiate between traditional and modern perspectives of literacy.
3. Analyze the role of literacy in social, cultural, and digital contexts.
4. Apply contemporary literacy teaching strategies in classroom settings.
5. Design literacy activities that promote critical thinking and learner engagement.

## Course units

1. **Unit 1: Concept and Evolution of Literacy** - Nature, traditional and modern views, historical development, and literacy as lifelong learning.
2. **Unit 2: Contemporary Forms of Literacy** - Critical literacy, digital literacy, media and information literacy, and visual/multimodal literacy.
3. **Unit 3: Literacy, Language, and Society** - Literacy as social practice, language and identity, citizenship, and multilingualism.
4. **Unit 4: Literacy in the Digital Era** - Screen reading and digital writing, online communication, ethics, and copyright.
5. **Unit 5: Pedagogical Approaches to Contemporary Literacy** - Learner-centred instruction, inquiry-based learning, ICT integration, and assessment.
6. **Unit 6: Issues, Challenges, and Future Directions** - Developing-context barriers, equity and inclusion, the teacher's role, and future trends.

## Assessment

Assessment follows the default distribution: **60% summative / 40% formative** (Constitution Art. III.7).
Course guide components (reflective journals, mid-term assessment, project work, and final examination)
are integrated into unit learning cycles and end-of-unit assessments.
