# Issues, Challenges, and Future Directions

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain how linked barriers affect literacy learning in developing contexts, using a Pakistani example. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-406-6-1)*
- Distinguish access, equity and inclusion, and analyse whether a classroom response supports participation and learning. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-406-6-1)*
- Apply a teacher's classroom, school, home and community roles to a literacy problem. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-406-6-2)*
- Evaluate a proposed district response to low literacy and justify a feasible improvement. *(Evaluate; both SLOs)*
- Judge an emerging literacy tool against learning, fairness and evidence, while explaining why reading and writing remain necessary. *(Evaluate; SLO:EFMP-406-6-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 3 on literacy as a social practice, language and power. Recall Unit 5 on learner-centred teaching, inquiry, ICT and assessment. Before reading, name a text a child uses outside school and a way to find out whether that child understands it. You need no device or internet connection for the activities in this unit.

## How to use this unit

Read the topics in order. Follow the worked cases before trying your own analysis. All named classroom scenes and numerical classroom examples are invented teaching cases, not reports about real schools or district statistics. Work with a partner for the activities and keep a reflection journal.

Try the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) before reading its final self-study guidance. Unit assessment uses the default 40% formative and 60% summative balance. The course guide's separate course-wide weights remain unchanged. The [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes) explain the group work, presentations and inclusive lesson-plan connection.
