# Pedagogical Approaches to Contemporary Literacy

The first four units of this course asked what literacy is and what forms it takes today. This unit asks a teacher's question: how do I teach it? Many Pakistani classrooms still teach reading as listening, repeating and copying. This unit shows other ways that keep the pupils reading, talking and writing for real purposes, that use the little technology a school may have, and that let the teacher see what each pupil can actually do.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain learner-centred literacy instruction and describe what the teacher does in a learner-centred lesson. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-406-5-1)*
- Compare a teacher-centred and a learner-centred literacy lesson, and redesign one into the other. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-406-5-1)*
- Plan a short literacy project that uses inquiry, from a question to a product shared with real readers. *(Create; SLO:EFMP-406-5-1)*
- Distinguish using ICT from integrating it, and match low-cost tools to literacy purposes. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-406-5-2)*
- Choose and combine methods to assess and evaluate literacy skills, including a simple reading record. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-406-5-2)*
- Design a short literacy activity for a Grade 5 class that uses one approach from this unit, with a way to assess it, and justify the design. *(Create; both outcomes)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

You need Units 2 and 4 of this course. Unit 2 described the contemporary forms of literacy, such as critical, digital, media and information, and visual literacy. Unit 4 looked at reading and writing in the digital era. This unit is about how to teach those literacies, so keep your notes from both units nearby. It also helps to recall one literacy lesson you have watched on practicum: who did the talking, and what did the pupils do?

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Topic 5.1 sets out the teaching approaches; Topic 5.2 adds technology and assessment to them. Each topic starts with a real school situation, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before you try the move yourself. Do the group activity with classmates, then answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) has practice questions and, at the end, a public self-study guidance section. Try the questions first. In formal course assessment the default weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative; your teacher will tell you which work is collected. Teachers will find planning help in the [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes).

This unit is a good place to start the lesson plan you will need for the course project. Keep any activity you design here in your portfolio.
