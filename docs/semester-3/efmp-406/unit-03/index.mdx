# Literacy, Language, and Society

Unit 1 introduced literacy as an evolving concept, and Unit 2 examined its contemporary forms,
including critical and multimodal literacies. This unit situates literacy firmly within its social,
cultural, linguistic, and political contexts.

Literacy is never simply an abstract, neutral technical skill that individuals acquire in isolation.
It is an active social practice shaped by community traditions, cultural institutions, and power
dynamics. In Pakistan's rich multilingual federation, reading and writing are deeply intertwined
with questions of identity, mother-tongue validation, equitable educational access, and democratic
citizenship. As prospective teachers, understanding how language hierarchies operate in schools
enables you to design classrooms that affirm every learner's cultural voice and foster critical,
active participation in society.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Explain literacy as a situated social and cultural practice rather than a purely autonomous skill.
2. Analyze how language policies, dominant literacies, and classroom practices shape learner identity and power relations.
3. Evaluate the role of literacy in enabling democratic citizenship and active participation in public life.
4. Design pedagogical strategies that embrace Pakistan's multilingual reality through additive bilingualism and translanguaging.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds directly upon the foundational concepts established in Units 1 and 2:

- Understanding literacy as a multifaceted practice reaching beyond basic decoding and encoding.
- The principles of critical literacy: questioning how texts are constructed, whose voices are privileged, and whose perspectives are missing.
- Basic familiarity with classroom observation and the linguistic diversity present in Pakistani schools.

## In this unit

1. [Literacy, culture and power](./topic-01) - exploring literacy as a social practice, the ideological model, and how language choices intersect with institutional power and learner identity.
2. [Literacy for citizens in a multilingual country](./topic-02) - examining civic literacy for democratic participation, the roles of English, Urdu and local languages in schools, and additive multilingual pedagogy.

## How to use this unit

Each topic is structured as a complete, self-contained nine-part learning cycle designed to connect
educational theory to authentic Pakistani classroom practice. You will examine concrete classroom
scenarios, study research-backed explanations, collaborate with peers in practical activities, check
your comprehension through retrieval questions, reflect on your learning, and plan practical transfer
tasks for your school placement.

Work through the two topics in sequential order before attempting the unit-end assessment. Keep a
reflective journal during your practicum visits: record the languages your pupils speak in the
playground versus the classroom, note how textbooks position different language varieties, and observe
how everyday community literacies can enrich your teaching.
