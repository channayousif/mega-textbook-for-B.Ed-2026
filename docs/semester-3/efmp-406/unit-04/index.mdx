# Literacy in the Digital Era

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

1. Explain how a digital tool can support a literacy goal and how it can introduce a new barrier.
2. Compare reading on paper and screens, using research with attention to its limits.
3. Plan a digital writing task that includes an audience, feedback and revision.
4. Analyze online messages for purpose, evidence, audience and possible harm.
5. Evaluate whether a classroom resource can be used and shared responsibly.
6. Design a phone-supported reading lesson with an equally useful route for pupils without devices.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 2: digital literacy involves judging and making meaning, as well as operating a device. Media and information literacy includes asking who produced a message, what evidence supports it and whose interests it serves. Bring one example of a message you have read on a phone. You need neither a phone nor an online account to study this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Reading and writing on screens](./topic-01) connects technology choices to literacy goals and develops routines for reading, drafting, commenting and revising.
2. [Online communication and using digital resources responsibly](./topic-02) examines audience, participation, source checking, ownership, credit and safe sharing.

## How to use this unit

Read the classroom situation before studying each explanation. Follow the worked decisions before trying your own. Complete the pair or group activity using paper if a device is unavailable. Answer the retrieval questions from memory, then return to the explanation to repair gaps. Use the checklist to decide what you still need to practise.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) brings both topics together. Attempt its questions before consulting the final self-study guidance. For unit practice, the default balance is 40% formative work and 60% summative work. This does not replace the course guide's separate course assessment scheme. Your teacher can use the [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes) to organise offline participation and the guide's group work and presentations.

All named schools and pupils in the teaching cases are fictional. The examples are planning exercises, not reports of research carried out in Sindh. This is an English draft awaiting independent review and Urdu translation; completing the exercises does not certify a unit or authorise publication.
