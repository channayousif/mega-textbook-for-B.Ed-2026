## Teaching strategies and sequencing

These notes apply the course guide's group discussion, case analysis, role-play, reflection, group assignments and presentations to Unit 4. They do not add a compulsory platform, a new course assessment scheme or a requirement for pupils to open accounts. The guide also lists visits to inclusive schools. A visit is not necessary to complete this unit, and no child should be observed, photographed or recorded without the school's appropriate arrangements.

Begin by asking trainees to recall Unit 2's distinction between operating a device and judging information. Use a fictional notice drawn on the board. Ask trainees to locate its purpose, audience, source and date. This common starting point avoids sorting the class into those who own a phone and those who do not. Do not ask students to display personal messages as evidence of experience. A prepared example is enough for the learning purpose.

Teach Topic 4.1 before Topic 4.2. The first topic gives trainees a concrete reading and writing cycle. The second adds choices about sources, permission, audience and responsible sharing. Learners can then revisit the first lesson proposal and ask whether its resource is suitable to use. This return shows why resource checks belong inside planning rather than being a final decoration after an appealing lesson has already been designed.

A workable sequence uses several meetings rather than treating the unit as a single demonstration of an application. In one meeting, discuss the reading case and model a purpose-and-evidence routine. In the next, run the writing exchange and inspect the initial and revised drafts. Then use the resource clinic to practise separating the checks. End with group presentations and private reflection on a decision that changed. Adjust the timetable to the actual programme; these meetings are a suggested sequence, not guide-specified weeks.

## Modelling and discussion

Think aloud at a genuine decision point. For example, show a heading, a short passage and an unrelated button. State the reading purpose, explain why the passage is relevant and leave the button unopened. Invite trainees to identify what evidence would challenge your choice. The aim is to make the reasoning inspectable. A demonstration that only lists buttons risks teaching an interface procedure while leaving the literacy judgment invisible.

When discussing the screen-reading study, keep three labels on the board: participants, task and outcome. Trainees should connect a claim to those labels before using it to justify a lesson. If a learner says that the research proves all screens are harmful, ask which age range and conditions were actually considered. If another says that enjoyment proves improvement, ask what understanding would look like. Correct overstatement in either direction without turning the discussion into a vote for paper or technology.

For writing, prepare a deliberately incomplete but plausible message. A date, named item or clear audience can be missing. Model a specific reader comment and then make a revision that addresses it. Retain both versions. Trainees should be able to explain the connection between the comment and the change. This is more informative than displaying only a polished final message and telling learners that revision matters.

Use role-play to practise disagreement about a resource. One trainee proposes a copied story, another asks about permission and another considers access. Require each to name the information they need rather than portray the source checker as someone who simply blocks all use. The goal is a workable, defensible decision. A teacher-created alternative can be a successful outcome when it preserves the literacy task and avoids unresolved resource questions.

## Likely misconceptions and responses

When trainees equate engagement with comprehension, return to the evidence. A replay request may show interest; an explanation linked to story events shows understanding. Neither observation should be dismissed, but they answer different questions. Ask learners to name which observation would inform the next teaching step. Do not invent percentages of improvement for the fictional classroom cases.

When trainees dismiss social media as non-writing, inspect a message's choices about reader, tone, facts and response. Then distinguish recognising literacy from recommending a public platform for children. The small study used in the topic concerns older teenagers in a particular setting. Its value here is to broaden what teachers notice, while its limits constrain the claim they can make.

When trainees say a reference gives permission, separate the two questions physically on the board. Under one heading write who made the work. Under the other write what uses are allowed. Add privacy as a third heading when an example contains a person. Ask what information belongs under each heading and which remains missing. An example can be well credited yet still require replacement for the planned use.

When trainees assume a no-device route is inferior, compare the thinking required. A paper exchange can preserve audience awareness, source checking and revision. It cannot establish competence in operating a specific application. Help trainees record the achievement accurately rather than either devalue the paper task or overclaim what it proves. This distinction supports fair feedback and clearer planning.

## Practical work

Use the guide's group work and presentations with the two topic activities. Each group keeps a small portfolio: its first lesson sequence, another group's comment, its revision and a short explanation of the decision. A reflection journal can record which evidence changed the trainee's view. These are learning records, not requests for personal device histories, photographs or screenshots.

Prepare all materials before class: fictional notices, a short teacher-created passage, an incomplete draft, permission scenarios and paper copies. If using an actual digital resource, inspect the source and applicable terms yourself. Open it in the intended offline or supervised setting before the lesson. A live search during a short class can introduce unsuitable material and consume time meant for reading. The unit does not require trainees to test a real platform's safeguards on children.

If there is one device, use it at a station with rotating roles. Other groups can work with the same passage on paper. Make the roles visible and change them so one confident operator does not become the permanent demonstrator. Collect an individual explanation after the group task. Group agreement is useful, but it cannot show whether each member understands the reason for the decision.

For presentations, require groups to explain one trade-off: a useful feature they rejected, a missing source detail that changed the plan, or a privacy concern that led to a fictional substitute. A presentation should make the decision understandable to classmates who did not create the plan. It need not contain elaborate slides. A sheet with the goal, evidence and revised sequence is enough.

## Feedback and access

Keep feedback tied to the taught concepts and the actual task. Distinguish an incomplete explanation from a language or typing barrier. Invite a spoken explanation where that helps reveal reasoning, while preserving the writing evidence required for a writing goal. Provide clear instructions in familiar language when necessary. Do not infer weak digital judgment from slow keyboard use alone.

All essential material should be available during scheduled learning time. Do not require personal data purchase, a new account or public posting for participation. If a proposed practicum activity raises a school policy question, have trainees consult their supervising teacher before proceeding. A paper simulation is an appropriate way to practise decisions while a real-use question remains unresolved.

These notes contain no additional assessment items. Use the topic checks, tasks and unit bank for evidence. Independent English review, the Urdu mirror, figure production and later publication remain separate stages. No classroom activity in this unit supplies G3 or G5 certification.
