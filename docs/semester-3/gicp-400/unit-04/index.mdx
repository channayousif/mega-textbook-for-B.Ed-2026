## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

1. Distinguish martial law, abrogation and suspension, using Pakistan's constitutional history as evidence. This serves SLO:GICP-400-4-1.
2. Explain how constitutional government returned after major interruptions, and why holding an election is one step in restoring democracy. This serves SLO:GICP-400-4-1.
3. Trace the making of the 1973 Constitution from the crisis of 1971 through the interim settlement to its commencement. This serves SLO:GICP-400-4-2.
4. Explain selected Islamic provisions without confusing the original 1973 text with later additions. This serves SLO:GICP-400-4-2.
5. Compare the histories and implementation of Pakistan's three permanent constitutions, and evaluate why the 1973 framework survived interruptions. This serves both unit outcomes.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 3's account of the adapted Government of India Act 1935, the Objectives Resolution and the 1956 and 1962 constitutions. You should already know that a parliamentary system makes the government answerable to the elected assembly, while a presidential system gives the President a separate executive role. Recall that federalism shares governing responsibilities between the federation and provinces. Explain these ideas in your own words before starting; return to Unit 3 if you need help.

## In this unit

1. [Martial laws and constitutional breakdowns](./topic-01): identify interruptions and distinguish them from changes within constitutional rules.
2. [Making the 1973 Constitution](./topic-02): follow the drafting process and connect Islamic provisions with institutions and rights.
3. [Pakistan's constitutions in historical perspective](./topic-03): compare constitutional lifetimes and test the difference between rules and their implementation.

## How to use this unit

Read each classroom situation before the explanation. Write down your first response, then revise it after studying the worked example. Complete the group activity and answer the three retrieval questions from memory. Use the checklist to decide which idea needs another reading. Each topic ends with a task that asks you to explain relationships or defend a judgement.

After the three topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). Its 10 multiple-choice, 10 restricted-response and 5 extended-response questions are public self-study practice. Attempt them before consulting the final guidance. The course default is 40% formative and 60% summative assessment; your institution decides how to use this practice material.

This is a historical unit. References to constitutional articles use the Ministry of Law and Justice's 2024 edition through the Twenty-sixth Amendment, hosted by Sindh Laws, unless a passage states otherwise. That edition helps distinguish original provisions from later amendments; it is not a statement of current law. Keep dates attached to claims. For present legal questions, consult the latest official text rather than treating a historical classroom example as legal advice.
