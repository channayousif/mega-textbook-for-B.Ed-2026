## Unit summary

Pakistan's constitutional history includes both changes made within governing rules and interruptions that displaced them. The distinction matters because a change of leader is not automatically a constitutional breakdown. Martial law displaced civilian constitutional government. The 1956 and 1962 constitutions were abrogated in 1958 and 1969 respectively. The 1973 framework was later held in abeyance and revived. Different descriptions identify different fates for the governing text.

Restoration also requires careful description. Elections can bring representatives back into institutions before military control ends or normal checks fully recover. A good account identifies what returned, under which rules, and what remained limited. It does not confuse the 1985 election with the 1988 party-based election, or the 2002 election with the civilian transition following the 2008 election.

The 1973 settlement followed the crisis and separation of 1971. Temporary constitutional arrangements allowed government to continue while representatives prepared a permanent framework. A committee draft preceded Assembly passage on 10 April 1973, authentication on 12 April and commencement on 14 August. The settlement linked parliamentary responsibility, federal representation and Islamic commitments. Its broad acceptance offered a basis for handling disagreement rather than a promise that disagreement would disappear.

Islamic provisions have different roles and historical layers. Article 2 concerns state religion, Article 31 concerns a Principle of Policy, Article 227 concerns conformity of laws with Islamic injunctions, and Articles 228-230 concern the Council of Islamic Ideology. Advice is different from legislation or adjudication. Citizenship safeguards must be read alongside the state's Islamic identity. Article 2A was inserted in 1985, while the Federal Shariat Court was introduced in 1980; neither should be placed in a box labelled "all original 1973 provisions".

Finally, survival is different from uninterrupted operation. The 1973 framework endured through revival and amendment, but that does not erase interruptions. Implementation asks how text, institutions and practice connect. A rule states a commitment; an institution has responsibilities; evidence from practice shows what happened. A sound historical judgement uses all three layers and states what its evidence cannot establish.

## Summative assessment

Attempt the questions before reading the final guidance. For MCQs, select one option and record a brief reason. For RRQs, stay within the stated response bound. For ERQs, use accurate dated examples, explain relationships and consider the limits of your conclusion. These are public self-study questions, not a protected graded quiz bank. Suggested marks are one per MCQ, four per RRQ and ten per ERQ. Writing time is separate from the page's reading estimate.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which action ended the 1956 Constitution's operation in October 1958? *(Remember)*
   - A) Amendment through the ordinary parliamentary process
   - B) Abrogation accompanying the declaration of martial law
   - C) Expiry after completion of a fixed constitutional term
   - D) Replacement by the interim settlement of April 1972

2. A chart says the 1973 Constitution was revived after an interruption. What does "revived" mean here? *(Understand)*
   - A) A different permanent constitution replaced the framework.
   - B) The original framework had operated without interruption.
   - C) The existing framework resumed operation after being set aside.
   - D) An election removed the need for constitutional institutions.

3. A trainee sees soldiers rescuing families during floods in Sindh. What further evidence is needed before classifying the situation as martial law? *(Apply)*
   - A) Whether military authority has displaced civilian constitutional government
   - B) Whether the rescue teams are using vehicles supplied by the armed forces
   - C) Whether pupils can identify the military ranks of the rescue workers
   - D) Whether the flooded district has more than one government school

4. What was the purpose of the interim constitutional arrangement of 1972? *(Understand)*
   - A) To make constitution-writing unnecessary after the 1971 crisis
   - B) To revive the abrogated 1956 text without further deliberation
   - C) To substitute an advisory council for the elected Assembly
   - D) To provide governing rules while a permanent settlement was prepared

5. Which action occurred on 10 April 1973? *(Remember)*
   - A) Commencement of the permanent Constitution
   - B) Passage of the Constitution by the National Assembly
   - C) Introduction of the Federal Shariat Court
   - D) Insertion of the Objectives Resolution as Article 2A

6. A lesson describes the Council of Islamic Ideology as an institution that gives recommendations on relevant legislative questions. Which constitutional role does that description identify? *(Apply)*
   - A) Conducting general elections for the National Assembly
   - B) Replacing the Cabinet in running ordinary administration
   - C) Advising within the constitutional law-making framework
   - D) Acting as the second House of the federal legislature

7. Which date belongs to the insertion of Article 2A rather than the original 1973 settlement? *(Remember)*
   - A) 1985
   - B) 1972
   - C) 1956
   - D) 1962

8. Which comparison describes the main executive systems of the three permanent constitutions? *(Remember)*
   - A) 1956 presidential; 1962 parliamentary; 1973 presidential
   - B) 1956 parliamentary; 1962 parliamentary; 1973 presidential
   - C) 1956 presidential; 1962 presidential; 1973 parliamentary
   - D) 1956 parliamentary; 1962 presidential; 1973 parliamentary

9. A school has a written complaints rule, a designated council and evidence that complaints receive no reply. Which layer does the missing reply most directly concern? *(Apply)*
   - A) The date on which the rule was written
   - B) The historical identity of the rule's author
   - C) The implementation of the rule in practice
   - D) The existence of the rule as a written commitment

10. Which conclusion is best supported by the revival of the 1973 framework after military interruptions? *(Understand)*
   - A) The framework experienced no interruptions after its commencement.
   - B) The framework remained a durable reference despite interruptions.
   - C) Every public institution complied with every provision throughout.
   - D) Its parliamentary design alone explains all later political outcomes.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, distinguish abrogation from abeyance and give one dated Pakistani example of each. *(Understand)*
2. In four sentences, compare the significance of the 1985 and 1988 elections for restoration after 1977. Identify one limit of using a single election date to describe the whole process. *(Analyze)*
3. A fictional handout calls a lawful parliamentary change of Prime Minister a constitutional breakdown. In three sentences, explain the missing distinction and identify the evidence you would check. *(Analyze)*
4. Give two constitutional problems facing the post-1971 settlement. In one additional sentence, explain why temporary governing arrangements were useful while representatives negotiated. *(Understand)*
5. In three sentences, connect committee drafting, Assembly passage and commencement to their different purposes in 1972-1973. Include the passage and commencement dates. *(Understand)*
6. In four sentences, distinguish the Council of Islamic Ideology from a legislature and from the Federal Shariat Court. Include one historical date that prevents a later institution being projected into the original settlement. *(Analyze)*
7. A fictional poster states, "Islam is the state religion, so the Constitution has no religious safeguards for non-Muslim citizens." In three sentences, identify the unsupported inference and refer to two relevant article numbers. *(Analyze)*
8. Compare the historical fates of the 1956, 1962 and 1973 constitutions in three sentences. Distinguish the end of a text from interrupted operation. *(Understand)*
9. In four sentences, use the text-institutions-practice method to explain why inserting an education commitment does not prove that every child can attend school. Identify one relevant form of practical evidence. *(Analyze)*
10. In three sentences, give one reasoned explanation for the durability of the 1973 framework, one supporting historical fact and one limit of your explanation. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Compare the 1958 and 1999 interruptions in 400-500 words. Explain what happened to the governing text, what authority changed and how later milestones contributed to restoration. Judge the claim that an election date alone identifies complete democratic recovery. Use at least two dated milestones and state the limit of your evidence. *(Evaluate)*
2. Prepare a 400-500-word explanation of the making of the 1973 Constitution for a trainee teacher. Connect the post-1971 problems to the drafting sequence and one institutional choice. Then explain how Islamic provisions fit within representative government and citizenship safeguards. Distinguish one later addition from the original settlement. *(Analyze)*
3. Evaluate, in 400-500 words, why the 1973 framework lasted longer than its predecessors. Compare all three constitutional fates, weigh at least two possible factors and discuss the counter-evidence of interruption. Explain why duration alone cannot prove successful implementation. *(Evaluate)*
4. Create a plan for a 30-minute teacher-training lesson that corrects three claims: "all constitutions ended the same way", "every article in a later edition dates from 1973", and "written rights prove complete implementation". Include a worked example, a collaborative task and one check for each claim. Justify your sequence in a 150-word note. Use material from all three topics. *(Create)*
5. A fictional exhibition has four labels: "1970: election therefore completed restoration"; "1973: all Islamic institutions introduced together"; "1985: entirely new permanent constitution"; and "1973 framework survives: every provision always implemented". Write a 450-word audit that replaces each label, identifies the evidence needed and explains one connection between political recovery, constitutional design and implementation. Address all three topics. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - the 1956 Constitution was abrogated when martial law was declared in October 1958.
2. C - revival resumes the existing framework after interrupted operation; it does not establish uninterrupted compliance.
3. A - the classification concerns governing authority, rather than the presence of military personnel in a relief operation.
4. D - an interim arrangement provided rules while a permanent framework was prepared.
5. B - passage was on 10 April; commencement followed on 14 August 1973.
6. C - the Council's recommendations are an advisory function within the constitutional scheme.
7. A - Article 2A was inserted in 1985, although the Objectives Resolution already appeared in the preamble.
8. D - the main systems were parliamentary, presidential and parliamentary respectively.
9. C - the absence of a reply is evidence about practice under the stated rule.
10. B - revival supports a limited claim of durability, not uninterrupted compliance or a single-cause explanation.

Award one mark per selected option. In self-study, also inspect your reason. A guessed letter may earn the practice mark, but it does not show that you can explain the distinction. Return to the topic if your reason treats abrogation as revival, an election as complete restoration, or a written promise as proof of fulfilment.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Each response carries four marks. Award points for the stated ideas rather than identical wording. The response bounds encourage concise explanation; a longer response does not gain extra marks. Do not reward a confident generalisation when it contradicts the dated evidence.

1. **Model:** Abrogation ends a constitution as the governing framework, as in the 1956 Constitution's abrogation in 1958. Abeyance interrupts normal operation without treating the framework as permanently replaced. The 1973 Constitution was held in abeyance after the 1977 takeover and later revived. **Marks (4):** one for abrogation, one for abeyance, one for the accurate abrogation example and one for the accurate abeyance example.
2. **Model:** The 1985 non-party election restored elected assemblies under an altered 1973 framework. Martial law ended later in 1985, but Zia remained President and army chief. The 1988 party-based election was a later stage towards elected civilian government. One election date cannot by itself show that normal constitutional checks and civilian authority fully returned. **Marks (4):** one for the 1985 election's character, one for the remaining limit or separate end of martial law, one for 1988 and one for the reasoned warning about a single date.
3. **Model:** A change within parliamentary rules differs from an action that overrides them. The handout has not shown that the constitutional procedure was displaced. Check how the change occurred and whether the required constitutional authority and assembly procedure were followed. **Marks (4):** one for change within rules, one for displacement outside rules, one for applying the distinction to the handout and one for identifying relevant procedural evidence.
4. **Model:** The settlement needed an accepted basis for representative government after the failed transfer of power and the crisis of 1971. It also needed arrangements for provincial participation and the distribution of authority. Interim rules allowed government to function while representatives negotiated the permanent settlement. **Marks (4):** one for each of two relevant problems, one for explaining their constitutional significance and one for the interim arrangement's purpose. Accept other supported problems, including the place of Islamic commitments and citizenship safeguards.
5. **Model:** A committee prepared proposals for representatives to examine and debate. The Assembly's passage on 10 April 1973 established its decision on the text. Commencement on 14 August 1973 brought the framework into operation under the stated arrangements. **Marks (4):** one for drafting, one for passage with its date, one for commencement with its date and one for clearly distinguishing the actions. Authentication on 12 April may be added but does not substitute for a missing required stage.
6. **Model:** The Council gives advice and recommendations on relevant legislative questions; it is not Parliament. A legislature makes laws through constitutional procedures. The Federal Shariat Court is a judicial institution, not the Council under another name. It was introduced in 1980, so it should not be described as part of the original 1973 institutional arrangement. **Marks (4):** one for advisory role, one for legislative distinction, one for judicial distinction and one for the correct historical layer.
7. **Model:** State religion does not imply the absence of citizenship safeguards. Article 20 concerns religious freedom, subject to its qualifications, while Article 22 contains safeguards concerning religious education. These articles must be read alongside the state's Islamic identity. **Marks (4):** one for identifying the unsupported inference, one for each accurately explained article and one for connecting them to the whole framework. Accept Article 25 as an alternative when its equality principle is accurately linked to the claim.
8. **Model:** The 1956 Constitution was abrogated in 1958. The 1962 Constitution was abrogated in 1969. The 1973 framework was revived and amended after interruptions, so survival of its identity does not establish uninterrupted operation. **Marks (4):** one for each earlier fate, one for revival of the 1973 framework and one for the survival-operation distinction.
9. **Model:** A constitutional education provision states a legal commitment. Responsible institutions must turn it into laws, administration and services. Evidence about attendance, school access or functioning facilities is needed to examine practice. The insertion of Article 25A in 2010 therefore does not by itself prove that every child could attend school. **Marks (4):** one for each layer and one for a relevant observable indicator linked to the limited conclusion. Accept other suitable indicators; do not demand invented national statistics.
10. **Model:** Broad agreement and federal parliamentary institutions may have helped make the 1973 framework a common reference. Its revival after the 1977 and 1999 interruptions supports that interpretation. Revival does not prove that design was the only cause, or that implementation was continuous. **Marks (4):** one for a plausible factor, one for supporting evidence, one for explaining how they connect and one for a meaningful limit. Accept a different reasoned explanation supported by the unit.

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ carries ten marks across four criteria. The highest band requires explanation supported by accurate evidence. Listing several dates without showing their relevance cannot receive full analysis marks. Accept a conclusion different from the model direction when it addresses counter-evidence and respects the historical record.

|---|---|---|---|
| Fate of texts (3) | 3: distinguishes 1958 abrogation and 1999 abeyance, then connects replacement or revival to each | 1-2: identifies one accurately or names both without explaining consequences | 0: treats both as identical replacements |
