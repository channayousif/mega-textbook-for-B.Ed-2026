# Education for Ideology and the Constitution - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

This unit asked what education has to do with the ideology and the Constitution of Pakistan. Civic education prepares pupils for the everyday work of citizenship. It connects civic knowledge (the Constitution, Fundamental Rights, rule of law, institutions of government), civic skills (collaborative problem-solving, decision-making, dialogue) and civic values and dispositions (civic mindedness, equal dignity, mutual respect). A school that teaches only about elections has taught the smallest part of citizenship.

National integration in Pakistan is unity in diversity. Under Fundamental Rights, Article 28 protects the right of citizens to preserve distinct languages, scripts and cultures. Under Principles of Policy, Article 33 directs the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices, and Article 36 safeguards minorities. In Part XII, Article 251 establishes Urdu as the national language and permits Provincial Assemblies to legislate measures for provincial languages. Integration is not assimilation: it does not ask a Sindhi, Balochi, Punjabi or Pashto speaker to stop being one. It asks every citizen to stand on equal ground under one constitutional order.

Teachers carry this civic responsibility in the daily life of the classroom. Article 4, Article 5(2) and Article 10A frame the rule of law, equal protection and due process. Article 25A and Article 37(b) frame the constitutional duty to educate. A teacher who is fair, non-partisan and reasoned makes the classroom a living model of the constitutional order. The unit ends with a design task: a short classroom activity that teaches one constitutional value through experience.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the topic, not a list of definitions. All names, schools and cases in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of the following best defines civic education? *(Remember)*
   - A) Teaching pupils how to vote in an election
   - B) Education that prepares students to understand their rights and responsibilities and take part in democratic life
   - C) Teaching the names of government departments and historical dates
   - D) Teaching pupils to support a political party
2. According to Dean (2005), effective citizenship education in schools requires developing knowledge alongside: *(Remember)*
   - A) Relevant skills, values and dispositions, and participatory experience
   - B) Rote memorisation and examination drills
   - C) Strict obedience and political compliance
   - D) Only factual recall of government departments
3. What is the main difference between assimilation and integration? *(Understand)*
   - A) Assimilation respects diversity; integration removes it
   - B) Assimilation demands one uniform identity; integration keeps diversity and unites through equal rights
   - C) Assimilation is a school policy; integration is a government policy
   - D) There is no difference between the two
4. Article 33 of the Constitution requires the State to discourage: *(Remember)*
   - A) The teaching of provincial languages in schools
   - B) Parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices
   - C) Freedom of trade and commerce between provinces
   - D) The study of constitutional rights
5. Under Article 251(3), a Provincial Assembly is permitted to: *(Remember)*
   - A) Abolish the national language within the province
   - B) Prescribe measures for teaching, promoting and using a provincial language alongside Urdu
   - C) Restrict citizens from speaking regional languages at home
   - D) Prohibit the federal government from using Urdu
6. A secondary school in Hyderabad organizes a collaborative project where pupils investigate how water from the Indus river system supports agriculture across different provinces and districts, discussing mutual interdependence. This classroom activity primarily promotes: *(Apply)*
   - A) Forced cultural assimilation
   - B) National integration through shared knowledge and mutual cooperation
   - C) Partisan political advocacy
   - D) Rote memorisation of geography terms
7. Article 25A of the Constitution guarantees every child aged five to sixteen the right to: *(Remember)*
   - A) Free and compulsory education
   - B) Guaranteed public sector employment
   - C) Admission to any private college without fee
   - D) Unrestricted participation in political parties
8. A pupil says, "Civic education is only about elections." Which response best corrects this? *(Apply)*
   - A) "You are right. Elections are the most important civic act."
   - B) "Civic life happens every day, in how we treat neighbours and use public institutions."
   - C) "Civic education is not part of the school curriculum."
   - D) "Only adults need civic education."
9. Dean (2005) identified traditional transmission-style civics teaching in Pakistani schools as producing: *(Remember)*
   - A) Highly participatory democratic leaders
   - B) Passive citizens who could repeat facts for examinations but lacked civic skills and dispositions
   - C) Independent critical thinkers
   - D) Community dispute mediators
10. When two pupils in a group dispute disagree over the use of shared materials, the teacher hears both sides calmly, applies a collectively agreed classroom rule, and avoids arbitrary penalty. This practice primarily models: *(Apply)*
    - A) The rule of law and procedural due process
    - B) Cultural assimilation
    - C) Partisan alignment
    - D) Transmission-model instruction

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain in two or three sentences why civic education is more than learning about elections. *(Understand)*
2. Name the core components of civic education (knowledge, skills, values and dispositions) and explain why they must be joined together. *(Understand)*
3. Explain the difference between assimilation and integration, using one example from school life. *(Understand)*
4. What does Article 33 ask the State to discourage, and why does this matter for national integration? *(Understand)*
5. Explain in two or three sentences how Article 28 and Article 251 balance national unity with respect for linguistic diversity. *(Understand)*
6. Dean (2005) argues that traditional civics teaching produces "passive citizenship". Analyse how transmission pedagogy leads to passive citizenship rather than democratic participation, using one classroom example. *(Analyze)*
7. A school teaches only in Urdu and tells pupils that speaking a regional language in school is "backward". Analyse how this practice affects national integration. *(Analyze)*
8. A teacher says, "I should not teach about the Constitution because it is political." Analyse two reasons why this view is mistaken. *(Analyze)*
9. Analyse how a school assembly that presents the cultures of all provinces could build national integration. Use Article 33 in your answer. *(Analyze)*
10. Compare the roles of the teacher and the Constitution in protecting a child's right to education. Use Article 25A and Article 37(b). *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A colleague says, "National integration means everyone must speak one language and follow one culture." Analyse this claim. Use Article 28, Article 33, Article 251 and one school example, and reach a judgement. *(Analyze)*
2. Evaluate the claim: "Civic education in Pakistani schools should focus on knowledge of the Constitution rather than on skills and values." Use Dean (2005), at least one constitutional article, and at least one classroom example. *(Evaluate)*
3. Design a 20-minute classroom activity that teaches one constitutional value to a Class 9 pupil. Your design must name the value and its supporting constitutional article, outline the activity steps, provide one reflection question, and explain how the activity teaches the value through experience. *(Create)*
4. Analyse how a teacher's daily classroom practice can either strengthen or weaken national integration. Use one example of each, and refer to Article 33. *(Analyze)*
5. This unit says a teacher is a "civic actor". Evaluate this claim by connecting it to one idea from Unit 1 (ideology and constitution), one from Unit 5 (Fundamental Rights or Principles of Policy) and one from Unit 6 (structure of government). Reach a judgement about how far the claim is true. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Compare your own attempt with it only after you have tried every question. Answers may use different examples and still earn full marks if the reasoning is sound.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Civic education prepares students for rights, responsibilities and democratic participation. A describes only elections, C describes the old transmission model and D is not its purpose.
2. **A.** Dean (2005) emphasizes that effective citizenship requires a body of knowledge along with relevant skills (such as problem-solving), values and dispositions (such as civic mindedness), and active participatory experience. B, C and D reflect the passive transmission model she criticized.
3. **B.** Assimilation demands one uniform identity; integration keeps diversity and unites through equal rights. A reverses the two.
4. **B.** Article 33 specifically directs the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices among citizens.
5. **B.** Article 251(3) is permissive: a Provincial Assembly may by law prescribe measures for the teaching, promotion and use of a provincial language without prejudice to the national language.
6. **B.** The project engages pupils in understanding regional interdependence and shared national resources, which builds integration without erasing regional identity.
7. **A.** Article 25A establishes the Fundamental Right to free and compulsory education for children aged five to sixteen.
8. **B.** Civic life happens daily, in treatment of neighbours and use of public institutions. A accepts the misconception; C and D are false.
9. **B.** Dean (2005) found that transmission teaching produced passive citizenship where pupils could recite facts but lacked problem-solving skills, critical consciousness and civic mindedness.
10. **A.** By listening fairly, applying agreed rules consistently, and respecting pupils' dignity, the teacher models procedural fairness and the rule of law (Articles 4, 5(2) and 10A).

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Civic education is more than elections because citizenship is daily. It teaches knowledge of the Constitution, skills for public life and values such as equal dignity. Elections are one event; civic life is how we live together all year. **Marks (3):** 1 for the daily point; 1 for one dimension; 1 for the contrast with elections.
2. **Model:** Civic knowledge (understanding constitutional rights, rule of law and institutions), civic skills (problem-solving, decision-making and peaceful dialogue) and civic values and dispositions (civic mindedness, equal dignity and mutual respect). They must be joined because knowledge alone cannot act in public life, skills without democratic values can be misused, and values without knowledge lack constitutional grounding. **Marks (4):** 1 mark for each component explained (knowledge, skills, values, up to 3 marks); 1 mark for explaining why they must be joined together.
3. **Model:** Assimilation asks every group to give up its language and customs for one identity. Integration keeps diversity and unites through equal rights. Example: a school that teaches only Urdu and calls a regional language "backward" is assimilating; a school that teaches Urdu and also respects Sindhi or Pashto is integrating. **Marks (3):** 1 for each definition; 1 for a fitting example.
4. **Model:** Article 33 asks the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices. This matters for integration because prejudice is what divides citizens; removing it lets equal citizenship work. **Marks (3):** 1 for the article's content; 1 for the link to prejudice; 1 for the link to integration.
5. **Model:** Article 251(1) designates Urdu as the national language to provide a shared medium for communication and unity across the federation. At the same time, Article 28 guarantees citizens the Fundamental Right to preserve and promote distinct languages and cultures, while Article 251(3) allows Provincial Assemblies to legislate for provincial languages. Together, they achieve national cohesion without suppressing linguistic diversity. **Marks (3):** 1 for Urdu as a shared national medium; 1 for Article 28/251(3) protecting and promoting linguistic diversity; 1 for the synthesis of unity in diversity.
6. **Model:** Transmission pedagogy positions pupils as passive recipients of textbook content rather than active civic actors. Pupils memorise facts about government structures to pass tests, but never practise inquiry, deliberation or collective problem-solving. For example, a class that merely memorises the three tiers of local government cannot identify which local agency fixes a sanitation hazard outside the school gate or how to petition for repair. **Marks (4):** 2 for analysing how rote transmission causes civic passivity; 2 for a concrete, fitting classroom example.
7. **Model:** Describing a regional language as "backward" undermines pupil dignity, fosters alienation, and pushes forced assimilation. This practice conflicts with the Fundamental Right in Article 28 protecting distinct languages and cultures, and violates the Principle of Policy in Article 33 directing the State to discourage provincial and parochial prejudices. **Marks (4):** 2 for analysing the damaging effect on pupils and national integration; 2 for the constitutional grounding (Articles 28 and 33).
8. **Model:** First, the Constitution is the basic law of citizenship, not a party programme, so teaching it is not partisan. Second, a teacher who avoids it leaves pupils to learn citizenship from less reliable sources. Civic education teaches how the Constitution works, not what party to support. **Marks (4):** 2 for each reason.
9. **Model:** An assembly that presents all provinces' cultures teaches pupils to know and respect diversity. Article 33 tells the State to discourage prejudice, and a school that celebrates every province puts that into practice. Pupils learn that a classmate's culture is part of the nation, not a threat to it. **Marks (4):** 2 for the mechanism; 2 for the Article 33 link.
10. **Model:** Article 25A gives the child a right to free and compulsory education, and Article 37(b) tells the State to remove illiteracy and provide secondary education. The teacher carries this into daily practice by teaching fairly, keeping a child in school and explaining the right. The Constitution sets the duty; the teacher makes it real in the classroom. **Marks (4):** 2 for the articles; 2 for the teacher's role.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.
