# Education for Ideology and the Constitution - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

This unit asked what education has to do with the ideology and the Constitution of Pakistan. Civic education prepares pupils for the everyday work of citizenship. It joins civic knowledge (the Constitution, the rights, the institutions), civic skills (deliberation, peaceful conflict resolution, critical media literacy, collaborative problem-solving) and civic values (social justice, equal dignity, pluralism, human rights). A school that teaches only about elections has taught the smallest part of citizenship.

National integration in Pakistan is unity in diversity. Article 33 tells the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices. Article 36 safeguards minorities. Article 251 declares Urdu the national language and lets a Provincial Assembly protect a provincial language. Integration is not assimilation: it does not ask a Sindhi or Pashto speaker to stop being one. It asks every citizen to stand on equal ground.

Teachers carry this responsibility in the daily life of the classroom. Article 37(b) and Article 25A frame the State's duty to educate. Article 5 frames the citizen's duty of loyalty and obedience to the Constitution. A teacher who is fair, non-partisan and reasoned makes the classroom a small model of the constitutional order. The unit ends with a design task: a short classroom activity that teaches one constitutional value through experience.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the topic, not a list of definitions. All names, schools and cases in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of the following best defines civic education? *(Remember)*
   - A) Teaching pupils how to vote in an election
   - B) Education that prepares students to understand their rights and responsibilities and take part in democratic life
   - C) Teaching the names of government departments and historical dates
   - D) Teaching pupils to support a political party
2. According to Dean (2005), which three dimensions make up meaningful civic education? *(Remember)*
   - A) Knowledge, skills and values
   - B) Reading, writing and arithmetic
   - C) History, geography and civics
   - D) Knowledge, discipline and obedience
3. What is the main difference between assimilation and integration? *(Understand)*
   - A) Assimilation respects diversity; integration removes it
   - B) Assimilation demands one uniform identity; integration keeps diversity and unites through equal rights
   - C) Assimilation is a school policy; integration is a government policy
   - D) There is no difference between the two
4. Article 33 of the Constitution requires the State to discourage: *(Remember)*
   - A) Provincial languages
   - B) Parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices
   - C) The study of the Constitution
   - D) National unity
5. Article 251(3) allows a Provincial Assembly to prescribe measures for: *(Understand)*
   - A) Abolishing the national language
   - B) Teaching, promoting and using a provincial language alongside Urdu
   - C) Declaring independence from the federation
   - D) Setting its own foreign policy
6. A teacher in a Karachi school asks a mixed group of pupils to prepare a talk on "the river that feeds my province". This activity mainly promotes: *(Apply)*
   - A) Assimilation
   - B) National integration through shared knowledge of diverse regions
   - C) Religious instruction
   - D) Preparation for a geography examination
7. Article 25A of the Constitution gives every child aged five to sixteen the right to: *(Remember)*
   - A) Free and compulsory education
   - B) A government job
   - C) Vote in an election
   - D) Freedom of speech without limits
8. A pupil says, "Civic education is only about elections." Which response best corrects this? *(Apply)*
   - A) "You are right. Elections are the most important civic act."
   - B) "Civic life happens every day, in how we treat neighbours and use public institutions."
   - C) "Civic education is not part of the school curriculum."
   - D) "Only adults need civic education."
9. Dean (2005) described traditional civics instruction in Pakistani schools as producing: *(Understand)*
   - A) Active citizens
   - B) Passive citizens who could repeat facts but lacked skills and values
   - C) Skilled political leaders
   - D) Strong community organisers
10. A teacher handles a classroom dispute by asking both pupils to state what happened, letting the class discuss what fairness requires, and following a rule the class agreed earlier. This mainly models: *(Apply)*
    - A) The rule of law
    - B) Assimilation
    - C) Partisanship
    - D) Rote learning

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain in two or three sentences why civic education is more than learning about elections. *(Understand)*
2. Name the three dimensions of civic education and explain in one sentence why each matters. *(Understand)*
3. Explain the difference between assimilation and integration, using one example from school life. *(Understand)*
4. What does Article 33 ask the State to discourage, and why does this matter for national integration? *(Understand)*
5. Explain in two sentences how Article 251 protects both national unity and provincial languages. *(Understand)*
6. Dean (2005) argues that traditional civics teaching produces "passive citizenship". Analyse what she means, using one example of a passive classroom practice. *(Analyze)*
7. A school teaches only in Urdu and tells pupils that speaking a regional language in school is "backward". Analyse how this practice affects national integration. *(Analyze)*
8. A teacher says, "I should not teach about the Constitution because it is political." Analyse two reasons why this view is mistaken. *(Analyze)*
9. Analyse how a school assembly that presents the cultures of all provinces could build national integration. Use Article 33 in your answer. *(Analyze)*
10. Compare the roles of the teacher and the Constitution in protecting a child's right to education. Use Article 25A and Article 37(b). *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A colleague says, "National integration means everyone must speak one language and follow one culture." Analyse this claim. Use Article 33, Article 251 and one school example, and reach a judgement. *(Analyze)*
2. Evaluate the claim: "Civic education in Pakistani schools should focus on knowledge of the Constitution rather than on skills and values." Use Dean (2005) and at least one example. *(Evaluate)*
3. Design a 20-minute classroom activity that teaches one constitutional value to a Class 9 pupil. Explain how the activity works and why it teaches the value through experience. *(Create)*
4. Analyse how a teacher's daily classroom practice can either strengthen or weaken national integration. Use one example of each, and refer to Article 33. *(Analyze)*
5. This unit says a teacher is a "civic actor". Evaluate this claim by connecting it to one idea from Unit 1 (ideology and constitution), one from Unit 5 (Fundamental Rights or Principles of Policy) and one from Unit 6 (structure of government). Reach a judgement about how far the claim is true. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Compare your own attempt with it only after you have tried every question. Answers may use different examples and still earn full marks if the reasoning is sound.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Civic education prepares students for rights, responsibilities and democratic participation. A describes only elections, C describes the old transmission model and D is not its purpose.
2. **A.** Dean (2005) names civic knowledge, civic skills and civic values. The other options list school subjects or qualities she did not propose.
3. **B.** Assimilation demands one uniform identity; integration keeps diversity and unites through equal rights. A reverses the two.
4. **B.** Article 33 names parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices. The other options are not in the article.
5. **B.** Article 251(3) lets a Provincial Assembly prescribe measures for teaching, promoting and using a provincial language alongside Urdu.
6. **B.** The activity joins pupils across regions through shared knowledge, which is integration. A is the opposite; C and D are not the main point.
7. **A.** Article 25A gives the right to free and compulsory education for ages five to sixteen.
8. **B.** Civic life happens daily, in treatment of neighbours and use of public institutions. A accepts the misconception; C and D are false.
9. **B.** Dean (2005) found that transmission teaching produced passive citizens who could repeat facts but lacked skills and values.
10. **A.** The teacher follows a fair, agreed rule and hears both sides, which models the rule of law.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Civic education is more than elections because citizenship is daily. It teaches knowledge of the Constitution, skills for public life and values such as equal dignity. Elections are one event; civic life is how we live together all year. **Marks (3):** 1 for the daily point; 1 for one dimension; 1 for the contrast with elections.
2. **Model:** Civic knowledge (understanding the Constitution and rights), civic skills (deliberation, conflict resolution, media literacy) and civic values (social justice, equal dignity, pluralism). Each matters because knowledge without skills cannot act, skills without values can harm, and values without knowledge cannot find the right article. **Marks (4):** 1 for each dimension named; 1 for a reason why each matters.
3. **Model:** Assimilation asks every group to give up its language and customs for one identity. Integration keeps diversity and unites through equal rights. Example: a school that teaches only Urdu and calls a regional language "backward" is assimilating; a school that teaches Urdu and also respects Sindhi or Pashto is integrating. **Marks (3):** 1 for each definition; 1 for a fitting example.
4. **Model:** Article 33 asks the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices. This matters for integration because prejudice is what divides citizens; removing it lets equal citizenship work. **Marks (3):** 1 for the article's content; 1 for the link to prejudice; 1 for the link to integration.
5. **Model:** Article 251(1) makes Urdu the national language, which gives all citizens a shared medium. Article 251(3) lets a Provincial Assembly protect a provincial language, so no language is forced to disappear. Unity and diversity are protected together. **Marks (3):** 1 for Urdu as shared; 1 for provincial protection; 1 for the combined point.
6. **Model:** Dean (2005) means that transmission teaching makes pupils passive recipients of facts. They can repeat the names of departments or dates but cannot use them. Example: a class that copies the three tiers of local government and memorises them for a test, but cannot say who is responsible for a blocked drain. **Marks (4):** 2 for the meaning of passive citizenship; 2 for a fitting passive example.
7. **Model:** Telling pupils that a regional language is "backward" shames their identity and teaches them that diversity is a problem. This weakens integration because it pushes assimilation and breeds resentment. It also breaks Article 33's command to discourage prejudice and Article 251's protection of provincial languages. **Marks (4):** 2 for the effect on pupils; 2 for the constitutional link.
8. **Model:** First, the Constitution is the basic law of citizenship, not a party programme, so teaching it is not partisan. Second, a teacher who avoids it leaves pupils to learn citizenship from less reliable sources. Civic education teaches how the Constitution works, not what party to support. **Marks (4):** 2 for each reason.
9. **Model:** An assembly that presents all provinces' cultures teaches pupils to know and respect diversity. Article 33 tells the State to discourage prejudice, and a school that celebrates every province puts that into practice. Pupils learn that a classmate's culture is part of the nation, not a threat to it. **Marks (4):** 2 for the mechanism; 2 for the Article 33 link.
10. **Model:** Article 25A gives the child a right to free and compulsory education, and Article 37(b) tells the State to remove illiteracy and provide secondary education. The teacher carries this into daily practice by teaching fairly, keeping a child in school and explaining the right. The Constitution sets the duty; the teacher makes it real in the classroom. **Marks (4):** 2 for the articles; 2 for the teacher's role.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.
