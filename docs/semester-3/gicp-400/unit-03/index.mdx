# Constitution-Making, 1947–1962

<Figure id="fig-U3-5" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/gicp-400/unit-03/fig-U3-5.webp" alt="Trainee teachers in Sindh arrange a sequence of cards and check reference books together." width={1376} height={768} />

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, teacher candidates will be able to:

1. Explain how the Government of India Act 1935, as adapted by the Pakistan (Provisional Constitution) Order 1947, provided interim constitutional continuity for the newly independent state.
2. Analyze the core principles of the Objectives Resolution (1949) regarding state sovereignty, democratic representation, social justice, and minority safeguards, tracing its influence on subsequent constitutional texts.
3. Identify the salient features of the Constitution of 1956, evaluating its compromises on parliamentary democracy, federal structure, Islamic character, and provincial representation through the principle of parity.
4. Critically examine the structure of the Constitution of 1962, focusing on its strong presidential executive, the indirect electoral system of Basic Democracies, and its centralist federal design.
5. Evaluate the political, regional, and institutional factors that contributed to the abrogation of the 1962 Constitution in March 1969.
6. Compare and contrast the 1956 and 1962 constitutions across executive authority, legislative powers, judicial independence, federalism, and the protection of citizens' fundamental rights.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds directly upon concepts and historical contexts developed in earlier units of the course:

- The ideological foundations of Pakistan, including the relationship between Islamic values, statehood, and constitutional governance (Unit 1).
- The historical development of the Pakistan Movement, the political leadership of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah, and the demand for autonomous provincial governance articulated in the 1940 Lahore Resolution (Unit 2).
- Basic civic concepts of constitutional supremacy, the separation of powers between executive, legislature, and judiciary, and the difference between unitary and federal arrangements of government.

## In this unit

1. [From independence to the 1956 Constitution](./topic-01) - examining the interim constitutional framework under the adapted 1935 Act, the passage and significance of the 1949 Objectives Resolution, the prolonged deliberations of the First and Second Constituent Assemblies, and the salient features of the 1956 parliamentary Constitution.
2. [The 1962 Constitution](./topic-02) - exploring the imposition of martial law in 1958, the constitutional philosophy of the 1962 presidential system, the Basic Democracies indirect voting mechanism, the causes of its abrogation in 1969, and a systematic comparative analysis of the 1956 and 1962 constitutional models.

## How to use this unit

Read each classroom case, then study the explanation. Work with a partner on the activity. Try the recall questions without looking back, and use the checklist to find what you need to study again. The practicum task helps you teach these ideas at school.

After completing both topics, proceed to [the unit assessment](./unit-assessment). The bank includes ten multiple-choice questions, ten restricted-response questions, and five extended-response questions with complete model answers, marking schemes, and analytical rubrics to help you evaluate your constitutional knowledge and pedagogical reasoning.
