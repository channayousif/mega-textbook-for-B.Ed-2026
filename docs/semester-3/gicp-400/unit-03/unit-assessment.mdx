# Unit 3 Assessment: Constitution-Making, 1947–1962

## Unit summary

This unit investigated the foundational fifteen years of Pakistan's constitutional evolution, examining the legal transition from a British dominion to an independent republic and the complex ideological and structural debates that shaped early statehood.

In Topic 3.1, we analyzed the transition from colonial rule to the first written constitution. We established that independence did not occur in a legal vacuum: under Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act 1947, the Government of India Act 1935 was adapted via the Pakistan (Provisional Constitution) Order 1947 to serve as Pakistan's interim constitution, stripping British imperial discretion while maintaining civil administration, judiciary, and federal revenue machinery. We analyzed the Objectives Resolution, moved by Prime Minister Liaquat Ali Khan and adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 12 March 1949. We explored its main principles, emphasizing that sovereignty over the universe belongs to Allah Almighty alone, with state power delegated to the people's elected representatives as a sacred trust. Drawing upon Shah (2026), we established that the Resolution served as a philosophical compass and preamble across all three subsequent constitutions, rather than a legally effective code. We then traced the nine-year struggle to frame a permanent constitution, examining the Basic Principles Committee reports, the controversial 1954 dissolution of the First Constituent Assembly by Governor-General Ghulam Muhammad, the assent and jurisdiction issue in the Tamizuddin Khan case, and the establishment of the One Unit scheme in 1955. Finally, we examined the salient features of the 1956 Constitution, which created a federal, parliamentary Islamic Republic with a single-chamber National Assembly with 300 general seats and 10 reserved seats for women based on equal wing representation despite unequal populations between East and West Pakistan, enforceable fundamental rights in Part II, and a rule against laws conflicting with Islam under Article 198.

In Topic 3.2, we examined the abrogation of the 1956 Constitution following the declaration of martial law on 7 October 1958 and the subsequent promulgation of the Constitution of 1962 by Field Marshal Muhammad Ayub Khan on 1 March 1962. We analyzed the structure of the 1962 presidential system, which concentrated executive, appointment, and ordinance-making authority in the President, while curtailing the National Assembly's control over recurring budget expenditures. We evaluated the Basic Democracies system, in which 80,000 local councillors acted as an exclusive indirect Electoral College for national office, limiting direct public choice at national level. We traced how public outrage forced the regime to pass the First Amendment Act 1963 (Act I of 1964), restoring court-enforceable fundamental rights and the title "Islamic Republic of Pakistan". We explored the causes of the 1962 Constitution's abrogation in March 1969, analyzing the corruption of the Basic Democracies, regional and economic disparity between East and West Pakistan, the 1965 presidential election controversy, and the nationwide popular uprising of 1968–1969 that forced Ayub Khan to resign and transfer power to General Yahya Khan. Finally, we conducted a systematic comparative analysis of the 1956 and 1962 constitutions, evaluating how differences in executive accountability, legislative budget authority, and electoral mechanics influenced democratic development in Pakistan.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Under Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act 1947, which legal document served as the adapted interim constitution of Pakistan at independence? *(Remember)*
   - A) The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946
   - B) The Government of India Act 1935
   - C) The Objectives Resolution of 1949
   - D) The Lahore Resolution of 1940

2. What core constitutional doctrine regarding state sovereignty was established by the Objectives Resolution on 12 March 1949? *(Understand)*
   - A) Sovereignty belongs to the Governor-General without limits.
   - B) Sovereignty belongs to the Constituent Assembly without limits.
   - C) Sovereignty belongs to Allah; state authority through the people is a sacred trust.
   - D) Sovereign power belongs unconditionally to parliament without moral or constitutional limits.

3. Why did the Federal Court reverse the Sindh Chief Court decision in the Tamizuddin Khan case in 1955? *(Understand)*
   - A) It held that the 1956 Constitution had replaced the interim framework.
   - B) It held that the law granting the requested writ jurisdiction lacked the Governor-General's assent.
   - C) It held that the Objectives Resolution had already abolished the courts.
   - D) It held that only the provincial assembly could make a national constitution.

4. Under the Constitution of 1956, how was legislative representation divided between East Pakistan and West Pakistan in the National Assembly? *(Remember)*
   - A) Strictly according to population, giving East Pakistan 175 seats and West Pakistan 125 seats.
   - B) Under the principle of parity, assigning exactly 150 general seats to each province.
   - C) Through a bicameral compromise with an upper house representing territories and a lower house for population.
   - D) Through equal seats for every western province and East Pakistan separately.

5. A secondary civics teacher asks students why the adapted Government of India Act 1935 had to be amended by the Pakistan (Provisional Constitution) Order 1947. Which explanation is historically accurate? *(Apply)*
   - A) To eliminate the discretionary powers of the British Crown and Governor-General so that the new government operated under cabinet responsibility.
   - B) To replace the interim framework with a permanent republican constitution.
   - C) To turn the Objectives Resolution into the complete interim constitution.
   - D) To give the British Parliament continuing control of Pakistan’s domestic laws.

6. What electoral mechanism was established under the Constitution of 1962 to elect the President and the National Assembly? *(Remember)*
   - A) Direct voting by adult citizens for all national offices
   - B) An indirect Electoral College consisting of 80,000 Basic Democrats
   - C) An electoral college formed by members of the national and provincial assemblies
   - D) A single national referendum approving each assembly candidate

7. How did the Constitution of 1962 restrict the financial authority of the National Assembly over state revenues? *(Understand)*
   - A) The Assembly could reduce every spending item but could not debate taxes.
   - B) The Assembly could vote on recurring expenditure but not on new expenditure.
   - C) The Assembly could vote only on new expenditures, leaving recurring and charged expenditures beyond parliamentary reduction.
   - D) The Assembly could dismiss ministers through an ordinary budget vote.

8. When the Constitution of 1962 was first promulgated on 1 March 1962, how did it treat citizens' fundamental rights? *(Understand)*
   - A) It fully incorporated Part II court-enforceable fundamental rights with immediate High Court writ enforcement.
   - B) It omitted enforceable fundamental rights, providing only advisory Principles of Law-Making that courts could not enforce.
   - C) It protected rights only through a chapter identical to that of 1956.
   - D) It left the 1956 rights chapter in force without changing its legal status.

9. An educational historian observes that the 1962 presidential system lacked democratic feedback loops. Which institutional feature best illustrates this structural defect? *(Apply)*
   - A) The existence of regular parliamentary question hours where ministers answered backbenchers' queries.
   - B) The creation of an independent Election Commission conducting direct secret ballots across all union councils.
   - C) The cabinet answering to the President and voters choosing national representatives through local electors.
   - D) The requirement that all cabinet ministers hold elected seats in the National Assembly.

10. A trainee teacher asks why Field Marshal Ayub Khan's transfer of power to General Yahya Khan on 25 March 1969 was unconstitutional. What provision of the 1962 Constitution did this act violate? *(Apply)*
    - A) The succession rule directed the Speaker of the National Assembly to act as President when that office became vacant.
    - B) The succession rule required the provincial governors to elect a new President.
    - C) The succession rule required the cabinet to appoint a new Prime Minister.
    - D) The succession rule required the electoral college to take over executive office.

5. In three sentences, explain how the One Unit scheme of 1955 altered the political and administrative landscape of West Pakistan. *(Understand)*
6. State two significant changes introduced by the Constitution (First Amendment) Act 1963 (Act I of 1964) to the original 1962 Constitution. *(Understand)*
7. In two sentences, contrast the method of electing the head of state under the 1956 Constitution with the electoral method under the 1962 Constitution. *(Apply)*
8. A trainee teacher asserts that the 1962 Constitution established a parliamentary form of government. Give two structural reasons why this assertion is incorrect. *(Apply)*
9. Analyze two key reasons why the Basic Democracies system alienated the majority of the population in East Pakistan. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act 1947 provided that the Government of India Act 1935, as adapted, would serve as the interim constitution.
2. C - The Objectives Resolution explicitly stated that sovereignty over the universe belongs to Allah alone, delegated to the people as a sacred trust.
3. B - The Federal Court held that the law conferring the relevant writ jurisdiction lacked assent; necessity arose in the later Governor-General’s Reference.
4. B - Under the principle of parity, the 1956 Constitution allocated exactly 150 general seats to East Pakistan and 150 general seats to West Pakistan in a single-chamber assembly.
5. A - The provisional constitution order eliminated the autocratic discretionary vetoes of the colonial Governor-General, establishing cabinet-based governance.
6. B - The 1962 Constitution established an indirect Electoral College composed of 80,000 Basic Democrats (40,000 per wing) to elect the President and assemblies.
7. C - The 1962 National Assembly could vote only on new expenditures, leaving recurring and charged expenditures beyond its voting power.
8. B - The original 1962 text omitted court-enforceable fundamental rights, providing only advisory Principles of Law-Making that courts could not enforce.
9. C - Presidential control of the cabinet and indirect national elections reduced direct parliamentary and public accountability.
10. A - The 1962 Constitution’s succession rule required the Speaker of the National Assembly to take over as acting president; transferring power to the army chief was unconstitutional.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model answer:** Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act 1947 established that until the Constituent Assembly framed a permanent constitution, the newly created dominion of Pakistan would be governed under the Government of India Act 1935, subject to adaptations made by the Governor-General. This ensured that courts, civil administration, revenue collection, and public order operated without legal disruption at independence.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for naming Section 8 of the Indian Independence Act; 1 mark for citing the adapted Government of India Act 1935; 1 mark for explaining that it prevented a legal vacuum in civil and judicial administration.

2. **Model answer:** The Objectives Resolution described that ultimate, absolute sovereignty over the entire universe belongs to Allah Almighty alone. It established that the authority exercised by the State of Pakistan is a sacred trust, delegated by Allah to the people, who exercise it through their freely chosen representatives within prescribed divine limits.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for stating that absolute sovereignty belongs to Allah alone; 1 mark for explaining the concept of authority as a delegated sacred trust; 1 mark for noting that this power is exercised through elected representatives of the people.

3. **Model answer:** First, the federal dilemma regarding legislative representation between the populous eastern wing (East Bengal) and the geographically larger western provinces delayed consensus. Second, severe political controversy erupted over the national language, as East Bengal demanded equal official status for Bengali alongside Urdu. (A third major issue was determining the institutional Islamic character of the state).
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for identifying the representation and federal dilemma between East and West wings; 1.5 marks for identifying the language controversy or the role of Islamic provisions.

4. **Model answer:** The principle of parity allocated exactly equal representation (150 seats each) to East Pakistan and West Pakistan in the single-chamber National Assembly under the 1956 Constitution. Its political objective was to reassure West Pakistani political leaders against the population majority of East Pakistan (which held 55 percent of the national population), while securing Bengali agreement to a united federal legislature.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for defining parity as equal legislative seat allocation (150 each) among the 300 general seats; 1.5 marks for explaining its objective of balancing West Pakistani fears against Bengali population numbers.

5. **Model answer:** The One Unit scheme, enacted in October 1955, merged the western provinces, British Balochistan and princely states of West Pakistan into a single unified province called West Pakistan, administered from Lahore. It aimed to create two provincial units with equal legislative representation with East Pakistan, but it deeply alienated the smaller provinces by suppressing their administrative autonomy and cultural identities.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for explaining the administrative merger of western provinces into a single province in 1955; 1.5 marks for explaining how it facilitated parity while alienating smaller provinces like Sindh and Balochistan.

6. **Model answer:** First, the First Amendment Act 1963 restored Part II fundamental rights as court-enforceable, giving High Courts writ jurisdiction to strike down laws violating human liberties. Second, it amended Article 1 of the Constitution to officially restore the state title from "Republic of Pakistan" to the "Islamic Republic of Pakistan".
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for explaining the restoration of enforceable fundamental rights in courts; 1.5 marks for noting the change of state name to the "Islamic Republic of Pakistan".

7. **Model answer:** Under the 1956 Constitution, the President was a constitutional head of state elected by an electoral college consisting of members of the National Assembly and both provincial assemblies. Under the 1962 Constitution, the President was an powerful chief executive elected through the indirect Electoral College of 80,000 Basic Democrats, bypassing direct popular vote and parliamentary control.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for explaining the 1956 presidential election by the National and Provincial Assemblies; 1.5 marks for explaining the 1962 presidential election by 80,000 Basic Democrats.

8. **Model answer:** First, in a parliamentary system, the prime minister is the head of government and emerges from the legislature; the 1962 Constitution completely abolished the office of Prime Minister and concentrated all executive power in the President. Second, in a parliamentary system, cabinet ministers must be members of parliament and are collectively accountable to it; under the 1962 Constitution, ministers were appointed solely by the President, could not sit in the National Assembly, and were accountable only to the President.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for noting the abolition of the prime minister and executive concentration in the presidency; 1.5 marks for explaining that ministers were personal presidential appointees not accountable to the assembly.

9. **Model answer:** First, by limiting the franchise to 40,000 local electors in East Pakistan, the system denied the general Bengali populace direct participation in choosing the national leadership, enabling central civil servants and intelligence agencies to manipulate elections. Second, the system maintained parity and executive centralism, preventing the Bengali population from translating its population majority into political and economic decision-making, which exacerbated severe disparities in federal development funds and civil service jobs.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for analyzing how the narrow 40,000 elector pool enabled administrative manipulation and excluded the general public; 1.5 marks for analyzing how it locked East Pakistan into parity and perpetuated economic and political marginalization.

10. **Model answer:** The Federal Court held in April 1955 that the law granting the Chief Court the requested writ jurisdiction lacked the Governor-General's assent. It therefore reversed the Sindh decision on jurisdictional grounds. This left the Assembly without that judicial remedy and strengthened the executive in the immediate crisis. The later Governor-General's Reference used necessity; these were separate decisions.
    - **Marks (3):** 1 for the assent issue; 1 for its effect on the Chief Court's jurisdiction; 1 for explaining how loss of the remedy weakened the Assembly.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: The Objectives Resolution and Constitutional Continuity

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Doctrinal accuracy and textual analysis (0–3 marks):* Accurately details the key clauses of the 1949 Objectives Resolution (divine sovereignty, delegated trust, representative democracy, Islamic social justice, fundamental rights, judicial independence, minority safeguards) citing primary historical context.
  - *Philosophical vs legally effective distinction (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Carefully analyzes why the Resolution operated as a foundational charter and constitutional compass rather than an enforceable statute, drawing upon Shah (2026) to explain its function.
  - *Constitutional continuity tracing (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Analyzes how the Resolution served as the Preamble to the 1956, 1962, and 1973 constitutions, and evaluates the significant legal impact of its transformation into substantive law under Article 2A of the 1973 Constitution in 1985.
  - *Structure and legal register (0–1 mark):* Clear, coherent academic prose utilizing appropriate constitutional terminology.

#### ERQ 2: The 1956 Parliamentary Compromise

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Institutional coverage of the 1956 Constitution (0–3 marks):* Accurately details the structure of the 1956 Constitution: parliamentary cabinet system, single-chamber National Assembly with 300 general seats and 10 reserved seats for women, three legislative lists, and enforceable fundamental rights in Part II.
  - *Critical evaluation of the parity formula and One Unit (0–4 marks, **Evaluate**):* Critically evaluates the compromises made to achieve consensus, assessing how the One Unit scheme reduced the influence of smaller provinces in West Pakistan (Sindh, Balochistan, NWFP) while the parity formula denied East Bengal its population majority, generating lasting political resentment.
  - *Evaluation of Islamic provisions (0–2 marks, **Analyze**):* Evaluates the compromise between modernist democracy and religious demands, specifically examining the title "Islamic Republic", the Muslim presidential qualification, and Article 198's rule against laws conflicting with Islam.
  - *Clarity and academic coherence (0–1 mark):* Well-organized essay supported by precise historical facts and institutional reasoning.

#### ERQ 3: The Architecture of the 1962 Presidential System

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Executive concentration and powers (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Thoroughly analyzes the strong presidency under the 1962 Constitution, detailing the President's appointment of governors, ministers, and judges, strong veto powers, ordinance-making authority, and difficult procedures for legislative removal.
  - *Legislative and financial subordination (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Carefully analyzes the curtailment of the National Assembly, particularly its inability to vote on recurring and charged expenditures and its single-chamber composition under parity.
  - *The Basic Democracies electoral mechanism (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Explains the structure of the Basic Democracies system under the 1959 Order, analyzing how using 80,000 local ward members as an exclusive Electoral College insulated the regime from popular accountability and facilitated bureaucratic manipulation.
  - *Scholarly tone and terminology (0–1 mark):* Precise analysis utilizing correct legal and constitutional concepts.

#### ERQ 4: The Causes of the 1969 Abrogation

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Political and electoral grievances (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Analyzes how the Basic Democracies system, administrative coercion, and the controversial 1965 presidential election against Mohtarma Fatima Jinnah destroyed public legitimacy and alienated the urban intelligentsia.
  - *Regional and economic disparity (0–3 marks, **Evaluate**):* Evaluates the economic and regional imbalances between East and West Pakistan, assessing how the One Unit scheme in the west and perceived economic exploitation in the east fueled Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's Six Points movement.
  - *Mass mobilization and unconstitutional transfer (0–3 marks, **Evaluate**):* Evaluates the 1968–1969 mass uprising across both wings, critically evaluating Ayub Khan's unconstitutional decision to transfer power to General Yahya Khan instead of the National Assembly Speaker under the constitutional succession rule.
  - *Clarity and argument structure (0–1 mark):* Compelling, logical historical argument supported by sound historical evidence.

#### ERQ 5: Comparative Constitutional Design and Democratic Principles

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Comparative constitutional synthesis (0–3 marks, **Analyze**):* Carefully compares the 1956 and 1962 constitutions across executive-legislative relations, legislative budget control, electoral suffrage, and the protection of fundamental rights.
  - *Evaluation of democratic failure (0–3 marks, **Evaluate**):* Evaluates why both early constitutions failed to take deep root, analyzing the impact of executive interference, military intervention, delayed elections, and suppressed provincial autonomy.
  - *Formulation of enduring constitutional principles (0–3 marks, **Create**):* Formulates three compelling, practical institutional principles derived from these early failures (e.g. parliamentary supremacy with fiscal oversight, uncompromised direct universal adult franchise, authentic federal autonomy with bicameral representation) necessary for sustaining democracy.
  - *Coherence and pedagogical insight (0–1 mark):* Insightful reflection suitable for teacher educators cultivating democratic values in future teachers.
