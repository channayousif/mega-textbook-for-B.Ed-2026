# The 1973 Constitution: Features, Fundamental Rights and Principles of Policy

<Figure id="fig-U5-7" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/gicp-400/unit-05/fig-U5-7.webp" alt="A trainee teacher in Sindh goes through an open book with parents and teachers on a school committee, helping them see what the Constitution promises." width={1376} height={768} />

"Who decides what happens in our school, Karachi or Islamabad?" "Can I say anything I like, because I have freedom of speech?" "The Constitution promises this, so why can we not go to court?" Pupils and parents ask teachers questions like these. Each one has a clear answer in the Constitution. This unit teaches you to find it.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain federalism in the 1973 Constitution, and work out which legislature can make a law on a given matter. *(Understand, Apply; SLO:GICP-400-5-1)*
- Explain how the parliamentary system makes the Prime Minister answerable to the National Assembly. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-5-1)*
- Explain separation of powers and give one check that each organ of the State has on another. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-5-1)*
- Define Fundamental Rights, explain why they matter, and show from the text that most of them can be limited by law. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-5-2)*
- Match a school situation to the right article, and describe the route for enforcing a right. *(Apply; SLO:GICP-400-5-2)*
- Explain the Principles of Policy on the Islamic way of life, social justice and equality, and education. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-5-2)*
- Analyse the difference between a Fundamental Right and a Principle of Policy, using the right to education in Article 25A as the case. *(Analyze; SLO:GICP-400-5-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

You should have studied Unit 4, which shows how the 1973 Constitution was made. Bring what you remember about why it was adopted by agreement among the political parties and why it chose a parliamentary system. Unit 1 showed how values become articles. This unit reads those articles closely.

## In this unit

1. [Salient features of the 1973 Constitution](./topic-01) - federalism, the parliamentary system and separation of powers.
2. [Fundamental rights](./topic-02) - what they are and why they matter, the rights guaranteed by the Constitution, and how they are protected and enforced.
3. [Principles of policy](./topic-03) - the Islamic way of life, social justice and equality, and the promotion of education.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Keep a copy of the Constitution nearby and check each article yourself. Reading the words of an article is a skill, and this unit trains it.

**A note on the text.** Unit 1 used the National Assembly edition amended to 28 February 2012 for its introductory study. This unit needs a later text, because amendments have changed some of the articles it teaches. It uses the accessible transcription linked in each topic, which shows amendments up to the Constitution (Twenty-seventh Amendment) Act of 13 November 2025. The most important change for you is in Topic 5.2: older books say that Article 184(3) lets the Supreme Court enforce Fundamental Rights, but that article has been omitted. The Constitution can be amended again, so check the current text before you teach an article. This unit is for study. It is not legal advice.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish the three topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
