# The 1973 Constitution: Features, Fundamental Rights and Principles of Policy - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

The 1973 Constitution divides power in more than one direction. Federalism divides it between the federation and the provinces. Parliament alone makes laws on the Federal Legislative List, such as defence, external affairs and currency. Both levels can make laws on criminal law, criminal procedure and evidence. The provinces make laws on everything else, including school education since the Eighteenth Amendment removed the Concurrent Legislative List in 2010. The provinces have real power, but they are not independent: Article 143 lets a valid Act of Parliament prevail, and they work under one Constitution.

The parliamentary system makes the government answerable to the elected Assembly. The National Assembly elects the Prime Minister and can remove the Prime Minister by a no-confidence vote. The President acts on advice. Separation of powers divides the work of the State among the legislature, the executive and the judiciary. They check each other, and the courts stand most clearly apart.

Fundamental Rights (Articles 8-28) stand above ordinary law: a law that takes them away is void. They mainly protect people against public power, and they come with citizens' duties. Most of them can be limited, but only as the Constitution allows. A person enforces them in the High Court under Article 199 when no other adequate remedy exists. A question of public importance can go to the Federal Constitutional Court under Article 175E(3). That power was held by the Supreme Court under Article 184(3) until the Twenty-seventh Amendment of 2025 omitted that article.

The Principles of Policy (Articles 29-40) set goals on matters such as the Islamic way of life, social justice and education. They are real duties on every organ of the State, and they are checked by a yearly report, elected members and voters. But Article 30(2) stops them being enforced in court, and Article 29(2) lets them depend on resources. The right to education shows the difference clearly. Article 37(b) is a Principle, and Article 25A is a Fundamental Right that can be claimed in court, though it still has to be claimed and carried out. Before making any constitutional claim, ask whether you are holding a right or a Principle.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the articles, not a list of definitions. All names, schools and cases in the questions are examples for practice. Questions refer to the Constitution as amended to the Twenty-seventh Amendment of 2025.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Article 1(1) of the Constitution describes Pakistan as: *(Remember)*
   - A) A unitary republic
   - B) A federal republic
   - C) A confederation of independent provinces
   - D) A presidential union
2. After the Eighteenth Amendment, who makes laws on a matter that is not on the Federal Legislative List and is not criminal law, criminal procedure or evidence? *(Understand)*
   - A) Parliament only
   - B) Parliament and the Provincial Assemblies together
   - C) The Provincial Assembly
   - D) The Council of Common Interests
3. In the parliamentary system of the 1973 Constitution, the Prime Minister is elected by: *(Remember)*
   - A) The voters directly
   - B) The President, at his discretion
   - C) The Senate
   - D) The National Assembly
4. A new law is passed by a Provincial Assembly and a High Court later decides that it takes away a Fundamental Right. This is an example of: *(Apply)*
   - A) The judiciary checking the legislature
   - B) The executive checking the judiciary
   - C) The federation overruling a province
   - D) The legislature checking the executive
5. Under Article 8(2), a law that takes away or abridges a Fundamental Right is, to that extent: *(Remember)*
   - A) Valid until Parliament repeals it
   - B) Valid unless the President objects
   - C) Void
   - D) Sent to the Council of Common Interests
6. A head teacher at a government school tells a Christian pupil that he must receive Islamiat instruction. Which article is the closest fit? *(Apply)*
   - A) Article 22(1)
   - B) Article 31(2)
   - C) Article 11(3)
   - D) Article 19A
7. Since the Twenty-seventh Amendment of 2025, which provision gives the Federal Constitutional Court power over a question of public importance about enforcing a Fundamental Right? *(Remember)*
   - A) Article 184(3)
   - B) Article 199(1)(c)
   - C) Article 175E(3)
   - D) Article 233(2)
8. Which statement best describes the legal force of the Principles of Policy? *(Understand)*
   - A) A court can strike down any law that does not follow them, on a citizen's application
   - B) They are duties on every organ of the State, but they cannot be the ground of a court challenge
   - C) They bind only the provinces, and each Provincial Assembly decides how to apply them
   - D) They carry no duty of any kind, and the State may ignore them freely
9. Article 31 of the Constitution concerns: *(Remember)*
   - A) The rights of minorities to manage their religious institutions
   - B) Enabling the Muslims of Pakistan to order their lives in accordance with Islam
   - C) The rule that no law may be repugnant to the Injunctions of Islam
   - D) The making of Islam the State religion
10. A group of parents wants to go to court because the State has not provided free secondary education for their fifteen-year-old children. Which article gives them the strongest legal claim? *(Apply)*
    - A) Article 37(b)
    - B) Article 38(d)
    - C) Article 37(c)
    - D) Article 25A

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain in two or three sentences why a province has real law-making power but is still not independent. Use one article as evidence. *(Understand)*
2. Say which legislature makes a law on each of these, and name the step or article that decides it: (a) external affairs; (b) free schooling in Sindh; (c) the law of evidence. *(Apply)*
3. In three sentences, explain how a no-confidence vote shows that Pakistan has a parliamentary system. *(Understand)*
4. "Separation of powers means the executive and the legislature must be completely apart." In three sentences, analyse what is wrong with this statement in Pakistan's system. *(Understand)*
5. Name two reasons why Fundamental Rights are important, and give one school-related example for each. *(Understand)*
6. A pupil says, "Article 19 means nobody can ever stop anyone from saying anything." In three sentences, use the wording of Article 19 to correct this, and say why a limit cannot be just anything an official wants. *(Understand)*
7. A government school refuses a nine-year-old girl from a Bheel family because of her caste. Name two relevant articles, and list the steps her family could follow to enforce her rights, in order. *(Apply)*
8. In two or three sentences, explain what Article 29(1) and Article 29(3) add if the Principles of Policy cannot be enforced in court. *(Understand)*
9. Using one example for each, explain the difference between legal equality under Article 25 and social justice under Article 38(a). *(Understand)*
10. Explain why a school management committee would do better to rely on Article 25A than on Article 37(b) when a child aged eleven has no free school. Give two reasons. *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Evaluate this claim: "Since the Eighteenth Amendment, the provinces of Pakistan are almost independent in making law." Use Articles 142 and 143, at least one federal matter and at least one provincial matter, and the debate reported by Hussain and Kokab (2012). Reach a clear judgement. *(Evaluate)*
2. A district officer orders all schools to ban a language other than Urdu at break, stops a teachers' peaceful meeting, and tells parents who complain that "rights do not apply to government orders". Analyse the case. Identify the rights affected and any limits written into them, decide which limits might apply, and describe how the people affected could seek enforcement. *(Analyze)*
3. A provincial education minister says, "Education is only a Principle of Policy, so nobody can take us to court, and we will open schools when money allows." Analyse this statement. Use Article 25A and Article 37(b) to distinguish a Fundamental Right from a Principle of Policy, and judge how far the minister is right. *(Analyze)*
4. Use a single invented case about a girl's schooling in a rural part of Sindh to show how federalism, separation of powers, a Fundamental Right and a Principle of Policy all come into play. Show which body acts at each stage and which article applies. *(Create)*
5. Should a teacher in Pakistan spend class time teaching pupils about Fundamental Rights and Principles of Policy, given that some topics may upset officials or parents? Evaluate this, using at least three articles from this unit and at least one school example, and defend a practical plan for doing it fairly. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Compare your own attempt with it only after you have tried every question. Answers may use different examples and still earn full marks if the reasoning is sound.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Article 1(1) says Pakistan shall be a Federal Republic. C is the misconception that provinces are independent.
2. **C.** Article 142(c) gives all remaining matters to the Provincial Assembly. B describes the old Concurrent List, which was removed in 2010.
3. **D.** Article 91(3) and (4) say the National Assembly elects the Prime Minister from among its Muslim members. B was a feature of an earlier amended text, and no longer applies.
4. **A.** Courts are applying Article 8(2) to an Assembly's law. C is wrong because no federal law is involved.
5. **C.** Article 8(2) makes such a law void to that extent. The other options are invented.
6. **A.** Article 22(1) protects a person from receiving instruction in a religion other than their own. Article 31(2) concerns the Muslims of Pakistan, so it gives no ground here.
7. **C.** Article 175E(3) is the current provision. A was correct until Article 184 was omitted on 13 November 2025. B is the High Court's power.
8. **B.** Articles 29(1) and 30(2) together. A is the common misconception and D is the opposite one.
9. **B.** Article 31. C describes Article 227, D describes Article 2, and A describes Article 20(b).
10. **D.** A fifteen-year-old falls within ages five to sixteen, and Article 25A is enforceable as a Fundamental Right. A names the same subject, but Article 30(2) bars a court action on it.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** A province can make laws on every matter not on the Federal Legislative List, so its power is real. But it works under one Constitution, and under Article 143 a valid Act of Parliament prevails over a conflicting provincial Act. It also has no power over matters such as currency. **Marks (3):** 1 for real power; 1 for a limit; 1 for an article used correctly.
2. **Model:** (a) Parliament: Federal Legislative List, entry 3. (b) Sindh Assembly: not on the list, Article 142(c). (c) Both: Article 142(b). **Marks (3):** 1 each for the legislature with its reason.
3. **Model:** The Prime Minister holds office only with the support of the National Assembly. A majority of its total membership can pass a no-confidence resolution, and the Prime Minister then ceases to hold office (Article 95). So the government depends on the Assembly, which is the mark of a parliamentary system. **Marks (3):** 1 for the Article 95 process; 1 for dependence on the Assembly; 1 for the link to the definition.
4. **Model:** The Prime Minister and ministers come from Parliament, so the two organs overlap by design. They also check each other through the no-confidence vote and the advice to dissolve. The clearer separation is that of the courts (Article 175(3)). **Marks (3):** 1 for the overlap; 1 for a check; 1 for the point about the courts.
5. **Model:** They protect the weaker person against power: Article 22(1) protects a pupil from forced instruction in another religion. They set the terms of citizenship: Article 25 tells a girl she is equal before law. **Marks (4):** 1 for each reason; 1 for each fitting example. Accept the link to duties under Article 5 as a reason, if explained.
6. **Model:** Article 19 gives freedom of speech "subject to any reasonable restrictions imposed by law" on named grounds, such as public order, decency and incitement to an offence. So speech can be limited. But the limit must come from law and be reasonable, and it must fit a named ground, so an official's wish is not enough. **Marks (3):** 1 for the wording; 1 for an example ground; 1 for the limit on limits.
7. **Model:** Articles 25A and 25(1), and Article 22(3)(b) if the school receives public funds. A government school will normally be funded from public revenues. Steps: speak to the head teacher; complain in writing to the education department; if there is no other adequate remedy, file a writ petition in the Sindh High Court under Article 199(1)(c). **Marks (4):** 2 for two relevant articles; 2 for the steps in a sensible order.
8. **Model:** Article 29(1) makes acting on them a responsibility of each organ and of each person acting for it. Article 29(3) requires a yearly report to Parliament and the Provincial Assemblies, with provision for discussion. So they are real duties, checked through politics and not through courts. **Marks (3):** 1 for each article; 1 for the conclusion.
9. **Model:** Legal equality: a clerk refuses a form to a girl because she is a girl, which breaks Article 25(2) and can go to court. Social justice: a poor village has weaker schools than a rich one, a gap that Article 38(a) asks the State to narrow, but no one can sue on that ground alone. Article 25 concerns treatment by law, and Article 38(a) concerns real-life chances. **Marks (4):** 1 for each example; 2 for analysing the difference, including the difference in court.
10. **Model:** Article 25A is a Fundamental Right covering a child of eleven, so it can be enforced through Article 199. Article 37(b) is a Principle, and Article 30(2) bars a court action on it. Article 29(2) also lets a Principle depend on resources, which does not apply in the same way to a right. **Marks (4):** 2 for each reason, with article.
