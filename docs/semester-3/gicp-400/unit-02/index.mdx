## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Explain the different contributions of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, Allama Muhammad Iqbal and Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah to Muslim political thought. This addresses SLO:GICP-400-2-1.
2. Compare an educational reform programme, a political vision and a constitutional demand without treating them as the same thing. This addresses SLO:GICP-400-2-1.
3. Trace the changing role of the All-India Muslim League and explain selected events between 1906 and 1947. This addresses SLO:GICP-400-2-2.
4. Analyze how political ideas, organisation, elections and negotiations interacted in the creation of Pakistan. This addresses both unit SLOs.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 1's distinction between an ideology and a constitution. An ideology expresses guiding beliefs; a constitution sets out the rules and institutions of government. Bring your understanding of Muslim political identity, while remaining open to evidence about how particular leaders' demands changed. You need no detailed knowledge of later constitutions.

## In this unit

1. [Muslim political thought in South Asia](./topic-01) compares Sir Syed's educational response, Iqbal's account of community and selfhood, and Jinnah's political and civic vision.
2. [The Pakistan Movement, 1906-1947](./topic-02) follows the Muslim League, key decisions and the creation of Pakistan.

## How to use this unit

Read each classroom situation before studying its explanation. Keep two notebook columns: what a leader or organisation sought at the time, and what happened later. This helps you explain change without giving earlier actors knowledge of the future.

Complete each topic's group activity, retrieval check and summative task. Then attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) before consulting its final self-study guidance.
