## Unit summary

Muslim political thought in South Asia developed through different responses to changing conditions. Sir Syed's Aligarh Movement connected education to public capacity. Iqbal considered responsible selfhood, Muslim collective life and self-government. Jinnah's political leadership addressed representation and constitutional arrangements, while his August 1947 address set out principles for governing a new state. These contributions connect, but they are not interchangeable.

The Muslim League's early programme did not contain Pakistan's final territorial settlement. Its history includes demands for safeguards, cooperation, a later statehood demand, electoral mobilisation and negotiations. The Lahore Resolution expressed a political goal; the 1946 elections strengthened a representative claim; the independence legislation established legal arrangements. A strong explanation shows how these stages related and what each left unresolved.

Dates help organise this history. They do not explain it alone. Ask who acted, what changed and which source supports the claim. A proposal should not be treated as a final boundary decision, an election as a universal vote on every detail, or a statement of equal citizenship as proof of complete implementation. Independence also brought responsibilities of public service and constitution-making amid the human consequences of partition.

## Summative assessment

Attempt the questions before reading the final section. For short responses, use the stated bound to select the most relevant points. For essays, refer to this unit's named sources without inventing quotations. No external book purchase is required: the linked open readings and topic explanations provide a starting point.

For practice, allow about 15 minutes for MCQs, 35 minutes for RRQs and 20–25 minutes per essay. These are working times, not reading estimates. The bank is intentionally larger than a short classroom test. Complete it over several sittings if needed. It is public self-study material, separate from any graded platform quiz.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which institution most directly represents Sir Syed's Aligarh educational programme? *(Remember)*
   - A) The first Constituent Assembly at Karachi
   - B) Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh
   - C) The Cabinet Mission delegation to India
   - D) The boundary commission for Punjab

2. What is the most accurate meaning of responsible selfhood in this topic's account of Iqbal? *(Understand)*
   - A) Personal success measured by avoiding public duties
   - B) Collective unity achieved by ending individual judgement
   - C) Personal purpose linked to responsibility and ethical action
   - D) Political authority based on the possession of a college degree

3. What did the Allahabad Address principally contribute to the debate discussed here? *(Remember)*
   - A) A proposal for a north-western Muslim political unit
   - B) A completed boundary award dividing Punjab and Bengal
   - C) A law transferring authority to two independent Dominions
   - D) A constitution adopted by Pakistan's Constituent Assembly

4. A trainee says Sir Syed's educational work influenced later Muslim public life. Which explanation gives a defensible link? *(Apply)*
   - A) His college fixed the boundaries later used for Pakistan.
   - B) His college replaced the need for political representation.
   - C) His college made later constitutional negotiations unnecessary.
   - D) His college helped develop learning and skills for public participation.

5. Which classroom practice best applies the equal-citizenship principle discussed in Jinnah's August 1947 address? *(Apply)*
   - A) Assessing pupils' evidence by the same standards across religions
   - B) Giving exhibition speaking roles according to religious identity
   - C) Requiring pupils to adopt one faith before joining a civic activity
   - D) Letting a religious majority decide which pupils may ask questions

6. What distinguishes the League's early representation concerns from its later Pakistan demand? *(Understand)*
   - A) Early concerns set final borders, while later demands concerned textbooks.
   - B) Early concerns sought safeguards, while later demands sought a separate state.
   - C) Early concerns created a state, while later demands formed the organisation.
   - D) Early concerns ended British rule, while later demands restored colonial rule.

7. Which event gives evidence of Congress-League cooperation over constitutional demands before the Pakistan demand? *(Remember)*
   - A) The 1947 Indian Independence Act
   - B) The 1940 Lahore Resolution
   - C) The 1916 Lucknow Pact
   - D) The 1930 Allahabad Address

8. A display calls the 1940 Lahore Resolution "the law that immediately created Pakistan". What type of event should its label identify instead? *(Apply)*
   - A) A final territorial award
   - B) A completed constitution
   - C) A transfer-of-power ceremony
   - D) A political resolution setting a demand

9. What is the strongest conclusion supported by the League's success in Muslim constituencies in the 1946 provincial elections? *(Understand)*
   - A) It strengthened the League's representative claim in negotiations.
   - B) It established that every resident had voted for the same borders.
   - C) It enacted the final independence legislation without further action.
   - D) It removed the need to discuss constitutional arrangements.

10. Two labels give 14 August for Pakistan's independence commemoration and 15 August for the Act's appointed day. How should a teacher handle them? *(Apply)*
    - A) Treat the commemoration as the date on which the Act received assent.
    - B) Describe the different events and functions represented by the dates.
    - C) Treat the appointed day as the date of the Lahore Resolution.
    - D) Describe both labels as dates of the same parliamentary debate.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, explain how one Aligarh institution could contribute to Muslim public capacity without being a demand for Pakistan. Include one limit on the claim. *(Understand)*
2. In two or three sentences, distinguish responsible selfhood from selfishness using a teacher-training group as your example. *(Understand)*
3. A pupil claims Iqbal fixed Pakistan's exact 1947 borders in 1930. In three sentences, identify the relevant proposal, analyze the leap in the claim and name the later kind of evidence needed. *(Analyze)*
4. In three sentences, explain how a demand for Muslim political self-government and equal citizenship for people of different religions answer different questions. Refer to Jinnah's August 1947 address. *(Understand)*
5. Compare Sir Syed and Iqbal in four sentences. Identify one contribution of each, one connection and one difference. *(Analyze)*
6. In three sentences, distinguish separate electorates from a separate state. Explain why the distinction matters when reading the League's early history. *(Understand)*
7. In four sentences, explain why the Lucknow Pact and the Lahore Resolution challenge the claim of an unchanged League programme. Identify the role of each event. *(Analyze)*
8. In three sentences, distinguish the political significance of the 1946 electoral results from a claim that every person voted for partition. Include one limitation of the voting system. *(Analyze)*
9. In four sentences, explain how the Cabinet Mission negotiations, partition planning and independence legislation performed different functions. Do not simply list their dates. *(Analyze)*
10. In three sentences, distinguish the 14 August commemoration from the Act's 15 August appointed day, then name one task that remained for the new state. *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A lesson plan describes Sir Syed, Iqbal and Jinnah as three people who announced an identical programme for Pakistan. In 450–550 words, evaluate this plan. Compare all three contributions, use at least two specific institutions or texts, and propose a more accurate central explanation for the lesson. *(Evaluate)*
2. Design a 20-minute group activity for a Sindh secondary classroom that connects Iqbal's political vision and Jinnah's civic principles. In 400–500 words, explain the concept to be taught, provide a worked source-reading example, assign collaborative roles and justify how your final question tests understanding rather than recognition. Use only material introduced in Topic 2.1. *(Create)*
3. In 450–550 words, analyze how the League's changing demands and two turning points between 1906 and 1946 contributed to the Pakistan Movement. Include at least one earlier attempt at cooperation or safeguards. Explain what each turning point changed and what it left unresolved. *(Analyze)*
4. A school exhibition jumps from the Lahore Resolution to "Pakistan created" and omits the intervening years. In 450–550 words, evaluate that sequence and design a replacement with four or more stages. Distinguish electoral support, negotiation and legal change, and explain the date distinction at independence. Include one responsibility that continued after transfer. *(Evaluate)*
5. In 500–600 words, trace one idea associated with Sir Syed, Iqbal or Jinnah to a later development of the Pakistan Movement. Identify the idea, a plausible mechanism of influence and the later event. Use evidence from both topics, distinguish influence from proof of direct causation, and explain why the development did not make the final result inevitable. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

These responses are intentionally public self-study guidance. Equivalent accurate wording earns credit. A citation must support the point for which it is used. Do not reward a quotation merely because it sounds familiar. A learner may explain uncertainty carefully and still show strong historical reasoning.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** The MAO College is directly associated with the Aligarh educational programme; the other bodies belong to later constitutional decisions.
2. **C.** Responsible selfhood combines purposeful personal development with ethical action; it does not mean ignoring others or abandoning judgement.
3. **A.** The address proposed a north-western Muslim unit; it was neither a final boundary award nor a law establishing Pakistan.
4. **D.** Education can develop capacities for public participation. This identifies a mechanism without treating the college as a territorial or legislative decision.
5. **A.** Applying the same evidence standards across religions illustrates equal civic standing while allowing religious difference.
6. **B.** Safeguards within a wider constitutional arrangement differ from the later demand to establish a separate state.
7. **C.** The Lucknow Pact demonstrates cooperation over constitutional demands. The other events have different political or legal functions.
8. **D.** The resolution expressed a political demand. Later decisions were required to establish a state and settle implementation.
9. **A.** The results strengthened a representative claim. Restricted voting rights and subsequent negotiations prevent the broader conclusions in the other options.
10. **B.** The dates refer to distinct functions: national commemoration and the legal appointed day. Neither is the Act's assent date, 18 July 1947.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Each RRQ is worth **4 marks**, for a total of 40. Award points independently where the response communicates the required idea. A serious contradiction cancels the point it contradicts, not unrelated accurate points. These are model responses, so they may exceed the question's sentence count slightly when explaining the allocation.

1. The MAO College offered modern learning that could develop skills for public participation. Such learning could help later debate and organisation. This was educational preparation, not Sir Syed's demand for the state created after his death. **Marks:** 1 for a relevant institution; 1 for the capacity it develops; 1 for a plausible public-life connection; 1 for distinguishing contribution from a later statehood demand.
2. Selfhood involves developing a purposeful and responsible self. A prepared group member who explains a view and listens illustrates this better than a dominant member who refuses to hear others. **Marks:** 1 for purpose or agency; 1 for responsibility; 1 for a concrete group example; 1 for explaining why it differs from selfishness. Do not require a memorised poetic line.
3. Iqbal proposed a north-western Muslim political unit in the Allahabad Address. The pupil turns that proposal into a later completed territorial settlement without evidence. Records of the 1947 negotiations and boundary decisions would be needed to establish the final arrangements. **Marks:** 1 for identifying the address and proposal; 1 for identifying the time or scope mismatch; 1 for explaining the unsupported conclusion; 1 for an appropriate later evidence type.
4. Muslim political self-government concerns who should exercise collective authority and under what arrangement. Equal citizenship concerns the treatment owed to all citizens once a state exists. Jinnah's August address expressed civic equality across religious communities, so the two questions should not be confused. **Marks:** 1 for the self-government question; 1 for the citizenship question; 1 for accurate use of the address; 1 for explaining compatibility rather than merely naming both ideas.
5. Sir Syed built educational and social capacity through the Aligarh Movement. Iqbal explored selfhood, community and a political form for Muslim self-government. Both addressed Muslim collective life. They differed in their immediate work and historical setting, so educational preparation is not identical to a territorial proposal. **Marks:** 1 for each contribution; 1 for an explained connection; 1 for an explained difference. Lists of dates alone earn no comparison points.
6. Separate electorates enabled a community's eligible voters to choose representatives for designated seats within a larger system. A separate state has distinct governing authority and territory. The League's early representation concerns therefore do not prove it already demanded the final Pakistan settlement. **Marks:** 1 for eligible community voters and designated representation; 1 for operation within a larger system; 1 for statehood distinction; 1 for its historical significance.
7. The Lucknow Pact shows Congress-League cooperation on constitutional demands and representation. The Lahore Resolution expressed a later demand involving Muslim-majority regions and independent arrangements. Their different goals show development in the League's programme. They do not support a claim that the final state was specified from 1906. **Marks:** 1 for each event's function; 1 for an explicit comparison; 1 for the justified conclusion about change.
8. Success in Muslim constituencies strengthened the League's representative claim and bargaining position. Franchise restrictions meant that not every adult voted. An electoral result in those seats therefore cannot establish universal consent to every detail of partition. **Marks:** 1 for the constituency scope; 1 for stronger representative claim; 1 for restricted voting rights; 1 for explaining the limit on the universal-consent claim.
9. The Cabinet Mission sought agreement on a constitutional arrangement. Its failure to secure lasting agreement left further choices open. Partition planning addressed the division and transfer process, while the Act established legal arrangements for independence. **Marks:** 1 for the Mission's negotiation function; 1 for the unresolved settlement; 1 for partition planning; 1 for the legal function of the Act. Merely naming three dates earns at most 1 mark.
10. Pakistan commemorates independence on 14 August, when the Karachi transfer ceremony took place. The Act specified 15 August as its appointed day. Constitution-making, administration, public protection or refugee assistance remained tasks of the new state. **Marks:** 1 for commemoration; 1 for the ceremony connection; 1 for the appointed day; 1 for an appropriate continuing task. Do not infer that every part of the transfer happened in one instant.

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is worth **12 marks**. Four criteria are scored separately from 0 to 3. For each criterion: **3** means accurate, specific and fully reasoned; **2** means mostly accurate with one relevant gap; **1** means a relevant assertion or example without sufficient explanation; **0** means absent, unrelated or seriously incorrect. Apply the criterion-specific descriptions below to decide what counts as fully reasoned. Total 60 marks across five essays.

1. **Comparison of leaders, 0–3:** distinguishes educational preparation, political philosophy and political leadership in their settings. **Use of sources, 0–3:** accurately uses two or more particular institutions or texts for claims they support. **Evaluation of the plan, 0–3:** explains why an identical-programme account distorts the relationships, rather than merely labelling it wrong. **Replacement explanation, 0–3:** offers a clear classroom explanation retaining a connection and a difference. Full evaluation credit requires weighing the plan's useful shared theme against its misleading claim of sameness; praise or criticism without reasons earns at most 1 on that criterion.
2. **Conceptual alignment, 0–3:** accurately connects Iqbal's responsible collective life with Jinnah's equal-citizenship principles without merging their texts. **Worked source example, 0–3:** models a claim, a supporting source and a limit before asking pupils to perform the move. **Collaborative design, 0–3:** provides purposeful roles and feasible steps within 20 minutes using available materials. **Assessment justification, 0–3:** explains how the final question makes pupils produce a reasoned connection, rather than recognise a slogan. A polished activity with no conceptual justification cannot earn more than 1 on the final criterion.
3. **Historical range, 0–3:** places early safeguards or cooperation and the two chosen turning points in accurate order. **Analysis of change, 0–3:** identifies what each turning point changed in demands, support or bargaining. **Causal links, 0–3:** explains mechanisms joining developments instead of treating succession as proof. **Limits and evidence, 0–3:** supports claims with particular sources or events and identifies what remained unresolved. Full marks do not require covering every event in the unit. They require a defensible selection that answers the question rather than an unexplained list of milestones.
4. **Diagnosis of omission, 0–3:** explains why the jump hides decisions needed to translate a demand into statehood. **Replacement sequence, 0–3:** gives at least four relevant stages and distinguishes support, negotiation and law. **Date precision, 0–3:** accurately distinguishes 14 August commemoration and ceremony, 15 August appointed day and the different July assent event where used. **Evaluation and continuing responsibility, 0–3:** justifies the redesigned display and links independence to a remaining state task. A four-stage display earns only partial sequence credit if its arrows falsely suggest that every later outcome was predetermined.
5. **Idea and event, 0–3:** identifies both accurately and across the two topics. **Mechanism and evidence, 0–3:** explains a plausible connection with source support, such as education developing public capacity or a political vision informing later claims. **Causal caution, 0–3:** distinguishes contribution, direct evidence of influence and unproved inference. **Judgement about contingency, 0–3:** identifies a later choice or negotiation showing why the outcome was not fixed by the earlier idea alone. An alternative interpretation can earn full credit if it is supported and bounded. Do not require the learner to claim a direct personal influence where the available evidence does not establish one.

For self-study, record the criterion on which you lost most credit and revise that part of one response. If your weakness is chronology, check event order. If it is analysis, add a sentence explaining what changed and how. If it is evidence, identify exactly which source supports the disputed claim. A longer answer is not automatically a stronger one.
