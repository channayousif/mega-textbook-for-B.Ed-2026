# Structure of Government and Constitutional Amendments

<Figure id="fig-U6-5" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/gicp-400/unit-06/fig-U6-5.webp" alt="Pupils in a Sindh classroom hold a mock Parliament, one speaking and others raising hands to vote, while a trainee teacher watches." width={1376} height={768} />

"Does the President run the country?" "Can Parliament change the Constitution with an ordinary vote?" "Did the Eighteenth Amendment do anything more than rename a province?" Pupils and even colleagues will ask you questions like these. Unit 5 showed what the Constitution promises. This unit shows who carries out those promises, and how the rules themselves can change.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Describe the Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament), and explain why it has two Houses chosen in different ways. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-6-1)*
- Explain who holds real executive power, and correct the claim that the President runs the government. *(Understand, Analyze; SLO:GICP-400-6-1)*
- Name the main courts, and decide which court deals with a given kind of case. *(Remember, Apply; SLO:GICP-400-6-1)*
- Trace an amendment through Article 239, and work out whether a given vote is enough. *(Apply; SLO:GICP-400-6-2)*
- State the main changes made by the 18th, 19th and 25th Amendments. *(Remember, Understand; SLO:GICP-400-6-2)*
- Evaluate the effect of these amendments on federalism and provincial autonomy, using evidence. *(Analyze, Evaluate; SLO:GICP-400-6-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

You should have studied Unit 5. Bring what you learned about federalism, the parliamentary system and separation of powers, and about the removal of the Concurrent Legislative List in 2010. This unit builds on each of these.

## In this unit

1. [Legislature, executive and judiciary](./topic-01) - the three organs of the federal government: who is in them, how they are chosen, and what they do.
2. [Amending the Constitution](./topic-02) - the procedure in Article 239, the 18th, 19th and 25th Amendments, and their effect on federalism.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Keep a copy of the Constitution nearby, and check each article yourself.

**A note on the text.** Some of what this unit teaches has changed recently. It uses the accessible transcription linked in each topic, which shows amendments up to the Constitution (Twenty-seventh Amendment) Act of 13 November 2025. That Act created a Federal Constitutional Court and moved some powers to it from the Supreme Court. Older books, including many you will find in school libraries, do not show this. The Constitution can be amended again, so check the current text before you teach an article. This unit is for study. It is not legal advice.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
