# Structure of Government and Constitutional Amendments - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

The federal government has three organs that check each other. The Majlis-e-Shoora is made up of the President, the National Assembly and the Senate. The National Assembly gives weight to population, and the Senate gives each Province an equal voice. Parliament makes federal laws, controls federal money and holds the government to account. The Prime Minister and Cabinet hold real executive power and answer to both Houses. The President is Head of State and mostly acts on advice, although executive actions are expressed in the President's name. The courts interpret the law. High Courts deal with orders against public authorities, the Supreme Court hears many appeals, and since the Twenty-seventh Amendment of 2025 the Federal Constitutional Court decides disputes between governments and major constitutional questions.

The Constitution can be amended only by a harder route than an ordinary law. A Bill needs two-thirds of the total membership of each House, with no joint sitting to rescue it. If it would alter a Province's limits, it also needs two-thirds of that Province's Assembly. Three amendments are studied here. The Eighteenth (2010) was far more than a renaming. It removed the Concurrent List, protected provincial shares of finance, strengthened shared councils, added rights and reshaped appointments. The Nineteenth (in force 2011) was mainly about judges, and its effect on federalism was small. The Twenty-fifth (2018) merged the tribal areas into the Provinces and ended separate federal rule there.

To judge the effect of an amendment on federalism, ask who makes the law, who holds the money, who decides together, and whose territory it is. Then weigh the evidence against its limits. The evidence supports a clear growth of provincial autonomy in law, with real limits and with questions about practice that a single early paper cannot answer.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the articles and the readings, not a list of definitions. All names, schools and cases in the questions are examples for practice. Questions refer to the Constitution as amended to the Twenty-seventh Amendment of 2025.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Under Article 50, the Majlis-e-Shoora (Parliament) consists of: *(Remember)*
   - A) The National Assembly and the Provincial Assemblies
   - B) The President, the Prime Minister and the Cabinet
   - C) The President, the National Assembly and the Senate
   - D) The National Assembly and the Senate only
2. Sindh and Punjab have very different numbers of National Assembly seats but the same number of Senate members. This is because: *(Understand)*
   - A) Assembly seats follow population, and each Province has equal Senate membership
   - B) Senate seats follow population, and Assembly seats are equal for each Province
   - C) Senators are chosen by the President for each Province
   - D) Assembly seats follow provincial area, and Senate seats follow tax paid
3. A government notice says, "The President has been pleased to appoint Ms Y as a Federal Minister." Who actually made the choice? *(Apply)*
   - A) The President, using personal discretion
   - B) The Prime Minister, whose advice the President follows
   - C) The Chief Justice, on the advice of the Judicial Commission
   - D) The Senate, through a vote on the nominee
4. The Prime Minister is elected by: *(Remember)*
   - A) All registered voters, in a national poll
   - B) An electoral college of both Houses and the Provincial Assemblies
   - C) The Senate, by a two-thirds vote of its total membership
   - D) The National Assembly, by a majority of its total membership
5. The Federal Government and a Provincial Government have a legal dispute with each other. Which court has original jurisdiction? *(Apply)*
   - A) The Federal Constitutional Court
   - B) The High Court of that Province
   - C) The Federal Shariat Court
   - D) A subordinate court in the provincial capital
6. Under Article 239, a Constitution Amendment Bill needs in each House: *(Remember)*
   - A) A majority of the members present and voting
   - B) A majority of the total membership
   - C) Two-thirds of the members present and voting
   - D) Two-thirds of the total membership
7. Using the full seat numbers, an Amendment Bill gets 240 votes in the National Assembly and 60 in the Senate. What happens? *(Apply)*
   - A) It passes, because the combined total is above two-thirds of both Houses
   - B) It fails, because the Senate needed 64 votes
   - C) It goes to a joint sitting, which decides by majority
   - D) It passes, because the National Assembly's vote prevails
8. A Bill would alter the limits of a Province. Besides passing in both Houses, it needs: *(Understand)*
   - A) A national referendum on the change
   - B) Approval from all four Provincial Assemblies by a majority
   - C) Two-thirds of the total membership of that Province's Assembly
   - D) A decision of the Council of Common Interests
9. The Eighteenth Amendment removed which part of the legislative lists? *(Remember)*
   - A) The Federal Legislative List, Part I
   - B) The Concurrent Legislative List
   - C) The Federal Legislative List, Part II
   - D) The Provincial Legislative List
10. A pupil asks what the Twenty-fifth Amendment did to the Federally Administered Tribal Areas. The best short reply is that it: *(Understand)*
    - A) Made them a separate fifth Province
    - B) Placed them under the Supreme Court's direct rule
    - C) Merged them into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
    - D) Gave them extra seats in the Senate

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, explain two differences between how the National Assembly and the Senate are chosen, and say why those differences matter in a federation. *(Understand)*
2. In two or three sentences, explain what "in the name of the President" means in Articles 90(1) and 99(1). *(Understand)*
3. Compare the President and the Prime Minister on two points: who elects each, and who holds real executive power. Use two articles. Keep to four sentences. *(Analyze)*
4. For each case, name the court and give a reason in one sentence. (a) A criminal trial for theft in Sukkur. (b) An official refuses to do what the law requires, and no other adequate remedy exists. (c) The Federal and Balochistan Governments dispute a legal power. *(Apply)*
5. Give two rules that keep judges apart from the executive and the legislature. Explain one weakness or open question about judicial independence. Keep to four sentences. *(Analyze)*
6. Give two differences between passing an ordinary Bill and passing a Constitution Amendment Bill. *(Understand)*
7. A Bill gets 225 votes in the National Assembly. In the Senate, 70 members are present and 65 vote for it. Using the full seat numbers, work out whether it reaches the President. Show your numbers in two or three sentences. *(Apply)*
8. In three sentences, explain why the Nineteenth Amendment was passed and what it changed about the appointment of judges. *(Understand)*
9. Explain how the Twenty-fifth Amendment shows Article 239(4) in use. Then give one way in which the amendment changed who governs the merged areas. Keep to three or four sentences. *(Analyze)*
10. A colleague says, "The Eighteenth Amendment only renamed a province." Correct this in four sentences, using three changes from different areas. Say which change you think matters most for federalism and why. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A newspaper letter says, "The President rules Pakistan. Every order is issued in the President's name." Analyse this claim using the articles on the President, the Prime Minister and accountability. Identify what is true in it, what is false, and where the President does have a real power of decision. Write 250-350 words. *(Analyze)*
2. Evaluate how far the courts are independent of the executive and the legislature. Use appointment, security in office and limits on discussion in Parliament. Also use one change since 2010. Reach a judgement. Write 250-350 words. *(Evaluate)*
3. Evaluate the Eighteenth Amendment's effect on provincial autonomy, using evidence from the readings. Refer to at least three provisions and to at least two views from Hussain and Kokab (2012). State one limit of that paper as evidence. Write 300-400 words. *(Evaluate)*
4. Analyse the 18th, 19th and 25th Amendments using the four federalism questions: who makes the law, who holds the money, who decides together, and whose territory it is. Rank them by their effect on federalism, and defend your ranking. Write 300-400 words. *(Analyze)*
5. A group proposes moving school curriculum back to federal control through a constitutional amendment. Create a plan for a 40-minute Class 10 mock Parliament on this proposal. Your plan must include: the steps and votes under Article 239; which organ does what, including what a court could or could not do afterwards; arguments on both sides, based on the Eighteenth Amendment; and your own defended position. Write 350-450 words. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is for self-study. Compare your own attempt with it only after you have tried every question. Answers may use different examples and still earn full marks if the reasoning is sound.

### MCQ answer key

1. **C.** Article 50 names the President and the two Houses. D is the common mistake of leaving out the President.
2. **A.** Article 51 allocates Assembly seats by population, and Article 59 gives each Province the same Senate membership.
3. **B.** Article 92(1) says the President appoints ministers on the Prime Minister's advice. A confuses the name on the notice with the decision.
4. **D.** Article 91(4). B is the way the President is elected, under Article 41(3).
5. **A.** Article 175E(1). Older books say the Supreme Court, under the now omitted Article 184.
6. **D.** Article 239(1)-(2). C is wrong because the base is total membership, and not those present.
7. **B.** The Senate needs 64 of 96. Votes are not added across the Houses, and Article 239 has no joint sitting route.
8. **C.** Article 239(4). Only the Province whose limits would change must agree, and not all four.
9. **B.** The Eighteenth Amendment omitted the Concurrent List and amended Article 142.
10. **C.** Article 246(d) merged these areas into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. In fact, Senate seats fell from 104 to 96, so D is wrong.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Voters elect general members of the Assembly directly, and seats follow population. Provincial Assembly members elect senators, and each Province has 23. This gives weight to the people in one House and an equal voice to the Provinces in the other. **Marks (4):** 1 each for two correct differences; 2 for a reason linked to federalism.
2. **Model:** It means that actions are formally expressed as the President's, while the Federal Government, acting through the Prime Minister, exercises the authority. The name does not show who decided. **Marks (3):** 1 for the formal name; 1 for the Federal Government deciding; 1 for a clear distinction.
3. **Model:** An electoral college of both Houses and the Provincial Assemblies elects the President (Article 41(3)), and the National Assembly elects the Prime Minister (Article 91). The Prime Minister is chief executive (Article 90(1)), and the President acts on advice (Article 48(1)). So the President is Head of State, and the real executive decisions lie with the Prime Minister and Cabinet. **Marks (4):** 1 for each election; 1 for each power point, cited. Up to 4 in total.
4. **Model:** (a) A subordinate court, as this is an everyday criminal case. (b) A High Court under Article 199, since no other adequate remedy exists. (c) The Federal Constitutional Court under Article 175E(1), as this is a dispute between governments. **Marks (3):** 1 for each court with its reason.
5. **Model:** A Judicial Commission, not the executive alone, nominates judges (Article 175A), and judges can be removed only through the Supreme Judicial Council (Article 209). One open question is how appointments should balance judges with elected members, since this has been changed several times since 2010. **Marks (4):** 1 for each rule; 2 for a reasoned weakness or open question.
6. **Model:** An ordinary Bill needs a majority of those voting, while an Amendment Bill needs two-thirds of the total membership of each House. An ordinary Bill can go to a joint sitting if the Houses disagree, while Article 239 has no such route. **Marks (2):** 1 for each. Provincial consent under Article 239(4) is also acceptable.
7. **Model:** The Assembly needs 224, and 225 is enough. The Senate needs 64 of 96, and 65 is enough, even though only 70 members were present. So it reaches the President, unless it alters a Province's limits. **Marks (3):** 1 for each correct test; 1 for the conclusion.
8. **Model:** The Eighteenth Amendment's appointment system was challenged, and in 2010 the Supreme Court referred the matter back to Parliament with suggestions. The Nineteenth raised the Supreme Court judges on the Commission from two to four. It also required the Committee to give recorded reasons for refusing a nominee. **Marks (3):** 1 for the reason; 1 for each change.
9. **Model:** Merging FATA changed the limits of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, so Article 239(4) required its Assembly's two-thirds vote, which it gave in May 2018. The amendment omitted Article 247, so the federal executive no longer governed FATA as a separate area. It became part of a Province. **Marks (4):** 2 for the Article 239(4) link; 2 for the change in who governs.
10. **Model:** The renaming was only one section of a long Act. The Act also removed the Concurrent List, added the right to education in Article 25A, and limited presidential dissolution by removing Article 58(2)(b). For federalism, the removal of the Concurrent List matters most, because it moved law-making on many subjects to the Provinces. **Marks (5):** 1 for each of three changes; 2 for a reasoned choice. Article 160(3A) or 172(3) are also acceptable choices.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.
