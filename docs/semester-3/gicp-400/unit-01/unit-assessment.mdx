# Ideology, Constitution and the Ideological Foundations of Pakistan - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

An ideology is a shared set of ideas about how people should live together and be governed. It says how things should be, and it guides action, but it creates no legal duty by itself. A constitution is the highest law of a state. It sets up institutions, limits power and protects rights. It also states values, as the Preamble of the Constitution of Pakistan does, so it is not only a legal document. Other laws must agree with it, and Article 239 makes it harder to change than an ordinary law.

Ideology and constitution are not the same, but a chain links them: values become principles, and principles become articles. Equality, for example, runs from Quaid-e-Azam's speech of 11 August 1947, through the words of the Preamble, to Articles 25 and 36. The chain can weaken. A Fundamental Right is enforceable, while a Principle of Policy guides the State but cannot be the sole ground for a challenge in court. Rights operate within the Constitution's scope and exceptions. An article can also be strong on paper and not fully carried out in practice.

Pakistan's founding ideas have two roots. The Two-Nation Theory, linked to Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, Allama Iqbal and Quaid-e-Azam, claimed that the Muslims of South Asia were a nation with a right to self-government. It was a claim about nationhood and political power. It was not a claim that people of different faiths cannot live side by side. The Islamic concept of state holds that sovereignty belongs to Allah, and that people hold authority as a trust, to be used with justice, consultation and equality. It is not a theocracy, or rule by a religious class.

These roots feed the Ideology of Pakistan, which joins self-government, Islamic principles in public life, and democracy with equal citizenship. The Objectives Resolution, the Preamble and articles such as 2, 2A, 20, 25, 36 and 227 carry these commitments into law. Historians weigh the causes of Pakistan differently, and Pakistanis debate how the commitments fit together. A good teacher presents this fairly, and in a way that respects every pupil as a citizen.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this course, an ideology is best described as: *(Remember)*

2. Article 25A of the Constitution of Pakistan deals with: *(Remember)*
   - A) The composition of Parliament
   - B) Islam as the State religion
   - C) Free and compulsory education for children aged five to sixteen
   - D) The procedure for amending the Constitution
3. What most clearly separates a constitution from an ordinary law? *(Understand)*
   - A) It is written in a formal language
   - B) It applies only to government servants
   - C) It is older than every other law in the country
   - D) Other laws must agree with it, and it is harder to change
4. A citizen wants a court to cancel a law only because the law does not follow Article 33, a Principle of Policy. Under Article 30(2), what is the likely result? *(Apply)*
   - A) The law's validity cannot be challenged on that ground alone
   - B) The law is automatically void
   - C) The court sends the law back to Parliament
   - D) Article 33 is enforced in the same way as Article 25
5. A student wants to show how the value of equality reached the Constitution of Pakistan. Which order is best? *(Apply)*
   - A) Article 25, then the speech of 11 August 1947, then the Preamble

   - C) The speech of 11 August 1947, then the Preamble, then Article 25
   - D) Article 25, then the Preamble, then the speech of 11 August 1947
6. The Two-Nation Theory is mainly a claim about: *(Remember)*

   - D) How land should be shared among farmers
7. Who, at Allahabad in 1930, spoke of "a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim State"? *(Remember)*

   - B) Liaquat Ali Khan

   - D) Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah
8. According to the Preamble, the authority exercised by the people of Pakistan is: *(Understand)*
   - A) Unlimited, because the people are sovereign
   - B) Held only by religious scholars
   - C) Given by the President to each province
   - D) A sacred trust, within limits prescribed by Allah
9. A friend says, "An Islamic state means religious leaders rule and people have no say." Which words of the Preamble most directly challenge this? *(Apply)*
   - A) "through the chosen representatives of the people"
   - B) "independence of the judiciary"
   - C) "honoured place amongst the nations"
   - D) "its sovereign rights on land, sea and air"
10. In a school assembly in Umerkot, a speaker says that only Muslim pupils truly belong to Pakistan. Which reply fits the Constitution best? *(Apply)*
    - A) This is true, because Article 2 makes Islam the State religion
    - B) This is a private opinion on which the Constitution is silent
    - C) Article 25 makes all citizens equal before law, and Article 36 safeguards minorities
    - D) This was true before 1973 but is no longer true

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Give the three parts of the definition of an ideology. Then explain, in two sentences, why "Sukkur is on the River Indus" is not an ideological statement. *(Understand)*
2. Name three jobs a constitution does. For each, give one article of the Constitution of Pakistan and what it says, in one line. *(Understand)*
3. In two or three sentences, explain why Article 239 makes the Constitution more stable than an ordinary law, and why this matters to ordinary citizens. *(Understand)*
4. Take the value "religious freedom". In three lines, trace it through a phrase of the Preamble to one article. *(Apply)*
5. Article 25 and Article 33 both deal with how groups of people are treated. In three or four sentences, analyse why they have different legal force, and what this means for a citizen. *(Analyze)*
6. In two or three sentences, explain what the Two-Nation Theory claimed and what it did not claim. *(Understand)*
7. Name the contribution of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, Allama Iqbal and Quaid-e-Azam to the Two-Nation Theory, in one line each. *(Understand)*
8. List four features of the Islamic concept of state, with one Qur'anic reference or Preamble phrase for each. *(Understand)*
9. In three or four sentences, analyse how Iqbal's account of the Islamic state differs from a theocracy, and why he still uses the word "theocracy" in one special sense. *(Analyze)*
10. In three or four sentences, compare how Qureshi and Jalal explain the creation of Pakistan, as reported by Hassan (2020), and state what Hassan concludes. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A trainee writes: "The Constitution is a legal document for lawyers. Values belong to religion and family, not to a constitution." In 300-400 words, analyse this statement using the Preamble and at least two articles of your choice. Show which parts of it are right, which are wrong and why. *(Analyze)*
2. The headteacher of a school in Larkana says: "In Class 9, pupils should learn the national ideals. They do not need to study the articles of the Constitution, because these are too technical." In 300-400 words, evaluate this view. Reach a clear judgement, and justify it using the differences between ideology and constitution and one example of an article that matters to a pupil. *(Evaluate)*
3. A pupil in Mirpurkhas says: "The Two-Nation Theory means Muslims and Hindus cannot live side by side." In 300-400 words, analyse this reading. Use evidence from at least two speeches and two articles. Explain why people believe this reading, and show exactly where it goes wrong. *(Analyze)*
4. A textbook says: "The Ideology of Pakistan means Islam, and nothing else needs to be said." In 300-400 words, evaluate this statement. Use the three commitments of the Ideology of Pakistan, evidence from the Preamble, and one tension between the commitments. *(Evaluate)*
5. Choose **one** ideological principle of Pakistan: equal citizenship, Islamic principles in public life, or government through the people's representatives. In 400-500 words, analyse how it appears in the Constitution. Trace it from a founding statement, through the Preamble, to at least two articles. Then analyse the legal force of those articles, and judge how fully the Constitution carries the principle. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer, so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** An ideology is shared, says how things should be and guides action. A describes a constitution; C and D are not sets of ideas.
2. **C.** Article 25A is the right to education. Parliament is Article 50, the State religion is Article 2 and amendment is Article 239.
3. **D.** Other laws must agree with a constitution, and Article 239 requires a two-thirds majority to amend it. The other options are not what defines a constitution.
4. **A.** Article 30(2) says the validity of a law or action cannot be questioned on the ground that it does not follow the Principles of Policy.
5. **C.** The value comes first, then the principle, then the article.
6. **B.** It is a claim about nationhood and self-government. C is the common misreading.
7. **C.** Iqbal used these words in his presidential address at Allahabad in 1930.
8. **D.** The Preamble calls this authority "a sacred trust", to be used "within the limits prescribed by Him".
9. **A.** Rule through chosen representatives directly contradicts the idea that people have no say. The other phrases concern other matters.
10. **C.** Equality before law (Article 25) and the protection of minorities (Article 36) answer the speaker. Article 2 does not take away equal citizenship, and the Constitution is not silent.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** An ideology is shared, it is about how things should be, and it guides action. "Sukkur is on the River Indus" describes a fact, not how things should be. It also guides no action, so it is not ideological. **Marks (3):** 1 for all three parts; 2 for the explanation (1 for each point).
2. **Model:** It sets up institutions (Article 50 creates Parliament, or Article 175 establishes the courts). It limits power (Article 8(2): the State shall not make a law that takes away Fundamental Rights). It protects rights (Article 25: all citizens are equal before law). **Marks (3):** 1 for each job with a fitting article.
3. **Model:** An amendment needs at least two-thirds of the total membership of each House, which is harder than passing an ordinary law. So a government cannot easily change the basic rules when they are inconvenient. Citizens can then rely on their rights and institutions lasting. **Marks (3):** 1 for the two-thirds rule; 1 for the comparison with ordinary law; 1 for why it matters to citizens.
4. **Model:** Value: people should be free to follow their faith. Principle: the Preamble says minorities shall be free "to profess and practise their religions". Article: Article 20 gives every citizen the right to profess, practise and propagate religion. **Marks (3):** 1 for each link. Accept other fitting phrases and articles.
5. **Model:** Article 25 is a Fundamental Right. Under Article 8, a law that conflicts with it is void, so a citizen can challenge such a law in court. Article 33 is a Principle of Policy, and Article 30(2) stops a law being challenged only because it does not follow one. So a citizen has a legal remedy under Article 25, but under Article 33 can only press the State through politics and public opinion. **Marks (4):** 1 for placing each article correctly (2); 1 for naming Articles 8 or 30(2) as the reason; 1 for the consequence for a citizen.
6. **Model:** It claimed that the Muslims and Hindus of South Asia were two nations, and that Muslims had a right to govern themselves in their own homeland. It did not claim that people of different faiths cannot live side by side. **Marks (3):** 1 for two nations; 1 for self-government; 1 for what it did not claim.
7. **Model:** Sir Syed is called the pioneer of the idea after 1857 and the Hindi-Urdu controversy. Iqbal gave it a philosophical basis and asked for a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim state in 1930. Quaid-e-Azam turned it into a political demand at Lahore in 1940. **Marks (3):** 1 for each leader.
8. **Model:** Sovereignty of Allah (Preamble); authority as a trust (Preamble, "a sacred trust"); justice (Qur'an 4:58); consultation (Qur'an 42:38). Equality (Qur'an 49:13) and freedom of faith (Qur'an 2:256) are also acceptable. **Marks (4):** 1 for each feature with fitting support.
9. **Model:** In a theocracy, a religious class rules in God's name without the people's consent. Iqbal says the Islamic state is not headed by a representative of God who hides his will behind a claim of infallibility. For him it is an effort to make equality, solidarity and freedom real in a human organisation, and he calls a republic consistent with the spirit of Islam. He uses "theocracy" only in the sense that such a state serves ideal principles. **Marks (4):** 1 for the meaning of theocracy; 1 for Iqbal's rejection; 1 for his positive account; 1 for the special sense of the word.
10. **Model:** Hassan reports that Qureshi sees the Two-Nation Theory as the reason for Pakistan's creation, with Muslim identity at the centre. Jalal argues that religion was one reason but not the only one. Hassan concludes that the theory played an important role, but that Pakistan came from a series of events and decisions and cannot be linked to one cause. **Marks (4):** 1 for Qureshi; 1 for Jalal; 1 for the point of difference; 1 for Hassan's conclusion.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.

**ERQ 1: Are values outside the Constitution?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understanding of a constitution | Explains both its legal role and its statement of values | Explains one well | Vague | Absent or wrong |
| Use of the Preamble | Quotes fitting words and explains them | Quotes without explaining | Mentions it only | Absent |
| Use of two articles | Two fitting articles, each linked to a value | One article linked, or two only named | One only named | None |
| Analysis of the statement (**Analyze**) | Separates what is right from what is wrong, and gives reasons for each | Shows what is wrong, but not what is right | Simply disagrees | No analysis |

**ERQ 2: Ideals without articles?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Difference between ideology and constitution | Clear on what binds and what does not, with reasons | Partly clear | Vague | Absent or wrong |
| Example of an article | A specific article shown to matter to a real pupil | Article named with a general link | Article named only | None |
| Fair treatment of the view | States what is reasonable in the headteacher's concern | Mentions it briefly | Dismisses it | Absent |
| Judgement and justification (**Evaluate**) | Clear judgement that follows from the evidence | Judgement with general reasons | Opinion only | No judgement |

**ERQ 3: Can they live side by side?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What the theory claimed | Accurate: nationhood and self-government | Partly accurate | Definition only | Wrong or absent |
| Evidence | Two speeches and two articles used accurately | Three of these | One or two | None |
| Why people believe the reading | Gives a real reason, such as how "could not be united" is read | General reason | Mentions it only | Absent |
| Analysis of the error (**Analyze**) | Shows exactly how political union is confused with living as neighbours | Shows the error in general terms | States that the reading is wrong | No analysis |

**ERQ 4: Only Islam?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The three commitments | All three explained | Two explained, or all three only listed | One | Absent |
| Evidence from the Preamble | Fitting words linked to each point | Some words, partly linked | Mentions it only | Absent |
| The tension | A real tension, fairly stated from both sides | Stated from one side | Named only | Absent |
| Judgement (**Evaluate**) | Says how far the statement holds, and treats the central place of Islam fairly | Clear view with general reasons | Opinion only | No judgement |

**ERQ 5: One principle in the Constitution**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Founding statement and Preamble | Both accurate and clearly linked to the principle | Both given, with a weak link | One only | Absent or wrong |
| Two articles | Two fitting articles, with what each requires | Two named, one explained | One only | None |
| Analysis of legal force (**Analyze**) | Uses the difference between Fundamental Rights, Principles of Policy or other provisions to show how strongly the principle is carried | Mentions legal force without using it | Says that it is "the law" | No analysis |
| Judgement of how fully it is carried (**Evaluate**) | Reasoned judgement, including a gap or tension | Judgement with general reasons | Opinion only | No judgement |
