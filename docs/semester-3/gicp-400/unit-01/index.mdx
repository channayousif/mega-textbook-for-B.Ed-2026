# Ideology, Constitution and the Ideological Foundations of Pakistan

<Figure id="fig-U1-5" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/gicp-400/unit-01/fig-U1-5.webp" alt="Trainee teachers in a Sindh classroom discuss an open book with their cooperating teacher, connecting shared civic ideas to written rules." width={1376} height={768} />

"Is this an ideal, or is it a law?" "Does this mean my family should not be here?" Pupils ask teachers questions like these, and they deserve clear and honest answers. This unit gives you the ideas you need to answer them. The rest of the course, from the Pakistan Movement to the 1973 Constitution, builds on these ideas.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the meaning of ideology, and test whether a statement could belong to one. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-1-1)*
- Explain what a constitution does and why it matters, using articles of the Constitution of Pakistan. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-1-1)*
- Trace a value through principle to article, and analyse how far the article carries it. *(Analyze; SLO:GICP-400-1-1)*
- Explain what the Two-Nation Theory claimed and did not claim, using the words of its leaders. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-1-2)*
- Explain the main features of the Islamic concept of state, and how it differs from theocracy. *(Understand; SLO:GICP-400-1-2)*
- Analyse how the Ideology of Pakistan joins its two roots, and how one of its principles appears in the Constitution. *(Analyze; SLO:GICP-400-1-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

HSC-level history is enough. GPKS-402 Pakistan Studies in Semester 2 is useful background, but it is not required. Bring what you already remember about the Pakistan Movement, and be ready to look again at definitions you may have memorised.

## In this unit

1. [Ideology and constitution](./topic-01) - the meaning of ideology, the concept and importance of a constitution, and how the two are related.
2. [The ideological foundations of Pakistan](./topic-02) - the Two-Nation Theory, the Islamic concept of state and the Ideology of Pakistan.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each one begins with a real school situation, explains the ideas and shows a worked example before asking you to use them. Keep a copy of the Constitution nearby if you can, and check each article yourself. This unit uses the National Assembly edition amended to 28 February 2012 for introductory study, with an accessible transcription linked in each topic. It is not a guide to current court procedure.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
