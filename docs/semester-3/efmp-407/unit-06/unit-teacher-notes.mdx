# Economic Dimensions - Teacher Notes

These notes support the teacher educator who teaches this unit. They contain no assessment items. The course guide names lectures, class discussions, question-answer sessions and brainstorming as teaching strategies, with group work, assignments and presentations as practical work. The guide gives this unit Weeks 11 and 12.

## Sequencing

Teach the topics in order. Topic 6.1 gives the economic vocabulary and the habit of working out costs. Topic 6.2 moves from households to companies. Topic 6.3 returns to the individual buyer and uses both.

1. **Week 11, first session.** Open with Asif and Sana's objections before giving any definition. Brainstorm what "green" means, then teach the two conditions and work through the solar case.
2. **Week 11, second session.** Teach sustainable consumption. Ask trainees to bring an electricity bill, then run the fan calculation with their own tariff. Run "Move the cost".
3. **Week 12, first session.** Use the mill and the desks. Teach CSR, charity and greenwashing, work through Company R, then run "Desks and water".
4. **Week 12, second session.** Bring real packets and read the labels together. Work through the classroom fan, then run "The fair's gift bags".

## Discussion prompt and a likely wrong answer

Ask: "A company gives a large donation to flood relief. Is it a responsible company?" Many trainees will say yes at once, and some will say that questioning a gift is ungrateful. Do not correct them straight away. Ask how the company treats its workers and what its business does to the river. Let the difference between giving and earning come from their answers. Point out that one can be thankful for a gift and still ask the question.

## Likely misconceptions

- **"A green economy means slower growth and fewer jobs."** Use the ILO figures, and insist on their limits: a model, a world figure and real losers. Do not replace one slogan with another. The evidence here is about jobs and does not settle the question of growth.
- **"CSR is the same as charity."** Ask two questions side by side: what did it give, and how did it earn? The SECP's own 2009 press release mixes the two, so the confusion is understandable.
- **"One person's shopping choices cannot matter."** Accept the true part. Then ask who in the room chooses for others: a head teacher, a canteen owner, a parent.
- **"Sustainable products are always more expensive."** Separate first price from cost over time, then add the third category: choices that cost nothing. Keep the question of who can pay the first price on the table.

## Cautions on sensitive matters

- **Family money.** Some trainees and pupils come from households with very little choice. Do not run any activity that makes them list what their family cannot buy. Keep the focus on what can be done together.
- **Real companies.** Trainees may want to name local firms. Teach them to criticise a claim using evidence, and not to accuse people of what they cannot show. A trainee may have a parent working at the firm.
- **The Ali Enterprises fire.** People died, and some trainees may have links to Karachi's garment workers. Treat it briefly and with respect. Present both sides of the audit dispute as they stand in the sources.
- **The law.** The status of the CSR bill may have changed since this unit was written. Check it before teaching, and show trainees how you checked.
- **Halal.** Teach the halal logo as a serious assurance on the question it answers. The point is about the scope of any label, and it does not question religious practice.

## Practical work

- **Group work.** The three topic activities.
- **Group assignment.** A one-page chart of the labels found in a local shop, showing what each claims and who checks it.
- **Individual assignment.** The summative task in Topic 6.2, using a real company's claim.
- **Presentation.** Five minutes on one purchase for a school, with its cost over time and what remains unknown.

Check that sources used by trainees are real and can be found. Praise a trainee who writes "not known" over one who fills a gap with a guess.
