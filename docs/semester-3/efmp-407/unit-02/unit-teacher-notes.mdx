# Principles and Frameworks of ESD - Teacher Notes

## Teaching strategies

The EFMP-407 course guide recommends lectures, discussions, question-answer sessions and brainstorming, with group and individual assignments and presentations as practical work (University of Sindh, *B.Ed. third-semester course guide*, EFMP-407 teaching-strategy section). Use a short explanation to establish vocabulary, then let trainees use it in a case. These notes support that guide provision. They introduce no additional assessment items.

Begin with the Unit 1 dimensions. Ask trainees to recall what makes a development choice environmental, social and economic. Write the three headings on the board, leaving room for links. The purpose is to recover prior understanding, not to classify every daily act into exactly one box. Then introduce the three learning domains. Keep dimensions and domains visually separate: the first concern aspects of sustainability, the second concern what learners develop.

In Topic 2.1, model a single objective and its evidence before asking groups for three. A frequent weak response is “Pupils will become responsible”. Ask what the pupil could say, explain or produce during this lesson that would support that judgement. Do not assume a pupil's correct calculation reveals a private attitude. Equally, do not assume concern alone shows accurate knowledge.

In Topic 2.2, read both lesson descriptions aloud before assigning labels. Ask, “Which words in the description support your claim?” If a group says EE has no social dimension, return to the explanation of overlap. If it calls any group activity ESD, ask which sustainability relationships influence the decision. A method is not evidence of every intended outcome.

In Topic 2.3, work the invented tank calculation on the board. Ask a trainee to explain each flow while another checks the arithmetic. Next, add a low-level report and an adult's response. Trace the path back to the level. Many first drawings stop at the report; that is a chain with no return effect, not a complete feedback loop. Ask what response would close it and what happens if the response is delayed.

## Sequencing and inclusion

Use the approved weeks 3-4 for this unit. Reading time is separate from the longer discussion, activity and writing time. A possible sequence is framework and objective planning, comparison of lesson evidence, reflection and system modelling, then independent assessment. Adjust session lengths to the local timetable while retaining time for group explanation and revision.

Give a minute of quiet thinking before discussion. Invite trainees to rehearse an explanation in a familiar language and then record a short English account. Allow oral explanation, a diagram or a short written note during practice, while keeping the same conceptual demand. The English pages remain drafts awaiting independent review; they are not a completed Urdu resource.

Use rotating roles so the confident speaker does not perform all the reasoning. A recorder can ask for a reason, an assumptions checker can identify missing evidence, and a spokesperson can explain a revision. Let the next presentation use a different spokesperson. Assess the explanation rather than English fluency alone in a practice discussion.

## Likely misconceptions

“ESD needs a new timetable subject” confuses an approach to learning with an administrative label. Ask the trainee to keep an existing subject aim and add a connected decision. “EE is only learning animal names” ignores its concern with human-environment relationships and participation. Ask for a fair comparison rather than a ranking of labels.

“Transformative learning means making pupils agree” confuses learning with compliance. Invite a supported alternative and ask what could change either position. “Systems thinking means advanced mathematics” can be answered with a small stock-and-flow account and labelled arrows. The important next step is explaining links and limits.

A useful discussion prompt is: “If a repair has not happened, can a pupil still show successful learning?” A likely first response is that the project failed and nobody learned. Separate the adult-controlled outcome from the pupil's explanation, evidence and feasible proposal. This protects learners from being judged on resources they do not control.

## Practical work

Use clean paper role cards and hypothetical records for group assignments. Have groups present a first model, receive one challenge from peers, and show a revision. Individual reflection can briefly identify an initial assumption, the evidence examined and the reason for changing or retaining the explanation. Keep classroom examples fictional and label invented quantities clearly.

Practicum work is limited to a short discussion with the cooperating teacher or observation of a safe classroom routine. No task requires collecting household income, judging families, photographing pupils, handling rubbish, entering a tank area or testing drinking water. If a real maintenance issue arises, pass it to the responsible adult through school procedures. A learning activity does not authorise trainees or children to undertake repairs.
