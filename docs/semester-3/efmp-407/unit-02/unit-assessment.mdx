# Principles and Frameworks of ESD - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

An ESD lesson connects knowledge, consideration of others and feasible action. These learning domains need different evidence. Sustainability competencies help learners use their knowledge across situations, rather than repeat a list. A lesson can develop such learning within an existing subject.

EE and ESD overlap. Environmental education can include values, inquiry and participation. An ESD approach makes environmental, social and economic relationships explicit in decisions about shared futures. Judge a lesson through the work pupils do, and distinguish missing description from evidence of absence.

Transformative learning concerns the assumptions behind an explanation. It requires room for reasoned revision or disagreement. Systems thinking helps test explanations by tracing relationships, stocks, flows, feedback and delays. A useful proposal identifies both what an action can change and what remains uncertain. The cases and quantities in this assessment are fictional teaching materials.

## Summative assessment

Attempt the bank before reading its final section. MCQs carry 1 mark each, RRQs 4 marks each and ERQs 10 marks each: 100 raw marks in total. These raw marks can be scaled to the unit's 60% summative share; formative topic work supplies the other 40%. This is a self-study bank, not the restricted LMS quiz bank.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which learning domain principally concerns knowledge and thinking in UNESCO's ESD framework? *(Remember)*
   - A) Behavioural
   - B) Cognitive
   - C) Socio-emotional
   - D) Institutional
2. Pupils listen to a younger child's difficulty reaching a shared tap and explain why the plan should consider it. Which domain is most directly evidenced? *(Apply)*
   - A) Cognitive, through recalling tank capacity
   - B) Behavioural, through repairing the tap
   - C) Socio-emotional, through considering another's needs
   - D) Cognitive, through calculating water loss
3. A group compares what might happen to a school garden in a dry month and a wet month before choosing plants. Which competency is most directly practised? *(Apply)*
   - A) Anticipatory thinking
   - B) Self-awareness
   - C) Collaboration
   - D) Strategic thinking
4. A teacher adds water-saving work to an existing mathematics lesson. Which planning choice best supports an ESD approach? *(Apply)*
   - A) Replace calculation with copying a sustainability slogan.
   - B) Award marks for bringing a purchased water bottle.
   - C) Label the worksheet ESD while keeping its questions unchanged.
   - D) Connect quantities, fair access and a feasible reporting action.
5. What best describes the relationship between EE and ESD? *(Understand)*
   - A) EE studies nature without considering people or actions.
   - B) They overlap, while ESD explicitly links sustainability dimensions.
   - C) ESD replaces environmental knowledge with economic knowledge.
   - D) They differ mainly in whether pupils work in groups.
6. A lesson asks pupils to identify local birds and explain how litter harms them. The description says nothing about costs or access. Which judgement is best supported? *(Apply)*
   - A) It has no value because it lacks the ESD label.
   - B) It proves that the teacher rejects social learning.
   - C) It shows an EE emphasis; a broader ESD claim needs more evidence.
   - D) It shows all sustainability dimensions through its bird topic.
7. Pupils compare litter reduction, affordability and access before proposing a lunch-container rule. What most strongly supports an ESD interpretation? *(Apply)*
   - A) The connected concerns influence their proposed rule.
   - B) The teacher uses a recently printed worksheet.
   - C) The classroom has a labelled recycling display.
   - D) The group chooses a single shared slogan.
8. Which change is strongest evidence of transformative learning in a trainee's account? *(Understand)*
   - A) Adding another water fact without revisiting the explanation
   - B) Repeating the tutor's preferred judgement without reasons
   - C) Replacing a short slogan with a longer slogan
   - D) Revising an assumption about storage after examining supply evidence
9. A tank starts with 50 litres, receives 25, supplies 20 and leaks 5. What is its end stock? *(Apply)*
   - A) 45 litres
   - B) 50 litres
   - C) 70 litres
   - D) 75 litres
10. Which description contains a complete balancing feedback loop for a water shortage? *(Apply)*
   - A) A tank supplies taps, and pupils use the water.
   - B) A poster states a saving target, and pupils read it.
   - C) A low level prompts a report, an adult arranges delivery, and the level recovers.
   - D) A leak lowers the tank level, and the class records the loss.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, distinguish cognitive, socio-emotional and behavioural evidence in a lesson about sharing a school reading box. Give one example of each. *(Understand)*
2. A teacher gives marks only for reciting water-saving facts. In three or four sentences, analyze two learning demands this misses and propose evidence for each. *(Analyze)*
3. In three sentences, explain why sustainability competencies are more than names to memorise. Use collaboration and critical thinking as examples. *(Understand)*
4. In three or four sentences, compare EE and ESD through one shared aim and one difference of emphasis. Include a school example. *(Understand)*
5. Pupils list trees and soil features; another group uses the list to compare shade, path access and repair cost. In four sentences, analyze the evidence for classifying each task. State one limit of your judgement. *(Analyze)*
6. In three sentences, explain a trade-off in requiring all pupils to buy a particular reusable container. State an alternative and what information would help compare it. *(Understand)*
7. A trainee changes “families store water because they are careless” to “storage may respond to an unreliable supply”. In three or four sentences, explain what assumption changed and why the revision still needs evidence. *(Analyze)*
8. A pupil disagrees with a teacher's garden plan. In three sentences, explain how the teacher could support reflective learning without requiring agreement. Include one piece of assessable evidence. *(Understand)*
9. A tank starts with 90 litres, receives 30, supplies 25 and leaks 10. In a short calculation and two sentences, distinguish stock from flows and explain why reducing leakage alone may not guarantee a reliable supply. *(Analyze)*
10. In four sentences, trace a low-level report through an adult's delivery response back to the tank level. Explain one delay and one way to check whether the response helped. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

For each task, write about 250-350 words. A labelled diagram may accompany the text. Marks reward reasons grounded in the case; buying materials or agreeing with a teacher brings no extra credit.

1. A primary class copies slogans about reducing paper use. Its teacher claims the lesson develops all three UNESCO learning domains. Analyze that claim, then design three connected objectives with matching evidence and justify two sustainability competencies your revision would develop. Include the needs of a pupil who uses larger sheets. *(Analyze / Create)*
2. Lesson A asks pupils to explain how litter harms birds. Lesson B retains that learning and asks pupils to compare a bin plan for cost, access and safe adult collection. Evaluate the EE or ESD emphasis of each using four pieces of lesson evidence. Explain the overlap and one limit of classifying from these descriptions. *(Evaluate)*
3. A trainee believes a water shortage is caused only by pupils' careless use. Draw a model with a tank stock, delivery, use, a leak and a reporting loop with a delay. Analyze the claim through this model. Explain how a tutor could help the trainee question it without imposing an opinion, and propose a checkable action. *(Analyze)*
4. A school plans to require every family to buy the same reusable lunch container. Some trainees call this ESD because it aims to reduce waste. Create a revised lesson that connects UNESCO's three domains, compares the environmental and wider ESD concerns, and invites learners to question an assumption. Trace one relationship that could produce an unintended effect. Give evidence you would collect to judge the learning. *(Create)*
5. A school garden plan promises that planting more trees will solve heat, fairness and budget problems. Evaluate this promise using UNESCO's framework, the EE-ESD comparison and systems thinking. Identify an assumption worth revisiting, trace two consequences including a possible delay, and justify a modest action with a follow-up check. State one uncertainty. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

These are public self-study responses. Other examples can earn credit if they meet the stated demands. For RRQs, award each listed point independently; do not award the same idea twice. For ERQs, apply the analytic criteria separately. Unsupported claims and invented school data do not count as evidence.

### MCQ answer key

1. B - cognitive learning concerns knowing and thinking.
2. C - attention to another child's needs directly shows the socio-emotional domain.
3. A - comparing possible future conditions directly practises anticipatory thinking.
4. D - the work connects knowledge, fairness and a feasible action within mathematics.
5. B - the traditions overlap; ESD makes connections across dimensions explicit.
6. C - the described work supports EE without proving what else the teacher includes.
7. A - the dimensions affect the decision rather than simply decorate a label.
8. D - the trainee revisits the assumption behind the original explanation using evidence.
9. B - 50 + 25 - 20 - 5 = 50 litres.
10. C - the response returns to reduce the condition that triggered it.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Cognitive evidence: a pupil explains the borrowing arrangement. Socio-emotional evidence: a pupil considers a classmate who cannot reach the box. Behavioural evidence: pupils use or help trial an agreed borrowing routine. **4 marks:** 1 per correctly matched example; 1 for explicitly distinguishing knowledge, consideration and action.
2. Facts show some cognitive learning but do not establish concern for others or action. Ask pupils to explain a younger child's access needs and prepare an evidence-based report to the responsible adult. **4 marks:** 1 for each missing demand, 1 for each suitably matched piece of evidence. Do not require pupils to carry out repairs.
3. A competency is demonstrated through use. Collaboration could involve revising a plan after listening to another role. Critical thinking could involve questioning whether a proposed restriction addresses a leak. **4 marks:** 1 for ability in use, 1 for each apt example, 1 for explaining why reciting the labels alone is insufficient.
4. Both can develop responsible participation concerning environmental matters. ESD explicitly connects environmental, social and economic dimensions. For example, a waste investigation can be extended to compare costs and access without dropping its environmental evidence. **4 marks:** shared aim 1; distinction 1; apt example 1; overlap qualified without excluding social learning from EE 1.
5. The first task shows environmental observation, so an EE emphasis is supported. The second makes environmental evidence affect a decision alongside access and cost, supporting ESD. The descriptions may leave other learning unmentioned. **4 marks:** first classification with evidence 1; second classification with evidence 1; explains the connected decision 1; states the evidence limit 1.
6. A required purchase may reduce some discarded packaging but exclude pupils whose families cannot afford it. A class could compare a voluntary reuse plan using suitable existing containers. It needs information about safe reuse, costs and available cleaning. **4 marks:** intended benefit 1; plausible cost or exclusion 1; relevant alternative 1; decision-relevant information 1.
7. The original account assumed that storage proves lack of care. The revision recognises uncertain delivery as an alternative cause. It still needs evidence on timing, amounts and leaks; it does not prove all storage is necessary or loss-free. **4 marks:** identifies the assumption 1; links revision to supply 1; names relevant evidence 1; retains uncertainty rather than reversing the generalisation 1.
8. Ask the pupil to explain the concern and compare it with the plan's evidence. Invite another perspective and allow a reasoned revision or retention of the view. Assess a short account of the assumption, evidence and conclusion. **4 marks:** respectful invitation 1; comparison of evidence/perspectives 1; no imposed agreement 1; assessable reflection 1.
9. 90 + 30 - 25 - 10 = 85 litres. The tank's held amount is the stock; delivery, use and leakage are flows. A delayed or reduced delivery could still cause a shortage after a repair. **4 marks:** calculation 1; stock 1; flows 1; another linked influence or uncertainty 1.
10. A low tank level prompts a report. An adult responds by arranging a delivery, which raises the level and reduces the shortage. A delivery delay means the response is not immediate. Compare level and delivery records before and after, while checking for changed use or leaks. **4 marks:** trigger/report 1; return effect completing the loop 1; delay 1; relevant check 1.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task carries 10 marks. Use the levels within each criterion below. A sound alternative plan may earn full marks. The response must explain relationships; a list of domain or competency names is insufficient.

1. **Domains, 0-3:** 0 absent/incorrect; 1 one clear domain; 2 three named with partial distinctions; 3 all three distinguished through lesson evidence. **Objectives and evidence, 0-3:** 0 missing; 1 vague objectives; 2 partly matched products; 3 three observable, connected objectives with evidence. **Competencies, 0-2:** 0 labels only/absent; 1 one justified choice; 2 two task-specific choices. **Analysis of access, 0-2:** 0 ignored; 1 need named; 2 paper reduction and larger-sheet access balanced through a justified revision. This final criterion demands analysis of competing needs.
2. **Comparison evidence, 0-4:** 0 unsupported; 1-3 one mark per distinct relevant piece; 4 four pieces linked to the classifications. **Relationship of EE/ESD, 0-2:** 0 inaccurate separation; 1 partial distinction; 2 accurate emphasis and overlap. **Evaluation, 0-3:** 0 labels only; 1 asserted preference; 2 reasoned judgement about pupil work; 3 qualified judgement explaining how dimensions influence the bin decision. **Limit, 0-1:** 1 for recognising that a short description can omit other learning. Evaluation is required for the three-mark criterion.
3. **System model, 0-3:** 0 absent; 1 stock or flows only; 2 stock and directional flows; 3 stock, flows and complete reporting-response loop. **Causal analysis, 0-3:** 0 blame repeated; 1 alternative cause named; 2 explained relationships; 3 claim tested against more than one plausible influence. **Reflection, 0-2:** 0 imposed conclusion; 1 invitation to reconsider; 2 evidence-based dialogue preserving agency. **Action, 0-2:** 0 slogan; 1 feasible action; 2 action linked to a cause, delay and follow-up check. Causal analysis must go beyond naming parts.
4. **Connected domains, 0-3:** 0 absent; 1 labels only; 2 two domains with evidence; 3 all three connected with assessable evidence. **EE/ESD comparison, 0-2:** 0 inaccurate; 1 distinction stated; 2 environmental learning preserved while cost/access shape the decision. **Assumption and reflection, 0-2:** 0 missing; 1 assumption named; 2 a respectful process for testing it. **Unintended effect and revision, 0-3:** 0 missing; 1 effect asserted; 2 causal chain explained; 3 chain informs a feasible revised lesson. This criterion requires creating a response to the identified relationship, not prescribing a purchase.
5. **Framework and comparison, 0-3:** 0 absent; 1 names concepts; 2 applies either domains or EE/ESD accurately; 3 applies both with case evidence. **Systems analysis, 0-3:** 0 isolated slogan; 1 one consequence; 2 two explained consequences; 3 two consequences with a meaningful delay. **Assumption and uncertainty, 0-2:** 0 absent; 1 one identified; 2 both linked to the strength of the promise. **Justified action and check, 0-2:** 0 unsupported; 1 action or check; 2 modest action and relevant follow-up justified against the promise. Full credit requires evaluating the promise rather than declaring tree planting automatically sufficient or useless.
