# Principles and Frameworks of ESD

<Figure id="fig-U2-7" src="/img/figures/efmp-407/unit-02/fig-U2-7.webp" alt="Trainee teachers discuss water use and shared access with a cooperating teacher in a Sindh school courtyard." kind="illustration" />

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain how UNESCO's learning domains and sustainability competencies guide an ESD lesson. This serves SLO:EFMP-407-2-1.
- Compare education for sustainable development with environmental education, using lesson evidence rather than the teacher's label. This serves SLO:EFMP-407-2-1.
- Explain how a learner can question an assumption without being forced to adopt a teacher's opinion. This serves SLO:EFMP-407-2-2.
- Trace a change through a simple water-supply system and propose an action with a check for unintended effects. This serves SLO:EFMP-407-2-2.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Unit 1's environmental, social and economic dimensions of sustainable development. You should be able to explain why meeting a need today can affect people tomorrow. You need no advanced mathematics for this unit. Simple quantities, arrows and reasons are enough to begin thinking about systems.

Before reading, write one sentence about a school decision that involves more than one dimension. Keep it. At the end, revisit what your first sentence left out. This is a starting point for reflection, not a test of whether your family makes the right choices.

## How to use this unit

Read in order. Each topic begins with an invented but plausible Sindh classroom situation. These cases are teaching examples, not research reports about named schools. Study the worked example before attempting the pair task. Use paper and pencil; internet access is useful for further reading but is not needed for the activities.

Try the retrieval questions with the book closed. Reopen the explanation to check your reasons. Tick a self-assessment statement only when you can show the skill through an example. A disagreement can be useful if both learners explain their evidence and listen to the other view.

Use the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) after all three topics. Attempt its questions before reading the final public self-study guidance. In a teacher-led course, the default unit weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative. The course guide's separate course marks are mid-term 30, final term 50, assignment/presentation 10 and attendance 10. The unit bank does not replace those course rules.

The [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes) offer sequencing and practical-work suggestions. Practicum tasks are brief observations or discussions with your cooperating teacher. Do not collect family income, identify pupils, handle waste or test drinking water. Learning to explain a decision matters more here than delivering a large project.
