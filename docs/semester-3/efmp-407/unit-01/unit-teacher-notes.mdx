# Concept and Evolution of Sustainable Development - Teacher Notes

These notes support the teacher educator who teaches this unit. They contain no assessment items. The course guide names lectures, class discussions, question-answer sessions and brainstorming as teaching strategies, with group work, assignments and presentations as practical work. The guide gives this unit Weeks 1 and 2.

## Sequencing

Teach the topics in order. Topic 1.1 gives the vocabulary of needs and limits. Topic 1.2 tells the story of the milestones. Topic 1.3 gives the tool for analysis that the rest of the course uses.

1. **Week 1, first session.** Open with Marvi and Nasreen's disagreement before giving any definition. Brainstorm what "development" means, then give the definition and run the worked example.
2. **Week 1, second session.** Run the "Three decisions, four questions" activity. Set Topic 1.2 for reading.
3. **Week 2, first session.** Run the milestone jigsaw. Deal directly with the Brundtland question (see below).
4. **Week 2, second session.** Teach the dimensions through the Dadu school. Work through the dam example together, then begin the "One school, three chairs" activity.

## Discussion prompt and a likely wrong answer

Ask: "Is a tree-planting day an example of sustainable development?" Many trainees will say yes at once, because they think sustainability means the environment only. Do not correct them straight away. Ask who waters the trees, who owns the land, what pupils learned and what happens next year. Let the three dimensions appear from their answers.

## Likely misconceptions

- **"Sustainable development only means protecting the environment."** Use the forest example in Topic 1.3 and ask what happens to the people who lived from the forest.
- **"Development and sustainability are opposites."** Ask trainees to name a family that needs development. Then ask what it would mean for that development to fail in ten years.
- **"Sustainability is a concern for rich countries only."** Use the 2022 flood figures. Ask which families in your own district lost the most.
- **"The Brundtland Report was a conference."** The course guide lists it under conferences, and trainees may meet that wording in examinations. Keep the guide's list as the set of three milestones to know, and teach trainees to write that it is the report of a commission. Do not make them feel the guide has tricked them.

## A caution on sensitive cases

Dams, water sharing between provinces and housing schemes are live public arguments in Pakistan. Trainees may hold strong views or have family affected. Keep the discussion on evidence: what the source says, what it does not say and what is unknown. Do not ask trainees to take a side on a provincial dispute. The aim is the method of analysis.

## Practical work

- **Group work.** The three topic activities.
- **Group assignment.** A one-page chart of the three milestones for a school staffroom.
- **Individual assignment.** The summative task in Topic 1.3, using a local decision.
- **Presentation.** Five minutes on one local decision, with the three dimensions and one thing that is still unknown.

Check that sources used by trainees are real and can be found. Praise a trainee who writes "not known" over one who fills a gap with a guess.
