# Concept and Evolution of Sustainable Development - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Sustainable development means meeting today's needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs. It holds two ideas: the essential needs of the poor come first, and technology, society and the environment set limits. The idea grew from two complaints about economic growth: that it damaged nature and that it missed the poor. There is no single agreed definition, so a careful person asks who benefits, what limit is pressed and what is left for later.

Three milestones shaped the idea. The Stockholm Conference of 1972 made the environment a world issue, produced 26 principles and led to UNEP. The Brundtland Report of 1987, written by a commission, not a conference, gave the best-known definition and linked poverty to environmental harm. Rio+20 in 2012 began the process that led to the Sustainable Development Goals and asked educational institutions to act.

Sustainability is commonly discussed through three dimensions: environmental, economic and social. They depend on each other, so a gain on one side can cause a loss on another. Sound analysis looks at all three, names the trade-offs and says what is still unknown. A teacher who can do this can help pupils do it too.

For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All named pupils, teachers and villages in the questions are illustrative.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose A, B, C or D. Each item is worth 1 mark.

1. According to the Brundtland definition, development is sustainable when it meets present needs without harming what? *(Remember)*
   - A) The profits of existing businesses
   - B) The ability of future generations to meet their own needs
   - C) The growth rate of the national economy
   - D) The natural landscape in its original state

2. The Commission said its definition contains two key ideas. Which pair is it? *(Understand)*
   - A) Growth and profit
   - B) Conservation and tourism
   - C) Needs and limits
   - D) Laws and punishments

3. A factory near Kotri gives 200 people jobs, but its waste enters a canal that farms downstream depend on. Which question from the definition most directly exposes the problem? *(Apply)*
   - A) What limit does this press on, and what is left for others later?
   - B) How many workers does the factory employ?
   - C) Is the factory owner from this district?
   - D) How modern is the factory's machinery?

4. Which major result came from the 1972 Stockholm Conference? *(Remember)*
   - A) The Sustainable Development Goals
   - B) The report *Our Common Future*
   - C) The outcome document *The Future We Want*
   - D) The United Nations Environment Programme

5. What was the Brundtland Report? *(Remember)*
   - A) The final declaration of a meeting held in Oslo
   - B) The report of a commission set up by the UN General Assembly
   - C) A treaty signed by all UN member states
   - D) A UNESCO plan for schools

6. Why is the 2012 conference called Rio+20? *(Understand)*
   - A) Twenty countries attended it in Rio de Janeiro
   - B) It set twenty new goals
   - C) It was held twenty years after the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio
   - D) It lasted for twenty days

7. A head teacher in Larkana asks pupils, teachers and a local water committee to check and reduce water waste on the school campus. Which part of the milestones most directly supports this? *(Apply)*
   - A) Stockholm's Action Plan on global assessment
   - B) The Commission's public hearings
   - C) The Earth Summit's forest principles
   - D) Rio+20's call for sustainability practice on campuses and in communities

8. Which three dimensions of sustainability does this unit use? *(Remember)*
   - A) Environmental, economic and social
   - B) Political, religious and cultural
   - C) Local, national and global
   - D) Past, present and future

9. Why are the three dimensions usually drawn as overlapping circles? *(Understand)*
   - A) Each dimension is more important than the others
   - B) A change in one dimension can affect the others
   - C) Only experts can tell the dimensions apart
   - D) All three dimensions have the same cost

10. A housing scheme near Hyderabad is well financed and sells quickly, and it keeps a green belt. But the farming families who lived on the land were moved with no say and no new income. Which dimension is weakest? *(Apply)*
    - A) Environmental
    - B) Economic for the buyers
    - C) Social
    - D) Technical

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each question within the length stated. Marks are shown in brackets.

1. Explain the Brundtland definition in your own words, and give one Pakistani example of a need it would put first. Use no more than three sentences. [3] *(Understand)*
2. Explain, in three or four sentences, what the Commission meant by "limits", and why a new technology can move a limit but not remove it. [3] *(Understand)*
3. A friend says, "You must choose: either development or sustainability." Give two reasons why this is a false choice. Use no more than four sentences. [4] *(Analyze)*
4. State three outcomes of the Stockholm Conference. Then explain in one or two sentences why Principle 19 matters to teachers. [4] *(Understand)*
5. A pupil asks where the Brundtland conference was held. Write the reply you would give, in no more than four sentences, including why the name appears among the milestones. [3] *(Understand)*
6. State two things agreed at Rio+20, and explain in one sentence each how they connect to schools or to the later global goals. [4] *(Understand)*
7. Compare Stockholm (1972) and Rio+20 (2012) on how widely each framed the problem. Use no more than five sentences. [4] *(Analyze)*
8. In one sentence each, state what each dimension asks, using a single example from a school. [3] *(Understand)*
9. Using the Indus Delta, show in three or four sentences how an environmental change became an economic and a social one. [3] *(Apply)*
10. An illustrative town council in Sanghar plans to replace a shaded market with a concrete plaza. Shopkeepers will pay higher rents, more buyers will come, and the old trees will go. Name one trade-off, and say which group gains and which loses. Use no more than four sentences. [4] *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Write about 250-350 words for each task. Each task is marked out of 12.

1. A college speaker says, "Sustainability is a worry for rich countries. Pakistan must develop first." Evaluate this claim, using the Brundtland definition and at least one Pakistani case. Reach a clear judgement. *(Evaluate)*
2. Analyse how the idea of sustainable development changed from Stockholm in 1972, through the Brundtland Report in 1987, to Rio+20 in 2012. For each milestone, show what it added and what it still left to do. *(Analyze)*
3. Choose one Pakistani development decision (a dam or a housing scheme). Analyse it against the environmental, economic and social dimensions. Name at least one trade-off, separate what you know from what you do not know, and reach a judgement. *(Analyze)*
4. A school in Nawabshah holds one tree-planting day a year and calls itself a "sustainable development school". Evaluate this claim, using the definition, at least one milestone and all three dimensions. Say what would have to change for the claim to be justified. *(Evaluate)*
5. Design a 40-minute Class 8 lesson that teaches sustainable development through one local issue in your district. Your plan must use needs and limits, at least one milestone and all three dimensions, and it must include one pupil task. Justify your choices. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** The definition is about the ability of future generations. It does not ask to freeze the landscape (D) or protect profits (A).
2. **C.** The Commission names needs and limits. The others are not in its definition.
3. **A.** The waste presses on a limit and takes away what farms downstream depend on. The other questions do not test sustainability.
4. **D.** UNEP was a major result of Stockholm. *Our Common Future* came in 1987; *The Future We Want* and the process toward the goals came in 2012.
5. **B.** It came from the World Commission on Environment and Development, set up by the UN General Assembly in 1983. There was no Brundtland conference.
6. **C.** The "+20" counts the years since the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro.
7. **D.** Paragraph 234 of *The Future We Want* encourages this kind of practice, with students, teachers and local partners.
8. **A.** These are the three dimensions used in the unit and in the Rio+20 text.
9. **B.** The overlap shows that the dimensions depend on each other. It does not rank them.
10. **C.** The people affected had no voice and lost their income, which is a question of fairness. The finance (B) and the green belt (A) are stronger.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Development should meet people's needs today and leave later generations able to meet theirs. Safe drinking water for a village in Tharparkar is a need it would put first. **Marks (3):** 2 for the two parts of the definition in the learner's own words; 1 for a suitable essential need.
2. Limits are the point up to which the environment can meet needs, given our technology and how society is organised. Better irrigation can make the same water go further. But the river still holds only so much water, so the limit moves and does not disappear. **Marks (3):** 1 for the meaning; 1 for technology and society; 1 for the reason a limit remains.
3. The term was made to join the two, so choosing one misreads it. People without clean water or schooling need development, and development that fails or harms others does not serve them for long. **Marks (4):** 2 for each reason with explanation.
4. The Declaration with 26 principles, the Action Plan with 109 recommendations and UNEP. Principle 19 says environmental education is essential for the young and for adults, with care for the underprivileged, which is a task for teachers. **Marks (4):** 1 each for three outcomes; 1 for the link to teachers.
5. There was no such conference. The Brundtland Report is the 1987 report of a UN-established commission, named after its chair. It appears among the milestones because it gave the best-known definition, and the course guide lists it with the conferences. **Marks (3):** 1 for saying it was not a conference; 1 for what it was; 1 for why it is listed.
6. Sample: the process toward global goals (paragraph 248), which led to the Sustainable Development Goals; and the resolve to promote ESD beyond the Decade (paragraph 233), which asks education systems to act. **Marks (4):** 1 for each thing agreed; 1 for each connection.
7. Stockholm made the environment the main issue, though it began a discussion of how growth and pollution connect with well-being. Rio+20 framed the problem from the start as economic, social and environmental together, and moved toward goals for all. So the frame widened from environment to all three dimensions. **Marks (4):** 1 for each milestone's frame; 2 for a comparison that states the change.
8. Environmental: does the new school tap waste water from the well? Economic: can the school afford to maintain it? Social: can all pupils, including girls and pupils with disabilities, reach it safely? **Marks (3):** 1 for each dimension with a sensible question.
9. Falling freshwater flows have let the sea move up the river, and mangrove species have been lost. Catches of valuable fish have fallen, which is an economic loss. Coastal families who depend on these resources have become poorer, which is a social harm. **Marks (3):** 1 for the environmental change; 1 for the economic effect; 1 for the social effect.
10. Sample: the council gains more buyers and activity, and some shopkeepers may gain customers, but small shopkeepers who cannot pay higher rents lose, and everyone loses shade and trees. The trade-off is more trade against fairness and a cooler market. **Marks (4):** 1 for naming a trade-off; 1 for who gains; 1 for who loses; 1 for a reason that links dimensions.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated.

**ERQ 1: Rich countries only?**

| Criterion | What a 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Use of the definition | Uses needs (the poor first) and limits, and notes that the Commission applied the idea to all countries |
| Pakistani evidence | Uses a checked case, such as the 2022 floods or the Indus Delta, accurately |
| Analysis of the claim (**Analyze**) | Shows why "develop first" assumes development and sustainability are separate, and what that costs the poor |
| Judgement (**Evaluate**) | Reaches a clear judgement that follows from the evidence, and is fair to the speaker's concern about poverty |

**ERQ 2: How the idea changed**

| Criterion | What a 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Dates, kinds of event and outcomes are correct, including that Brundtland was a commission's report |
| What each added | States one real addition for each milestone |
| Analysis of change (**Analyze**) | Traces a line of change, such as environment, then needs and limits, then integrated goals |
| What remained | Shows what each milestone left undone, with a reason |

**ERQ 3: One Pakistani decision**

| Criterion | What a 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Three dimensions | Treats all three accurately with case evidence |
| Trade-offs and links (**Analyze**) | Shows how the dimensions affect each other and names a real trade-off |
| Honesty about evidence | Separates known from unknown, with no invented figures |
| Judgement | Gives a reasoned judgement that follows from the analysis, on a contested decision without taking sides beyond the evidence |

**ERQ 4: The tree-planting school**

| Criterion | What a 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Use of ideas | Applies the definition, a milestone (such as Rio+20 paragraph 234) and all three dimensions |
| Analysis of the case (**Analyze**) | Separates what the event does from what the claim implies |
| Judgement (**Evaluate**) | Reaches a justified verdict and gives credit for what the event does achieve |
| What must change | Proposes realistic, specific changes the school could make |

**ERQ 5: A lesson of your own**

| Criterion | What a 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Needs and limits, one milestone and all three dimensions are all really taught |
| Local issue | The issue is real and suited to Class 8 in the district, and any facts are checked |
| Pupil task (**Create**) | The pupils produce or decide something; the task fits 40 minutes |
| Justification (**Evaluate**) | Explains why these choices suit these pupils and these aims |
