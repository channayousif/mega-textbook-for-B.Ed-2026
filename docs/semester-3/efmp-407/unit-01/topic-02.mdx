# Topic 1.2 - The global conferences that shaped the idea

## A real classroom situation

In this illustrative teaching case, Kamran is a trainee teacher at a boys' high school in Sukkur. He has written on the board: "Three global conferences: Stockholm, Brundtland, Rio+20." He copied this from his notes. Then Zubair, in Class 10, raises a hand and asks, "Sir, in which city was the Brundtland conference held? And why should we care about meetings in foreign cities?" Kamran does not know how to answer. His first problem is that the three names are not the same kind of thing. His second problem is the better question: what did those meetings change for a school in Sukkur?

## Explanation

Each of the three milestones added something the earlier ones did not have. Read them as a story: first the world noticed the problem, then it named a way forward, then it set about making goals.

### The Stockholm Conference (1972)

The United Nations Conference on the Human Environment met in Stockholm, Sweden, from 5 to 16 June 1972. The UN calls it the first world conference to make the environment a major issue (United Nations, n.d.). Representatives of 113 UN member states attended (United Nations, 1972).

The conference produced three main things.

- **The Stockholm Declaration**, with 26 principles.
- **An Action Plan**, with 109 recommendations.
- **The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP)**, created as a result of the conference.

It also started a discussion between industrialised and developing countries about how economic growth, pollution and people's well-being connect (United Nations, n.d.). Purvis et al. (2019) describe it as the first major attempt to bring economic development and care for the environment together, at a time when many people thought the two could not be combined.

Two principles matter to a teacher. Principle 1 says people have a responsibility to protect and improve the environment "for present and future generations". Principle 19 says education in environmental matters is essential, for the young and for adults, with due care for the underprivileged (United Nations, 1972).

**A Pakistani example.** Principle 19 is about more than a science chapter on pollution. A teacher in Sukkur who explains safe drinking water to pupils, and then to their parents at a school meeting, is doing what this principle asks: reaching both young people and adults, including those with the least access to schooling.

### The Brundtland Report, Our Common Future (1987)

Now we can answer Zubair. **There was no Brundtland conference.** The Brundtland Report is a report. Its full title is *Our Common Future*.

In 1983, the UN General Assembly set up the World Commission on Environment and Development. The Commission was an independent body, linked to governments and the UN but outside their control (World Commission on Environment and Development, 1987). It was chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland of Norway, which is why the report carries that name. The Commission held public hearings on five continents, and it reported in 1987.

**About the course guide.** Your course guide lists "Stockholm, Brundtland, Rio+20" under major global conferences. The list is useful, because these are the three milestones to know, and this topic follows it. But be exact when you write. If a question names Brundtland among conferences, answer about the 1987 report and say clearly that it came from a commission. Treating the report as a meeting is a common error, and you should not pass it on to your pupils.

The report added three things.

1. **The definition** you studied in Topic 1.1, with its two ideas of needs and limits.
2. **A central finding.** Many development trends were leaving more people poor and vulnerable while also damaging the environment. The report treats poverty and environmental harm as linked problems.
3. **Wide use.** Purvis et al. (2019) note that the Commission is widely credited with bringing the term into international policy.

**A Pakistani example.** The Indus Delta in Sindh shows what the report means by needs and limits. Water taken upstream meets real needs for farming. Yet the IUCN Pakistan (2003) study reports that the sea has moved far up the river's main channel, and many experts link this to falling freshwater flows below Kotri Barrage. Only three of the eight mangrove species recorded in the 1950s still flourish there, and coastal families who live by fishing, farming and forestry have become poorer. One group's need has pressed on a limit that another group lives by.

### Rio+20 (2012)

The name needs explaining. In 1992 the UN held its Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro (Purvis et al., 2019). Twenty years later, governments met in the same city from 20 to 22 June 2012. That meeting, the United Nations Conference on Sustainable Development, is called **Rio+20**: Rio plus twenty years.

Its outcome document is called *The Future We Want* (United Nations, 2012). It did several things.

- It renewed the commitment to sustainable development, and it stated that economic, social and environmental aspects must be integrated (paragraphs 1 and 3).
- It began the process that would produce **global sustainable development goals**, through an open working group (paragraph 248). These became the Sustainable Development Goals, which you will study in Unit 3.
- It decided to set up a high-level political forum to follow up on progress (paragraph 84).
- It resolved to promote Education for Sustainable Development and to continue beyond the UN Decade of Education for Sustainable Development, 2005 to 2014 (paragraph 233). UNESCO (2014) reports on that Decade in its final report.
- It encouraged educational institutions to use good sustainability practices on their campuses and in their communities, with students, teachers and local partners, and to teach sustainable development across subjects (paragraph 234).

**A Pakistani example.** Paragraph 234 describes something a Pakistani school can do. Pupils, teachers and a local water committee could check how much water their school wastes, fix leaking taps and share the results. Sustainable development can also enter the Urdu, mathematics and science lessons, rather than sitting in one chapter of one book.

#### Worked example: reading a claim against the milestones

Suppose a poster in the staffroom says: "Rio+20 created UNEP and gave us the definition of sustainable development." Here is how to check it.

1. **Name the claim's two parts.** One part is about UNEP. The other is about the definition.
2. **Find each in the story.** UNEP came from Stockholm, 1972. The definition came from the Brundtland Report, 1987.
3. **State what Rio+20 did instead.** It began the process toward the global goals and made commitments on education.
4. **Rewrite the poster.** "Stockholm led to UNEP, the Brundtland Report gave the definition, and Rio+20 began the process that led to the Sustainable Development Goals."

The method is simple: separate the claims, place each one at its milestone, then correct the sentence.

## Activity: Milestone jigsaw

Work in groups of three, then in new groups. About 25 minutes.

Each group takes one milestone. In ten minutes, without looking at the text first, write what you remember about who met or worked, what was agreed or added, and one thing it means for a school in Sindh. Then check the text and correct your notes.

Now form new groups with one member from each milestone. Each person teaches their milestone in two minutes. Together, write three poster sentences about the milestones. Make one of them wrong on purpose. Swap with another group and use the worked example's method to find and repair the wrong sentence.

## Check your understanding

Answer from memory, then check against the explanation.

1. Name one thing that came out of Stockholm in 1972, the full title of the Brundtland Report, and the name of the Rio+20 outcome document. *(Remember)*
2. Explain why it is wrong to speak of a "Brundtland conference", and why the name still appears among the milestones. *(Understand)*
3. A head teacher wants to act on what Rio+20 asked of educational institutions. Suggest one action for a government school in your district, and say which part of the Rio+20 text supports it. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Stockholm in 1972 made the environment a world issue. The Brundtland Report of 1987, written by a commission, not a conference, defined sustainable development and tied poverty to environmental harm. Rio+20 in 2012 began the process toward global goals and asked schools and colleges to act. The three are not equal events, but together they show how an idea became a shared plan.

## Summative task

Write a short talk of about 300 words for a school assembly, explaining why meetings held far away matter to pupils in your town. Use all three milestones in order, state correctly what kind of thing each one was, and give one local action linked to each. Then add a two-line note for teachers: which of the three milestones matters most for your school, and why.

| Criterion | Strong | Partial | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy about the milestones (3 marks) | Dates, kinds of event and outcomes all correct | One or two errors | Mostly incorrect |
| Link to local action (3 marks) | Each milestone tied to a realistic local action | Some links general or unrealistic | No local links |
| Judgement of importance (4 marks) | Compares the milestones and justifies the choice with reasons from the school's own situation | Gives a choice with general reasons | No comparison or no reasons |
