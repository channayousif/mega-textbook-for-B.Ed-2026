# Concept and Evolution of Sustainable Development

People use the words "sustainable development" for almost anything: a new road, a tree-planting day, a government plan. This unit gives you a precise meaning, and a way to test whether a real decision in your own district deserves the name.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Define sustainable development and explain the ideas of needs and limits. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-407-1-1)*
- Describe how the idea evolved, and what the Stockholm Conference, the Brundtland Report and Rio+20 each added. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-407-1-1)*
- Explain the environmental, economic and social dimensions and how they depend on each other. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-407-1-2)*
- Analyse one Pakistani development decision, such as a dam or a housing scheme, against all three dimensions. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-407-1-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This is the opening unit of the course. You need only the general science and social studies you learned up to HSC level. Before you begin, write down one change in your own area, such as a road, a water scheme or a new colony. Write one line on who gained from it and one line on who lost. You will come back to this note.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Each one starts with an illustrative classroom situation, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before asking you to try the same thing. Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions without looking back, then check your answers against the explanation.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) has practice questions and, at the end, a public self-study guidance section. Try the questions before you read that guidance. In formal course assessment the default weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative; your teacher will tell you which work is collected. Teachers will find planning help in the [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes).
