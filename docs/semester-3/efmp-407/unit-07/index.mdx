# ESD in Pakistan

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain how a national education policy can support environmental, social and economic learning. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-407-7-1)*
- Distinguish a written commitment from evidence that children have learned. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-407-7-1)*
- Connect curriculum reform with changes in materials, assessment and teacher preparation. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-407-7-2)*
- Evaluate one gap between an ESD commitment and classroom practice, and defend a feasible response. *(Evaluate; SLO:EFMP-407-7-1; SLO:EFMP-407-7-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Revisit Units 2 and 3: ESD frameworks, sustainability's three dimensions, SDG 4.7 and Pakistan's national SDG framework. Recall that ESD involves knowledge, values and informed action. It can be integrated into existing subjects. Before reading, recall a school activity about water, fairness or resource use. What did pupils actually learn from it?

## How to use this unit

Read the topics in order. The school situations are invented teaching cases, not reports of named schools. Study each worked example before doing the pair or group task. Try the retrieval questions with your book closed. Use your checklist to choose what needs another reading.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) contains 10 multiple-choice, 10 restricted-response and five extended-response questions. Attempt them before reading the final public self-study guidance. The unit uses the constitutional default of 40% formative and 60% summative work. This does not replace the guide's course-level allocation of marks.

You may use a fictional case if placement access is unavailable. Ask permission before observing a lesson or discussing school records. Record no pupil names or sensitive family details. The [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes) offer discussion and practical-work guidance. English is available first; Urdu translation and independent expert review remain pending.
