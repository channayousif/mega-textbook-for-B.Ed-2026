# Cultural and Social Dimensions

<Figure id="fig-U5-7" src="/img/figures/efmp-407/unit-05/fig-U5-7.webp" alt="An inclusive classroom group of boys and girls with their trainee teacher sitting under a neem tree in a Pakistani school courtyard, listening to a community elder explain traditional water conservation." kind="illustration" />

Sustainable development is not only about solar panels, forests and river flows. It is about how people live together, how resources and burdens are shared, and whose knowledge and voices count. This unit examines the social and cultural foundations of education for sustainable development in Pakistan: building positive peace, securing gender equality and social justice, protecting universal human rights through inclusive classrooms, and honouring the rich ecological wisdom developed across generations in our own communities.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain how peace, gender equality and social justice support sustainable development and reinforce each other. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-407-5-1)*
- Analyse the relationship between human rights frameworks, constitutional guarantees, and inclusive educational practice in Pakistan. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-407-5-1)*
- Identify the barriers that marginalize learners and propose concrete strategies for access, participation and achievement in inclusive classrooms. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-407-5-1)*
- Describe traditional ecological knowledge systems across Pakistan and evaluate how they contribute to sustainability education. *(Evaluate; SLO:EFMP-407-5-2)*
- Analyse one local traditional practice, such as water conservation or soil management, to design an inquiry-based sustainability lesson. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-407-5-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Before starting this unit, review Units 1 and 3. You need to understand the three dimensions of sustainable development (environmental, economic and social) from Unit 1, and the global goals under SDG 4.7 and global citizenship from Unit 3. Think about your own community: note one group whose voice is rarely heard in public resource decisions, and one traditional practice your grandparents used to manage water, food or soil.

## How to use this unit

Study the three topics in sequence. Each topic opens with a real classroom dilemma from a Pakistani school, explains the core concepts with concrete examples, addresses a common misconception, and walks through a worked analysis before asking you to work with peers. Complete the collaborative activities and test yourself using the retrieval questions before checking your answers.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) contains ten multiple-choice questions, ten restricted-response questions, and five extended-response questions, followed by self-study guidance. Work through the questions independently before consulting the marking guidance. In formal B.Ed assessment, the default balance is 40% formative coursework and 60% summative evaluation. Teacher educators will find pedagogical guidance and sequencing suggestions in the [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes).
