# Cultural and Social Dimensions - Assessment

## Unit summary

Sustainable development is fundamentally a human and social undertaking. While environmental science examines biophysical systems and technology provides tools, the success of sustainability depends on how human communities organize relationships, distribute resources, protect fundamental rights, and honour collective wisdom.

This unit explored the cultural and social dimensions across three interconnected topics:
1. **Peace, gender equality and social justice:** Environmental degradation causes resource scarcity, which fuels social conflict. Negative peace is simply the absence of violence, whereas positive peace builds cooperative relationships, mutual trust, and non-violent conflict resolution. Gender equality is an ecological necessity; women and girls frequently bear the daily brunt of water and fuel scarcity but are historically excluded from governance. Social justice guarantees that vulnerable groups do not shoulder unfair environmental burdens.
2. **Human rights and inclusion:** Grounded in Article 25A of Pakistan's Constitution and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, education is a legally enforceable right. Inclusion goes far beyond physical disability; it is a universal commitment ensuring that "all means all". True inclusion aligns three concentric pillars: physical and legal access, active classroom participation, and equitable learning achievement for every child.
3. **Indigenous knowledge systems:** Traditional ecological knowledge represents centuries of empirical observation, adaptive practices, and circular worldviews. Practices such as Balochistan's Karez water systems, Indus basin legume crop rotation, Rod-Kohi spate irrigation, and northern glacier grafting demonstrate sophisticated models of living within natural limits. By applying "two-eyed seeing", educators integrate local community wisdom with rigorous modern scientific inquiry.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which statement best defines "positive peace" in the context of education for sustainable development? *(Remember)*
   - A) The total suppression of student disagreement through strict administrative discipline.
   - B) The presence of mutual trust, social justice, and fair channels for non-violent conflict resolution.
   - C) The period immediately following a natural disaster when armed forces maintain civil order.
   - D) An agreement between two disputing groups to stop speaking to each other.

2. In rural dryland communities of Pakistan, why is gender equality considered an essential requirement for sustainable water management? *(Understand)*
   - A) Because men are legally prohibited from touching irrigation infrastructure under local customary law.
   - B) Because women and girls manage daily domestic water collection and possess direct practical insight into household water needs.
   - C) Because international donors only fund environmental projects that employ all-female engineering teams.
   - D) Because female pupils score higher on written environmental science examinations than male pupils.

3. An industrial tannery discharges untreated chemical effluent into an open drainage canal flowing through a low-income agricultural settlement, causing skin diseases and crop failure among tenant farmers. Which concept does this situation directly illustrate? *(Apply)*
   - A) Circular economic design.
   - B) Environmental injustice.
   - C) Traditional ecological adaptation.
   - D) Negative peace.

4. What legal entitlement is guaranteed by Article 25A of the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan? *(Remember)*
   - A) Free and compulsory education for all children aged five to sixteen years.
   - B) Free higher professional education in engineering and medicine for rural citizens.
   - C) A legal guarantee of government employment for every university graduate.
   - D) Compulsory military training for secondary school students.

5. According to the UNESCO Global Education Monitoring Report (2020), which definition reflects an accurate, contemporary understanding of inclusive education? *(Understand)*
   - A) Creating specialized separate institutions exclusively for learners with diagnosed intellectual disabilities.
   - B) A process of removing systemic barriers to ensure access, participation, and achievement for every learner without exception.
   - C) Enrolling children with physical impairments in regular classrooms while exempting them from academic assessments.
   - D) Providing monetary scholarships exclusively to top-ranking students from high-income urban schools.

6. Under Article 29 of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, what core dimension must the education of a child foster? *(Understand)*
   - A) Immediate entry into industrial wage labor at the age of twelve.
   - B) Development of respect for human rights, fundamental freedoms, and the natural environment.
   - C) Memorization of national trade laws and industrial patent regulations.
   - D) Loyalty to economic growth regardless of ecological consequences.

7. A middle-school head teacher installs a concrete entrance ramp for wheelchair users, but all Class 8 group activities require students to sprint across an unpaved sports field. In terms of inclusive schooling, what does this school demonstrate? *(Apply)*
   - A) Full achievement without access.
   - B) Physical access at the door, but systemic exclusion from active classroom participation.
   - C) Complete compliance with universal design for learning.
   - D) A model example of positive peace.

8. What is the defining characteristic of an indigenous knowledge system (IKS)? *(Remember)*

9. Why is the traditional *karez* groundwater system in Balochistan ecologically sustainable compared to motorized deep tube-wells? *(Understand)*

   - B) It transports water underground using gravity at a flow rate that naturally matches aquifer recharge limits without consuming fossil fuels.
   - C) It extracts water exclusively from seawater and desalinates it using chemical filters.
   - D) It was constructed using imported industrial steel pipes subsidized by foreign agencies.

10. Peasant farmers in the Indus River basin rotate chickpeas (*chana*) and lentils with wheat crops. What ecological principle explains why this traditional practice sustains soil fertility? *(Apply)*

    - B) Legume crops harbor symbiotic bacteria in root nodules that fix atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable soil nitrates.
    - C) Lentil crops absorb excess water from the soil to prevent seasonal monsoon rains from falling.
    - D) Chickpea roots convert chemical pesticide residues into petroleum fuels.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A government secondary school in an arid district has received a small development grant to install a new drinking water storage tank and plant fifty fruit saplings. During project planning, prominent local male leaders attempt to control water tap access and assign all maintenance and cleaning to female students and children of minority tenant farmers.
   Analyze this situation through the three analytical lenses of peace, gender equality, and social justice. Detail the diagnostic failures present in the project, and develop a comprehensive governance charter that ensures positive peace, equal leadership for girls, and equitable access for vulnerable children. *(Analyze)*

2. A district education officer states in a public meeting: "Our district has constructed school buildings in every union council, so educational access is solved. We do not need expensive inclusive education policies because our schools only enroll regular, normal children."
   Evaluate the education officer's claim using constitutional mandates (Article 25A), international human rights frameworks (UNCRC Articles 28 and 29), and the UNESCO "all means all" inclusion model. Propose an evaluation framework that measures whether schools are delivering genuine access, meaningful participation, and equitable achievement for all marginalized groups. *(Evaluate)*

3. Select one traditional ecological knowledge practice from Pakistan (such as the Karez subterranean water system in Balochistan, Rod-Kohi spate irrigation in D.G. Khan, legume crop rotation in the Indus basin, or glacier grafting in Gilgit-Baltistan).
   Analyze the selected practice by detailing its geographic context, the specific ecological resource it manages, and the scientific principles that explain its effectiveness. Evaluate what this traditional practice offers modern sustainability education, and outline an inquiry-based classroom learning sequence in which student-teachers guide middle-school pupils to investigate this practice with local community elders. *(Analyze)*

4. Following the catastrophic 2022 monsoon floods in Sindh, thousands of displaced families were relocated to temporary relief settlements. When temporary schools were established in tents, attendance of adolescent girls and children from marginalized minority castes dropped below twenty percent due to lack of private sanitation facilities, safety concerns along road embankments, and social stigmatization by more affluent families.
   Evaluate the interaction between gender inequality, social injustice, and educational exclusion in this disaster recovery scenario. Synthesizing constitutional guarantees (Articles 25 and 37) with inclusive educational principles, formulate an integrated educational recovery strategy that restores safe access, guarantees dignified participation, and addresses the psychosocial and academic needs of displaced learners. *(Evaluate)*

5. Design a comprehensive whole-school sustainability project proposal for a Pakistani public school titled "Our School, Our Community, Our Future". Your project must address a real local sustainability challenge (such as water scarcity, heat stress, soil degradation, or solid waste).
   Construct an integrated implementation plan that:
   - Identifies and integrates at least one proven piece of traditional ecological knowledge from the local region alongside modern scientific testing;
   - Establishes explicit structural safeguards to ensure gender equality in project leadership, decision-making, and physical tasks;
   - Incorporates positive peace and peer mediation to resolve student resource disagreements collaboratively; and
   - Implements differentiated scaffolds to ensure the full participation and achievement of learners with disabilities, low-income pupils, and linguistic minorities. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Positive peace requires constructive conditions including mutual trust, social justice, and fair conflict resolution; option A is authoritarian coercion, option C is military policing, and option D is passive avoidance.
2. B - Women and girls bear daily responsibility for household water collection in rural Pakistan and hold vital experiential knowledge of water quality and conservation; options A, C and D state false claims.
3. B - Environmental injustice occurs when low-income vulnerable communities bear disproportionate environmental hazards while others enjoy profits; options A, C and D describe unrelated concepts.
4. A - Article 25A explicitly mandates free and compulsory education for all children aged five to sixteen; options B, C and D state rights not found in Article 25A.
5. B - Modern inclusive education encompasses removing all barriers to ensure access, participation, and achievement for every child ("all means all"); option A is segregation, option C is cosmetic integration, and option D is meritocratic elitism.
6. B - UNCRC Article 29 explicitly directs that education must foster the development of respect for human rights, fundamental freedoms, and the natural environment.
7. B - A wheelchair ramp provides physical entry (access), but inaccessible field activities prevent active engagement (participation); options A, C and D misdiagnose the barrier.
8. B - Indigenous knowledge systems represent cumulative, place-based ecological knowledge and cultural practices handed down across generations through lived experience.
9. B - The Karez system transports water underground by gravity without fossil fuels, and its discharge naturally matches aquifer recharge rates; options A, C and D describe incorrect technologies.
10. B - Legumes possess symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules that biologically fix atmospheric nitrogen into soil nitrates, naturally restoring fertility without chemical fertilizers.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model answer:** Negative peace is merely the absence of direct physical violence or active conflict, whereas positive peace is the presence of constructive social conditions, including mutual trust, justice, human dignity, and fair mechanisms for non-violent conflict resolution. In a school community, positive peace creates collaborative relationships and psychological safety, enabling students to cooperate on shared sustainability projects (such as managing school water or waste) without resentment or conflict.
   **Mark scheme (3 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Accurately defines negative peace as the absence of overt violence.
   - 1 mark: Accurately defines positive peace as the presence of justice, trust, and non-violent resolution.
   - 1 mark: Explains how positive peace enables cooperative environmental action in a school.

2. **Model answer:** First, women and school-age girls in rural Pakistan bear primary daily responsibility for domestic water and fuel collection; when water tables plunge or droughts occur, girls must walk farther, directly increasing physical exhaustion and causing school dropout. Second, cultural norms and social restrictions often exclude women from village decision-making committees (such as irrigation or disaster councils), leaving them without voice despite managing household resources.
   **Mark scheme (2 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Identifies domestic water and fuel gathering burdens and educational impact on girls.
   - 1 mark: Identifies exclusion from local community resource governance and decision-making bodies.

3. **Model answer:** Environmental injustice refers to the inequitable practice where impoverished, marginalized communities bear the toxic pollution and ecological hazards of industrial activity, while wealthier groups reap the financial benefits. Article 37 of the Constitution of Pakistan directs the state to promote with special care the educational and economic interests of backward classes and areas and to remove social evils, requiring protection for disadvantaged populations against exploitation.
   **Mark scheme (3 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Defines environmental injustice as the unequal distribution of ecological hazards on poor groups.
   - 1 mark: Links the situation to unequal economic power and health vulnerabilities.
   - 1 mark: Cites and correctly explains the mandate of Article 37 of the Constitution of Pakistan.

4. **Model answer:** Article 25A guarantees that the state shall provide free and compulsory education to all children aged five to sixteen years. Two non-academic barriers are: (1) hidden economic costs such as required uniforms, shoes, stationery, and examination fees that impoverished families cannot afford; and (2) physical or infrastructure barriers such as lack of boundary walls, lack of functional girls' latrines, or absence of accessible roads and wheelchair ramps.
   **Mark scheme (3 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Accurately states the Article 25A entitlement (free and compulsory education, ages 5 to 16).
   - 1 mark: Identifies one real economic barrier (uniforms, transportation, stationery).
   - 1 mark: Identifies one real physical/infrastructural barrier (latrines, boundary walls, ramps).

5. **Model answer:** Defining inclusion as only disability support is restrictive because it blinds educators to the diverse structural barriers that exclude other children. In Pakistan, millions of out-of-school or marginalized learners are excluded not by bodily impairment, but by extreme household poverty, child labor, gender discrimination, ethnic and linguistic minority status, or climate displacement. Limiting inclusion to disability leaves systemic classroom biases against poor, minority, or displaced children unaddressed.
   **Mark scheme (3 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Names at least two non-disability marginalized groups (poverty, gender, language, displacement).
   - 1 mark: Explains how a narrow medical definition causes teachers to ignore pedagogical exclusion.
   - 1 mark: **(Analyze)** Connects broader inclusion to the principle that "all means all" under rights frameworks.

6. **Model answer:** Access means the physical, legal, and economic opportunity to enter school (e.g. fee-free enrollment and wheelchair ramps). Participation means active psychological and social engagement in classroom life (e.g. speaking in group work and having one's home language respected). Achievement means real learning progress and mastering competencies (e.g. demonstrating understanding through differentiated tasks tailored to the child's learning pace).
   **Mark scheme (3 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Defines access and gives a concrete example (ramps, fee-free entry).
   - 1 mark: Defines participation and gives a concrete example (group dialogue, peer roles).
   - 1 mark: Defines achievement and gives a concrete example (differentiated assessment, skill mastery).

7. **Model answer:** First, the teacher can adopt a "translanguaging" approach, pairing the migrant pupils with bilingual peers who can explain scientific concepts in their shared home language before transitioning to written Urdu or English terms. Second, the teacher can utilize concrete visual diagrams, hands-on physical objects (such as real plant specimens or soil jars), and pictorial graphic organizers so pupils can understand and participate in scientific inquiry without depending solely on complex Urdu lecture text.
   **Mark scheme (2 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Proposes peer-pairing or home language bridging (translanguaging).
   - 1 mark: Proposes visual, tactile, or graphic scaffolding for scientific inquiry.

8. **Model answer:** Indigenous ecological knowledge is not superstition because it is grounded in generations of systematic, empirical observation, trial, and adaptation within specific ecosystems. Traditional practices - such as timing planting by seasonal climatic cues, constructing underground gravity aqueducts, or rotating nitrogen-fixing legumes - were developed and refined through centuries of real-world testing to ensure survival within strict ecological limits.
   **Mark scheme (2 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Identifies the empirical basis: generational observation, trial, and adaptation.
   - 1 mark: Refutes the superstition label by linking traditional practices to verifiable ecological outcomes.

9. **Model answer:** Motorized tube-wells consumed subsidized electric and diesel power to pump water continuously from deep underground, extracting water at a rate far exceeding natural rainfall recharge; this rapidly exhausted ancient aquifers and caused regional water tables to collapse. In contrast, the traditional Karez system channeled groundwater by gravity through subterranean tunnels; its flow rate naturally fluctuated with aquifer recharge levels, preventing over-extraction, and its communal *shabana* governance prevented individual hoarding.
   **Mark scheme (3 marks):**
   - 1 mark: Identifies that motorized tube-wells pump continuously beyond natural recharge limits.
   - 1 mark: Explains that Karez gravity flow naturally fluctuates with water table levels, preventing over-pumping.
   - 1 mark: **(Analyze)** Contrasts private, unmetered extraction with communal customary governance (shabana).

10. **Model answer:** "Two-eyed seeing" means using the strengths of indigenous knowledge from one eye and the strengths of modern scientific inquiry from the other, bringing both into respectful dialogue. In a lesson on food storage, a teacher can have students investigate traditional community methods (such as storing grain in earthen granaries lined with dried neem leaves) and then use modern science to analyze the chemical properties of azadirachtin in neem, comparing costs, ecological safety, and efficacy against synthetic chemical fumigation tablets.
    **Mark scheme (2 marks):**
    - 1 mark: Accurately defines two-eyed seeing as the complementary combination of IKS and modern science.
    - 1 mark: Provides a concrete lesson example integrating traditional practice with scientific testing.
