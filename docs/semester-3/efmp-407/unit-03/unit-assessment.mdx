# Unit 3 Assessment: SDGs and Education

This assessment evaluates your mastery of the concepts, governance frameworks, and pedagogical strategies introduced across Unit 3. Complete the questions independently before consulting the marking guidance and rubrics at the end of the chapter.

## Unit summary

Unit 3 explored the interconnected architecture of the United Nations 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development and its vital implications for teacher education in Pakistan. Topic 3.1 examined the 17 Sustainable Development Goals organized through the 5 Ps framework (People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace, and Partnership) and analyzed SDG Target 4.7 as the educational compass of the 2030 Agenda. Target 4.7 requires that all learners acquire knowledge, values, and action competencies across cognitive, socio-emotional, and behavioural dimensions, correcting the misconception that educational development is measured by classroom enrollment alone.

Topic 3.2 analyzed Pakistan's National SDG Framework, adopted unanimously by the National Assembly in 2016. It detailed the three-tier national priority categorisation and examined how the 18th Constitutional Amendment necessitates provincial localization through provincial planning boards, sector plans, and local school development initiatives. Topic 3.3 investigated Global Citizenship Education (GCED) and its convergence with Education for Sustainable Development under Target 4.7. It resolved the perceived contradiction between national patriotism and global solidarity, demonstrating that authentic global citizenship is rooted in active local community responsibility and universal human dignity.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Transforming our World: The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development was adopted by all 193 UN member states in September 2015; option A relates to Education for All in 2000, option C to climate emissions in 1997, and option D to Jomtien in 1990.
2. B - Planet encompasses SDGs 6, 12, 13, 14, and 15, focusing on fresh water, responsible consumption, climate action, marine life, and terrestrial ecosystems; option A describes People, option C describes Prosperity, and option D describes Peace and Partnership.
3. C - Target 4.7 is the dedicated target within SDG 4 specifying knowledge, skills, values, and attitudes for sustainable development, human rights, gender equality, peace, and global citizenship; Target 4.1 covers primary/secondary schooling, Target 4.4 technical skills, and Target 4.a physical school facilities.
4. B - Carrying out practical tasks, such as watering plants, caring for schoolyard trees, and maintaining clean water vessels, represents the behavioural domain (action competencies); cognitive represents knowledge, and administrative is not an educational learning domain.
5. C - The National Assembly of Pakistan unanimously passed its legislative resolution adopting the SDGs as National Development Goals in February 2016; 2010 was the 18th Amendment, and 2018 was the publication of the National Framework.
6. B - Category 1 focuses on urgent baseline human survival, dignity, and foundational development (poverty, hunger, health, education, water, sanitation, and clean energy); the framework does not forbid other investments and recognizes all goals as interconnected.
7. B - Clean drinking water (SDG 6) and female educational retention (SDG 4 and SDG 5) directly address Category 1 immediate national priorities; commercial test booklets, computer suites, and private tuition do not address baseline equity priorities.
8. B - UNESCO defines GCED as empowering learners to become active, ethical, and collaborative contributors to a more peaceful, tolerant, inclusive, and secure world; GCED does not issue legal passports, replace national curricula, or focus solely on multinational corporations.
9. B - ESD supplies the ecological understanding of planetary limits and natural cycles, while GCED supplies the socio-political and ethical understanding of human rights, equity, and civic justice; they are complementary partners under Target 4.7.
10. B - Structuring cooperative activities where pupils explore, understand, and value cultural and linguistic diversity builds empathy and inclusive civic identity; banning home languages or segregating classrooms reinforces prejudice and exclusion.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model answer:** The 5 Ps framework organizes the 17 Sustainable Development Goals around five interconnected dimensions: People (ending poverty, hunger, and ensuring dignity and equity in health and education), Planet (protecting fresh water, terrestrial ecosystems, oceans, and combating climate change), Prosperity (ensuring economic, technological, and social progress in harmony with nature), Peace (fostering just, inclusive, and non-violent societies), and Partnership (mobilizing global and local solidarity and cross-sector cooperation).  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for naming all five dimensions accurately; 1 mark for explaining the focus of People, Planet, and Prosperity; 1 mark for explaining the role of Peace and Partnership.

2. **Model answer:** The six thematic strands explicitly articulated in SDG Target 4.7 are: (1) education for sustainable development and sustainable lifestyles, (2) human rights, (3) gender equality, (4) promotion of a culture of peace and non-violence, (5) global citizenship, and (6) appreciation of cultural diversity and culture's contribution to sustainable development.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for identifying 2 strands; 2 marks for identifying 4 strands; 3 marks for naming all 6 strands correctly.

3. **Model answer:** Courtyard tree planting supports the "People" dimension because shade trees lower surrounding ambient temperatures during extreme summer heatwaves, protecting children from heat exhaustion, while clean air and green surroundings enhance mental wellbeing and classroom concentration. It supports the "Prosperity" dimension because natural tree canopies reduce electricity and fan cooling costs for the school, and students develop practical horticulture and plant care skills that possess vocational and community value.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for explaining the direct connection to the People dimension (health/wellbeing/focus); 1 mark for explaining the connection to the Prosperity dimension (cost savings/practical skills); 1 mark for grounding the answer in a concrete school context.

4. **Model answer:** Educational access (Target 4.1) focuses on quantitative inputs and physical participation: ensuring that every child is enrolled in school and completes primary and secondary education. Target 4.7 focuses on qualitative purpose and transformative content: what pupils learn, the values they cultivate, and their ability to think critically, care for ecosystems, and uphold human rights. Expanding access without Target 4.7 fails to ensure sustainable development because schooling that relies on passive rote learning leaves learners unprepared to solve community problems like water contamination, gender inequality, and environmental destruction, and can even perpetuate social prejudice and unsustainable consumption.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for distinguishing access (enrolment/completion) from Target 4.7 (qualitative values/competencies); 1 mark for explaining the danger of rote/unreflective schooling; 1 mark for providing a specific sustainability consequence.

5. **Model answer:** The purpose of the three-tier priority categorisation in Pakistan's National SDG Framework (2018) is to sequence national policy and budgetary efforts pragmatically in a resource-constrained environment. Category 1 (Immediate / High Priority) focuses on basic survival and foundation needs like education, health, poverty, and water; Category 2 (Emerging Priority) addresses structural enablers like gender equality, decent work, and infrastructure; Category 3 (Long-Term Priority) addresses sustainable consumption, climate action, and global partnerships. This categorisation ensures that baseline social foundations are strengthened so that long-term industrial and environmental goals can succeed.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for explaining the rationale of phased sequencing under resource constraints; 1 mark for defining Category 1 and Category 2 focuses; 1 mark for explaining how baseline investments enable long-term sustainability.

6. **Model answer:** The 18th Constitutional Amendment (2010) devolved legislative and executive powers over school education and literacy from the federal government to the provinces, making education an exclusively provincial responsibility under Article 25-A. Consequently, the federal government cannot impose SDG 4 through centralized directives. Instead, SDG 4 must be localized through Provincial Planning and Development (P&D) Boards, provincial departments of education, and provincial education sector plans (such as in Sindh), which set their own district-level indicators, allocate funds, and adapt teacher training to local realities.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for identifying the devolution of education under the 18th Amendment and Article 25-A; 1 mark for explaining why federal decree is legally and practically ineffective; 1 mark for identifying provincial localization mechanisms (P&D boards, sector plans).

7. **Model answer:** Two practical, low-cost school-level interventions are: (1) Establishing a pupil-led clean drinking water and handwashing station: using traditional clay water pitchers with clean ladles and student hygiene monitors to conduct daily 2-minute soap-handwashing demonstrations before mid-day snacks, directly advancing SDG 3 (Health) and SDG 6 (Clean Water). (2) Setting up a peer-buddy retention system for female students: pairing Grade 8 and Grade 9 girls to walk to school together and provide peer tutoring, accompanied by teacher-parent home dialogues to address safety concerns, directly advancing SDG 4 (Education) and SDG 5 (Gender Equality).  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for proposing first viable low-cost intervention mapped to a Category 1 goal; 1 mark for proposing second viable low-cost intervention mapped to a Category 1 goal; 1 mark for detailing realistic operational roles for pupils and staff.

8. **Model answer:** Global Citizenship Education (GCED) is a pedagogical approach that empowers learners to understand interconnected world challenges, develop universal ethical values based on human rights and justice, and engage in collaborative action for a peaceful, sustainable world. It differs fundamentally from legal citizenship because legal citizenship is a formal legal status conferred by a sovereign state granting legal rights, voting eligibility, and a national passport. Global citizenship is not a legal status; it is a moral outlook, an ethical commitment to shared human dignity, and a sense of responsibility toward planetary wellbeing.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for defining GCED accurately; 1 mark for explaining legal citizenship (state status, passport, voting); 1 mark for contrasting legal status with moral/civic mindset and planetary responsibility.

9. **Model answer:** Critical global citizenship education refutes this misconception by demonstrating that global citizenship is not an abstract, foreign lifestyle or an elite cosmopolitan identity; it is deeply rooted in local community life. A person cannot be a genuine global citizen while ignoring social injustice, poverty, or environmental degradation in their own Pakistani community. Love for one's country and loyalty to constitutional values are strengthened when young people understand that local challenges (such as monsoon floods, heatwaves, and water contamination) are linked to global systems. Learning to address local problems with global awareness protects Pakistan's future while fostering empathy for all humanity.  
   **Marks (3):** 1 mark for identifying that authentic global citizenship is rooted in local community action; 1 mark for illustrating how global issues directly affect Pakistan's national wellbeing; 1 mark for explaining how national identity and global solidarity reinforce each other.

10. **Model answer:** Activity: "Walking in Another's Shoes" (15 minutes). The teacher divides the Grade 7 class into diverse pairs of students who do not normally sit together. Each student has 4 minutes to share a real community story: a time they felt excluded, an obstacle their family overcame, or a cultural celebration they cherish in their family. The partner listens without interrupting, then reflects back what they heard and names one value or emotion they shared with their partner. The teacher concludes with a 3-minute whole-class circle highlighting that every person experiences similar hopes, vulnerabilities, and dignity regardless of background.  
    **Marks (3):** 1 mark for designing a bounded, structured peer dialogue; 1 mark for explicitly targeting the socio-emotional domain (empathy, active listening, vulnerability); 1 mark for practical feasibility within a 15-minute timeframe.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Textbook lesson critique against SDG 4.7 strands and learning domains
- *Criterion 1: Analysis of Target 4.7 strands* (0-3 marks, **Analyze**): 3 marks for thoroughly analyzing how the chosen lesson touches or omits specific Target 4.7 strands (sustainable lifestyles, human rights, gender equality, peace, global citizenship, cultural diversity) with precise textual examples; 2 marks for sound analysis covering several strands; 1 mark for superficial identification.
- *Criterion 2: Three-domain pedagogical redesign* (0-3 marks, **Apply/Create**): 3 marks for designing concrete, creative activities integrating cognitive inquiry, socio-emotional empathy, and behavioural community action; 2 marks for proposing activities across all three domains with minor gaps; 1 mark for activities limited largely to cognitive recall.
- *Criterion 3: Contextual feasibility and student agency* (0-2 marks): 2 marks for highly realistic modifications suited to a Pakistani public classroom with strong pupil agency; 1 mark for generic modifications requiring costly resources.
- *Criterion 4: Concision and structure* (0-2 marks): 2 marks for clear, professional writing within the 400-500 word limit; 1 mark for unorganized or excessively brief/long prose.

#### ERQ 2: Institutional evaluation of school alignment with Pakistan's National SDG Framework
- *Criterion 1: Policy and governance analysis* (0-3 marks, **Analyze**): 3 marks for rigorously analyzing the multi-level cascade from federal priority categorization to provincial devolution (18th Amendment) and school-level action; 2 marks for clear description of federal and provincial roles; 1 mark for basic summary without governance depth.
- *Criterion 2: Evaluation of contextual barriers* (0-3 marks, **Evaluate**): 3 marks for incisively evaluating two systemic rural barriers in interior Sindh (such as infrastructure deficits, community poverty, or coordination gaps) with evidence-backed reasoning; 2 marks for sound evaluation of typical challenges; 1 mark for superficial or vague barriers.
- *Criterion 3: Actionable administrative strategies* (0-2 marks, **Create**): 2 marks for proposing highly practical, low-cost strategies engaging the SMC, teachers, and student monitors; 1 mark for generic proposals dependent on external funding.
- *Criterion 4: Concision and structure* (0-2 marks): 2 marks for well-structured, fluent prose within the 400-500 word limit; 1 mark for informal or unorganized text.

#### ERQ 3: Design of a community-based Global Citizenship Education project
- *Criterion 1: Three-domain learning objectives* (0-3 marks, **Apply**): 3 marks for formulating distinct, rigorous, measurable objectives spanning cognitive analysis, socio-emotional empathy, and behavioural collaborative action; 2 marks for clear objectives across two or three domains; 1 mark for vague or purely cognitive objectives.

- *Criterion 4: Concision and structure* (0-2 marks): 2 marks for structured, professional exposition within the 400-500 word limit; 1 mark for disorganized prose.

- *Criterion 2: Integration of Target 4.7 strands and national priorities* (0-3 marks, **Evaluate**): 3 marks for synthesizing ecological concepts with Pakistan's Category 1 priorities (SDG 6 water, SDG 3 health, SDG 5 gender equity in water collection) and global citizenship; 2 marks for clear links to several SDGs and Target 4.7; 1 mark for vague connections.

- *Criterion 4: Concision and structure* (0-2 marks): 2 marks for lucid, professional writing within the 400-500 word limit; 1 mark for poorly structured text.

- *Criterion 1: Synthesis of 5 Ps, national priorities, and GCED* (0-3 marks, **Create**): 3 marks for seamlessly synthesizing the 5 Ps framework, Pakistan's Category 1 national priorities, and global citizenship education into an inspiring whole-school model; 2 marks for clear integration of two or three core frameworks; 1 mark for disjointed listing of concepts.
