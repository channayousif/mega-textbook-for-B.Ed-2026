# Topic 3.1 - The SDGs and target 4.7

## A real classroom situation

During her teaching practicum at a government girls high school in Sukkur, student teacher Fariha observed a Grade 8 General Science class on "Natural Resources and Energy". The cooperating teacher asked the students to copy notes from the chalkboard defining fossil fuels, coal, and petroleum for their upcoming examination. When Fariha later asked whether the lesson could connect to the severe drinking water contamination in Sukkur or the recent Indus River floods that had damaged several pupils' homes, the cooperating teacher smiled gently and replied: "The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals are important international policy agendas, but our responsibility is to finish the prescribed textbook syllabus. Global goals belong to ministry seminars, not our crowded 40-minute science periods."

Fariha wondered whether teaching the syllabus and addressing sustainability were really in opposition. Could a teacher help students grasp required textbook concepts while also cultivating the awareness and skills needed to protect their immediate environment?

## Explanation

### Overview of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals

In September 2015, all 193 member states of the United Nations, including Pakistan, adopted *Transforming our World: the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development*. At the centre of this agenda are the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), underpinned by 169 specific targets to be achieved by the year 2030. The SDGs succeed the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs, 2000-2015), which focused largely on basic human survival thresholds in developing countries. Unlike the MDGs, the SDGs are universal: they apply to every country, recognize that environmental, social, and economic progress cannot be separated, and commit to "leave no one behind".

To make these 17 broad goals practical for education and policy planning, the 2030 Agenda groups them around five fundamental dimensions, widely known as the **5 Ps framework** (Purvis et al., 2019; UNESCO, 2017):

1. **People (SDGs 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5):** Focused on ending poverty and hunger in all forms and ensuring that all human beings can fulfil their potential in dignity, health, equality, and a healthy environment.
2. **Planet (SDGs 6, 12, 13, 14, and 15):** Focused on protecting the Earth from degradation through sustainable consumption, responsible management of fresh water, oceans, forests, and ecosystems, and urgent action on climate change.
3. **Prosperity (SDGs 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11):** Focused on ensuring that all human beings can enjoy prosperous and fulfilling lives, where economic, social, and technological progress occurs in harmony with nature.
4. **Peace (SDG 16):** Focused on fostering peaceful, just, and inclusive societies that are free from fear, violence, and institutional corruption.
5. **Partnership (SDG 17):** Focused on mobilizing global solidarity and cross-sector cooperation among governments, communities, schools, and civic organizations to achieve the goals.

The 5 Ps demonstrate that sustainable development is not solely an environmental campaign. It is an integrated framework where social justice (People), economic opportunity (Prosperity), ecological integrity (Planet), and democratic security (Peace) are brought together through collaborative action (Partnership).

#### Worked example: Analysing a school clean water initiative using the 5 Ps

To understand how the 5 Ps operate in educational practice, consider a rural high school in Sindh that establishes a solar-powered water filtration plant and hygiene programme:

- **People:** Pupils and teachers drink clean, safe water, directly reducing water-borne illnesses like diarrhoea and typhoid, which improves school attendance and classroom focus (SDG 3, SDG 4).
- **Planet:** The school replaces single-use plastic water bottles with refillable clay vessels and conducts rainwater harvesting demonstrations in the school garden (SDG 6, SDG 12, SDG 15).
- **Prosperity:** Solar panels lower school electricity bills, and older students learn basic maintenance routines that introduce renewable energy skills (SDG 7, SDG 8).
- **Peace:** Water is distributed equally to all pupils regardless of caste, religious background, or social status, establishing classroom fairness and preventing disputes over scarce water taps (SDG 10, SDG 16).
- **Partnership:** The School Management Committee (SMC), village elders, local artisans, and teachers coordinate maintenance and funding to sustain the facility (SDG 17).

This worked example demonstrates that a single well-designed educational initiative does not serve just one SDG; it activates multiple interconnected goals simultaneously.

### SDG 4.7: Knowledge and skills for sustainable development

While SDG 4 is titled "Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all", its individual targets cover different dimensions of educational development - from early childhood care (Target 4.2) and technical vocational skills (Target 4.4) to school infrastructure (Target 4.a).

Among all ten targets of SDG 4, **Target 4.7** is unique. It defines the substantive purpose, values, and content of education:

> *"By 2030, ensure that all learners acquire the knowledge and skills needed to promote sustainable development, including, among others, through education for sustainable development and sustainable lifestyles, human rights, gender equality, promotion of a culture of peace and non-violence, global citizenship and appreciation of cultural diversity and of culture's contribution to sustainable development."* (UNESCO, 2017, p. 7)

Target 4.7 unites six interconnected thematic strands that every curriculum and school culture should embody:

1. **Education for sustainable development and sustainable lifestyles:** Developing ecological literacy, understanding planetary boundaries, and cultivating responsible habits of consumption, water conservation, and waste reduction.
2. **Human rights:** Fostering deep respect for the inherent dignity, equality, and legal rights of every person, reflecting constitutional protections and international standards.
3. **Gender equality:** Dismantling discriminatory stereotypes in teaching materials and school routines, ensuring girls and boys have equal opportunities to lead, speak, and learn.
4. **Promotion of a culture of peace and non-violence:** Cultivating constructive dialogue, non-violent communication, empathy, and restorative methods of resolving playground and classroom conflicts.
5. **Global citizenship:** Recognizing that local choices affect global systems and that every person shares ethical responsibilities toward fellow humans across geographical borders.
6. **Appreciation of cultural diversity and culture's contribution to sustainable development:** Valuing linguistic, regional, and indigenous knowledge traditions - such as traditional Indus irrigation practices and community solidarity - as vital assets for sustainability.

#### Addressing a common misconception: Access versus quality and purpose

A widespread misconception among education stakeholders is that **"SDG 4 is only about getting children into school"**. In Pakistan, where over 20 million children remain out of school, educational debates often focus entirely on enrolment figures, physical boundary walls, and textbook delivery.

While expanding school access (Target 4.1) is undeniably essential, physical presence in a classroom is only the starting point. If children sit silently copying notes for five years without learning to read critically, question assumptions, respect diversity, or care for their natural environment, schooling fails to achieve sustainable development. Education that promotes rote learning without ethical awareness can even exacerbate social divides and environmental destruction (UNESCO, 2017). Target 4.7 reminds educators that educational quality is defined by the values, critical thinking capabilities, and civic competencies that learners acquire.

#### The three dimensions of ESD learning

To move Target 4.7 from an abstract policy text into tangible classroom learning, UNESCO (2017) defines three core learning dimensions:

- **Cognitive domain:** Encompasses knowledge, comprehension, and analytical skills. Pupils understand ecological cycles, climate patterns, legal rights, and the systemic causes of poverty and environmental harm.
- **Socio-emotional domain:** Cultivates values, attitudes, empathy, and social skills. Pupils learn to listen to different perspectives, care for marginalized community members, appreciate cultural diversity, and feel a personal commitment to the common good.
- **Behavioural domain:** Develops practical action competencies. Pupils apply their knowledge and values in real life: organizing tree-planting drives, conserving water, resolving peer disputes peacefully, and launching school recycling initiatives.

As Khoo and Jørgensen (2021) point out, when education engages all three domains, it transcends passive knowledge transmission and becomes transformative praxis - empowering learners to understand their world, care about its future, and take collective action.

## Activity: Mapping a textbook lesson to the 5 Ps

**Format:** Small groups of 3 or 4 student teachers.  
**Time:** 25 minutes.

1. **Select a textbook topic:** Choose one lesson from a commonly used Pakistani school textbook (for example, Grade 7 General Science "Water Cycle and Water Scarcity", or Grade 6 Social Studies "Our Village Environment").
2. **Review through the 5 Ps:** On a large chart paper, draw five columns: *People*, *Planet*, *Prosperity*, *Peace*, and *Partnership*.
3. **Map and enrich:**
   - Write down what the textbook lesson currently teaches under each column.
   - For any column that is empty, propose one concrete discussion question or classroom task that would connect the topic to that dimension.
4. **Share:** Have each group present their enriched lesson idea to the class in two minutes. Notice how a conventional lesson can be broadened into a multi-dimensional sustainability inquiry without requiring additional textbook pages.

## Summary

The 17 Sustainable Development Goals represent a universal, integrated call to action organized around the 5 Ps framework: People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace, and Partnership. Target 4.7 serves as the educational compass of the 2030 Agenda, requiring that all learners acquire the knowledge, values, and action competencies needed for sustainable lifestyles, human rights, gender equality, peace, cultural diversity, and global citizenship. Educational quality cannot be measured by physical enrolment alone; it demands transformative learning across cognitive, socio-emotional, and behavioural dimensions that equip students to become thoughtful, caring change-makers in their communities.

## Try this at your practicum school

During your next school placement day, inspect one Grade 5 to Grade 8 textbook currently used in your cooperating school (such as Science, Social Studies, or Urdu). Select one chapter and complete a brief Target 4.7 audit:

1. Identify whether the chapter mentions any of the six strands of Target 4.7 (sustainable lifestyles, human rights, gender equality, peace, global citizenship, or cultural diversity).
2. Note whether the chapter questions engage only the cognitive domain (recalling facts) or include socio-emotional reflection (empathy and values) or behavioural action (doing a project).
3. Draft two supplementary classroom questions that invite pupils to apply the chapter's concepts to an environmental or social issue in their school neighbourhood.

Share your findings with your cooperating teacher and record your reflection in your practicum journal.

## Summative task

Write an analytical evaluation (400-500 words) of a typical Pakistani primary or middle school science lesson plan (for example, "Sources of Water" or "Pollution"). In your evaluation:

1. Analyze how the existing lesson addresses or overlooks the 5 Ps framework and the six thematic strands of Target 4.7.
2. Propose three concrete modifications to the lesson, demonstrating how to incorporate cognitive, socio-emotional, and behavioural learning dimensions.
3. Justify how your proposed modifications uphold gender equality and local cultural relevance in a Pakistani classroom setting.
