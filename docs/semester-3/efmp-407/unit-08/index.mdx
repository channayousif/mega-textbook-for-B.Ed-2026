# School and Community Projects

<Figure id="fig-U8-5" src="/img/figures/efmp-407/unit-08/fig-U8-5.webp" alt="A trainee teacher, pupils, and community members working together in a school courtyard in Sindh around a rainwater collection drum and tree nursery." kind="illustration" />

Classroom lessons give pupils words and ideas about sustainability. Real change happens when pupils, teachers, and neighbours step into the school grounds and surrounding streets to solve actual problems together. In this culminating unit of the course, you will learn how to turn sustainability concepts into practical, pupil-led projects that endure.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Plan a school-based sustainability project using a six-stage project cycle. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-407-8-1)*
- Implement and monitor sustainability activities using clear, measurable indicators of progress and success. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-407-8-1)*
- Analyze models of community participation, identifying active roles for parents, School Management Committees, local elders, and civic bodies. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-407-8-2)*
- Design accessible reporting formats that communicate project outcomes clearly to pupils, school heads, and community members. *(Create; SLO:EFMP-407-8-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

In Unit 4, you studied project-based learning, inquiry methods, and lesson planning. You know how to guide pupils through questions and investigations. In Unit 1, you learned the three dimensions of sustainability: environmental, economic, and social. 

Before starting this unit, reflect on your own school days: did your school ever organize a tree-planting day, a cleanliness drive, or a water project? Write down three lines describing what happened after the first day: did the trees survive, did the clean area stay clean, and did anyone tell the parents what was accomplished?

## How to use this unit

Work through the two topics in sequence. Each topic begins with an authentic classroom scenario from Sindh, explains the underlying concepts with practical Pakistani examples, and models a planning tool before asking you to practice. Work through the collaborative group activities with your peers. Complete the retrieval questions under "Check your understanding" before reviewing the explanatory text.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) contains a 25-item question bank (10 MCQs, 10 RRQs, and 5 ERQs) testing your understanding across the unit, followed by self-study answers and marking rubrics at the end. Teacher educators and instructors will find facilitation guidance, practical fieldwork strategies, and discussion plans in the [teacher notes](./unit-teacher-notes).
