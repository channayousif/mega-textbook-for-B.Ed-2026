# Unit 3 Assessment - Methods of Teaching English

This end-of-unit assessment evaluates your comprehensive understanding of English language teaching methodologies, structural pattern techniques, communicative task design, and morphological word building. Complete the summative assessment tasks independently before reviewing the model answers and marking guidance provided in the final section of this page.

## Unit summary

Unit 3 explored the theoretical foundations, historical evolution, classroom procedures, and instructional trade-offs of the major methods and approaches in English language teaching:

- **Grammar-Translation Method (GTM):** Focuses on reading and writing through deductive grammar rules, bilingual vocabulary lists, and sentence-level translation. It operates effectively in large, resource-constrained classrooms but neglects listening, speaking, and spontaneous communicative fluency.
- **Direct Method:** Prioritizes oral immersion and inductive grammar acquisition, completely eliminating mother tongue translation to forge direct cognitive connections between English words and concrete reality using realia, pictures, and demonstration.
- **Audio-Lingual Approach:** Views language learning as behaviorist habit formation, using intensive choral mimicry, dialogue memorization, and rapid pattern drills (repetition, substitution, and transformation) to automate structural speech habits.
- **Structural Approach:** Sequences English instruction around selected and graded sentence patterns. Substitution tables provide visual syntactic slots that enable learners to generate dozens of error-free sentences while overcoming mother tongue word-order interference.
- **Communicative Language Teaching (CLT):** Aims at communicative competence across grammatical, sociolinguistic, discourse, and strategic dimensions. Through authentic tasks, information-gap activities, and collaborative pair work, CLT prioritizes fluency before accuracy during meaning-making interaction.
- **Word Building (Morphology):** Empowers learners with generative vocabulary expansion strategies, analyzing base roots, high-frequency prefixes, functional suffixes, and compounding to decode and construct English words independently.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* What is the primary medium of instruction used by teachers in a traditional Grammar-Translation classroom?
   - A) English exclusively at all times
   - B) The pupils' mother tongue (L1)
   - C) Alternating English and French
   - D) Simplified visual sign language

2. *(Apply)* A Grade 6 teacher writes "Subject + had + Verb(3rd form)" on the board, explains its past perfect function in Urdu, and asks pupils to translate five sentences into Sindhi. Which method is the teacher demonstrating?
   - A) Direct Method
   - B) Audio-Lingual Approach
   - C) Grammar-Translation Method
   - D) Communicative Language Teaching

3. *(Remember)* Which instructional practice is strictly prohibited in the classic Direct Method?
   - A) Using physical realia and toys
   - B) Translating words into the mother tongue
   - C) Asking oral questions in English
   - D) Demonstrating actions through mime

4. *(Apply)* A primary teacher holds up an actual clay water pot, points to it, and says: "This is a pitcher. It keeps water cool." The teacher asks: "What is this?" and pupils reply in English without any Urdu gloss. This practice exemplifies which principle?
   - A) Deductive grammar analysis
   - B) Direct association without translation
   - C) Behaviorist transformation drilling
   - D) Morphological compounding

5. *(Understand)* On which psychological theory of learning is the Audio-Lingual Approach primarily based?
   - A) Cognitive constructivism
   - B) Behaviorist conditioning and habit formation
   - C) Humanistic self-actualization
   - D) Sociocultural mediation

6. *(Apply)* A teacher says: "Bilal bought a book." The teacher calls out the cue: "two pens." The pupils immediately chant: "Bilal bought two pens." What type of drill is being conducted?
   - A) Chain drill
   - B) Transformation drill
   - C) Substitution drill
   - D) Translation drill

7. *(Understand)* What is the main pedagogical purpose of using a multi-slot substitution table in the Structural Approach?
   - A) To test pupils on obscure bilingual dictionary terms
   - B) To enable pupils to assemble multiple grammatically accurate sentences within a fixed pattern
   - C) To teach pupils how to recite historical literary poems
   - D) To evaluate creative paragraph writing in examinations

8. *(Understand)* According to Dell Hymes and communicative language theorists, what does sociolinguistic competence involve?
   - A) Knowing how to pronounce individual phonemes accurately
   - B) Knowing how to use language appropriately according to social context and politeness norms
   - C) Knowing how to spell irregular plural nouns correctly
   - D) Knowing how to translate complex compound sentences into Urdu

9. *(Apply)* In a Grade 5 English class, Partner A has a picture of a park with five hidden birds, while Partner B has the same picture with five hidden rabbits. They must ask English questions to discover where their partner's animals are. What key CLT feature does this task demonstrate?
   - A) Mechanical repetition
   - B) Information gap
   - C) Deductive rule parsing
   - D) Bilingual translation

10. *(Apply)* A Grade 4 pupil encounters the word *unhelpful* in a story. She identifies the base root *help*, the prefix *un-* (not), and the suffix *-ful* (full of), deducing that the word means "not willing to assist." What language skill is she deploying?
   - A) Behaviorist imitation
   - B) Morphological word analysis
   - C) Syntactic substitution
   - D) Choral chanting

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Identify two practical reasons why the Grammar-Translation Method continues to be widely practiced in Pakistani government schools despite curriculum reforms.
2. *(Analyze)* Contrast deductive grammar teaching with inductive grammar teaching in two clear sentences, naming the method associated with each.
3. *(Understand)* Describe how a primary teacher can use real classroom objects (realia) to teach the meaning of the adjective *heavy* using the Direct Method without speaking Urdu.
4. *(Understand)* State the precise structural difference between a substitution drill and a transformation drill in the Audio-Lingual Approach.
5. *(Analyze)* Explain why a Grade 5 pupil who performs flawlessly during choral dialogue drills might become tongue-tied when greeting a foreign visitor outside the classroom.
6. *(Understand)* Explain the principles of "selection" and "gradation" as applied to sentence patterns in the Structural Approach.
7. *(Understand)* Define the concept of an "information gap" in Communicative Language Teaching, and give one concrete primary classroom example.
8. *(Analyze)* Identify two specific classroom management techniques a teacher can use to prevent chaos and excessive L1 noise during pair work in a crowded classroom of sixty pupils.
9. *(Understand)* Explain how prefixes and suffixes differ in their typical grammatical impact on English base root words.
10. *(Analyze)* Examine the following pupil error: *"The mango was very sweetness."* Identify the morphological confusion, state the correct target word, and explain the underlying grammatical rule.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* Compare and contrast the Grammar-Translation Method and the Direct Method across three specific dimensions: theoretical view of language learning, classroom procedures and materials, and outcomes in receptive versus productive language skills.

2. *(Analyze)* Evaluate the Audio-Lingual Approach and the Structural Approach as systematic frameworks for establishing grammatical accuracy in primary English. In your response, examine how both methods address error prevention and habit formation, and analyze their collective limitations in developing spontaneous communicative fluency.

3. *(Create)* Design a complete 35-minute Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) lesson plan for Grade 5 focused on the topic "At the Railway Station." Your plan must incorporate an authentic information-gap task, a guided role-play activity, collaborative pair interaction, and an explicit strategy for balancing fluency with accuracy.

4. *(Create)* Develop a comprehensive morphological word-building instructional sequence for primary school pupils. Select two common base roots (*care* and *play*) and design a step-by-step teaching routine that introduces six functional affixes (*-ful, -less, -er, -ly, un-, re-*), illustrating how morphological awareness expands learner vocabulary exponentially.

5. *(Evaluate)* Imagine you are appointed as an English curriculum specialist observing a typical government primary school in Sindh with sixty Grade 5 pupils seated in fixed wooden benches, limited audio-visual resources, and an examination system emphasizing written grammar. Critically evaluate whether any single method (GTM, Direct Method, Audio-Lingualism, or CLT) can adequately address all learner needs, and formulate an evidence-based, eclectic instructional model that combines structural pattern accuracy with meaningful communicative interaction.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - The mother tongue (L1) serves as the primary medium of instruction and explanation in traditional Grammar-Translation classrooms.
2. C - Deductive presentation of a grammar formula followed by sentence translation into L1 is the hallmark of the Grammar-Translation Method.
3. B - The Direct Method strictly prohibits mother tongue translation, demanding direct association between target words and concrete meaning.
4. B - Presenting a real object (realia) and modeling English labels without L1 translation exemplifies the direct association principle of the Direct Method.
5. B - The Audio-Lingual Approach is grounded in behaviorist psychology, defining language learning as habit formation through stimulus-response conditioning.
6. C - Swapping a single lexical item ("two pens" for "a book") into a stable syntactic slot is a substitution drill.
7. B - A substitution table enables learners to assemble multiple grammatically valid sentences by selecting items across aligned syntactic columns.
8. B - Sociolinguistic competence refers to the ability to produce language that is socially appropriate to the context, role, and cultural setting.
9. B - When each partner possesses information the other must discover to complete a task, an authentic information gap is established.
10. B - Segmenting a complex word into its constituent prefix, root, and suffix morphemes to deduce meaning is morphological word analysis.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model Answer:** GTM persists in Pakistani government schools because: (1) it operates effectively in large, crowded classrooms with minimal physical or audio-visual resources, and (2) it requires lower oral English fluency from teachers while directly aligning with provincial board examinations that test written translation and formal grammar rules.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for identifying large class size / resource constraints / teacher fluency.
     - 1 mark for identifying examination alignment with written translation and grammar.

2. **Model Answer:** Deductive grammar instruction (characteristic of GTM) presents the explicit grammatical rule or formula first before learners practice applying it to sentences. In contrast, inductive grammar instruction (characteristic of the Direct Method and CLT) exposes learners to meaningful language examples first, allowing them to deduce the underlying grammatical pattern intuitively.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for defining deductive grammar and associating it with GTM.
     - 1 mark for defining inductive grammar and associating it with Direct Method / CLT.

3. **Model Answer:** The teacher places a light feather or empty paper cup in one hand and a heavy brick or full bucket of stones in the other hand. The teacher lifts the feather effortlessly with a smile, saying "Light, light," and then pretends to strain and sweat while lifting the brick, groaning "Heavy, heavy! This brick is heavy!" Pupils feel the objects and repeat the label directly.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for contrasting concrete objects (realia) to show weight.
   - 1 mark for physical mime and spoken English modeling without translation.

4. **Model Answer:** In a substitution drill, the grammatical pattern remains fixed while learners replace a single lexical item in a designated slot (e.g. changing "mango" to "apple"). In a transformation drill, the structural category of the sentence is actively altered (e.g. converting an affirmative statement into a question or a negative construction).
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for correctly explaining slot replacement in substitution drills.
     - 1 mark for correctly explaining syntactic form change in transformation drills.

5. **Model Answer:** Choral drills develop mechanical motor habits in controlled, predictable environments with scripted lines. In authentic real-world communication, conversations are unpredictable and require immediate semantic processing, personal decision-making, and sociolinguistic appropriateness, which mechanical habituation alone does not develop.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for noting the predictable, scripted nature of choral drilling.
     - 1 mark for explaining the cognitive and pragmatic demands of unscripted communication.

6. **Model Answer:** "Selection" involves choosing the most essential, high-frequency, teachable, and productive grammatical structures for a specific age level. "Gradation" involves arranging those selected structures into a logical pedagogical sequence from simple patterns (Subject + Verb) to increasingly complex structures (Subject + Verb + Object + Clause).
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for defining selection based on frequency, simplicity, and teachability.
     - 1 mark for defining gradation as sequential progression from simple to complex.

7. **Model Answer:** An information gap exists when one participant in an activity possesses information that the other participant does not know and must discover through communication. Example: Pupil A has a bus schedule with arrival times, and Pupil B has a blank schedule and must ask questions in English to fill in the missing departure times.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for defining the information gap as asymmetric knowledge.
     - 1 mark for providing a realistic, concrete classroom example.

8. **Model Answer:** The teacher can: (1) establish a clear non-verbal signal (such as raising a hand or tapping a chime) that requires immediate silence and eye contact, and (2) assign strictly defined pair roles with a physical task sheet that requires writing specific English answers within a strict five-minute time limit.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for an explicit non-verbal acoustic or visual signal for regaining attention.
     - 1 mark for structured task accountability (e.g. time limits, defined roles, worksheets).

9. **Model Answer:** Prefixes attach to the beginning of base root words and primarily alter semantic meaning or polarity (e.g. *happy -> unhappy*) without changing the part of speech. Suffixes attach to the end of base words and frequently change the grammatical part of speech (e.g. changing the verb *teach* into the agentive noun *teacher*, or the noun *care* into the adjective *careful*).
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark for explaining that prefixes modify meaning without changing word class.
     - 1 mark for explaining that suffixes change the grammatical part of speech.

10. **Model Answer:** The pupil confused the abstract noun suffix *-ness* with the adjective suffix *-ful* or the base adjective *sweet*. The correct target sentence is *"The mango was very sweet."* The rule is that the linking verb *was* followed by the intensifier *very* requires a predicate adjective (*sweet*), not an abstract noun (*sweetness*).
    - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
      - 1 mark for identifying the incorrect suffix and providing the correct sentence.
      - 1 mark for explaining the syntactic requirement for a predicate adjective after a linking verb.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Comparative Analysis of GTM and Direct Method
- **Analytic Rubric (Total 10 marks):**
  - *Theoretical view of language (0-3 marks):* Contrasts GTM's view of language as an intellectual system of rules and written literature with the Direct Method's view of language as living spoken communication acquired naturally like L1.
  - *Classroom procedures and materials (0-3 marks):* Analyzes GTM's use of chalkboard formulas, bilingual texts, and translation versus the Direct Method's use of realia, demonstration, pictures, and strict English immersion.
  - *Skill outcomes and evaluation (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Evaluates the trade-offs in learner competence, demonstrating how GTM produces strong reading and grammar analysis but deficient oral fluency, while the Direct Method develops listening and speaking confidence but struggles with abstract concepts.

#### ERQ 2: Audio-Lingualism vs. Structural Approach in Grammar Instruction
- **Analytic Rubric (Total 10 marks):**
  - *Methodological principles (0-3 marks):* Explains behaviorist habit formation in Audio-Lingualism and linguistic selection/gradation in the Structural Approach.
  - *Instructional techniques (0-3 marks):* Compares dialogue mimicry and pattern drills (repetition, substitution, transformation) with multi-slot substitution tables.
  - *Critical evaluation of communicative transfer (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Critically examines why neither method alone fosters spontaneous communicative fluency, diagnosing the cognitive gap between mechanical habit execution and creative linguistic generation.

#### ERQ 3: Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) Lesson Plan
- **Analytic Rubric (Total 10 marks):**
  - *Lesson structure and outcomes (0-2 marks):* Outlines clear behavioral learning outcomes and a coherent 35-minute lesson sequence.
  - *Information gap and pair task design (0-3 marks):* Designs a genuine information-gap activity with complementary student worksheets set in an authentic railway station context.
  - *Interactive management and role-play (0-2 marks):* Describes practical classroom organization for bench partners in crowded rooms.
  - *Accuracy and fluency balance (0-3 marks, **Create/Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Explains how the teacher monitors fluency without disruptive interruption, implementing a post-task feedback session to address structural errors systematically.

#### ERQ 4: Morphological Word-Building Sequence
- **Analytic Rubric (Total 10 marks):**
  - *Morpheme definitions and categorization (0-2 marks):* Clearly defines base roots, prefixes, suffixes, and compound words with linguistic accuracy.
  - *Step-by-step instructional sequence (0-4 marks, **Create**):* **Create:** Designs a creative, scaffolded classroom routine for roots *care* and *play*, demonstrating how affixation and compounding generate twelve or more valid English words.
  - *Pedagogical justification (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Analyzes how explicit morphological instruction builds generative decoding strategies, reduces working memory load, and prevents spelling errors in primary learners.

#### ERQ 5: Integrative Eclectic Model for Pakistani Primary Schools
- **Analytic Rubric (Total 10 marks):**
  - *Contextual diagnosis (0-2 marks):* Thoroughly diagnoses the real-world constraints of Pakistani public schools: 60+ pupils, fixed furniture, limited tech, and textbook-bound examinations.
  - *Critique of single-method purism (0-3 marks, **Evaluate**):* **Evaluate:** Evaluates the fatal flaws of adopting any single method dogmatically (GTM neglects speech; Direct Method struggles with abstract terms; Audio-Lingualism breeds mindless parroting; unguided CLT breeds chaotic inaccuracy).
  - *Synthesized eclectic framework (0-5 marks, **Evaluate/Create**):* **Evaluate/Create:** Synthesizes an evidence-based eclectic pedagogical model that thoughtfully integrates: (a) Direct demonstration for initial vocabulary, (b) Structural substitution tables for syntactic accuracy, (c) Audio-lingual repetition for pronunciation cadence, and (d) Communicative pair tasks for authentic fluency, justifying how this balanced approach fulfills curriculum objectives while preparing pupils for written examinations.
