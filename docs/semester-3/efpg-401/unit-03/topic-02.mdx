## A real classroom situation

Rashida is a teacher trainee completing her practical teaching in Khairpur. She is assigned to teach a Grade 3 English class at a rural primary school. Having noticed that her pupils constantly ask for Urdu translations during lessons, she decides to experiment with the Direct Method under the guidance of her mentor teacher, Madam Salma.

Rashida enters the classroom carrying a basket containing several fresh items from the local market: a yellow mango, a green guava, a shiny red apple, and a small metal spoon. She does not write any Urdu words on the board. Instead, she holds up the mango, smiles, and says clearly: "This is a mango. It is sweet and yellow." She repeats the sentence twice with natural cadence and facial expression. Next, she points to the mango and asks the front bench: "What is this?"

At first, the children hesitate and whisper among themselves in Sindhi: "آم چئون يا انب؟" (Should we say aam or amb?). Rashida gently shakes her head, smiles, points again to the fruit, and models the English response: "It is a mango." Within minutes, the pupils catch on. One boy in the second row raises his hand and says: "It is a mango!" Rashida immediately reinforces his success: "Excellent, Ali! It is a mango." She proceeds to demonstrate each fruit, using gestures and tasting motions. When she introduces the spoon, she mimes eating soup and says: "This is a spoon. We eat with a spoon." By the end of the lesson, the pupils are chorusing the names and simple descriptions entirely in English without a single word of translation spoken by the teacher.

## Explanation

The Direct Method emerged in Europe during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as an explicit reaction against the sterile, book-bound nature of the Grammar-Translation Method. Pioneers of language reform, such as Francois Gouin and Maximilian Berlitz, argued that second-language learning should simulate the natural, immersive conditions of first-language acquisition. When a child learns Sindhi, Urdu, or Pashto at home, they do not study grammar books or memorize bilingual vocabulary lists; rather, they hear meaningful language spoken in context, connect words directly to objects and actions, and internalize grammatical patterns inductively.

A frequent misconception among Pakistani teachers is that "the Direct Method strictly forbids every word of the mother tongue under all circumstances, making it cruel or impossible in rural primary schools." This view misunderstands the pedagogical intent. Celce-Murcia (1991) explains that while the Direct Method establishes the principle of maximum target-language exposure, its core objective is not dogmatic language policing, but rather creating direct cognitive pathways between concepts and English forms. The goal is to eliminate the inefficient mental detour where a pupil thinks of an object in Urdu, translates the word into English, and pauses before speaking. Brief, compassionate use of L1 for emotional reassurance or rare emergencies does not destroy the method; however, during instructional practice, meaning must be conveyed directly through sensory experience rather than translation.

### Direct method

The Direct Method operates on a set of consistent instructional principles designed to foster spontaneous oral communication:

1. **Direct Association of Meaning:** Words and grammatical forms are connected directly to the learner's physical senses and mental concepts without using the native language as an intermediary bridge. When teaching the word *table*, the teacher taps a physical desk rather than writing "میز" on the chalkboard.

2. **Target-Language Exclusivity:** Classroom instruction, administrative commands, praise, and social greetings are conducted entirely in English. The teacher establishes an English-speaking micro-environment inside the classroom walls.

3. **Inductive Grammar Instruction:** Instead of explaining explicit rules deductively, the teacher presents numerous spoken and visual examples that illustrate a pattern. Learners induce the grammatical rule intuitively from the examples, mirroring first-language acquisition.

4. **Oral Communication Precedes Written Text:** Listening and speaking are taught before reading and writing. Pupils listen to teacher models and practice oral question-and-answer routines before encountering the written forms in textbooks.

5. **Extensive Use of Realia and Visual Scaffolding:** Teachers rely heavily on real objects (realia), flashcards, wall posters, chalkboard sketches, pantomime, and physical gestures to clarify meanings of nouns, adjectives, and verbs.

#### Worked Example: Direct Method Lesson on Action Verbs (Present Continuous)

To see how the Direct Method operates in a Grade 4 Pakistani classroom, study the following step-by-step worked lesson sequence:

- **Target Language:** Present continuous action verbs: *walking, writing, jumping, opening*.
- **Setting:** A classroom of forty pupils in Khairpur; no audio-visual technology required.

- **Step 1: Demonstration and Oral Modelling (5 minutes):**
  - Teacher stands in front of the class, walks across the room, and states: "Look at me. I am walking. I am walking."
  - Teacher walks again and repeats: "I am walking."
  - Teacher walks to the board, picks up chalk, writes a word, and says: "I am writing. I am writing."
  - Teacher jumps lightly twice: "I am jumping. I am jumping."
  - Teacher walks to the classroom door, pushes it open, and says: "I am opening the door."

- **Step 2: Choral and Individual Physical Imitation (7 minutes):**
  - Teacher commands two pupils: "Kamran and Bilal, stand up, please. Walk to the window."
  - As they walk, the teacher prompts the class: "What are they doing?"
  - Teacher models the response: "They are walking." Class choruses: "They are walking!"
  - Teacher tells Sana: "Sana, please write your name on the board."
  - While Sana writes, teacher asks: "What is Sana doing?"
  - Class answers: "She is writing."

- **Step 3: Question-and-Answer Exchange (8 minutes):**
  - Teacher performs an action and asks individual pupils:
    - Teacher jumps: "Bilal, am I writing?"
    - Bilal: "No, you are jumping."
    - Teacher walks: "Farzana, what am I doing?"
    - Farzana: "You are walking."
  - Pupils ask each other: Pupil A mimes an action, and Pupil B identifies it in a complete English sentence.

- **Step 4: Reading and Writing Consolidation (10 minutes):**
  - Only after oral mastery is established does the teacher write the sentences on the board:
    1. *I am walking.*
    2. *She is writing.*
    3. *He is jumping.*
    4. *We are opening the door.*
  - Pupils read the sentences aloud with correct pronunciation and copy them neatly into their exercise books.

#### Operational Strengths and Practical Limitations

When implemented thoughtfully in Pakistani primary schools, the Direct Method offers profound instructional benefits:
- It breaks the habit of mental translation, enabling pupils to think and respond in English spontaneously.
- It accelerates listening comprehension and oral pronunciation through continuous immersion in authentic spoken models.
- It makes lessons lively, engaging, and physically active, which naturally suits younger elementary learners (Grades 1 to 5).
- It embeds language in memorable sensory experiences, significantly improving long-term vocabulary retention.

However, prospective teachers must also navigate serious practical limitations:
- **Abstract Concepts:** Demonstrating concrete objects (like fruits or classroom furniture) is straightforward; however, conveying abstract concepts (such as *justice*, *freedom*, *honesty*, or complex verb tenses) solely through mime and pictures can be exhausting, time-consuming, and prone to pupil misinterpretation.
- **Teacher Fluency Requirements:** The method demands high oral English fluency, spontaneous conversational confidence, and animated acting skills from the teacher. Teachers who lack spoken confidence often feel anxious or revert to lecture mode.
- **Class Size Pressures:** Conducting individualized oral question-and-answer checks in crowded Pakistani classrooms of sixty to eighty pupils requires exceptional classroom management and disciplined choral organization.

## Activity: Designing non-translation vocabulary routines

**Work in pairs.** Time allocation: approximately 20 minutes.

Select three target vocabulary words from a Grade 4 English textbook:
1. *Heavy* (adjective)
2. *Whisper* (verb)
3. *Under* (preposition)

For each word, design a concrete Direct Method presentation technique that conveys the meaning completely without using Urdu or Sindhi translation:
- Describe the physical object, realia, gesture, or physical demonstration the teacher will use.
- Write the exact spoken English sentences the teacher will model.
- Write two checking questions the teacher will ask pupils to verify understanding without asking "What does this mean in Urdu?"
- Role-play your presentation with your partner acting as a primary school pupil who speaks only basic English.

## Summary

The Direct Method emphasizes target-language immersion, inductive grammar acquisition, and direct cognitive association between English words and concrete reality. By eliminating mother tongue translation, the method trains learners to process and produce English spontaneously. Meaning is conveyed through real objects, pictures, mime, and demonstration, while listening and speaking precede reading and writing. While demanding high oral proficiency from the teacher and requiring creative techniques to explain abstract vocabulary, the Direct Method establishes active, vibrant classroom environments that build genuine spoken competence in primary school pupils.

## Try this at your practicum school

During your next practicum lesson, teach a five-minute vocabulary segment (such as four classroom items or four action verbs) entirely in English using real objects or mime:
- Prepare your physical objects or gestures in advance.
- Avoid using any Urdu or Sindhi words during this five-minute segment.
- Observe how pupils react to your physical cues and facial expressions.
- Note whether pupils are able to deduce the meaning and respond in complete English phrases.
Reflect on what was challenging about avoiding translation, and discuss your experience with your mentor teacher.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1 mark) | Proficient (2 marks) | Exemplary (3-4 marks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct demonstration** | Relies on abstract explanations or slips into mother tongue translation; visual aids are missing or unclear. | Uses realia and physical demonstration effectively to present all four prepositions in English. | Deploys exceptionally creative, culturally resonant physical demonstrations that establish unambiguous meaning without a single word of L1. |
| **Inductive questioning** | Asks rote yes/no questions that require minimal language production from pupils. | Conducts varied question-and-answer exchanges that elicit complete sentences with prepositions. | Crafts a scaffolded sequence of oral questions moving systematically from choral imitation to independent pupil-to-pupil inquiry. |
