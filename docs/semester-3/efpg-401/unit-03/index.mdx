# Methods of Teaching English

Language teaching methodology has evolved through distinct pedagogical movements, each reflecting differing perspectives on how learners acquire a second language. In Pakistani elementary classrooms, teachers encounter diverse instructional traditions, ranging from classical grammar analysis and immersive direct techniques to behavioral oral drills, structural pattern tables, and modern communicative interaction. In addition, morphological word-building strategies provide learners with generative tools to expand their English vocabulary independently. This unit equips prospective teachers with the historical foundations, classroom procedures, instructional trade-offs, and practical techniques necessary to make informed methodological choices that address the linguistic realities of Pakistani primary and middle school learners.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Explain the historical foundations, core principles, and classroom procedures of the Grammar-Translation Method, evaluating its strengths and limitations in resource-constrained schools. *(Understand, Evaluate; SLO:EFPG-401-3-1)*
2. Apply the Direct Method to primary English lessons through inductive presentation, oral immersion, real objects, and physical demonstrations without mother tongue translation. *(Apply, Analyze; SLO:EFPG-401-3-1)*
3. Design and execute Audio-Lingual dialogue drills, including repetition, substitution, transformation, and chain drills, to establish grammatical habits through oral-aural practice. *(Apply, Analyze; SLO:EFPG-401-3-1)*
4. Construct graded sentence structures and multi-slot substitution tables using the Structural Approach to help pupils produce syntactically accurate English sentences. *(Apply, Create; SLO:EFPG-401-3-2)*
5. Implement Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) principles by organizing information-gap tasks, role plays, and collaborative pair work that foster functional communicative competence. *(Apply, Analyze; SLO:EFPG-401-3-2)*
6. Teach morphological word-building techniques, guiding primary pupils to analyze and construct word families using roots, prefixes, suffixes, and compound words. *(Apply, Analyze; SLO:EFPG-401-3-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds directly upon Unit 1 (Four Skills of Language Learning) and Unit 2 (Teaching of English: Prose, Poetry, Composition, Vocabulary, and Pronunciation). Prospective teachers should have a firm understanding of the distinction between receptive skills (listening and reading) and productive skills (speaking and writing), as well as basic familiarity with elementary English curriculum standards.

## In this unit

1. [The grammar-translation method](./topic-01) - principles of deductive grammar instruction, bilingual vocabulary lists, translation exercises, and their classroom trade-offs.
2. [The direct method](./topic-02) - oral immersion, inductive grammar acquisition, meaning conveyance through realia and actions, and classroom demonstration techniques.
3. [The audio-lingual approach](./topic-03) - behaviorist habit formation, dialogue memorization, pattern drill archetypes, and oral-aural mastery in primary grades.
4. [The structural approach](./topic-04) - principles of selection and gradation, core sentence patterns, and multi-slot substitution tables for error-free production.
5. [The communicative approach](./topic-05) - communicative competence, authentic purpose, information-gap tasks, interactive pair work, and balancing fluency with accuracy.
6. [Word building](./topic-06) - morphological analysis, base roots, high-frequency prefixes and suffixes, compounding, and generative vocabulary expansion.

## How to use this unit

Study each topic sequentially, paying close attention to how subsequent teaching methods developed in response to the limitations of earlier approaches. Each topic opens with an authentic Pakistani classroom situation, introduces theoretical principles and concrete classroom techniques, presents worked instructional examples, and provides collaborative activities, formative retrieval questions, and practicum transfer tasks. Complete each self-assessment checklist before proceeding to the next topic. Once you have completed all six topics, attempt the comprehensive unit assessment to evaluate your pedagogical mastery across all methods.
