# Teaching of English - Course Overview

This Semester 3 course prepares prospective teachers to teach English effectively in Grades I to VIII. It equips future teachers to develop pupils' listening, speaking, reading and writing skills, use active-learning methods, integrate audio-visual aids, design structured lesson plans and assess language progress in English as a second or foreign language.

## Course objectives

The University of Sindh B.Ed course guide outlines six core objectives for prospective teachers:

1. Become familiar with the four language skills - listening, speaking, reading and writing.
2. Identify and prepare activities for developing all four language skills.
3. Apply modern methods and approaches in the teaching of English.
4. Prepare lesson plans for prose, poetry, composition and grammar.
5. Make effective use of audio-visual aids in English lessons.
6. Measure and evaluate students' progress in English as a foreign or second language.

## Begin with Unit 1

[Four Skills of Language Learning](./unit-01/) introduces the foundational receptive and productive language abilities across four topics: listening comprehension, speaking skills, reading skills and writing skills, followed by a comprehensive unit assessment. Subsequent units cover genres of English teaching, teaching methods, grammar instruction, lesson planning, instructional media and assessment techniques.

## Assessment and readings

The course guide specifies assessment criteria including class tests, mid-term examinations, evaluated assignments, attendance and active participation. In line with the B.Ed programme standard, assessment weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative. Unit 1 provides retrieval questions, collaborative classroom tasks, transfer activities for practicum placement and extended tasks with self-study guidance.

Each topic provides verified references and readings. Recommended sources include classic works by Cook (1991), Celce-Murcia (1991) and Nolasco and Arthur (1987), alongside contemporary open-access research on audio-visual listening, reading strategies and writing pedagogies. Prospective teachers should study each topic thoroughly before attempting the unit assessment.
