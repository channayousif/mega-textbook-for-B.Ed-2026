# Unit 2 Assessment - Teaching of English

This end-of-unit assessment measures your mastery of the five teaching tasks in Unit 2: teaching prose, teaching poems, teaching composition, teaching vocabulary and teaching pronunciation. Complete the summative assessment tasks before reviewing the model answers and marking guidance at the end of this page.

## Unit summary

Unit 2 established that teaching English draws on five core classroom tasks, each with its own aim and method.

- **Teaching prose:** A prose lesson teaches pupils to read a continuous English text for meaning. The stages are warm-up, new words, model reading, silent reading, comprehension questions and follow-up. Comprehension questions move from literal facts to inference and personal response. Translation may support understanding, but it is not the goal.
- **Teaching poems:** A poetry lesson teaches pupils to enjoy, understand and respond to a poem. The stages are listening and enjoyment, understanding the poem, and reading, responding and performing. Memorisation may be a result, but it is not the aim. Rhythm, rhyme and imagery help pupils experience the poem before they analyse it.
- **Teaching composition:** Composition teaches pupils to express their own ideas in English. The guided-to-free continuum moves from copying to completing, guided writing and free writing. Each step reduces the support the teacher provides and increases the pupil's responsibility for content.
- **Teaching vocabulary:** Vocabulary teaching helps pupils acquire, retain and use new words. A new word can be presented with a picture, an object, mime, an example sentence, a synonym or translation. Learners rate reading and repeating, and using words in context, as effective, while superficial attention and excessive translation are ineffective. Pupils need strategy training, not just word lists.
- **Teaching pronunciation:** Pronunciation teaching aims at intelligibility, not a native accent. The three areas are individual sounds, word stress and sentence intonation. Minimal pairs help pupils hear and produce sounds that do not exist in their mother tongue.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Which stage of a prose lesson comes immediately after the teacher's model reading?
   - A) Warm-up
   - B) New words
   - C) Silent reading
   - D) Comprehension questions

2. *(Understand)* Which type of comprehension question asks pupils to read between the lines?
   - A) Literal
   - B) Inferential
   - C) Personal response
   - D) Translation

3. *(Remember)* Which stage of a poetry lesson should come first?
   - A) Memorisation
   - B) Listening and enjoyment
   - C) Word-by-word translation
   - D) Writing a summary

4. *(Apply)* A teacher asks pupils to tap the beat of a poem. Which feature of poetry is this activity developing?
   - A) Imagery
   - B) Rhythm
   - C) Spelling
   - D) Translation

5. *(Remember)* Which of the following is the first step on the guided-to-free continuum?
   - A) Free writing
   - B) Guided writing
   - C) Copying
   - D) Completing

6. *(Understand)* Why is copying a model paragraph not enough on its own?
   - A) It is too difficult for pupils.
   - B) It does not require pupils to produce their own ideas.
   - C) It takes too much time.
   - D) It prevents pupils from learning handwriting.

7. *(Remember)* Which of the following is a good way to present a new word?
   - A) A list of twenty words with Urdu meanings
   - B) A picture and a simple English sentence
   - C) A dictionary definition copied five times
   - D) A translation test

8. *(Apply)* A teacher explains a new word in English with an example and then asks pupils to use it in a sentence. Which finding from Tamimi Sa'd and Rajabi (2018) does this match?
   - A) Explanation in the mother tongue is the most effective strategy.
   - B) Explanation in the target language with examples is an effective strategy.
   - C) Dictation is the only effective strategy.
   - D) Superficial attention to words is effective.

9. *(Remember)* Which pair of words is a minimal pair?
   - A) Cat and dog
   - B) Ship and sheep
   - C) Book and books
   - D) Run and running

10. *(Apply)* A teacher says "I want a cup, not a cap" while pointing to the vowel sounds. Which pronunciation feature is being taught?
    - A) Word stress
    - B) Sentence intonation
    - C) A vowel sound contrast
    - D) The /r/ sound

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Explain the purpose of the warm-up stage in a prose lesson.
2. *(Analyze)* A teacher asks only literal questions after a story. Analyse two effects this has on pupils' reading development.
3. *(Understand)* Explain two differences between a prose lesson and a poetry lesson.
4. *(Analyze)* Analyse why memorising a poem before discussing its meaning limits pupils' response to it.
5. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between guided writing and free writing.
6. *(Analyze)* A pupil copies a model paragraph and changes three words. Analyse why this task does not develop composition skills.
7. *(Understand)* Explain two ways a teacher can present a new word without using translation.
8. *(Analyze)* Analyse why learners' frequent use of translation and mental repetition may be less effective than using words in context.
9. *(Understand)* Explain what a minimal pair is and give one example.
10. *(Analyze)* Analyse why the goal of pronunciation teaching is intelligibility rather than a native accent.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* A Grade 6 teacher reads a story aloud, translates every sentence into Sindhi, and asks pupils to copy the translation. Pupils can recite the translation but cannot answer a question about the story in English. Analyse the flaws in this lesson and redesign it using the six stages of a prose lesson.
2. *(Analyze)* A Grade 5 teacher asks pupils to memorise a poem and recite it in chorus. The pupils memorise it perfectly but cannot say what it means. Analyse the lesson against the three stages of a poetry lesson and propose a revised plan.
3. *(Create)* Design a 35-minute guided-writing lesson for Grade 5 on the topic "A Day at the Market." Your plan must include a copying step, a completing step, a guided-writing step with three prompts, and a free-writing step.
4. *(Evaluate)* A Grade 5 teacher writes twenty words with Urdu meanings and tests them the next day. Pupils score well but cannot use the words in sentences. Evaluate this practice against the findings of Tamimi Sa'd and Rajabi (2018) and propose an alternative lesson.
5. *(Analyze)* Compare how a prose lesson and a poetry lesson on the same theme, such as rain, should differ in aim, stages and pupil response. Draw on the teaching of vocabulary and pronunciation to show how the two lessons would support each other.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. C - Silent reading follows the teacher's model reading, giving pupils time to read the text themselves.
2. B - Inferential questions ask pupils to read between the lines and use clues in the text.
3. B - Listening and enjoyment comes first so that pupils experience the poem before analysing it.
4. B - Tapping the beat develops awareness of the poem's rhythm.
5. C - Copying is the first step of the continuum and gives the pupil full support.
6. B - Copying does not require pupils to generate their own ideas, so it does not develop composition.
7. B - A picture and a simple English sentence present the word in a memorable, meaningful way.
8. B - The study found explanation in the target language with examples to be an effective teaching strategy.
9. B - Ship and sheep differ in one vowel sound, so they form a minimal pair.
10. C - Cup and cap differ in the vowel sound, so the teacher is teaching a vowel sound contrast.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model Answer:** The warm-up connects the lesson to pupils' lives, activates what they already know about the topic and creates interest in the text. It prepares pupils to read.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Names one purpose (connection to prior knowledge or interest).
     - 1 mark: Explains how it helps pupils read the text.

2. **Model Answer:** Only literal questions check facts. Pupils do not practise inference or personal response, so they learn to find answers rather than to think about the text. Over time, their reading stays at surface level and they cannot discuss a text in depth.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies that only facts are checked.
     - 1 mark: Names the missing skills (inference, personal response).
     - 1 mark: Explains the long-term effect on reading.

3. **Model Answer:** A prose lesson aims at understanding a continuous text, while a poetry lesson aims at enjoyment and response. Prose reading is often silent for meaning; poetry is often read aloud for sound and feeling. Prose questions move from literal to inferential; poetry questions move from feeling to meaning.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Names the different aims.
     - 1 mark: Names the different role of reading aloud.
     - 1 mark: Names the different question path.

4. **Model Answer:** Memorising before discussing the meaning puts the words before the experience. Pupils focus on accurate recall rather than understanding, so they cannot respond to the poem. Enjoyment and response require the poem to be understood and felt first.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies that recall comes before understanding.
     - 1 mark: Explains the effect on response.
     - 1 mark: Explains that enjoyment requires understanding first.

5. **Model Answer:** Guided writing gives pupils a frame, prompts or questions that guide their ideas, but they write the sentences themselves. Free writing asks pupils to choose and organise ideas independently with no frame.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines guided writing with support.
     - 1 mark: Defines free writing without support.

6. **Model Answer:** Copying and changing three words keeps the pupil at the copying step. The pupil does not generate ideas, organise content or make choices, so the task does not develop composition. It only practises handwriting and spelling.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies that the pupil stays at copying.
     - 1 mark: Names the missing skills (ideas, organisation).
     - 1 mark: Explains that only handwriting and spelling are practised.

7. **Model Answer:** A teacher can use a picture, a real object, mime, an example sentence or a synonym. Each method shows the meaning in English without translating.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Names two methods.
     - 1 mark: Explains how one method shows meaning in English.

8. **Model Answer:** Translation and mental repetition are quick but shallow. They store the meaning as a pair of words rather than as a usable tool. Using words in context shows how a word behaves in a sentence, which helps pupils recall and use it later.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies that translation and repetition are shallow.
     - 1 mark: Explains the difference in depth.
     - 1 mark: Explains how context supports later use.

9. **Model Answer:** A minimal pair is two words that differ in one sound, such as ship and sheep. Drilling a minimal pair helps pupils hear and produce the difference.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines a minimal pair.
     - 1 mark: Gives a correct example.

10. **Model Answer:** A native accent is not necessary for understanding. Intelligibility means a listener can understand the message. If a pupil keeps a Pakistani accent but produces sounds, stress and intonation clearly, they are understood. Chasing a native accent can embarrass pupils and reduce speaking practice.
    - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
      - 1 mark: Defines intelligibility.
      - 1 mark: Explains why a native accent is not necessary.
      - 1 mark: Explains the effect on pupil confidence and practice.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Analysis and Redesign of a Translation-Based Prose Lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis of flaws (0-3 marks):* Identifies translation as a substitute for reading, the lack of silent reading, and the absence of comprehension questions.
  - *Redesign with six stages (0-3 marks):* Produces a complete prose lesson with warm-up, new words, model reading, silent reading, comprehension questions and follow-up.
  - *Analysis of reading development (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies how each stage builds understanding in English and why translation alone fails, with reference to how pupils read for meaning.

#### ERQ 2: Analysis of a Memorisation-Based Poetry Lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis of the poem lesson (0-3 marks):* Identifies that memorisation comes before enjoyment and understanding, and that pupils have no chance to respond.
  - *Revised three-stage plan (0-3 marks):* Produces a poetry lesson with listening and enjoyment, understanding, and reading and responding.
  - *Analysis of response (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies why enjoyment and understanding must come before memorisation, with reference to how pupils respond to a poem.

#### ERQ 3: Guided-Writing Lesson for Grade 5
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Continuum design (0-3 marks):* Includes copying, completing, guided writing and free writing in order.
  - *Prompt and task quality (0-3 marks):* Prompts guide ideas without writing the paragraph for the pupil; the free-writing step is a genuine independent task.
  - *Analysis of progression (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies how each step reduces support and prepares pupils for the next, with reference to how learners move to independent writing.

#### ERQ 4: Evaluation of Word-List Vocabulary Teaching
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Evaluation of the practice (0-3 marks):* Evaluates word-list teaching against the study's findings, identifying superficial attention and lack of context.
  - *Alternative lesson (0-3 marks):* Proposes a lesson with pictures, objects, example sentences, pair use and strategy training.
  - *Analysis of depth (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies why deeper processing supports retention and use, drawing on the mapped reading.

#### ERQ 5: Comparison of Prose and Poetry Teaching on One Theme
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Comparison of aims and stages (0-3 marks):* Compares the prose and poetry lessons on the same theme, naming differences in aim, stages and pupil response.
  - *Integration of vocabulary and pronunciation (0-3 marks):* Shows how vocabulary and pronunciation teaching support both lessons.
  - *Analysis of integration (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies why the two lessons should differ and how they support each other, with reference to the unit's teaching.
