## A real classroom situation

Bilal is a prospective teacher on practicum at a government primary school in Larkana. For his Grade 5 English lesson, he writes a model paragraph on the board and asks the pupils to copy it. He then asks them to change three words and write the paragraph again. The pupils produce neat copies, but when he asks them to write a paragraph on "My Village," they stare at the page. One pupil whispers, "Sir, we do not know how to start without the model."

His cooperating teacher, Master Ghulam, explains after the lesson. "You taught them to copy, not to write," he says. "Copying is a beginning, but composition means the pupils produce their own ideas. You must move them step by step from the model to their own writing."

## Explanation

Composition is writing that expresses the learner's own ideas in English. It ranges from careful practice, where the teacher controls almost every word, to free writing, where the pupil chooses and organises ideas independently. A good composition lesson moves pupils along this range instead of asking them to write freely before they are ready.

A common misconception is that "composition means copying a model essay." A model can help pupils see the shape of a paragraph, but copying alone does not teach them to generate ideas. If pupils only copy, they learn to reproduce someone else's thoughts. The teacher's job is to reduce support gradually so that pupils can write their own.

### Teaching of composition

The guided-to-free continuum has four steps. Each step gives the pupil less support and more responsibility for the content.

1. **Copying:** The pupil traces or copies a model text. This builds handwriting and spelling and shows the shape of a paragraph. It is a starting point only.
2. **Completing:** The pupil finishes a partly written text. For example, the teacher writes the first two sentences of a paragraph and the pupil writes the rest. This asks the pupil to think about content while the frame reduces the burden.
3. **Guided writing:** The pupil writes with support from a frame, prompts or questions. For example: "What is the name of your village? How many people live there? What is one good thing about it?" The frame guides the ideas, but the pupil writes the sentences.
4. **Free writing:** The pupil writes independently on a new topic. The teacher gives a clear task and time, but no frame. This is the goal of composition teaching.

<Figure id="fig-U2-6" src="/img/figures/efpg-401/unit-02/fig-U2-6.svg" alt="Table of composition tasks by grade band with the support each needs and the teacher's role." />

**Worked example: a Grade 5 guided-writing lesson for "My Village" (35 minutes).**

- Copying (5 minutes): Pupils copy two model sentences: "My village is small. It has green fields."
- Completing (7 minutes): The teacher writes "My village is called ______. There are ______ people in it." Pupils complete the sentences.
- Guided writing (13 minutes): The teacher asks three prompts: "What is one good thing about your village?" "What is one problem?" "What do you like to do there?" Pupils write a five-sentence paragraph using their answers.
- Free writing (7 minutes): Pupils write three new sentences beginning "I wish my village had..." without prompts.
- Sharing (3 minutes): Two pupils read their paragraphs aloud.

## Activity: Design a guided-writing task

**Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.**

Choose a Grade 4 topic, such as "My Favourite Season." Design a guided-writing sequence that moves from completing a partly written paragraph to writing a full paragraph with prompts. Write the exact prompts you will give. Then exchange tasks with another pair and check whether the prompts guide the ideas without writing the paragraph for the pupil.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name the four steps of the guided-to-free continuum in order.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why completing a partly written text is easier for pupils than free writing.
3. *(Apply)* A pupil can copy a model paragraph but cannot write one without it. Design two tasks, one at the completing step and one at the guided-writing step, that would help this pupil move forward.

## Summary

Composition teaches pupils to express their own ideas in English. The guided-to-free continuum moves from copying to completing, guided writing and free writing. Each step reduces the support the teacher provides and increases the pupil's responsibility for content. Copying is a beginning, not the goal.

## Try this at your practicum school

During your next practicum visit, look at a sample of pupils' English writing. Note whether the writing looks copied or independently produced. Ask your cooperating teacher how the class is moved from copying to free writing, and suggest one guided-writing task for the next lesson.

## Summative task

Plan a 35-minute guided-writing lesson for Grade 5 on the topic "A Day at the Market." Your plan must include a copying step, a completing step, a guided-writing step with three prompts, and a free-writing step. Add a short note explaining how each step reduces support.
