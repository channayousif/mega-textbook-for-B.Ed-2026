## A real classroom situation

Farzana is a prospective teacher on practicum at a government primary school in Karachi. For her Grade 5 English lesson, she writes twenty new words on the board with their Urdu meanings and asks the pupils to memorise them for a test. The pupils score well on the test the next day. But in the following week, when she asks them to use five of the words in sentences, most cannot do it. One pupil says, "We learnt the meanings for the test, madam, but we do not know how to use the words."

Her cooperating teacher, Madam Rukhsana, explains after the lesson. "A word is not its meaning on a list," she says. "A word is a tool pupils must learn to use. If they only memorise meanings, they forget them. You must help them meet the word in context, practise it, and use it."

## Explanation

Vocabulary teaching helps learners acquire, retain and use new words. A pupil who knows a word can understand it when reading or listening and can use it when speaking or writing. This is a large task, and it is not finished by memorising a list of meanings.

A common misconception is that "the more words on the list, the better." Teachers often write twenty or thirty words with translations and test them the next day. Pupils may score well, but they quickly forget words they have not used. Deep learning comes from meeting a word in context, processing its meaning, and using it in speech or writing.

### Teaching of vocabulary

There are many ways to present a new word. The best method depends on the word, the grade and the resources available.

- **Picture:** Show a flash card, a photograph or a drawing. Good for concrete nouns such as *mango*, *bridge* and *umbrella*.
- **Object (realia):** Show the real thing or a model. Good for classroom objects such as *pencil*, *basket* and *lamp*. Real objects make the word memorable because pupils can touch them.
- **Mime:** Act out the meaning. Good for verbs and adjectives such as *run*, *laugh*, *tall* and *heavy*. Mime works without any resources.
- **Example sentence:** Use the word in a simple English sentence. Good for words that depend on context, such as *honest* and *flood*. The sentence shows how the word is used.
- **Synonym:** Give a known English word with a similar meaning. Good for building connections, such as *big* for *large* and *happy* for *glad*.
- **Translation:** Give the mother-tongue meaning as a final support. Translation is quick, but it should not be the first or only method. If pupils only meet the translation, they learn the meaning but not the English word.

A good vocabulary presentation uses two or three methods together. For example, teach *mango* with a picture, the real fruit if possible, and the sentence "The mango is sweet."

<Figure id="fig-U2-8" src="/img/figures/efpg-401/unit-02/fig-U2-8.svg" alt="Table of vocabulary strategies showing which learners rate effective and ineffective, with a classroom example for each." />

Tamimi Sa'd and Rajabi (2018) studied how Iranian EFL learners and their teachers handle vocabulary. They found that learners reported a limited repertoire of vocabulary learning strategies. The strategies learners rated most effective were reading and repeating words and using words in a specific context. Teachers' most effective strategies were explanation in the target language with examples and repetition. The strategies rated ineffective were superficial attention to words, complicated or inexplicable explanation, and excessive use of the mother tongue. The study also found that learners underused dictionaries and vocabulary boxes, which research shows are effective. The authors conclude that learners need strategy training: they need to be taught how to acquire vocabulary, not just told to memorise it.

**Worked example: a Grade 4 vocabulary lesson for five new words (20 minutes).**

- Picture and object (4 minutes): Show a picture of a *bridge* and a model or drawing of one. Say: "A bridge crosses a river."
- Mime (3 minutes): Act out *carry* and *lift*. Pupils guess the words.
- Example sentence (4 minutes): Write "The farmer carries water to his fields." Pupils repeat the sentence.
- Synonym and translation (4 minutes): Give *big* as a synonym for *large*, then the mother-tongue meaning as a check.
- Practice and use (5 minutes): In pairs, pupils ask and answer: "What can you carry?" and "What is big in our school?"

## Activity: Present a new word three ways

**Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.**

Choose five words from a Grade 4 English lesson. For each word, decide which presentation method you will use first (picture, object, mime, example sentence, synonym or translation) and write the sentence or action you will use. Then present two of your words to another pair as if they were the class. Ask them to say which method helped them most and why.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name four ways to present a new English word without using translation.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why a word learnt only from a list with its translation is easily forgotten.
3. *(Apply)* A teacher writes ten new words with Urdu meanings and tests them the next day. Design a short follow-up activity that would help pupils use three of the words in speaking.

## Summary

Vocabulary teaching helps pupils acquire, retain and use new words. A new word can be presented with a picture, an object, mime, an example sentence, a synonym or translation. Learners rate reading and repeating, and using words in context, as effective, while superficial attention and excessive translation are ineffective. Pupils need strategy training, not just word lists.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can present a new word using at least two methods other than translation.
- [ ] I can explain why using a word in context helps pupils remember it.
- [ ] I can identify which vocabulary strategies learners rate effective and which they rate ineffective.

## Try this at your practicum school

During your next practicum visit, ask your cooperating teacher how new vocabulary is presented in the English lesson. Note the methods used. Then plan a short presentation for two new words using a picture and an example sentence, and try it with the class if the teacher agrees.

## Summative task

Plan a 20-minute vocabulary lesson for Grade 5 on five new words from a current English lesson. For each word, name the presentation method you will use and write the sentence or action. Then design a short pair activity that makes pupils use at least three of the words. Add a note explaining why you chose each method.
