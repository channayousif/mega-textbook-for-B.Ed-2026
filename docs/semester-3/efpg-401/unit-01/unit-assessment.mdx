# Unit 1 Assessment - Four Skills of Language Learning

This end-of-unit assessment measures your mastery of the four language skills: listening comprehension, speaking skills, reading skills, and writing skills. Complete the summative assessment tasks before reviewing the model answers and marking guidance at the end of this page.

## Unit summary

Unit 1 established that language competence rests upon the balanced, integrated development of four primary skills:
- **Receptive Skills (Listening and Reading):** Learners process spoken and written input to construct meaning. Both listening and reading are active cognitive processes, not passive reception. Effective pedagogy scaffolds comprehension through structured three-stage lesson designs (pre, while, and post), purposeful tasks (skimming, scanning, listening for gist, and extracting specific details), and multimodal scaffolding (audio recordings, video contexts, and visual aids).
- **Productive Skills (Speaking and Writing):** Learners actively encode and express thoughts, intentions, and information. Productive language flourishes in low-anxiety, supportive environments where errors are treated as natural stages of learning. Speaking progress transitions from structured dialogues and language games to open communicative exchanges, underpinned by intelligible pronunciation and intonation. Writing development bridges mechanical foundations (handwriting and spelling) with creative and functional composition (paragraphs, letters, and applications) through the five-stage process writing approach.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* What is the primary characteristic that distinguishes listening comprehension from physical hearing?
   - A) Hearing requires conscious concentration, whereas listening occurs automatically.
   - B) Listening is an active cognitive process of constructing meaning, whereas hearing is acoustic perception.
   - C) Hearing only occurs in a second language, whereas listening happens in the mother tongue.
   - D) Listening requires reading printed text, whereas hearing relies entirely on visual gestures.

2. *(Understand)* What is the main pedagogical objective of the pre-listening stage in a primary English lesson?
   - A) To test pupils on grammatical terminology before they hear the audio passage.
   - B) To activate pupils' prior knowledge, pre-teach blocking words, and establish an explicit purpose for listening.
   - C) To make pupils copy the complete audio transcript into their notebooks from the board.
   - D) To evaluate pupils' individual pronunciation through loud recitation before audio playback.

3. *(Apply)* A Grade 4 teacher plays a short recorded weather report and asks pupils: "Is tomorrow going to be rainy or sunny in Sukkur?" Which listening sub-skill is this task primarily assessing?
   - A) Intensive grammatical parsing.
   - B) Listening for specific detail.
   - C) Sub-vocal choral translation.
   - D) Extensive literary appreciation.

4. *(Remember)* Which intonation contour is typically used in English for standard yes/no questions (such as "Are you coming today?")?
   - A) Monotone level pitch.
   - B) Rising intonation.
   - C) Falling intonation.
   - D) Abruptly dropping pitch.

5. *(Understand)* Why are information-gap activities particularly effective for developing spoken English fluency?
   - A) They require pupils to memorize long dramatic scripts before speaking.
   - B) They create a genuine communicative need to exchange unknown information between partners.
   - C) They eliminate all possibilities of making grammatical errors during conversation.
   - D) They allow the teacher to speak for the entire duration of the classroom period.

6. *(Apply)* During an interactive pair-speaking game, a pupil says: "Yesterday my brother buyed two book." How should the teacher respond to foster spoken fluency?
   - A) Interrupt immediately, make the pupil stand, and force him to recite irregular verbs ten times.
   - B) Note the error unobtrusively and review past tense forms collectively during delayed feedback at the end of the lesson.
   - C) Deduct marks immediately from the pupil's notebook and award zero for speaking.
   - D) Instruct the rest of the class to laugh at the mistake so the pupil remembers the rule.

7. *(Remember)* What reading sub-skill is defined as glancing rapidly over a text to locate a specific date, number, or name while ignoring unrelated sentences?
   - A) Inferencing.
   - B) Scanning.
   - C) Skimming.
   - D) Choral chanting.

8. *(Understand)* Why does structured silent reading typically produce higher comprehension than round-robin loud reading?
   - A) Silent reading splits attention between vocal articulation and decoding.
   - B) Silent reading forces all pupils to read at the identical speed set by the teacher.
   - C) Silent reading allows readers to allocate full cognitive resources to meaning, pacing, and re-reading.
   - D) Silent reading prevents pupils from seeing the spelling of unfamiliar words.

9. *(Remember)* What is the correct sequence of stages in the five-stage process approach to writing?
   - A) Drafting, Publishing, Pre-writing, Editing, Revising.
   - B) Pre-writing, Drafting, Revising, Editing, Publishing.
   - C) Editing, Drafting, Pre-writing, Publishing, Revising.
   - D) Publishing, Editing, Revising, Drafting, Pre-writing.

10. *(Apply)* A Grade 5 pupil is drafting a formal application to the Headmaster for sick leave. Which closing phrase is conventionally appropriate?
    - A) Your best friend forever,
    - B) Cheers and warm regards,
    - C) Yours obediently,
    - D) With lots of love,

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Contrast physical hearing with active listening comprehension in two concise sentences.

2. *(Apply)* A Grade 4 teacher is planning a listening lesson using an audio story about "A Visit to the Karachi Zoo." Design two distinct while-listening tasks that target different listening sub-skills.

3. *(Analyze)* Analyse two specific cognitive reasons why young second-language learners comprehend a dialogue presented on video more easily than the same dialogue presented as an audio-only recording.

4. *(Understand)* Explain how a teacher can lower pupils' affective filter during English speaking activities, identifying two concrete classroom techniques.

5. *(Apply)* Describe a simple language game suitable for a Grade 3 class that provides repetitive oral practice of the question structure "Do you have a...?" without relying on rote chorus recitation.

6. *(Analyze)* Analyse the pedagogical difference between accuracy-focused speaking drills and fluency-focused communicative tasks, explaining when delayed error feedback is preferable to immediate interruption.

7. *(Understand)* Distinguish between the phonic method and the look-and-say (whole-word) method of teaching reading, providing one classroom example of words suited to each method.

8. *(Apply)* Formulate one skimming question and one scanning question based on a short 80-word informational text describing the historical fortress of Kot Diji in Khairpur district.

9. *(Understand)* Explain two reasons why primary teachers should evaluate creative writing compositions on original idea generation and organization rather than solely on neat handwriting and spelling accuracy.

10. *(Apply)* Outline the seven mandatory structural components of a formal school application to a Headmaster in their conventional top-to-bottom sequence.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* In a rural Grade 4 English class, the teacher reads a 200-word story aloud once at normal speed without any introductory discussion or guiding questions. At the end, the teacher asks five detailed factual questions. When pupils fail to answer, the teacher concludes that "rural pupils have poor listening ability." Analyse the instructional flaws in this lesson design using the Three-Stage Listening Framework, and construct a revised lesson plan demonstrating how pre-listening, while-listening, and post-listening activities would scaffold successful comprehension.

2. *(Evaluate)* Many elementary schools in Pakistan rely almost exclusively on whole-class choral repetition for speaking practice, arguing that it keeps large classes orderly. Evaluate the pedagogical effectiveness of whole-class choral repetition versus small-group communicative games (such as Information-Gap and Twenty Questions) for developing functional spoken English proficiency, justifying your stance with linguistic and psychological evidence.

3. *(Analyze)* A Grade 5 teacher complains that her pupils can read English chapters aloud with loud voices and reasonable pronunciation, yet they fail simple comprehension tests based on the same chapters. Analyse the cognitive breakdown occurring during round-robin loud reading, and design a balanced 30-minute reading lesson that integrates pre-reading schema activation, guided silent reading with skimming and scanning tasks, and post-reading comprehension checks.

4. *(Create)* Design a comprehensive process-writing lesson plan for Grade 5 on the topic "An Informal Letter Inviting a Friend to My Village Festival." Your plan must detail explicit teacher and student activities across all five stages of the writing process (Pre-writing, Drafting, Revising, Editing, and Publishing), including a peer-editing checklist and an analytic marking rubric.

5. *(Analyze)* An elementary textbook unit covers the theme "Conserving Water in Our School." Design an integrated four-day lesson sequence (one day each for Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing) that teaches this single theme across all four language skills. Analyse how each receptive skill provides linguistic input and schema models for the subsequent productive skill, demonstrating the natural interdependence of the four language domains.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Listening is an active, meaning-making cognitive process of interpreting spoken sounds, whereas hearing is the physiological perception of sound waves.
2. B - Pre-listening activates prior knowledge (schema), removes blocking lexical barriers, and gives pupils a clear purpose before audio begins.
3. B - Asking for a specific piece of factual information (the weather in a named city) tests listening for specific detail, not global gist or parsing.
4. B - Standard yes/no questions in English end with a rising intonation contour (pitch rises on the final key word) to signal an open inquiry.
5. B - Information-gap activities require an authentic exchange of unknown information, giving pupils a genuine communicative reason to speak.
6. B - Delayed feedback preserves student confidence and speaking flow during fluency tasks; errors are reviewed collectively at lesson closure.
7. B - Scanning is rapid eye movement across text seeking a specific target item (dates, digits, names) while ignoring unrelated sentences.
8. C - Silent reading frees working memory from the motor demands of vocalization, allowing the reader to concentrate entirely on meaning, pacing, and re-reading.
9. B - The five-stage writing process follows the sequence: Pre-writing (planning), Drafting (writing), Revising (content improvement), Editing (mechanics), and Publishing (final presentation).
10. C - "Yours obediently," is the conventional formal complimentary close used in Pakistani school applications to a Headmaster or Principal.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model Answer:** Physical hearing is the passive physiological reception of acoustic sound waves by the ear, which occurs without conscious effort. Active listening comprehension is the deliberate cognitive process of attending to speech, segmenting sounds into words, and constructing meaning from the spoken message.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Accurately defines hearing as passive physiological reception of sound.
     - 1 mark: Accurately defines listening comprehension as active cognitive meaning-making.

2. **Model Answer:**
   - *Task 1 (Listening for Gist):* "Listen to the whole story and tick the main reason why the family visited the zoo: (a) To feed the birds, (b) To celebrate a birthday, (c) To buy a pet."
   - *Task 2 (Listening for Specific Detail):* "Listen to the middle part of the story and write down the names of the two animals that were sleeping near the pond."
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1.5 marks: Task 1 correctly targets global gist with a low-writing cognitive load.
     - 1.5 marks: Task 2 correctly targets specific detail extraction.

3. **Model Answer:** Video provides visual kinesic support (facial expressions, mouth movements, and hand gestures) that helps learners decode phonemes and infer emotional attitudes that voice alone cannot convey. Additionally, the physical background of a video provides environmental context that grounds unfamiliar vocabulary within a concrete visual setting, reducing cognitive load on working memory.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1.5 marks: Explains paralinguistic/kinesic cues (mouth movements, facial expressions, gestures).
     - 1.5 marks: Explains environmental/situational visual context and its role in easing working memory load.

4. **Model Answer:** A teacher can lower the affective filter by shifting speaking practice from whole-class recitation into structured, low-stakes pair work where pupils speak to a single peer without fear of public embarrassment. Second, the teacher should separate fluency practice from accuracy drills, avoiding immediate interruptions during communicative talk and offering delayed, anonymous feedback at the end of the lesson.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies structured pair work to remove the whole-class public spotlight.
     - 1 mark: Identifies delayed non-threatening error correction to protect learner confidence.

5. **Model Answer:** In the game "Classroom Bag Mystery," Pupil A places three school items (e.g. ruler, eraser, sharpener) inside a cloth bag without showing Pupil B. Pupil B must guess what is inside by asking: "Do you have a pencil?" Pupil A answers: "No, I do not." Pupil B asks: "Do you have a ruler?" Pupil A answers: "Yes, I do." Pupils switch roles after three correct guesses, tallying who guessed the contents in the fewest questions.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Provides a clear, practical classroom game structure (e.g. guessing game, information gap).
     - 1 mark: Embeds genuine repetitive use of the target question/response frame.
     - 1 mark: Explains how the activity operates interactively without rote chorus recitation.

6. **Model Answer:** Accuracy-focused drills aim at linguistic correctness, phonological precision, and grammatical form; immediate gentle correction is appropriate here so pupils do not fossilize incorrect patterns. Fluency-focused communicative tasks prioritize meaning exchange, spontaneous interaction, and confidence; immediate interruptions destroy the speaker's train of thought and heighten anxiety, making delayed group feedback at lesson closure far more effective.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Clearly distinguishes the instructional aims of accuracy (form) versus fluency (meaning).
     - 1 mark: Justifies immediate correction during controlled accuracy drills.
     - 1 mark: Justifies delayed error feedback during communicative fluency tasks.

7. **Model Answer:** The phonic method teaches the systematic correspondence between written letters and speech sounds, blending phonemes into words (suited to regular decodable words like *cat*, *frog*, *nest*, and *sun*). The look-and-say (whole-word) method teaches words as complete visual units without sounding out individual letters (suited to irregular high-frequency sight words like *said*, *was*, *one*, *there*, and *two*).
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines phonic blending with a suitable regular word example.
     - 1 mark: Defines look-and-say visual recognition with a suitable irregular sight-word example.
     - 1 mark: Contrasts the generative nature of phonics with the visual memorization of sight words.

8. **Model Answer:**
   - *Skimming Question (Gist):* "Read the first two sentences of the text in 20 seconds. What is Kot Diji: an ancient fortress, an agricultural farm, or a modern seaport?"
   - *Scanning Question (Specific Detail):* "Look quickly through the passage and write down the exact year in which archaeologists excavated the fort."
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Formulates a valid skimming question testing overall gist with a time constraint.
     - 1 mark: Formulates a valid scanning question targeting a specific date/fact.

9. **Model Answer:** Writing is fundamentally an expressive cognitive process of generating, organizing, and communicating ideas; overemphasizing surface penmanship and spelling penalizes children who have rich imaginative thoughts but developing motor skills. When teachers grade only calligraphy and spelling, pupils learn to write safe, bland, memorized sentences rather than developing authentic voice, critical thinking, and communicative competence.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies the expressive, cognitive, and communicative core of writing versus surface transcription.
     - 1 mark: Explains the negative washback effect (pupils writing memorized text to avoid spelling penalties).

10. **Model Answer:**
    1. Recipient designation and school address (The Headmaster / Principal, School Name, City).
    2. Date.
    3. Subject line (concise summary of request).
    4. Formal salutation (Respected Sir / Madam).
    5. Body of application (formal statement of reason, specific dates of requested leave, and polite request).
    6. Formal complimentary close (Yours obediently).
    7. Applicant identity (Student name, class, section, roll number).
    - **Marking Scheme (3.5 marks - 0.5 marks per component in correct sequence):**
      - Full marks for correctly ordering all seven components; deduct 0.5 for omitted or misplaced components.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Analysis and Redesign of a Grade 4 Listening Lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis of Instructional Flaws (0-3 marks):* Identifies absence of schema activation, cognitive overload of single rapid playback, lack of guided while-listening purpose, and unfair testing of raw memory.
  - *Application of Three-Stage Framework (0-3 marks):* Structures a comprehensive lesson plan containing clear Pre-Listening (schema prompt and vocabulary), While-Listening (staged gist and detail tasks), and Post-Listening (meaningful pair extension).
  - *Pedagogical Analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Critically evaluates how acoustic, linguistic, and working-memory constraints hinder second-language listeners, justifying how specific pre-listening scaffolding and active while-listening tasks resolve the pupils' comprehension breakdown.

#### ERQ 2: Evaluation of Choral Repetition versus Communicative Language Games
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Analysis of Choral Drilling (0-3 marks):* Identifies mechanical articulation benefits while exposing severe limitations: lack of communicative choice, zero information exchange, and false illusions of mastery.
  - *Analysis of Communicative Games (0-3 marks):* Demonstrates how Information-Gap and guessing games establish genuine communicative imperatives, active negotiation of meaning, and authentic listener response.
  - *Critical Evaluation and Synthesis (0-4 marks, **Evaluate**):* **Evaluate:** Weighs the practical constraints of large classrooms against modern second-language acquisition principles (e.g. Krashen's affective filter, Long's interaction hypothesis), making a well-reasoned, evidence-based judgment on how primary teachers should balance pronunciation practice with interactive oral fluency games.

#### ERQ 3: Analysis of Loud vs Silent Reading Breakdown and Lesson Design
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis of Round-Robin Reading (0-3 marks):* Explains the cognitive split between oral articulation anxiety and semantic interpretation, highlighting whole-class boredom and lack of individual pacing.
  - *Lesson Plan Architecture (0-3 marks):* Constructs a 30-minute reading lesson featuring pre-reading orientation, guided silent reading with skimming/scanning signposts, and post-reading pair synthesis.
  - *Cognitive and Diagnostic Analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Critically analyzes the distinct cognitive pathways of phonological decoding aloud versus silent internal meaning-making, justifying how silent reading fosters higher-order comprehension and reading autonomy.

#### ERQ 4: Process-Writing Lesson Plan for an Informal Letter
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Five-Stage Process Progression (0-3 marks):* Systematically articulates Pre-writing (brainstorming/mind maps), Drafting, Revising (content/clarity), Editing (mechanics/spelling), and Publishing (clean sharing).
  - *Genre and Structural Conventions (0-3 marks):* Accurately teaches all informal letter components (address, date, informal greeting, warm opening, conversational body, friendly close, signature).
  - *Rubric and Pedagogical Creation (0-4 marks, **Create**):* **Create:** Designs a robust peer-editing checklist and an analytic marking rubric that separates idea quality, organization, and tone from surface spelling and handwriting, justifying this separation to support developing writers.

#### ERQ 5: Four-Day Thematic Integration Across the Four Skills
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Thematic and Structural Coherence (0-3 marks):* Develops four coherent daily lesson outlines (Day 1: Listening; Day 2: Speaking; Day 3: Reading; Day 4: Writing) centered on "Conserving Water in Our School."
  - *Pedagogical Task Design (0-3 marks):* Employs authentic methods for each skill (three-stage listening, communicative speaking game, reading sub-skills, process paragraph writing).
  - *Cross-Skill Input-Output Analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Critically traces how receptive skills (listening on Day 1, reading on Day 3) provide comprehensible linguistic input, thematic schema, and sentence models that directly empower pupils to succeed in the corresponding productive skills (speaking on Day 2, writing on Day 4), demonstrating the organic unity of language learning.
