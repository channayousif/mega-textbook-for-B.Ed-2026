# Unit 5 Assessment - Lesson Planning

This end-of-unit assessment measures your mastery of lesson planning: why activities matter, the steps of a plan, activity-based techniques, and how to plan structural, prose, poetry, writing and grammar lessons. Complete the tasks before reviewing the model answers and marking guidance at the end of this page.

## Unit summary

Unit 5 established that a lesson plan is a sequence of steps with a purpose, and that the steps work together to move pupils from hearing language to using it.

- **Activities and outcomes.** Language is a skill, and a skill improves through use. Every lesson needs pupil activities because activities give pupils practice, give the teacher evidence, and hold attention. The teacher's talk should shrink as the lesson moves on, while pupil work grows from controlled practice to free production.
- **The steps of a plan.** A lesson plan has seven steps: learning outcomes, warm-up, presentation, practice, production, assessment and homework. Each step has a purpose, and the plan is a guide the teacher can adjust when the class needs it.
- **Activity-based approaches.** Pair work, group work, games and role play each suit different aims and class sizes. Group work needs four teacher moves: set up, monitor, support and report back.
- **Lesson types.** A structural lesson moves through presentation, controlled practice and free practice. A prose lesson moves from pre-reading to comprehension and extension. A poetry lesson moves from listening for enjoyment to personal response. A writing lesson follows the guided writing sequence of model, analyse, plan together, write and share. A functional grammar lesson begins with a context and moves from meaning to form to use.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* What is the main reason every English lesson needs pupil activities?
   - A) Activities keep the class quiet while the teacher prepares the next part.
   - B) Language is a skill, and a skill improves through use.
   - C) Activities make the textbook pages go further.
   - D) Activities are required by the head teacher for inspection.

2. *(Understand)* In a well-paced lesson, how does the balance of teacher talk and pupil activity usually change?
   - A) Teacher talk grows while pupil activity stays the same.
   - B) Teacher talk shrinks while pupil activity grows.
   - C) Teacher talk and pupil activity stay equal throughout.
   - D) Teacher talk disappears after the warm-up.

3. *(Remember)* Which step of a lesson plan states what the pupils will be able to do by the end of the lesson?
   - A) Warm-up
   - B) Presentation
   - C) Learning outcome
   - D) Homework

4. *(Apply)* A teacher's plan has a detailed presentation and homework but no assessment step. What is the most likely consequence?
   - A) The pupils will finish the lesson early.
   - B) The teacher will not know whether the outcome was reached.
   - C) The presentation will take too long.
   - D) The homework will be too difficult.

5. *(Understand)* Why is pair work often the best technique in a class of fifty pupils?
   - A) It needs no preparation at all.
   - B) It allows every pupil to speak at the same time.
   - C) It keeps the class completely silent.
   - D) It needs no clear outcome.

6. *(Apply)* A group-work task is running, but two pupils do all the work while the others watch. Which change to the set-up move would make the group fairer?
   - A) Give each pupil a role such as reader, writer or reporter.
   - B) Let the strongest pupil finish the task alone.
   - C) Give the watching pupils a separate worksheet.
   - D) Stop the group work and return to a lecture.

7. *(Remember)* Which stage of a structural lesson comes immediately after the presentation?
   - A) Free practice
   - B) Controlled practice
   - C) Warm-up
   - D) Homework

8. *(Understand)* Why does a poetry lesson begin with listening rather than silent reading?
   - A) Because the pupils cannot read yet.
   - B) Because a poem is meant to be heard for its sound and mood.
   - C) Because the teacher has no time to explain the poem.
   - D) Because silent reading is never useful.

9. *(Apply)* A teacher asks Grade 5 pupils to write an essay with no model and no planning frame. Which step of guided writing would help them most?
   - A) Share
   - B) Model
   - C) Check
   - D) Warm-up

10. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to teach the present perfect functionally. Which opening is most likely to help?
    - A) Write the rule on the board and ask pupils to memorise it.
    - B) Show a photo of a broken window and ask "What has happened?".
    - C) Give the pupils a list of past participles to learn.
    - D) Ask pupils to translate ten sentences into English.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Contrast an activity with an exercise the teacher performs, in two sentences.
2. *(Understand)* Explain the purpose of the warm-up step in a lesson plan, in two or three sentences.
3. *(Apply)* A lesson plan has no production step. Describe two consequences for the pupils, and add a production activity that would fix them.
4. *(Understand)* Explain why a teacher should give each pupil a role during group work, giving one example of a role.
5. *(Analyze)* Analyse how the teacher's role changes across the four moves of group work: set up, monitor, support and report back.
6. *(Apply)* A teacher presents the pattern "There is / There are" and then asks pupils to write a paragraph about their favourite place. Explain why this jump is too large, and design a controlled-practice task to bridge it.
7. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between a comprehension question and an inferential question in a prose lesson, giving one example of each.
8. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to teach a story lesson. Describe two ways the teacher could use a story map to support pupils before they write.
9. *(Understand)* Explain why a model text is important in a writing lesson, giving one example of how the teacher might use it.
10. *(Analyze)* Analyse how a functional grammar lesson differs from a grammar lesson that begins with a rule, and explain which one helps pupils use the form in their own speech.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* A Grade 6 teacher explains the present continuous for twenty minutes and then gives pupils a worksheet. Only a few pupils speak. Analyse the lesson using the idea of teacher talk and pupil activity, and construct a revised plan with an activity at every stage.
2. *(Create)* Choose one lesson type from this unit (structural, prose, poetry, paragraph, story, essay or grammar). Write a complete lesson plan for Grade 6 that shows all seven steps, with a time box and a pupil activity for each. Include a short note explaining how the activities move the pupils from controlled practice to free production.
3. *(Analyze)* A teacher plans a structural lesson on "There is / There are" with a good presentation but no free-practice stage. Analyse what the pupils will miss, and design a free-practice task that would give them ownership of the pattern.
4. *(Evaluate)* A school requires teachers to use the same lesson plan template for prose, poetry and grammar lessons. Evaluate whether one template can fit all three, using examples from this unit to justify your judgement.
5. *(Analyze)* Design a short sequence of two lessons for Grade 6, one on a prose text and one on a grammar point. Analyse how the two lessons would differ in their stages and activities, and explain how the prose lesson could provide the context for the grammar point.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Language is a skill, and a skill improves through use, which is the core reason activities matter.
2. B - In a well-paced lesson the teacher's talk shrinks as the pupils' activity grows from controlled to free work.
3. C - The learning outcome states what the pupils will be able to do by the end of the lesson.
4. B - Without an assessment step the teacher has no evidence of whether the outcome was reached.
5. B - Pair work lets every pupil speak at the same time, which is essential in a large class.
6. A - Giving each pupil a role shares the work and stops one pupil from doing everything.
7. B - Controlled practice follows the presentation, so the pupils use the pattern with support.
8. B - A poem is meant to be heard for its sound and mood, so listening comes before reading.
9. B - A model shows pupils what a good piece of writing looks like before they write.
10. B - A photo of a broken window gives the present perfect a visible meaning before the rule is stated.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model Answer:** An activity is a planned task in which the pupils use language to do something, such as ordering cards or asking a partner a question. An exercise the teacher performs is something the teacher does while the pupils watch or listen, such as explaining a rule on the board.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines an activity as pupil language use.
     - 1 mark: Contrasts it with teacher-centred work the pupils only watch or hear.

2. **Model Answer:** The warm-up settles the class, brings English into the room and activates vocabulary the pupils already know. It gives every pupil a first success so the lesson starts with confidence rather than confusion.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: States that the warm-up settles the class and activates prior knowledge.
     - 1 mark: Explains that it gives pupils an early success or a reason to use English.

3. **Model Answer:** Without a production step, pupils never use the language for a real purpose, so it stays controlled and they cannot own it. They also leave the lesson without practising fluency. A production activity such as "tell your group about your own morning routine" would give them that free use.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies that pupils never use the language freely.
     - 1 mark: Explains that the language stays controlled and is not owned.
     - 1 mark: Provides a realistic production activity.

4. **Model Answer:** Giving each pupil a role makes sure the work is shared and stops the strongest pupil from doing everything. Roles also give every pupil a reason to take part. For example, one pupil can be the reader, one the writer, one the timekeeper and one the reporter.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Explains that roles share the work and prevent one pupil dominating.
     - 1 mark: Gives a concrete example of a role.

5. **Model Answer:** In the set-up move the teacher explains the task, the time and the roles, so the pupils know what to do. In the monitor move the teacher steps back and listens, noting who is stuck. In the support move the teacher helps with a question rather than an answer. In the report-back move the teacher brings the class together and corrects errors for everyone. The role moves from director to observer to helper to evaluator.
   - **Marking Scheme (4 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Describes the set-up move as giving the task and roles.
     - 1 mark: Describes monitoring as listening and noting problems.
     - 1 mark: Describes support as helping without taking over.
     - 1 mark: Describes report back as sharing and correcting for the whole class.

6. **Model Answer:** The jump is too large because the pupils have only seen the pattern; they have not had any controlled practice to make it automatic. A controlled-practice task would be to hold up flashcards of rooms and ask "What is there?", so every pupil answers "There is a ..." or "There are ..." several times with support.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Explains that the pupils have not had controlled practice.
     - 1 mark: Explains why the jump is too large.
     - 1 mark: Designs a controlled-practice task with repetition.

7. **Model Answer:** A comprehension question asks for something stated in the text, such as "What did the boy do?". An inferential question asks the pupil to use clues from the text to work out something not directly stated, such as "Why did he help the goat?".
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines a comprehension question and gives a valid example.
     - 1 mark: Defines an inferential question and gives a valid example.

8. **Model Answer:** A story map shows the parts of a story, such as the setting, characters, problem and solution, so pupils can see the shape before they write. The teacher can also use the map to ask pupils what happens next, which helps them plan their own story.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Explains that the map shows the story parts.
     - 1 mark: Explains how the teacher uses it to support planning or prediction.

9. **Model Answer:** A model text shows pupils what a good piece of writing looks like, so they know what to aim for. The teacher can read the model aloud and then ask the class to name its parts, such as the topic sentence and the supporting sentences.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Explains that the model shows the target.
     - 1 mark: Gives a concrete example of how the teacher uses it.

10. **Model Answer:** A functional grammar lesson begins with a context that gives the form a meaning, then helps pupils notice the form, and finally asks them to use it in a task. A rule-first lesson begins with the rule and asks pupils to practise the form without a context. The functional lesson helps pupils use the form in their own speech because they have seen when it is needed.
    - **Marking Scheme (4 marks):**
      - 1 mark: Describes the functional lesson's order (context, form, use).
      - 1 mark: Describes the rule-first lesson's order (rule, practice).
      - 2 marks: Explains why the functional lesson leads to real use, with a clear comparison.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Analysis and redesign of a teacher-talk lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis of the lesson (0-3 marks):* Identifies that the teacher talks for most of the lesson, that pupil activity is limited to a worksheet, and that few pupils speak.
  - *Application of the talk-activity balance (0-3 marks):* Constructs a revised plan with an activity at every stage and a shrinking amount of teacher talk.
  - *Pedagogical analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze*):* **Analyze:** Critically evaluates why the original lesson left pupils unable to use the language, and justifies how the revised activities give every pupil practice and the teacher evidence.

#### ERQ 2: Complete lesson plan for one lesson type
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Seven steps (0-3 marks):* All seven steps present with a time box and a pupil activity.
  - *Suitability to the lesson type (0-3 marks):* The activities suit the chosen lesson type and the outcome is clear.
  - *Justification (0-4 marks, **Create*):* **Create:** Designs a coherent plan and justifies how the activities move the pupils from controlled practice to free production, showing a clear understanding of the lesson type.

#### ERQ 3: Analysis of a missing free-practice stage
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis (0-3 marks):* Explains that without free practice the pattern stays controlled and the pupils cannot own it.
  - *Task design (0-3 marks):* Designs a free-practice task that requires pupils to use the pattern in a new situation.
  - *Pedagogical analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze*):* **Analyze:** Analyses how the presentation, controlled practice and free practice work together, and why the missing stage matters for fluency.

#### ERQ 4: Evaluation of a single template for prose, poetry and grammar
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Comparison (0-3 marks):* Identifies at least two differences between the three lesson types, such as listening before reading in poetry and context before rule in grammar.
  - *Use of examples (0-3 marks):* Supports the argument with concrete examples from the unit.
  - *Critical evaluation (0-4 marks, **Evaluate*):* **Evaluate:** Weighs the benefits of a shared template against the need for lesson-type-specific activities, and reaches a justified judgement about whether the school's requirement is reasonable.

#### ERQ 5: Analysis of a prose-then-grammar sequence
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Two-lesson sequence (0-3 marks):* Designs a coherent prose lesson and a grammar lesson that fit together.
  - *Differences (0-3 marks):* Explains how the stages and activities differ between the two lessons.
  - *Cross-lesson analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze*):* **Analyze:** Analyses how the prose text can supply the context for the grammar point, showing how the receptive reading provides the language and situation that the grammar lesson then uses.
