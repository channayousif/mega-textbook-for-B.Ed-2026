## A real classroom situation

Nazim teaches Grade 7 in a school in Khairpur. He plans a grammar lesson on the present perfect. He writes the rule on the board, gives two example sentences, and asks the pupils to write ten sentences using the rule. The pupils write the sentences correctly. The next day he asks them to describe something that happened to them, and they use the simple past every time.

Nazim is frustrated. His colleague says, "The pupils learned the form, but they never learned when to use it. A grammar lesson must start with the meaning, not the rule."

## Explanation

A grammar lesson teaches a form such as a tense or a structure. The functional approach, which you met in Unit 4, says that grammar is learned best through meaning and use, not through rules alone. A grammar lesson therefore begins with a situation, not a rule on the board.

### The functional approach in a grammar lesson

Every grammar point has three sides. The **form** is how it is built: "have" plus a past participle. The **meaning** is what it says: an action that happened at an unknown time or that matters now. The **use** is when a speaker chooses it: to talk about experience, to report news, or to describe a recent event. A grammar lesson should touch all three, and the order matters. The teacher presents the form inside a meaningful context, helps the pupils understand the meaning, and then sets a task where the pupils must choose the form for themselves.

### A filled grammar-lesson plan

Study this plan for Grade 7 on the present perfect. Notice that the rule comes last, not first.

| Stage | Time | Teacher action | Pupil action | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Context | 6 min | Show a photo of a classroom with broken windows; ask "What has happened?" | Guess and answer "Someone has broken the window" | Meaning |
| Presentation | 8 min | Write two or three pupil answers on the board and highlight the form "has + past participle" | Notice the form and repeat the sentences | Form |
| Guided practice | 8 min | Give pairs a picture and ask them to make three sentences with "has" or "have" | Make sentences in pairs | Form and meaning |
| Task | 10 min | Ask groups to describe three changes in their school this year | Describe the changes and prepare to report | Use |
| Check | 4 min | Ask two groups to report and correct errors gently | Report to the class and listen | Use |

### Worked example: a grammar point in context

The broken-window photo does three jobs at once. It gives the grammar a meaning the pupils can see, it makes the target form useful because the pupils need it to describe the photo, and it avoids a long explanation. If Nazim had started with the rule, the pupils would have practised the form without ever choosing it. The photo forces them to choose: "Someone has broken the window" is the natural thing to say.

### Common misconceptions

One wrong idea is that grammar is learned by memorising rules. A pupil who can recite the rule for the present perfect may still use the wrong tense in speech. Another wrong idea is that a functional approach means no grammar teaching. The functional approach teaches grammar harder, not softer: the pupils must understand the meaning, choose the form and use it correctly. Celce-Murcia (1991) reminds teachers that grammar should be presented in a meaningful context and then practised through tasks that require the pupil to make choices.

## Activity: Turn a rule into a context

**Work in pairs.** Time allocation: approximately 20 minutes.

Read this grammar point: the past continuous, "was/were + verb-ing", used for an action in progress at a time in the past.

1. Describe a picture or a short situation that could present the meaning of the form.
2. Write two model sentences the teacher would say in that context.
3. Design a guided-practice task that keeps the language controlled.
4. Design a task where pupils must choose the form to describe their own past.
5. Exchange your plan with another pair. Check that the context makes the meaning clear and that the final task requires the pupils to choose the form, not just repeat it.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name the three sides of a grammar point and give one example of each for the present perfect.
2. *(Understand)* Explain why a grammar lesson should begin with a context rather than a rule.
3. *(Apply)* A teacher teaches the present perfect by writing the rule and asking pupils to memorise it. Describe two changes that would make the lesson functional, and say what each change achieves.

## Summary

A functional grammar lesson begins with a context that gives the form a meaning. The teacher then helps the pupils notice the form, practise it under control, and finally use it in a task where they must choose it for themselves. The three sides of a grammar point are form, meaning and use, and a good lesson touches all three.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask your cooperating teacher whether you can plan and teach a short functional grammar lesson. Choose a grammar point from the textbook, find a picture or situation that gives it meaning, and ask the teacher to observe how many pupils use the form correctly in the task.

## Summative task

Plan a complete grammar lesson for Grade 7 on the present continuous. The plan must show the stages, the context you will use, the teacher action, the pupil action and the focus on form, meaning or use at each stage. Then write a short note explaining how the final task requires pupils to choose the form.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1 mark) | Proficient (2 marks) | Exemplary (3-4 marks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Context and stages** | The grammar point is presented as a rule with no context. | A context is used and the stages are in order. | The context makes the meaning visible and the stages are paced and purposeful. |
| **Form, meaning and use** | Only the form is addressed. | The meaning is clear and the form is practised. | The lesson moves from meaning to form to use, and the task requires the pupil to choose the form. |
| **Justification** *(Analyze)* | Says the context is "interesting" without explaining its role. | Explains how the context helps pupils understand the grammar. | **Analyze:** Analyses how the context, the controlled practice and the free task work together to move the pupil from understanding the form to choosing it. |
