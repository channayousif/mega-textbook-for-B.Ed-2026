# Teaching of Grammar

Grammar is often the part of an English lesson that pupils fear most. Many teachers present a rule, drill its form, and hope pupils will use it in speech and writing. Yet pupils who can recite a rule often cannot use the structure when they need it. This unit introduces the functional approach to teaching grammar, which treats grammar as a resource for making meaning rather than a list of rules to memorise. It shows how to analyse any grammar point into its form, meaning and use, how to present grammar inductively or deductively, and how to move pupils from controlled practice to real communication.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Explain the functional approach to teaching grammar and analyse a grammar point into its form, meaning and use. *(Understand; SLO:EFPG-401-4-1)*
2. Distinguish between inductive and deductive presentation and choose the right one for a given class and grammar point. *(Understand, Apply; SLO:EFPG-401-4-1)*
3. Design grammar practice that moves from controlled exercises to meaningful communicative tasks. *(Apply, Analyze; SLO:EFPG-401-4-1)*
4. Analyse a described grammar lesson or exercise and judge whether it teaches grammar as a resource for meaning. *(Analyze; SLO:EFPG-401-4-1)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 3 of this course, especially the grammar-translation and communicative approaches. You should already understand how those two methods treat grammar, because the functional approach builds on the communicative approach and corrects the weaknesses of grammar-translation.

## How to use this unit

This unit has one topic, but it is a complete teaching cycle. Study it in order: read the classroom situation, work through the explanation with its worked example, try the collaborative activity, and check your understanding before moving on. Then attempt the unit assessment. The topic file ends with a self-assessment checklist and a task for your practicum school, so complete those before you finish.
