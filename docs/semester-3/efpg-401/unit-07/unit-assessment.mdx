# Unit 7 Assessment - Assessment

This end-of-unit assessment measures your understanding of objective-type and subjective-type tests in the teaching of English. Complete the summative assessment tasks before reviewing the model answers and marking guidance at the end of this page.

## Unit summary

Unit 7 established that a teacher constructs a test from the lesson outcomes, not from the textbook page. An **objective-type test** gives one fixed answer for each item, which makes it quick and fair to mark; the four common item types are multiple choice, true/false, matching and fill in the blank. A good objective item has a clear stem, exactly one fixed key and plausible distractors, and it can measure understanding and application as well as memory. A **subjective-type test** asks the pupil to produce a longer answer, which the marker judges against a rubric; its three parts are the task, the model answer and the marking rubric. An analytic rubric breaks an answer into criteria and gives each a mark range, so two markers reach similar results and marking becomes fair and consistent. Objective and subjective tests complement each other: objective items check facts and rules quickly, while subjective tasks show how well pupils can produce language.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Which objective item type gives a stem followed by four options, exactly one of which is the key?
   - A) True/false
   - B) Matching
   - C) Multiple choice
   - D) Fill in the blank

2. *(Understand)* A multiple-choice item asks: "Which of the following is true?" followed by four unrelated facts. What is the main fault?
   - A) It has too many options.
   - B) The stem does not carry a single question.
   - C) The options are too short.
   - D) It has no distractors.

3. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to test whether Grade 5 pupils can use the past tense of regular verbs. Which item best matches this outcome?
   - A) Define the past tense in one sentence.
   - B) Fill in the blank: "Yesterday, Bilal _____ (walk) to school."
   - C) List three regular verbs from the textbook.
   - D) Match five verbs with their past tense forms.

4. *(Remember)* Which of these is a rule for writing true/false items?
   - A) Each statement should be clearly true or clearly false.
   - B) Use words such as "always" and "never" to make the statement stronger.
   - C) Write more false statements than true ones.
   - D) Each statement should contain a new word the pupils have not met.

5. *(Apply)* A teacher writes a fill-in-the-blank item: "_____ is the capital of Pakistan." What is the main fault?
   - A) The gap is at the start of the sentence.
   - B) There is more than one possible answer.
   - C) The sentence is too short.
   - D) The item tests a fact, not grammar.

6. *(Remember)* What is the correct order of steps for writing a multiple-choice item?
   - A) Stem, outcome, key, distractors, check
   - B) Outcome, stem, key, distractors, check
   - C) Key, stem, distractors, outcome, check
   - D) Distractors, stem, key, outcome, check

7. *(Understand)* Why are plausible distractors important in a multiple-choice item?
   - A) They make the item easier for the pupils.
   - B) They allow the teacher to give partial credit.
   - C) They make the item measure understanding rather than guessing.
   - D) They allow more than one fixed answer.

8. *(Apply)* A teacher wants to test whether Grade 6 pupils can write a short paragraph about their village. Which test type is most suitable?
   - A) A multiple-choice item
   - B) A true/false item
   - C) A subjective paragraph-writing task
   - D) A matching item

9. *(Remember)* Which of these is the best definition of a subjective-type test?
   - A) A test with one fixed answer for each item
   - B) A test where the pupil produces a longer answer judged against a rubric
   - C) A test made up only of true/false items
   - D) A test that measures only memory

10. *(Apply)* A teacher gives the task "Write a paragraph of six to eight sentences about a book fair." Which additional material would most improve the fairness of marking?
    - A) A longer task
    - B) A model answer and an analytic rubric
    - C) More time for the test
    - D) A list of vocabulary words

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between the stem and the key in a multiple-choice item.

2. *(Understand)* Give two rules for writing true/false items and explain why each rule matters.

3. *(Analyze)* A teacher writes this item: "Which of the following is true? A) Karachi is a big city. B) Lahore is a big city. C) Islamabad is a big city. D) All of the cities are in Pakistan." Analyse what is wrong with the item and rewrite it.

4. *(Apply)* Write one fill-in-the-blank item and one matching item for the outcome "Pupils can name five things in a market."

5. *(Understand)* Explain what an analytic rubric is and name its two main parts.

6. *(Understand)* Give two reasons why a model answer helps a marker mark an essay fairly.

7. *(Analyze)* A teacher marks the same essay 12 out of 20 in the morning and 16 out of 20 after lunch. Analyse what causes this difference and explain how a rubric would reduce it.

8. *(Apply)* For the task "Write a paragraph about your village", write a model answer of four sentences.

9. *(Analyze)* Analyse two differences between objective and subjective tests on what they measure and how reliable their marking is.

10. *(Understand)* Explain why a subjective test is more suitable than an objective test for measuring writing.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* A Grade 5 teacher gives a test with these three items: (1) "Which of the following is true? A) A noun is a naming word. B) A verb is an action word. C) An adjective describes a noun. D) All of the above." (2) "True or false: The sun rises in the west." (3) "Fill in the blank: _____ is the first month of the year." Analyse the faults in each item and explain how each fault affects what the test measures.

2. *(Create)* Construct a ten-item objective test for Grade 5 English on the topic "The Market". Include five multiple-choice, two true/false, two matching and one fill-in-the-blank items. Write a short note explaining how each item matches a stated outcome.

3. *(Analyze)* A teacher marks a set of essays and finds the marks vary widely between two markers. Analyse the causes of the variation and explain how a mark scheme would reduce it.

4. *(Create)* Build an analytic rubric for the task "Write a short paragraph about my favourite festival". The rubric must have at least four criteria with mark ranges and a short description of each level. Total 15 marks.

5. *(Create)* Design a short English test for Grade 6 with both objective and subjective items. Include five objective items, one paragraph-writing task and one essay task. For the subjective tasks, provide a model answer and an analytic rubric. Write a note explaining how the two test types complement each other.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. C - Multiple choice gives a stem and four options with exactly one fixed key.
2. B - The stem "Which of the following is true?" does not carry a single question; the options are unrelated facts.
3. B - The fill-in-the-blank item asks the pupil to use the past tense in a sentence, which matches the outcome.
4. A - A true/false statement must be clearly true or clearly false; "always" and "never" make statements false or misleading.
5. A - The gap at the start of the sentence is a fault; the missing word should be clear from the sentence.
6. B - The order is outcome, stem, key, distractors, check.
7. C - Plausible distractors make the item measure understanding rather than guessing.
8. C - A paragraph-writing task measures writing, which objective items cannot capture.
9. B - A subjective-type test asks the pupil to produce a longer answer judged against a rubric.
10. B - A model answer and an analytic rubric make the marking fair and consistent.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model Answer:** The stem is the statement or question that carries the problem; the key is the one fixed right option.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines the stem as the statement or question that carries the problem.
     - 1 mark: Defines the key as the one fixed right option.

2. **Model Answer:** Two rules: each statement must be clearly true or clearly false; avoid words such as "always" and "never"; write a balance of true and false statements. The first rule matters because a vague statement can be argued either way; the second because "always" and "never" usually make a statement false and give away the answer.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Names the rule that each statement must be clearly true or false.
     - 1 mark: Names the rule about avoiding "always" and "never".
     - 1 mark: Explains why at least one rule matters.

3. **Model Answer:** The stem "Which of the following is true?" does not carry a single question, and the options are all true, so the item has no single key. A better item is: "Which city is the capital of Pakistan? A) Karachi B) Lahore C) Islamabad D) Peshawar."
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies the stem fault.
     - 1 mark: Identifies the options fault.
     - 1 mark: Rewrites the item with one fixed key.

4. **Model Answer:** Fill-in-the-blank: "I bought a _____ from the market." (basket, scale, crate, sack or counter). Matching: Column A lists five market things (basket, scale, crate, sack, counter) and Column B lists five uses or pictures to match.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Writes a valid fill-in-the-blank item with one fixed answer.
     - 1 mark: Writes a valid matching item with equal columns.
     - 1 mark: Both items match the outcome "Pupils can name five things in a market".

5. **Model Answer:** An analytic rubric breaks an answer into criteria and gives each criterion a mark range. Its two main parts are the criteria and the mark ranges (or level descriptions).
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Defines an analytic rubric as criteria with mark ranges.
     - 1 mark: Names the criteria and the mark ranges as the two main parts.

6. **Model Answer:** Two reasons: a model answer shows the marker what a good answer looks like; it helps the marker apply the same standard to every answer; it also helps pupils know what is expected.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Gives one valid reason.
     - 1 mark: Gives a second valid reason.

7. **Model Answer:** The difference comes from the marker's mood, fatigue or general impression. A rubric makes the judgement explicit and consistent, so the same essay gets a similar mark at any time of day.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies the cause as the marker's general impression or condition.
     - 1 mark: Explains how a rubric reduces the difference.
     - 1 mark: Explains why the result is more consistent.

8. **Model Answer:** "My village is near the river. There are fields of wheat and rice. My father works in the fields. I go to school in the village."
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Writes a clear paragraph about a village.
     - 1 mark: Writes four sentences.

9. **Model Answer:** Two differences: objective tests measure facts and rules while subjective tests measure writing and thinking; objective marking is quick and consistent while subjective marking needs a rubric to be reliable.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Names one difference in what they measure.
     - 1 mark: Names one difference in reliability.
     - 1 mark: Explains the reliability difference with reference to the rubric.

10. **Model Answer:** Writing needs the pupil to produce language; an objective item can only ask the pupil to choose or fill a gap, so it cannot show how well the pupil organises and expresses ideas.
    - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
      - 1 mark: Explains that writing needs the pupil to produce language.
      - 1 mark: Explains the limit of objective items for measuring writing.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Analysis of Three Faulty Objective Items
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Stem fault identified (0-3 marks):* Identifies that "Which of the following is true?" does not carry a single question.
  - *Options fault identified (0-3 marks):* Identifies that the options are all true or that the item has no single key.
  - *Effect on measurement (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Explains how each fault affects what the test measures, distinguishing between recognition and understanding, and why a vague stem rewards guessing.

#### ERQ 2: Objective Test Construction on "The Market"
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Test construction (0-3 marks):* Constructs ten items with the required item types and a clear outcome for each.
  - *Outcome match (0-3 marks):* Matches each item to a stated outcome and writes items that test the level the outcome names.
  - *Item type justification (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies the choice of each item type against the outcome it tests, explaining why another type would measure less.

#### ERQ 3: Causes of Variation in Essay Marking
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Causes of variation (0-3 marks):* Identifies causes such as general impression, fatigue, mood or unclear criteria.
  - *Mark scheme design (0-3 marks):* Proposes a mark scheme with criteria and mark ranges that two markers can apply.
  - *Reliability analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Explains how the mark scheme makes marking reliable and consistent, and why the same essay would receive a similar mark from different markers.

#### ERQ 4: Analytic Rubric for a Festival Paragraph
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Criteria and mark ranges (0-3 marks):* Provides at least four criteria with mark ranges that add to 15.
  - *Level descriptions (0-3 marks):* Describes what earns full and low marks for each criterion.
  - *Fairness justification (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Justifies the choice of criteria against the task and explains how the rubric keeps marking fair and consistent.

#### ERQ 5: Integrated English Test with Objective and Subjective Items
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Test design (0-3 marks):* Designs a test with five objective items, one paragraph-writing task and one essay task.
  - *Model answers and rubrics (0-3 marks):* Provides a model answer and an analytic rubric for each subjective task.
  - *Complementarity analysis (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* **Analyze:** Explains how objective items check facts and rules quickly while subjective tasks show how well pupils can produce language, and why the two types give a fuller picture of learning together.
