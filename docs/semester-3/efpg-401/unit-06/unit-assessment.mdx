# Unit 6 Assessment - A.V. Aids in Teaching of English

This end-of-unit assessment measures your understanding of audio-visual aids in the teaching of English. Complete the summative assessment tasks before reviewing the model answers and marking guidance at the end of this page.

## Unit summary

Unit 6 established that audio-visual aids make English visible and audible, and that the right aid is the one that makes the new language concrete for the pupils in front of you.

- **Why aids matter (6.1):** An audio-visual aid holds attention, builds meaning, supports memory and invites participation. It does not need money or electricity; a chart, a flash card or a real object often teaches better than a screen.
- **Low-cost aids (6.2):** Charts, models, pictures, flash cards, toys and real objects suit crowded classrooms and schools with no power. A short flash-card routine - show, say, repeat, use in a sentence, review - moves new words into long-term memory.
- **Audio aids (6.3):** Radio, audio recordings and the language laboratory let pupils hear English from voices other than the teacher's. One recording with a task before it, a check during it and a follow-up after it teaches more than several aids used without a plan.
- **Video and film (6.4):** Television and video bring real situations into the room. A video teaches only when the teacher sets a preview task, checks understanding, leads a discussion and gives a follow-up activity.
- **Projected aids (6.5):** Slides, filmstrip, the overhead projector and the multimedia projector make one idea visible to the whole class. The rule is one idea, a few words and one picture; a crowded slide replaces the teacher instead of supporting her.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Which of the following is an audio-visual aid that costs almost nothing?
   - A) A chart drawn on a sheet of brown paper
   - B) A multimedia projector
   - C) A smartboard
   - D) A DVD player

2. *(Understand)* Why does a real object help a pupil remember a new English word better than a translation written on the board?
   - A) It is cheaper than a textbook.
   - B) It gives the pupil something concrete to link the word to.
   - C) It saves the teacher time during the lesson.
   - D) It allows the class to finish the lesson early.

3. *(Remember)* Which aid is a small card with a word or a picture on it, held up one at a time for the class to read?
   - A) A flash card
   - B) A wall chart
   - C) A filmstrip
   - D) A clay model

4. *(Apply)* A Grade 5 teacher in a school with no electricity wants her pupils to learn the names of six vegetables. Which aid is the best choice?
   - A) A chart of vegetables drawn on brown paper
   - B) A video clip about a vegetable market
   - C) A multimedia projector with a slide
   - D) A DVD player with a recorded lesson

5. *(Remember)* What is the main advantage of a language laboratory?
   - A) It plays the radio to the whole class.
   - B) It needs no electricity.
   - C) Every pupil can speak at the same time without disturbing the others.
   - D) It replaces the teacher during the lesson.

6. *(Apply)* A teacher plays a recording of a market conversation and asks the pupils to count how many things the customer buys. Which listening skill is this task mainly assessing?
   - A) Listening for the main idea
   - B) Listening for specific detail
   - C) Listening for the speaker's attitude
   - D) Listening for a story's moral

7. *(Understand)* Why does a video help pupils understand spoken English better than an audio recording of the same scene?
   - A) The pupils can see facial expressions, gestures and the setting.
   - B) The video is always shorter than the recording.
   - C) The pupils do not need to listen carefully.
   - D) The video has no sound to distract them.

8. *(Apply)* A teacher shows a two-minute clip and pauses it to ask, "What will the boy do next?" Which stage of the video lesson is this?
   - A) Preview task
   - B) Watch
   - C) Check understanding
   - D) Follow-up

9. *(Remember)* Which projected aid needs no computer and works with the classroom lights on?
   - A) A slide projector
   - B) A filmstrip
   - C) An overhead projector
   - D) A multimedia projector

10. *(Apply)* A teacher's slide carries a long paragraph copied from the textbook. What is the best change she can make?
    - A) Read the paragraph aloud more slowly.
    - B) Replace it with one idea, a few words and one picture.
    - C) Ask the pupils to copy the paragraph into their notebooks.
    - D) Show the paragraph on two slides instead of one.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Explain two reasons why an audio-visual aid helps pupils learn English.

2. *(Analyze)* A teacher says, "Audio-visual aids need money and electricity." Analyse why this statement is only partly true, giving two low-cost aids as examples.

3. *(Understand)* Describe two low-cost aids and give one English-lesson use for each.

4. *(Analyze)* Analyse why a real object is often a stronger teaching aid than a picture of the same object.

5. *(Understand)* Explain how a recorded voice helps pupils who hear only their teacher's voice all day.

6. *(Analyze)* Analyse why one recording with a clear task teaches more than three aids used without a plan.

7. *(Understand)* Describe the five stages of a video lesson in order.

8. *(Analyze)* Analyse why a two-minute clip with a preview task and a follow-up teaches more than a ten-minute film without them.

9. *(Understand)* Explain the difference between an overhead projector and a multimedia projector in terms of power needs and best use.

10. *(Analyze)* Analyse why a slide with six words and one picture teaches better than a slide with a long paragraph.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* In a Grade 4 lesson on "Things in the Kitchen", the teacher holds up a steel cup, a spoon and a pot. She asks, "What is this?" and the pupils answer in English. Analyse how the real objects help the pupils learn the words, referring to attention, meaning, memory and participation, and explain one thing the teacher could add to make the lesson even better.

2. *(Analyze)* A Grade 5 teacher plans a lesson on "A Visit to the Post Office". She makes a large chart of a post office counter and uses it to teach six words, but she never asks the pupils to use the words in a sentence. Analyse what works well in the lesson and what is missing, and describe two activities that would turn the chart into speaking practice.

3. *(Analyze)* A Grade 4 teacher plays a three-minute recording of a family visiting a zoo. She gives no task before playing it and asks only one question afterwards. Analyse why the pupils remember little, and redesign the lesson using the three stages of a listening lesson, explaining what each stage does.

4. *(Analyze)* A Grade 5 teacher plays a five-minute cartoon. The pupils enjoy it, but when she asks what the boy forgot at home, nobody can answer. Analyse why the video did not become teaching, and design a five-stage video lesson for the same cartoon that includes a preview task, a check, a discussion and a follow-up.

5. *(Evaluate)* You are teaching Grade 4 English in a school with no electricity and very little money. Choose three aids for a lesson on "A Market Day", one from the low-cost aids, one from the audio aids and one from the video or projected aids. Justify each choice against the school's resources and the lesson's outcome, and explain why the three aids work better together than any one of them alone.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. A - A chart drawn on brown paper is an audio-visual aid that costs almost nothing; the other options need equipment and power.
2. B - A real object gives the pupil something concrete to link the word to, which supports memory better than a translation.
3. A - A flash card is a small card with a word or a picture, held up one at a time.
4. A - A chart of vegetables drawn on brown paper needs no electricity and suits a crowded classroom.
5. C - A language laboratory lets every pupil speak at the same time without disturbing the others.
6. B - Counting how many things the customer buys tests listening for specific detail, not the main idea.
7. A - A video shows facial expressions, gestures and the setting, which support understanding.
8. C - Pausing to ask "What will the boy do next?" is the check-understanding stage.
9. C - An overhead projector needs no computer and works with the classroom lights on.
10. B - Replacing a long paragraph with one idea, a few words and one picture makes the slide a teaching aid.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model Answer:** An audio-visual aid holds the pupils' attention because a picture or an object is more interesting than a line of words on the board. It also builds meaning, because a new English word becomes tied to something the pupil can see or hold rather than to a translation.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies attention as a benefit.
     - 1 mark: Identifies meaning (or memory or participation) as a benefit.

2. **Model Answer:** The statement is partly true because some aids, such as a multimedia projector, do need money and electricity. It is false as a general rule because many aids cost nothing and need no power. A chart drawn on brown paper and a set of flash cards cut from old envelopes both teach effectively in a classroom with no electricity.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: States that the statement is partly true and identifies a paid aid.
     - 1 mark: States that the statement is false as a general rule.
     - 1 mark: Gives two low-cost aids as examples.

3. **Model Answer:** A chart is a large sheet with pictures or words, pinned to the wall for the whole class; it can teach the names of fruits in a Grade 3 lesson. A real object, such as a mango or a key, is something the pupils can hold; it can teach the names of things in the kitchen in a Grade 4 lesson.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Describes one aid and its use.
     - 1 mark: Describes a second aid and its use.

4. **Model Answer:** A real object is stronger because the pupil can hold it, look at it from all sides and connect the English word to a thing they already know. A picture shows only one view and cannot be touched, so the word and the thing remain more separate in the pupil's mind.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: States that a real object can be held and examined.
     - 1 mark: Explains that the word becomes linked to a real thing.
     - 1 mark: Contrasts this with the limited view a picture gives.

5. **Model Answer:** A recorded voice gives pupils a model of English that is not their teacher's, with a different pitch, speed and accent. It can also be repeated as many times as the pupils need, which a live teacher cannot do without losing the flow of the lesson.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Identifies a different voice or accent as a benefit.
     - 1 mark: Identifies repeatability as a benefit.

6. **Model Answer:** One recording with a clear task gives the pupils a reason to listen and a way to check their understanding. Three aids used without a plan compete for attention and leave the pupils with no single idea to hold on to, so they remember little of any of them.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: States that a task gives pupils a reason to listen.
     - 1 mark: States that a check confirms understanding.
     - 1 mark: Explains that unplanned aids compete and leave nothing clear.

7. **Model Answer:** The five stages are: preview task, watch, check understanding, discuss, and follow-up.
   - **Marking Scheme (2 marks):**
     - 1 mark: Lists three or four stages in order.
     - 2 marks: Lists all five stages in the correct order.

8. **Model Answer:** A two-minute clip with a preview task gives the pupils a reason to watch, and a follow-up lets them use the language themselves. A ten-minute film without these stages gives the pupils pictures but no reason to listen, so they remember the story but not the English.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: States that a preview task gives a reason to watch.
     - 1 mark: States that a follow-up turns watching into language use.
     - 1 mark: Contrasts this with a film that has no task or follow-up.

9. **Model Answer:** An overhead projector needs electricity but no computer; the teacher writes on a transparent sheet and the class works with the lights on. A multimedia projector needs electricity, a screen and a computer or other device, and is best used for pictures, slides and video.
   - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
     - 1 mark: States that the OHP needs no computer.
     - 1 mark: States that the OHP works with the lights on.
     - 1 mark: States that the multimedia projector needs a device and is best for pictures and video.

10. **Model Answer:** A slide with six words and one picture carries one idea and lets the teacher do the explaining. A slide with a long paragraph forces the pupils to read instead of listen, and the teacher ends up reading the slide aloud, so the slide replaces her instead of supporting her.
    - **Marking Scheme (3 marks):**
      - 1 mark: States that a clear slide carries one idea.
      - 1 mark: Explains that the teacher does the explaining.
      - 1 mark: Explains the harm of a crowded slide.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Analysis of a Real-Object Lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Identification of benefits (0-3 marks):* Names and explains how the real objects support attention, meaning, memory and participation.
  - *Use of classroom evidence (0-3 marks):* Uses the described objects (cup, spoon, pot) and the teacher's question to support the analysis.
  - *Pedagogical analysis* (0-4 marks, **Analyze**): **Analyze:** Explains how each benefit works in the lesson and justifies one improvement with a clear reason.

#### ERQ 2: Analysis of a Chart Lesson and Speaking Practice
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *What works (0-3 marks):* Identifies the strength of the chart in making the post office scene visible.
  - *What is missing (0-3 marks):* Identifies the absence of speaking practice and explains why it matters.
  - *Redesign and justification* (0-4 marks, **Analyze**): **Analyze:** Proposes two speaking activities that build on the chart and justifies how each one turns the chart into language use.

#### ERQ 3: Redesign of an Audio Lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis (0-3 marks):* Explains why the pupils remember little (no task, no check, no follow-up).
  - *Three-stage redesign (0-3 marks):* Structures a clear before-while-after listening lesson around the zoo recording.
  - *Pedagogical analysis* (0-4 marks, **Analyze**): **Analyze:** Explains what each stage does for the pupils' listening and why the stages work together.

#### ERQ 4: Redesign of a Video Lesson
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Diagnosis (0-3 marks):* Explains why the cartoon stayed entertainment (no preview, no check, no follow-up).
  - *Five-stage redesign (0-3 marks):* Structures a complete five-stage video lesson for the same cartoon.
  - *Pedagogical analysis* (0-4 marks, **Analyze**): **Analyze:** Explains how each stage turns watching into listening and speaking, with a clear link to the outcome.

#### ERQ 5: Evaluation of Aids for a School with No Electricity
- **Analytic Rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Selection of three aids (0-3 marks):* Chooses one low-cost aid, one audio aid and one video or projected aid, each suitable for a school with no electricity.
  - *Justification against resources and outcome (0-3 marks):* Justifies each choice against the school's resources and the lesson's outcome.
  - *Integrative evaluation* (0-4 marks, **Evaluate**): **Evaluate:** Weighs the three aids together and justifies why the combination teaches better than any single aid, with a clear judgement.
