# The Economic and Political Systems of Islam

Earlier units examined the foundational beliefs of Islam, the primary sources of Islamic guidance in the Holy Qur'an and Sunnah, and the individual disciplines of worship (*Ibadat*) and morality (*Akhlaq*). This unit expands those ethical foundations into the collective structures of human society: the economic order that sustains human livelihood, and the political administration that governs communal life.

In Islam, economic and political activities are not separated from moral accountability. Economic life is governed by divine stewardship (*amanah*), in which wealth must be earned through lawful (*halal*) effort, purified through institutional *Zakat*, and protected from the exploitative injustice of interest (*riba*). Similarly, political authority is an administrative trust rather than an absolute privilege, requiring collective consultation (*Shura*), impartial justice (*adl*), and uncompromising protection for the civil, cultural, and religious rights of minorities. For future teachers in Pakistan, understanding these institutional systems is essential for modeling transparent school administration, fostering collaborative pupil participation, and cultivating inclusive citizenship in diverse communities.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Distinguish between lawful (*halal*) and prohibited (*haram*) economic practices, and explain how the concept of divine trusteeship (*amanah*) governs property rights and commercial transactions in Islam.
2. Analyze the institutional mechanisms of *Zakat* and voluntary charity (*sadaqah*) in redistributing wealth and alleviating systemic poverty.
3. Evaluate the theological and economic arguments prohibiting interest (*riba*), contrasting debt-based financial extraction with equitable, risk-sharing commercial partnerships.
4. Explain the principles of consultative governance (*Shura*), institutional accountability (*muhasabah*), and the constitutional and Islamic protections guaranteed to religious minorities in Pakistan.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds directly upon concepts developed in earlier course units:

- The concept of Islam as a comprehensive code of life (*din*) governing social, financial, and civic affairs (Unit 1).
- The legal authority and methodology of deriving ethical principles from the Holy Qur'an and Sunnah (Unit 2).
- The spiritual and social dimensions of worship, particularly the communal obligation of *Zakat* (Unit 3).
- The foundations of the Islamic social system, family responsibilities, and mutual civic rights (Unit 4).
