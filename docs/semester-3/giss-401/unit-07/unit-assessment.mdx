# Islamic Ethics in Professional Life - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

A teaching post is an *amanah*, a trust. The Qur'an describes the Trust that human beings took on (33:72), commands that trusts be rendered to whom they are due (4:58), and praises those who are attentive to their trusts and promises (23:8). It also gives a test for a good worker: "the strong and the trustworthy" (28:26), which for a teacher means competence joined to honesty. A teacher's trust reaches four circles: learners, colleagues, parents and the institution. Each circle is owed something specific: prepared lessons and fair marking, cooperation and fair sharing of work, honest reports and refusal of favours, punctuality and care of school property.

Professional ethics is not separate from religious ethics. Islam is a complete code of life, and its moral teaching (*akhlaq*) reaches work as much as worship. The three signs of a hypocrite, lying, breaking promises and betraying trusts (*Sahih al-Bukhari* 33), are all failures that can happen at work. Seen as part of faith, professional ethics gains a deeper motive, accountability to Allah, and a wider reach than any written code, while lawful school rules are kept as commitments (17:34).

In Islam every person is a guardian, responsible for what is in their care (*Sahih al-Bukhari* 893). So the class teacher, the coordinator and the head teacher all lead. Leadership is a trust held for service, like a shepherd's care for the flock, not authority for its own sake. Five values guide it: justice (16:90; 4:135), consultation (42:38; 3:159), trustworthiness and competence (28:26), mercy and gentleness (3:159), and accountability.

Pupils often learn more from what a teacher does than from what the teacher says, especially when the two disagree. The Prophet (peace be upon him) is the excellent pattern (33:21) of great moral character (68:4), and the Qur'an condemns saying what one does not do (61:2-3; 2:44). Role modelling often happens in small daily acts: admitting a mistake, marking fairly, greeting everyone with respect, keeping promises. When a duty is unclear, a teacher can work through five steps: identify the issue and who is affected, consult principles, consult people, act, and reflect.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All named pupils, teachers and schools below are invented teaching cases.

The unit has one topic, so every item addresses Topic 7.1. MCQs 1-4 and RRQs 1-4 focus on a teacher's ethical responsibilities, MCQs 5-7 and RRQs 5-7 on leadership, and MCQs 8-10 and RRQs 8-10 on role modelling. Extended task 4 asks you to write your own code of professional conduct, and task 5 links this unit to Units 1, 3 and 6. ### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose A, B, C or D. Each item is worth 1 mark.

1. The Arabic word *amanah* means: *(Remember)*
   - A) A prayer said before work
   - B) A trust, and the quality of being trustworthy
   - C) A written contract of employment
   - D) Kindness shown to the poor
2. "The best one you can hire is the strong and the trustworthy" is from: *(Remember)*
   - A) 28:26
   - B) 4:58
   - C) 61:2
   - D) 42:38
3. According to *Sahih al-Bukhari* 33, which of these is one of the three signs of a hypocrite? *(Remember)*
   - A) Missing a voluntary fast
   - B) Praying alone instead of in congregation
   - C) Betraying a trust when trusted
   - D) Forgetting a lesson plan at home
4. A teacher draws a full salary but regularly leaves class fifteen minutes early. Which Qur'anic image fits this conduct most closely? *(Apply)*
   - A) The Trust offered to the mountains (33:72)
   - B) Those who give less than due when they measure (83:1-3)
   - C) Consultation among believers (42:38)
   - D) The Prophet's great moral character (68:4)
5. In the hadith "All of you are guardians and responsible for your wards", the Arabic word for guardian, *ra'i*, is also the word for: *(Remember)*
   - A) A judge
   - B) A shepherd
   - C) A merchant
   - D) A soldier
6. The Qur'an tells the Prophet (peace be upon him) to "consult them in the matter" in: *(Remember)*
   - A) 2:44
   - B) 33:21
   - C) 3:159
   - D) 23:8
7. A head teacher shares out invigilation duties by the number of free periods each teacher has, and gives the same rule to her own relative on the staff. Which value does this mainly show? *(Apply)*
   - A) Justice
   - B) Consultation
   - C) Mercy
   - D) Role modelling
8. The verse "why do you say what you do not do?" (61:2) is most directly about: *(Understand)*
   - A) Speaking politely to elders
   - B) Avoiding long speeches in class
   - C) The gap between a person's words and actions
   - D) Keeping secrets about other people
9. A pupil points out an error on the board. Which response by the teacher best models truthfulness? *(Apply)*
   - A) Telling the pupil to sit down and correcting it later
   - B) Saying the error was a test to see who was paying attention
   - C) Ignoring the comment and moving to the next question
   - D) Thanking the pupil, admitting the mistake and correcting it with the class
10. A teacher is unsure whether to accept a parent's gift after the results. In the five-step ethical decision process, what comes straight after identifying the issue? *(Remember)*
    - A) Consulting principles from the Qur'an, Sunnah and school rules
    - B) Accepting the gift and reflecting later
    - C) Asking the pupil's classmates what they think
    - D) Reporting the parent to the police

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each in the length stated. Each item is worth 3 marks.

1. In two or three sentences, explain why a teaching post is an *amanah*, using one Qur'anic reference. *(Understand)*
2. Explain, in three sentences, what the two qualities in 28:26 mean for a teacher, and why a teacher needs both. *(Understand)*
3. A colleague says, "Punctuality and marking are professional matters, not religious ones." In three or four sentences, analyse what this view leaves out, using *Sahih al-Bukhari* 33. *(Analyze)*
4. Name the four circles of a teacher's *amanah*, and give one specific duty owed to two of them. *(Understand)*
5. Explain, in two or three sentences, why a class teacher is a leader in the Islamic sense. *(Understand)*
6. Explain, in three sentences, the difference between leadership as authority and leadership as service, using one Qur'anic verse or hadith. *(Understand)*
7. A head teacher decides alone to move the school's annual sports day to the week before board examinations. In three or four sentences, analyse which two leadership values this decision ignores and what the effect might be. *(Analyze)*
8. Using 33:21 and 61:2-3, explain in three sentences why role modelling is central to Islamic teaching. *(Understand)*
9. A teacher lectures on respect, but shouts at the cleaner in front of the class. In three or four sentences, analyse what the pupils are likely to learn and why. *(Analyze)*
10. Give three daily actions, each tied to a different value, by which a teacher can model good character for pupils. *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer in 400-600 words. Each task is worth 12 marks.

1. **Professional and religious ethics.** "A good professional code is enough. A teacher does not need religion to be ethical at work." Analyse this claim from an Islamic point of view. Show what a written code and Islamic ethics share, what Islamic ethics adds, and why lawful school rules still matter. Use at least three Qur'anic verses or hadith and one school example. *(Analyze)*
2. **A head teacher's decisions.** A head teacher of a government high school in Sukkur makes three decisions in one month: she gives the best timetable slots to her friends, she spends the school council's funds on new furniture for her office without telling the council, and she excuses a teacher whose child is in hospital from duties for a week. Evaluate each decision against the Islamic values of educational leadership, reach an overall judgement on her leadership, and suggest practical steps she could take to restore trust. *(Evaluate)*
3. **What pupils really learn.** "Pupils learn from what teachers say, not what they do." Evaluate this statement. Use the Qur'anic teaching on role modelling, at least three specific classroom or school examples, and the research reported by Tambak and Sukenti (2020). Reach a clear judgement. *(Evaluate)*
4. **My code of professional conduct.** Write a short personal code of professional conduct for your first year as a teacher, grounded in Islamic values. It should have eight to ten commitments, covering all four circles of a teacher's *amanah* and at least one commitment about leadership and one about role modelling. Each commitment must be specific enough to check, and must name the value and the Qur'anic verse or hadith it rests on. End with a short paragraph explaining how you will check whether you are keeping your code. *(Create)*
5. **Belief, character and the teacher.** Using what you learned in Unit 1 (belief in Akhirat and Islam as a complete code of life), Unit 3 (the moral system of Islam) and Unit 6 (the role of the teacher in Islam), analyse how these earlier teachings explain why a Muslim teacher should keep the *amanah* of the profession. Then evaluate which one of them you think gives the strongest motive for ethical teaching, and justify your choice. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** *Amanah* is a trust placed in someone's care and also the quality of being trustworthy. A contract (C) can be one kind of trust, but the word is wider; A and D are unrelated.
2. **A.** 28:26 is said by a young woman about Prophet Musa (peace be upon him). 4:58 is about rendering trusts, 61:2 about saying what one does not do, and 42:38 about consultation.
3. **C.** The three signs are lying when speaking, breaking promises and betraying a trust. A, B and D are not among them.
4. **B.** Taking a full salary and giving less work is giving short measure, the conduct condemned in 83:1-3.
5. **B.** *Ra'i* means shepherd; the hadith uses the shepherd's care for the flock as the picture of responsibility.
6. **C.** 3:159 tells the Prophet (peace be upon him) to consult his companions. 2:44 is about forgetting oneself, 33:21 about the excellent pattern and 23:8 about trusts and promises.
7. **A.** One clear rule for everyone, including her own relative, is justice (4:135). Consultation, mercy and role modelling are not the main point of this decision.
8. **C.** The verse condemns a gap between word and action, which is the heart of role modelling.
9. **D.** Admitting the mistake openly models truthfulness. A hides the error, B adds a lie and C avoids it.
10. **A.** The steps are identify, consult principles, consult people, act and reflect.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Parents, pupils and the school each place something in the teacher's care: children, their learning and the hours paid for. The Qur'an commands that trusts be rendered to whom they are due (4:58), so teaching well is giving back what was entrusted. **Marks (3):** 1 for naming what is entrusted; 1 for a correct reference; 1 for linking the reference to teaching.
2. **Model:** "Strong" means competent: knowing the subject and how to teach it. "Trustworthy" means honest in using that competence, for example in marking and attendance. A teacher needs both, because skill without honesty can harm pupils and honesty without skill cannot teach them. **Marks (3):** 1 for each quality explained; 1 for why both are needed.
3. **Model:** The view treats Islam as worship only, but Islam is a complete code of life. The hadith lists lying, breaking promises and betraying trusts as signs of hypocrisy (*Sahih al-Bukhari* 33), and late arrival with a false excuse or careless marking are exactly these failures. So punctuality and marking are religious duties as well as professional ones. **Marks (3):** 1 for stating what the view leaves out; 1 for using the hadith accurately; 1 for applying it to punctuality or marking.
4. **Model:** Learners, colleagues, parents and the institution. For example: to learners, fair marking by the same standard for everyone; to the institution, arriving on time for the hours paid for. **Marks (3):** 1 for the four circles; 1 for each specific duty (two required).
5. **Model:** The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that all of us are guardians and responsible for what is in our care (*Sahih al-Bukhari* 893). A class teacher has forty pupils in her care and will be asked about them, so she is a leader of that class. **Marks (3):** 1 for the hadith; 1 for the idea of responsibility for those in one's care; 1 for applying it to the class teacher.
6. **Model:** Leadership as authority sees the leader's job as giving orders to be obeyed. Leadership as service sees authority as a trust held for the good of others, like a shepherd caring for the flock (*Sahih al-Bukhari* 893). The Qur'an joins leadership to gentleness and consultation (3:159). **Marks (3):** 1 for each view; 1 for a correct supporting reference.
7. **Model:** The decision ignores consultation (42:38), because the teachers and perhaps parents who know the examination pressure were not asked. It also weakens accountability or justice to pupils, whose preparation time is cut. The likely effect is a poorly attended sports day, stressed pupils and staff who feel ignored. **Marks (3):** 1 for each value correctly identified with a reason; 1 for a reasoned effect.
8. **Model:** The Prophet (peace be upon him) is presented as the excellent pattern to follow (33:21), so Muslims learn their faith by following an example. The Qur'an condemns saying what one does not do (61:2-3). So a teacher's conduct, not only the teacher's words, is how values are taught. **Marks (3):** 1 for each reference correctly used; 1 for the conclusion.
9. **Model:** The pupils are likely to learn that respect is owed only to important people, and that adults do not mean what they say. When words and actions disagree, pupils tend to believe the action. The lecture has taught the opposite of what was intended (61:2-3). **Marks (3):** 1 for what pupils learn; 1 for why action outweighs words; 1 for a supporting reference or a clear link to role modelling.
10. **Model:** Any three, each with a different value, for example: admitting "I do not know; I will find out" (truthfulness); marking every pupil by the same rubric (justice); returning work on the promised day (keeping promises); greeting the *chowkidar* warmly (respect). **Marks (3):** 1 for each action correctly tied to a different value.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks; a fluent answer with weak reasoning cannot.
