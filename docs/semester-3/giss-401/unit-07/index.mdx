# Islamic Ethics in Professional Life

This is the last content unit of the course, and it brings the course back to you as a teacher. In earlier units you studied the beliefs of Islam, its moral system and what it teaches about education. This unit asks what those teachings mean on an ordinary working day: when a lesson has to be prepared, a paper has to be marked, a parent offers a favour, or a head teacher has to share out duties. A school runs on trust. This unit is about keeping it.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the meaning of *amanah* and why a teaching post is a trust, with Qur'anic references. *(Understand; SLO:GISS-401-7-1)*
- Analyse a school situation to show which of a teacher's duties to learners, colleagues, parents and the institution are at stake. *(Analyze; SLO:GISS-401-7-1)*
- Evaluate a school leadership decision against the Islamic values of justice, consultation, trustworthiness, mercy and accountability. *(Evaluate; SLO:GISS-401-7-1)*
- Explain why pupils learn values from a teacher's conduct, and evaluate a teacher's conduct as a role model. *(Evaluate; SLO:GISS-401-7-1)*
- Write a short personal code of professional conduct grounded in Islamic values. *(Create; SLO:GISS-401-7-1)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds on two earlier units. From Unit 3 you need the moral system of Islam (*akhlaq*): truthfulness, justice, honesty, patience and character building. From Unit 6 you need the place of education in Islam and the role of teachers and learners. It also uses the idea of Islam as a complete code of life and belief in accountability in the Hereafter from Unit 1. Before you begin, write down one teacher from your own schooling whose conduct you still remember, and one thing that teacher did (not said) that taught you something.

## How to use this unit

The unit has one topic. It begins with a trainee teacher's first week at a school in Larkana, then explains the ideas and shows a worked example before asking you to use them. Qur'anic verses are cited by surah and ayah, for example (4:58) means Surah 4, verse 58. Short quotations are from the Saheeh International translation on Quran.com, and hadith are cited by collection and number. Look up each verse in a full translation, and with a tafseer where you can.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions without looking back, then check your answers against the explanation. The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) has practice questions, including the task of writing your own code of professional conduct, and at the end a public self-study guidance section. Try the questions before you read that guidance. In formal course assessment the default weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative; your teacher will tell you which work is collected.
