# Sources of Islamic knowledge: assessment and review

## Unit summary

Islamic knowledge needs identifiable sources and careful explanation. The Qur'an is the foundational source of guidance. The Sunnah is the authoritative Prophetic example, and Hadith reports help preserve knowledge of it. In the common Sunni introductory framework studied here, Ijma and Qiyas depend on revealed guidance. Ijma is not an ordinary group vote. Qiyas is reasoning through a relevant shared cause, not a comparison based on appearance. Neither permits a class to reverse revealed guidance because another result is convenient.

A reference is a route by which another reader can check a claim. Qur'anic references identify surah and ayah. Hadith references identify a collection and a report, with attention to numbering. A teacher must also distinguish the source's teaching from the school method proposed to apply it. A reading-group routine can reflect a commitment to justice without being specified in a verse. A gentle correction can reflect a Prophetic value without being an exact quotation from a report.

The four selected Qur'anic themes were knowledge (96:1–5), justice (4:135), kindness to parents (17:23–24) and honest, upright speech (33:70). Their school connections need reasons. Reading a passage in context comes before proposing an application. Translation and tafsir support understanding; a teacher's paraphrase must be labelled as their explanation. A passage's direct meaning and a wider classroom connection should remain distinguishable.

Hadith study distinguishes isnad, the chain of transmission, from matn, the report's text. Sahih, hasan and daif are introductory reliability categories, not judgments a trainee can assign from a message's tone. Weak does not automatically mean fabricated; missing documentation on a card means its attribution still needs checking. Good intentions do not remove the need for evidence, nor do they let a teacher claim to know a pupil's hidden intention.

Across the unit, the practical habit is the same: identify the claim, locate its source, explain what the evidence supports, state the proposed application and acknowledge a limit. This supports accurate teaching and respectful disagreement. It also shows when a question needs a qualified scholar rather than a classroom decision.

## Summative assessment

Attempt the bank before reading the final section. For self-study, answer the MCQs without the book, then use sources to check your reasoning. For RRQs, keep within the stated bounds. For ERQs, plan a reasoned response using the supplied scenario, rather than listing source names. Qur'anic and hadith references may be consulted when drafting extended responses. Do not reproduce long translations.

The bank samples Topics 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3. MCQs carry 1 mark each; RRQs carry 4 marks each; ERQs carry 10 marks each. These are practice marks, totalling 100. A course teacher may select tasks under the default 60% summative and 40% formative course weighting; that weighting is distinct from the distribution of marks within this practice bank. The guidance below supports public self-study and is separate from any protected LMS question bank.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which source is the foundational revelation in the framework studied in this unit? *(Remember)*
   - A) An agreement of a school committee
   - B) The Qur'an revealed to the Prophet
   - C) A jurist's comparison of two cases
   - D) A community's familiar local custom

2. Which description best distinguishes Hadith from Sunnah in this unit? *(Understand)*
   - A) Hadith is a school rule; Sunnah is a jurist's opinion.
   - B) Hadith is a group vote; Sunnah is a reported chain.
   - C) Hadith is a report; Sunnah is the Prophetic example.
   - D) Hadith is a translation; Sunnah is the Arabic Qur'an.

3. Four teachers agree to label library books. How should their agreement be described from the information given? *(Apply)*
   - A) A school decision requiring an ordinary explanation
   - B) A verified consensus of qualified Muslim jurists
   - C) A new revelation addressing a modern problem
   - D) A report established through a chain of narrators

4. A learner outlines Qiyas using a newly named intoxicating substance. Which feature is relevant to the simplified comparison in the topic? *(Apply)*
   - A) The language used on its container
   - B) The district in which it is sold
   - C) The material used for its bottle
   - D) The intoxicating effect in the cases

5. What does the reference 96:1–5 identify? *(Remember)*
   - A) Pages 1 to 5 in every printed Qur'an
   - B) Surah 96, ayat 1 through 5
   - C) Five chapters beginning with chapter 96
   - D) Hadith 96 in the first five collections

6. A trainee writes a reading-group routine inspired by 4:135. Which label best describes the routine? *(Apply)*
   - A) The exact Arabic wording of the ayah
   - B) A report's chain of transmission
   - C) A school application of a principle
   - D) A new chapter of Qur'anic commentary

7. Which reference was selected for the direct theme of kindness and respectful conduct towards parents? *(Remember)*
   - A) Al-Isra 17:23–24
   - B) Al-Ahzab 33:70
   - C) An-Nisa 4:135
   - D) Al-'Alaq 96:1–5

8. Which part of a hadith identifies its chain of transmission? *(Remember)*
   - A) The school application
   - B) The translator's paragraph
   - C) The reported matn
   - D) The recorded isnad

9. A quotation card has no reference. What is the best initial description of its attribution? *(Apply)*
   - A) Confirmed sahih because the message is helpful
   - B) Not yet verified from the evidence on the card
   - C) Confirmed daif because a reference is missing
   - D) Deliberately fabricated because the design is modern

10. What does daif mean at this introductory level? *(Understand)*
   - A) A report written in a language other than Arabic
   - B) A report that concerns conduct rather than worship
   - C) A report that fails one or more acceptance conditions
   - D) A report deliberately invented in every instance

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, explain why the Qur'an and Sunnah ground Ijma and Qiyas in the framework studied here. Include one limit on dependent reasoning. *(Understand)*
2. In three sentences, explain why agreement among classmates is not enough to establish Ijma. State what kind of agreement the technical term concerns. *(Understand)*
3. A learner compares drinks because both have green bottles. In four sentences, explain the weakness in this comparison, identify the relevant cause in the unit's intoxicant example and state the example's textual limit. *(Analyze)*
4. In three sentences, distinguish a Qur'anic verse, a translation and a teacher's application. Use a reading-lesson example. *(Understand)*
5. In three sentences, explain the common time-based Makki/Madani distinction and why chapter order is not a simple revelation timeline. *(Understand)*
6. A teacher gives a relative extra marks without checking the work. In four sentences, explain how 4:135 challenges the decision and identify a practical correction that the verse does not itself specify. *(Apply)*
7. In four short lines, pair the four selected themes with their references and give a possible school connection for each. Keep the direct meaning of 17:23–24 visible. *(Apply)*
8. In three sentences, distinguish isnad and matn and explain why identifying them is not the same as grading a report. *(Understand)*
9. In four sentences, distinguish sahih, hasan and daif at this introductory level. Include one reason daif should not automatically be called fabricated. *(Understand)*
10. A pupil struggles during a lesson. A trainee claims the pupil has a bad intention and cites Bukhari 1. In four sentences, analyze what the trainee can observe, what she cannot know and a more suitable response. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A group says that its unanimous vote allows it to ignore a clear revealed teaching. Another learner says any similarity between two cases is Qiyas. In 350–450 words, analyze both claims. Distinguish the four sources, explain the authority of dependent reasoning, and show how scholars use Ijma or Qiyas when the Qur'an and Sunnah do not state a ruling directly (analyzing either the Companions' consensus on compiling the Qur'an or the four-part analogy extending the Friday prayer trade prohibition to other contracts through a shared cause). Contrast this with cases covered by general texts, and explain the limits of dependent methods. This task focuses on Topic 2.1. *(Analyze)*
2. Design a 15-minute classroom discussion on knowledge and justice for a fictional Sindh primary class. In 350–450 words, use 96:1–5 and 4:135 accurately, separate their meanings from your proposed methods, and justify one change that helps a struggling reader without favouritism. Include how you would check that the discussion helped. This task focuses on Topic 2.2. *(Create)*
3. A colleague wants to display an attractive forwarded saying with no reference. In 350–450 words, evaluate the proposal, explain the jobs of isnad, matn and scholarly grading, and propose a checked alternative based on Bukhari 1 or Muslim 2593. Explain how its application could guide correction without pretending to know children's intentions. This task focuses on Topic 2.3. *(Evaluate)*
4. A school stores donated books. A teacher calls its new digital register "a revealed requirement", calls staff agreement "Ijma", and adds an unsourced saying to the display. In 400–500 words, analyze the three claims and write an improved explanation. Integrate all three topics using a relevant Qur'anic reference, the source/method distinction and responsible hadith attribution. *(Analyze)*
5. A trainee must prepare a lesson about honest speech for a class whose pupils have different levels of reading confidence. In 400–500 words, create and defend a short source-checking and teaching plan. Integrate 33:70 with an established hadith from the unit, distinguish revelation from human school choices, and state how uncertainty and differences of interpretation will be handled respectfully. Include observable evidence of learning and one revision you might make. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

These are public self-study notes. Equivalent accurate wording and well-justified applications are acceptable. Do not award marks merely for reproducing religious phrases. Reward accurate meaning, relevant evidence, clear reasoning and appropriate limits. A classroom plan is not a religious ruling. Where a response raises a disputed legal question, it should identify the need for qualified advice rather than invent a settlement.

1. B. The Qur'an is the foundational revelation in the framework taught.
2. C. Hadith is a report; Sunnah is the Prophetic example it helps preserve.
3. A. The stated evidence establishes a school agreement, not technical Ijma.
4. D. The relevant shared cause in the simplified example is intoxication.

6. C. The routine is the teacher's application, not the verse's wording.
7. A. The direct relationship in 17:23–24 concerns treatment of parents.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. The Qur'an is the foundational revelation and the Sunnah is the authoritative Prophetic example. Ijma and Qiyas depend on revealed guidance in this introductory framework. They do not license a group to reverse a clear teaching. **4 marks:** 1 for the Qur'an's role; 1 for the Sunnah's role; 1 for dependence; 1 for the stated limit. Do not require a claim that all Muslim legal traditions organise authority identically.
2. A class vote establishes the view of that class. Technical Ijma concerns agreement of qualified Muslim jurists of an era after the Prophet on a religious legal matter. Evidence for such consensus cannot be replaced by the class's own unanimity. **4 marks:** 1 for ordinary agreement; 1 for qualified jurists; 1 for the era and legal matter; 1 for explaining the evidential difference. Mentioning consultation without these distinctions is not enough for full marks.
3. Bottle colour does not explain the relevant prohibition. The worked example identifies the intoxicating effect as the shared cause. Its four parts are the established case, ruling, new case and shared cause. Qur'an 5:90 and Muslim 2003a also provide general textual coverage, so this example does not prove that analogy alone establishes the ruling. **4 marks:** 1 for irrelevant appearance; 1 for cause; 1 for structure; 1 for textual limit.
4. The Arabic verse is the Qur'anic text. A translation expresses its meaning in another language. A teacher may connect the justice principle in 4:135 with fair marking or suitable reading support, but the chosen procedure is an application. **4 marks:** 1 each for text and translation; 1 for a sensible application; 1 for explicitly separating application from wording. A claim that the verse prescribes a particular grouping method loses the last mark.
5. The common time-based distinction uses the Hijrah: Makki before it and Madani after it. It is not simply a check of physical city boundaries. The chapter sequence is not the chronological sequence of revelation, and a surah may include passages from different occasions. **4 marks:** 1 for each side of the time distinction; 1 for avoiding a city-only definition; 1 for the ordering point. No detailed chronology is required.
6. Qur'an 4:135 challenges favouritism even where close relationships are involved. The teacher should compare the actual script with the stated criteria and correct the record if needed. The reference supports justice, while a particular marking grid or review procedure is a human tool. **4 marks:** 1 for the principle; 1 for identifying favouritism; 1 for an evidence-based correction; 1 for distinguishing procedure from the verse. Publicly shaming the relative is not required.
7. Knowledge, 96:1–5: value and support learning to read. Justice, 4:135: mark work without favouritism. Kindness to parents, 17:23–24: first explain that direct duty, then carefully connect respectful speech with an invented classroom conversation. Honest speech, 33:70: record attendance or a book count accurately. **4 marks:** 1 per correct reference/theme/application combination. A wider kindness connection earns credit only if it preserves the passage's direct relationship.
8. Isnad is the chain through which the report is transmitted; matn is its reported text. Seeing these parts helps a learner understand what is being checked. Grading needs specialist examination of transmission, narrators and comparison, not merely recognition of the two labels. **4 marks:** 1 for each definition; 1 for the distinction from grading; 1 for a relevant specialist check. A learner need not name technical Arabic terms beyond the two requested.
9. Sahih meets the required acceptance conditions; hasan is accepted with a lower level of precision in the basic explanation; daif fails one or more conditions. A gap or weakness does not itself establish deliberate invention. **4 marks:** 1 for each grade; 1 for distinguishing weak from fabricated. Accept a careful note that scholars may differ and supporting routes can matter, but do not award extra marks for terminology unconnected to the distinctions.
10. The trainee can observe hesitation, errors or incomplete work. She cannot directly know the pupil's hidden intention. Bukhari 1 supports reflection on intention, not mind-reading. She should ask a supportive question, model the task or adjust help and check progress. **4 marks:** 1 for observable evidence; 1 for the limit; 1 for explaining misuse of the report; 1 for a suitable alternative. Different helpful responses are acceptable if they avoid an unsupported accusation.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task is marked out of 10 using four criteria. For a 3-mark criterion: 3 means accurate and reasoned with specific evidence; 2 means mostly accurate with a limited gap; 1 means relevant naming without adequate explanation; 0 means absent or fundamentally mistaken. For a 2-mark criterion: 2 means complete and suitable; 1 means partly developed; 0 means absent or mistaken. Apply these levels separately to each criterion, rather than giving one impression mark for the whole answer.

1. **Source distinctions (0–2):** distinguishes revelation, Prophetic example and dependent scholarly reasoning. **Authority analysis (0–3, Analyze):** explains why unanimity among classmates is not technical consensus and cannot override revealed guidance. **Non-direct-ruling analysis (0–3, Analyze):** analyzes how scholars use Ijma or Qiyas when revelation does not state a ruling directly, demonstrating either the four parts and shared cause (as in extending the Friday trade prohibition to leasing) or the consensus basis (as in compiling the Qur'an), and contrasting this with cases covered by general texts. **Limits and clarity (0–2):** avoids presenting an exercise as a new ruling and explains the claims in plain language. Full marks require reasons, not only a list of source names.
2. **Passage meanings (0–2):** accurately identifies both references and their teachings. **Lesson design (0–3, Create):** offers a feasible 15-minute discussion with a clear sequence and an accessible way for pupils to participate. **Justification (0–3, Analyze):** connects support for the struggling reader with learning and freedom from favouritism, while distinguishing teacher choices from scripture. **Evidence and care (0–2):** gives an observable learning check and avoids humiliating labels. Accept different grouping arrangements when their reasons and limitations are clear.
3. **Transmission knowledge (0–2):** accurately explains isnad, matn and the role of assessment. **Evaluation (0–3, Evaluate):** weighs the missing evidence, explains why appearance or forwarding count is insufficient and gives proportionate checking steps without guessing a grade. **Alternative and reasoning (0–3):** uses a traceable unit report and connects it with a suitable correction strategy. **Limits and observation (0–2):** avoids claims about hidden intentions and identifies what could be checked in class. A blanket claim that every unsourced message is fabricated cannot earn full evaluation marks.
4. **Integrated source accuracy (0–2):** uses a relevant Qur'anic reference and distinguishes the major source categories. **Claim analysis (0–3, Analyze):** separately diagnoses the register's false status, the misuse of Ijma and the unsupported attribution. **Improved explanation (0–3):** gives a coherent account of trust, an ordinary school decision and a checked or postponed saying, using all three topics. **Practical limits (0–2):** identifies human choices and a suitable referral for unresolved religious questions. A response that repairs only the register does not fully integrate the task.
5. **Evidence plan (0–2):** locates 33:70 and a relevant established unit hadith. **Teaching plan (0–3, Create):** provides a coherent, accessible sequence for pupils with differing reading confidence. **Defence of choices (0–3, Analyze/Evaluate):** explains how source meaning supports the plan without claiming the modern methods are revealed, and gives respectful handling of uncertainty. **Learning and revision (0–2):** specifies observable evidence and a justified possible change. Do not require one preferred medium: an oral retelling, labelled card or paired explanation can all be suitable if the reasoning is sound.

After marking, choose one lost mark caused by an unsupported claim and revise that sentence with a reference, a clearer connection or an explicit limit. Choose a second improvement concerning the proposed classroom action. This keeps source accuracy and teaching quality connected. Do not improve a response merely by adding more source names: each reference should support a stated part of the explanation.
