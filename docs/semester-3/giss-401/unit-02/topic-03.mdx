# Topic 2.3 - Hadith and Sunnah

## A real classroom situation

A trainee in Larkana receives a message containing a saying attributed to the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him. It has an attractive border and asks readers to forward it to ten people. The trainee wants to place it on a classroom wall because its message sounds helpful. Her cooperating teacher asks for the collection, number and scholarly assessment before they decide how to use it. The attractive design answers none of those questions.

## Explanation

### Importance of Hadith

A **hadith** is a report of the Prophet's sayings, actions or approvals. **Hadith**, as a field of study, concerns those reports and their transmission. The **Sunnah** is the authoritative Prophetic example. Reports are important because a Muslim learner today did not directly observe the Prophet. Learning his example depends on transmitted evidence rather than on making up a story that seems suitable for a lesson.

Topic 2.1 explained that the Qur'an directs obedience to the Messenger ([4:59](https://quran.com/4/59)). Hadith reports help learners understand that guidance in practical life. [Sahih al-Bukhari 631](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:631), for example, records instruction through observed prayer. It shows why learning a practice can involve watching and following an example, not simply hearing the name of the practice. The Qur'an and Sunnah belong together in the framework studied in this unit.

Two reports will anchor our classroom examples. [Sahih al-Bukhari 1](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1) relates actions and intentions; its original account includes migration. [Sahih Muslim 2593](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2593) teaches the value of gentleness. Read each report in its source. A short school explanation may focus on one implication, but it should not erase the original subject or present a new classroom sentence as an exact Prophetic quotation.

**Worked example: intention and a reading lesson.** A trainee offers extra reading practice. She first asks herself whether she intends to help learners or to display their mistakes so that she looks knowledgeable. This is a personal reflection connected with Bukhari 1. She then prepares appropriate materials and checks whether pupils learn. A good intention does not make an ineffective activity effective, nor does an observer have access to everything in another person's heart.

This last limit matters in teaching. A child who answers slowly may be shy, tired, unsure of the language or still thinking. It would be unjustified for the teacher to announce that the child has a bad intention. The hadith invites serious attention to intention; it does not supply a way to read pupils' minds. A responsible application distinguishes what the teacher can observe from what she cannot know.

**Worked example: gentleness and correction.** A pupil misreads a word. The trainee pauses, models the sound, allows another attempt and gives specific feedback. She explains that this is her application of the value of gentleness in Muslim 2593. She has not claimed that the report specifies this exact reading routine. Gentleness can coexist with a clear expectation: the pupil still practises and receives correction. Humiliation is not needed to make the expectation clear.

Accurate attribution is also part of the importance of Hadith. [Sahih Muslim, Introduction, narration 3](https://sunnah.com/muslim:3) warns against intentionally attributing falsehood to the Prophet. For a trainee, one practical implication is to check before placing a saying under his name. A statement may be wise, but wisdom alone does not establish who said it. The title "Prophetic saying" is a claim about origin as well as meaning.

Return to the forwarded message. The teacher can appreciate its intention to encourage good conduct and still pause its use. The source might be found later, or the wording might turn out to be a paraphrase, an ordinary proverb or an unsupported attribution. These possibilities require different labels. Keeping the message off the wall while checking it does not mean rejecting every moral reminder; it means taking the attribution seriously.

### Classification of Hadith

One reason to classify reports is to distinguish levels of confidence in their transmission. A report is not made equally reliable by being repeated many times in a messaging group. Repetition of the same unsourced image is different from independent, traceable evidence. Nor does a report become unreliable just because a trainee has not heard it before. Both acceptance and rejection require appropriate evidence.

Two parts of a report are central. The **isnad**, also called sanad, is the chain of people through whom it is transmitted. The **matn** is the reported text. In a full report, the chain identifies transmission, while the text gives the saying or describes the event. An English quotation card often shows only a shortened text and hides the information needed to trace it. It may also omit context or join separate statements.

**Worked reference reading.** Open Bukhari 1. Identify the named narrator, Umar ibn al-Khattab, and the report about intentions. The full Arabic entry supplies a chain of transmission before the reported words. A basic learner can distinguish the job of the chain from the job of the text without being able to evaluate every narrator. Merely recognising a famous companion's name does not establish that every later link in a different chain is sound.

Motala (2023), in his [explanation of sahih](https://hadithanswers.com/the-definition-of-as-sahih-li-dhatihi/), sets out continuity, narrators' integrity and precision, and the absence of hidden defects or contradiction with stronger reports. The assessment concerns both transmission and text. A pleasant message is not a substitute for these checks. Conversely, a learner's difficulty understanding a report is a reason to seek explanation, not an automatic proof that the report is false.

The three introductory grades in this unit are **sahih** (sound), **hasan** (good) and **daif** (weak). In a basic summary, sahih meets the required standards for a connected, reliable transmission without a disqualifying contradiction or hidden defect. Hasan is accepted but may have a lower level of narrator precision. Daif fails one or more acceptance conditions. Weak does not automatically mean deliberately fabricated. The Federal Territories Mufti Department's [2019 explanation of hasan](https://www.muftiwp.gov.my/en/artikel/irsyad-al-hadith/3233-irsyad-al-hadith-siri-ke-358-hadith-hasan) explains the role of precision and supporting routes and notes that hasan reports can generally serve as evidence.

Use these descriptions as an introduction, not as a three-question grading test. Some assessments take account of several routes of transmission. Scholars can differ over a narrator, a route or a defect. A reference page may therefore name a grader or give more than one judgment. Keep the scholar's name with the reported grade where it is supplied. Do not turn a particular assessment into a claim that every scholar has reached the same conclusion.

**Common misconception: every reported hadith carries equal authority.** A statement's attribution must be examined. A traceable report with a recognised assessment and an unsupported message are not equally established. This is not a ranking of which moral messages we like. It is a distinction about what evidence supports attributing a report to the Prophet.

**Another misconception: daif means a known lie.** A chain may have a gap, or a narrator's precision may not meet the required standard, without proof that a person invented the report. Fabrication is a separate judgment. At the same time, calling a report "only weak" does not justify presenting it to pupils as certain. Questions about using weak reports in particular settings involve scholarly conditions and differences; a trainee should consult a qualified scholar and use well-established material for this unit's lessons.

**Worked checking decision.** A forwarded card gives no collection and no number. The learner writes, "Attribution not yet verified," rather than "daif" or "fabricated." The absence of a reference on the card shows a problem with the card's documentation; it does not itself determine the historical report's grade. If a matching source is later found, the learner checks the whole wording and any assessment, because a card may have combined a genuine sentence with an unsupported addition.

Suppose a website gives the report number but no grade visible on the page. The next step is not to assign one from the report's tone. Record the collection, chapter or book information, numbering system and exact wording, then ask for help locating a reliable assessment. Bukhari and Muslim have a special standing as the two major sahih collections in Sunni study, but that does not license changing their text or attaching an unrelated internet sentence to their names.

### Role of Sunnah in Islamic life

The Sunnah connects religious knowledge with worship, character and relationships. The Qur'an presents the Messenger as an excellent example ([33:21](https://quran.com/33/21)). For the Muslim teacher, studying that example calls for more than finding a quotation to begin a lesson. It asks how the teacher's conduct reflects patience, fairness, truthfulness and care. Accurate words and suitable action should support one another.

**Worked school response.** Two pupils interrupt each other during a group task. The trainee stops the exchange calmly, gives each a turn and asks them to restate the other person's point before replying. She links her gentle manner with Muslim 2593 and her attention to justice with Qur'an 4:135. She describes the turn-taking routine as her teaching strategy. Neither source names a modern group-work protocol, and neither becomes less important because the practical method is humanly chosen.

Evaluate the response through observable evidence. Did each pupil receive a turn? Could the quiet pupil speak? Did the teacher correct the behaviour without calling either child worthless? Did the group return to learning? These questions help determine whether the proposed application worked. A religious label cannot answer them in advance. If the strategy fails, the teacher can improve it without implying that the source failed.

The Sunnah also helps a teacher avoid separating worship from character as though only one belongs in Islamic life. The example about prayer shows an instructional role in worship; the example about gentleness shows a moral role in relationships. A complete account should include both. A trainee should not claim that outward routines alone prove a person's whole character, or that good manners remove the significance of worship.

Distinguish an established Prophetic example from the tools used to teach it today. A microphone, printed worksheet or digital display can support instruction, but its use is not automatically a Prophetic practice. In the same way, a local seating arrangement may be useful without being a religious requirement. Keeping this distinction clear allows a teacher to choose suitable tools while making accurate claims about the source of religious teaching.

There are differences of interpretation and practice among Muslim scholars and communities. A practicum classroom is not the place to judge pupils' families from a short introductory summary. When a child mentions a different practice, the teacher can acknowledge the question, describe what the lesson covers and seek qualified advice if the issue is legal or disputed. Careful limits support respectful learning; they do not require pretending that differences do not exist.

The trainee in Larkana now has a clear decision. She postpones the unsourced card. She can prepare a reminder based on a checked report, give its collection and number, and label her own explanation as an application. If a later source check supports the original message, she can reconsider it. Her decision follows the evidence available at the time instead of rewarding the message's demand to be forwarded.

A short source note makes this repeatable: report wording or accurate paraphrase, collection and reference, assessment where relevant, and proposed application. Add a question for anything unresolved. Keep a paraphrase outside quotation marks and make clear whose explanation it is. A learner reading that note should be able to retrace the teacher's steps without needing to trust a decorative border, a large forwarding count or the teacher's confidence.

## Activity: Audit a quotation card together

Work in pairs for about 25 minutes. Use Bukhari 1 or Muslim 2593, not an unknown social-media message. One partner makes a three-line paraphrase card with a source reference and a clearly labelled classroom application. The other checks the original page, identifies what the card leaves out and asks whether any wording wrongly appears to be a quotation. Swap roles with the other report.

Then inspect a deliberately incomplete card supplied by your tutor: "A wise saying; source unknown." Agree on a responsible next step without assigning it a reliability grade. Exchange your response with another pair and explain the difference between checking documentation and grading transmission. Finish by improving both cards so another reader can find the report and distinguish its meaning from the proposed activity.

## Check your understanding

1. Define isnad and matn in one sentence each. *(Remember)*
2. Explain why a weak report is not automatically a fabricated report, and why a missing reference is not itself a grade. *(Understand)*
3. A trainee wants to display an attractive saying with no source. State three checks or actions she should take before attributing it to the Prophet. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Hadith reports help preserve knowledge of the Sunnah and need careful attribution. Isnad and matn have different jobs in a report, while reliability judgments require specialist evidence. The Sunnah guides worship and conduct; a teacher should distinguish established teaching from the classroom method chosen to apply it.

## Try this at your practicum school

With the cooperating teacher, prepare one checked reminder based on Bukhari 1 or Muslim 2593. Give the source and use a paraphrase label if the wording is your own. Agree on one observable teaching action that follows from it. After the lesson, record whether the action helped pupils and one improvement. Do not grade other teachers' faith or pupils' intentions. Refer an uncertain attribution privately to the cooperating teacher for further checking.

## Summative task

Write a 350–450 word response to the Larkana trainee. Analyze the difference between an unsourced card and a referenced report, explain the roles of isnad, matn and scholarly grading, and propose one suitable use of an established report in class. Include how you would check whether the teaching action worked. *(Analyze)*

Mini-rubric, 10 marks: report and Sunnah distinction, 0–2 (2: clear; 1: partial; 0: confused); explanation of assessment, 0–3 (3: both parts and grades explained with limits; 2: mostly accurate; 1: labels only; 0: unsupported); analysis of the card, 0–3 (3: identifies missing evidence and justified next steps without guessing a grade; 2: explains one part fully; 1: preference alone; 0: no analysis); classroom application, 0–2 (2: referenced, feasible and observable; 1: partly developed; 0: absent).
