# Sources of Islamic Knowledge: the Qur'an and the Sunnah

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the roles of the Qur'an, Hadith and Sunnah, Ijma and Qiyas, and distinguish revealed guidance from scholarly reasoning. This serves SLO:GISS-401-2-1.
- Use surah and ayah references to explain selected teachings about knowledge, justice, kindness and honesty. This serves SLO:GISS-401-2-2.
- Distinguish a report from the Prophetic example it records, and explain why hadith classification matters. This serves both unit SLOs.
- Analyze a simple example of Qiyas without treating a classroom comparison as a religious ruling. This serves SLO:GISS-401-2-1.
- Explain how a Muslim teacher can draw on reliable sources while acknowledging the limits of their own knowledge. This serves SLO:GISS-401-2-2.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Complete Unit 1 first. Recall Tauheed, Risalat and Akhirat, and the connection between belief and conduct. You should be able to distinguish a statement of belief from an example of behaviour. You need no prior training in Arabic or Islamic law for the activities here.

Keep a notebook with three columns: reference, meaning in your own words, and possible application. Keep the meaning and application separate. This makes it easier to see when you have moved from reporting a source to proposing a school practice.

## How to use this unit

Read the topics in order. Each begins with a fictional Sindh classroom situation and then models a way to examine it. Complete the three retrieval questions with the book closed. Reopen the text to check your reasoning before you tick the self-assessment statements.

Read the linked references alongside the explanations. Qur'anic references use surah:ayah, such as 4:135. Hadith references name a collection and a number. English accounts of verses in this unit are paraphrases; they do not replace the Arabic text or a full translation and commentary.

Use the pair activities to explain reasons, not to rank classmates' religious knowledge. At practicum, discuss a task with the cooperating teacher before using it with pupils. Do not ask children to disclose family beliefs or judge a family's religious practice.

The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) contains public self-study questions and guidance. Attempt the questions before reading its final section. The default weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative where a course teacher uses these tasks for assessment. A legal question that needs a religious ruling belongs with a qualified scholar; this unit develops careful reading and explanation.
