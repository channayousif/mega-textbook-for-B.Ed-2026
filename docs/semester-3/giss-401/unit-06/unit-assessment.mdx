# Unit 6 assessment: Knowledge, civilization and coexistence

## Unit summary

Islamic education links the search for knowledge with responsible conduct. Ilm and hikmah can be distinguished as knowledge and wise judgement about its use. A teacher needs both accurate subject knowledge and a reasoned choice about how to respond to learners. Learners also have duties, including honest effort, respectful questioning and care for other pupils. The relationship works best when respect protects understanding rather than concealing uncertainty.

Selected examples from the history of scholarship show knowledge being inherited, translated, questioned and developed. People needed materials, support and exchange as well as personal ability. Islamic civilization included varied communities and institutions across time. A careful account names the contribution and evidence without pretending that one person invented everything. A timeline's final date defines its coverage, not the end of scholarship.

Justice and tolerance have practical implications for diverse classrooms. A pupil's identity is not evidence for an allegation. Teachers need to protect safety and participation while facts are checked. Tolerance allows firm belief alongside respectful difference. Coexistence includes correcting exclusion and repairing harm, not merely keeping the classroom quiet. Selected parallels between the Farewell Pilgrimage report and modern rights themes do not make the sources legally identical.

Across all three topics, the same habits recur: distinguish a principle from its application, match a claim to evidence, acknowledge a limit and check the effect of a decision. The following questions use new invented cases. No case reports an actual school or family. Explain your reasoning in plain language. You may use the linked sources after making a first attempt, but keep your original response so you can see what changed.

## Summative assessment

MCQs carry 1 mark each. RRQs carry 4 marks each. ERQs carry 10 marks each, for a total of 100 marks. This is a public self-study bank, not the protected LMS assessment. In formal teaching, use the course's default 60% summative and 40% formative balance. Suggested ERQ length is 250-350 words per response. A reasoned alternative can earn full credit when it meets the criteria and stays within the evidence.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which description best distinguishes hikmah from simply possessing information in this unit? *(Understand)*
   - A) Remembering the exact wording of a definition.
   - B) Judging how to use knowledge responsibly in context.
   - C) Finding a source more quickly than classmates.
   - D) Speaking about a topic with strong confidence.

2. A pupil respectfully shows that the teacher omitted a step in a calculation. Which response best supports reciprocal learning duties? *(Apply)*
   - A) Retain the step because teachers have authority.
   - B) End the task because disagreement caused delay.
   - C) Let classmates vote on the teacher's reputation.
   - D) Check the working and acknowledge any correction.

3. A pupil repeats a definition correctly. Which follow-up best checks understanding beyond recall? *(Apply)*
   - A) Ask the pupil to explain a new example.
   - B) Ask the pupil to copy the definition again.
   - C) Ask the pupil to speak the definition louder.
   - D) Ask the pupil to count the definition's words.

4. What does Qur'an 96:1-5 connect with learning in the account used in this unit? *(Remember)*
   - A) A specified timetable for modern science lessons.
   - B) A rule assigning every pupil the same task.
   - C) Reading, teaching and the pen in relation to the Creator.
   - D) A list ranking the professions by examination marks.

5. What kind of claim is best supported by the Samarkand heritage case study? *(Understand)*
   - A) Every nearby child studied advanced astronomy.
   - B) Observation and calculation were supported in a learning centre.
   - C) The city's buildings prove that all education was equal.
   - D) Scientific learning ended when the observatory closed.

6. A display's source supports a scholar's improvement to an earlier method. Which wording most closely fits the evidence? *(Apply)*
   - A) The scholar originated the whole scientific field.
   - B) Earlier workers made no useful contribution.
   - C) The scholar developed an existing method further.
   - D) Later methods require no separate investigation.

7. What does a timeline ending in the fifteenth century establish by itself? *(Understand)*
   - A) The display's selected period of coverage.
   - B) The final date of all Muslim scholarship.
   - C) The start of an identical decline everywhere.
   - D) The absence of any later scientific work.

8. Pupils disagree about a religious belief but continue shared group work respectfully. Which concept best describes this relationship? *(Apply)*
   - A) Agreement on every religious claim.
   - B) Withdrawal of personal commitment.
   - C) Avoidance of meaningful participation.
   - D) Coexistence with respectful difference.

9. An unverified forwarded message has led pupils to exclude a classmate. What should the teacher do first? *(Apply)*
   - A) Stop exclusion and refer the allegation for a fair check.
   - B) Request a public apology to calm the larger group.
   - C) Count message forwards to decide the likely truth.
   - D) Ask classmates to debate the child's community.

10. What is the most accurate description of this unit's Farewell Pilgrimage and UDHR comparison? *(Understand)*
   - A) Proof that the documents have identical legal operation.
   - B) Evidence that every popular sermon sentence is in Muslim 1218a.
   - C) Selected thematic parallels between distinct sources and frameworks.
   - D) A replacement for qualified advice in a current legal case.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, explain why learning to interpret a seed packet can involve both useful knowledge and responsibility. Identify one observable learning check. *(Understand)*
2. A teacher says that asking about an error is disrespectful. In four sentences, explain one duty of the teacher and one of the learner, and propose a better response. *(Apply)*
3. A trainee knows that a pupil's response has six errors and plans to read them aloud to the class. In four sentences, distinguish knowledge from wisdom and analyse one likely consequence of that plan. *(Analyze)*
4. In three sentences, explain how translation can contribute to knowledge and why it does not imply that the translator originated every idea in the text. *(Understand)*
5. A pupil uses a photograph of an observatory to claim that every child in its city studied mathematics. In four sentences, identify the gap between evidence and conclusion and name evidence needed for the wider claim. *(Analyze)*
6. Two pupil groups record different shadow lengths but use different starting points. In four sentences, propose a method check and explain why it should come before a conclusion about the difference. *(Apply)*
7. In four sentences, explain why tolerance does not require religious agreement. Give one boundary that protects a pupil during shared school work. *(Understand)*
8. A rumour and exclusion occur together. In four sentences, distinguish an immediate protective action from a later fact check and explain why one need not wait for the other. *(Analyze)*
9. In three sentences, state one parallel and one limit in comparing the Farewell Pilgrimage report with a modern rights theme. Identify the sources precisely. *(Understand)*
10. A fluent paragraph may have been produced with an automated tool. In four sentences, analyse how the teacher can check understanding and sources while treating the pupil fairly. Keep your response focused on the contemporary classroom decision. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. In an invented Nawabshah class, pupils copy an accurate explanation but cannot apply it. The teacher discourages questions to keep order. Evaluate two possible lesson changes using educational purpose, reciprocal duties and the distinction between ilm and hikmah. Defend a sequence and explain what would count as improved understanding. *(Evaluate)* (Topic 6.1)
2. An invented scholarship display says: 'One scholar invented all astronomy; a large observatory proves education was equal; our timeline ends in the fifteenth century, so scholarship stopped then.' Analyse the three claims. Produce a more defensible account using two checked contributions and explain how institutions support learning. State what your evidence cannot establish. *(Analyze)* (Topic 6.2)
3. In an invented diverse school, a forwarded allegation causes a pupil's exclusion. The head asks a trainee for a recommendation. Evaluate a forced public apology against protection followed by fair fact-finding. Use Islamic teaching on justice and tolerance, name your classroom inferences, and propose a follow-up that tests whether harm has stopped. *(Evaluate)* (Topic 6.3)
4. Design a low-resource lesson joining a selected historical contribution with a present classroom inquiry. Explain how pupils will distinguish a source from a claim, practise respectful questioning and test a simple method. Justify how the lesson joins useful knowledge, wisdom and coexistence without making scientific ability depend on religious identity. *(Create)* (integrative, Topics 6.1-6.3)
5. An invented school proposes a compulsory online 'civilization quiz'. Some pupils have no home device; the questions repeat unsupported invention claims; pupils who challenge them are called disloyal. Evaluate the proposal and defend a revised plan that preserves historical evidence, thinking demand and fair participation. Explain how learning evidence would guide a second revision. *(Evaluate)* (integrative, Topics 6.1-6.3)

## Answers and marking guidance

These are intentionally public self-study answers. They do not certify religious interpretation or provide legal advice. Compare your reasons as well as the final choice. For constructed responses, accept other well-supported wording and feasible actions. Do not award credit for an invented quotation or for treating a pupil's identity as evidence of misconduct.

### MCQ answer key

1. B. Wisdom concerns responsible judgement about use, while recall, speed and confidence alone do not establish it.
2. D. Checking the working protects accuracy and shows that authority can acknowledge a reasoned correction.
3. A. A new example asks the pupil to transfer understanding; repeating or manipulating the same words mainly samples recall.
4. C. The reference connects reading, teaching and the pen with the Creator; the other options add specific modern arrangements absent from the passage.
5. B. The case study supports a particular setting of observation and calculation. It does not establish universal attendance, equal access or the end of scholarship.
6. C. Calling an improvement an improvement matches the evidence and acknowledges earlier work without diminishing the later contribution.
7. A. The final date defines the selected display's coverage. A claim about everything later requires further evidence.
8. D. Coexistence can include shared work with continuing belief differences; it does not depend on religious agreement.
9. A. The teacher can protect participation immediately while the responsible school adults check the allegation through a fair process.
10. C. A thematic comparison shows selected related concerns. It does not make distinct texts legally identical or authenticate every popular sermon version.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Each RRQ carries 4 marks. Award each point once. A response can use different examples if it demonstrates the same understanding. Do not require exact wording or advanced vocabulary. If a learner repeats a source reference without explaining how it bears on the case, award the source-identification point only where one is specified, not the reasoning point.

1. A pupil uses reading and number knowledge to interpret an instruction rather than merely repeat it. Responsibility includes explaining accurately and recognising uncertainty before advising someone else. Ask the pupil to explain a new quantity or instruction in their own words and describe the evidence you would observe. **Marks:** 1 for useful understanding; 1 for a responsibility; 1 for a transfer question; 1 for observable evidence of meaning rather than copying.
2. The teacher should check a reasoned challenge and correct an error if present. The learner should identify the step respectfully and allow a response. The teacher might ask the class to inspect the calculation rather than judge the speaker. Respect then protects accuracy and learning on both sides. **Marks:** 1 for teacher duty; 1 for learner duty; 1 for a feasible replacement action; 1 for explaining the reciprocal relationship.
3. Knowledge identifies the errors; wisdom concerns how to use that information to help the learner. Publicly listing every error may shame the pupil and overload the next attempt. A better plan could select the main misunderstanding and provide private, focused feedback followed by practice. **Marks:** 1 for each side of the knowledge/wisdom distinction; 1 for a plausible consequence linked to the case; 1 for a justified alternative. Generic claims that kindness fixes everything do not earn the last point.
4. Translation makes an existing work accessible to readers of another language and can enable later checking or development. That service is a contribution even where the original ideas came from earlier scholars. Credit should distinguish the original work, the translation and any new development. **Marks:** 1 for access; 1 for enabling further learning; 1 for distinguishing origin from translation; 1 for appropriate attribution.
5. The photograph may show a specialist building, not who attended or what every child learned. The pupil has moved from evidence of one institution to a universal attendance claim. Records of participation or other checked accounts would be needed for that wider conclusion. A narrower claim would describe the observatory as evidence of a particular scholarly setting. **Marks:** 1 for the actual evidence; 1 for identifying the unsupported generalisation; 1 for suitable further evidence; 1 for a defensible narrower claim.
6. Establish a common starting point, time and measurement procedure, then repeat the observation and retain both sets of records. Different procedures can create different results, so the difference does not yet establish a natural-world explanation. Record why the method changed rather than hiding the earlier result. **Marks:** 1 for a common procedure; 1 for repeating; 1 for explaining the method/result relationship; 1 for honest recording or an equivalent control. No pupil should look at the sun.
7. Tolerance permits continuing belief while respecting the other person's dignity and freedom from coercion. Ordinary shared school work does not require pupils to affirm one another's beliefs. A boundary could prevent insults, forced declarations or making one child represent a whole community. **Marks:** 1 for retained belief; 1 for respect despite difference; 1 for a relevant school boundary; 1 for explaining why it protects participation.
8. Stop the exclusion and secure a meaningful learning role now. Refer the allegation to responsible adults, hear relevant accounts privately and check evidence before blame. Protection need not wait because respectful treatment is owed while uncertainty remains. Immediate protection also does not imply that the allegation has been judged false. **Marks:** 1 for protection; 1 for a separate fair check; 1 for explaining the timing; 1 for keeping uncertainty explicit.
9. *Sahih Muslim* 1218a records protection of life and property during the Farewell Pilgrimage; UDHR Article 3 addresses life, liberty and security, while Article 17 addresses property. One may compare a selected protection, but the wording, framework and detailed scope differ. The comparison cannot settle a current legal case or show that every popular sermon sentence belongs to that report. **Marks:** 1 for a valid parallel; 1 for accurate report identification; 1 for an appropriate modern article; 1 for a stated limit.
10. Ask the pupil to explain the paragraph and attempt a related task, because fluency alone does not establish understanding. Check any cited source rather than assuming a convincing reference is real. Hear the pupil privately, apply clear rules about permitted assistance and offer a fair opportunity to demonstrate learning. **Marks:** 1 for the learning check; 1 for the evidence rule; 1 for a proportionate response; 1 for explaining the connection across all three topics.

### ERQ rubrics

Each response carries 10 marks. For each criterion, **2** means the response supplies the specific reasoning and evidence described; **1** means it identifies the relevant issue but explains it only partly; **0** means it is absent, substantially mistaken or contradicted by the proposed action. Use the five criteria in the relevant row below independently. A polished answer cannot compensate for a missing evidence check or an unfair proposal.

| Item | Criterion 1, 0-2 | Criterion 2, 0-2 | Criterion 3, 0-2 | Criterion 4, 0-2 | Criterion 5, 0-2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ERQ 1 | Connects understanding with responsible purpose | Distinguishes ilm and hikmah through the case | Gives reciprocal teacher and learner duties | Evaluates two changes using consequences and case details | Defines a feasible new-application learning check |
| ERQ 2 | Tests the invention claim against two supported contributions | Identifies the unjustified inference about universal access | Explains why a timeline endpoint proves no cessation | Links people, materials or support to actual learning work | Uses traceable sources and states limits of the account |
| ERQ 3 | Applies justice and tolerance accurately | Evaluates harm and uncertainty in both proposed responses | Separates immediate protection from fair fact-finding | Uses a precise scriptural reference and identifies the classroom inference | Proposes a feasible repair and observable participation follow-up |
| ERQ 4 | Designs a coherent inquiry with a stated learning goal | Links a specific historical contribution to checked evidence | Explains a safe method check without claiming a historical reconstruction | Builds respectful questioning and meaningful roles into the task | Justifies low-resource access and a learning check across the three topics |
| ERQ 5 | Analyses both evidence problems in the original quiz | Evaluates exclusion and the treatment of challenge | Defends an alternative preserving thinking demand | Joins historical attribution, wisdom and tolerance with reasons | Specifies learning evidence and a reason for a later revision |

For ERQ 1, a strong response might contrast copying the explanation again with using a new case and a question pause. It should explain why the latter reveals thinking while still maintaining clear lesson routines. Do not require one fixed teaching method. A teacher could use drawing, spoken explanation or short writing, provided the response shows what would be learned from it.

For ERQ 2, a strong response narrows claims instead of replacing favourable overstatement with unfavourable overstatement. It can use the selected astronomical examples and the supported institutional setting. It should not claim that no child outside the observatory learned mathematics; the evidence is insufficient for that universal claim too. Specific limits are part of the reasoning.

For ERQ 3, a strong response protects the child's role now, refers the allegation appropriately, and avoids public blame or forced identity statements. It can cite Qur'an 5:8 and 49:6, or another relevant checked reference from the topic. The follow-up needs evidence such as whether the pupil receives a meaningful role and whether harmful sharing has stopped, not merely whether the room is quiet.

For ERQ 4, a strong response might use a checked account of observation at a learning centre, followed by a simple board-based comparison of measurement methods. It should state where the analogy ends and avoid unsafe observation. It gives each pupil a thinking role and checks what each understands. Credit another feasible inquiry if all five criteria are met.

For ERQ 5, a strong response replaces unsupported invention statements with evidence-based questions, provides a paper or in-class route to the same task, and permits respectful challenges. It checks source judgement and understanding rather than rewarding loyalty labels or device ownership. It also names what evidence would trigger a further change, such as persistent confusion between an instrument and proof of universal access.
