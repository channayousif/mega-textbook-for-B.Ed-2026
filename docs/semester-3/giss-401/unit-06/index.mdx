# Islam, Education, Civilization and Contemporary Issues

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the importance of education in Islam and distinguish knowledge from its wise use. *(Understand; SLO:GISS-401-6-1)*
- Apply responsibilities of teachers and learners to a classroom decision. *(Apply; SLO:GISS-401-6-1)*
- Explain selected contributions of Muslim scholars and the institutions that supported learning. *(Understand; SLO:GISS-401-6-2)*
- Analyse a claim about Islamic civilization using evidence, dates and careful attribution. *(Analyze; SLO:GISS-401-6-2)*
- Evaluate a teacher's response to exclusion or an unverified message using Islamic principles of justice, tolerance and care. *(Evaluate; SLO:GISS-401-6-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Recall Units 1-5: Islamic belief, the Qur'an and Sunnah as sources, moral conduct, social responsibilities and justice. Before starting, explain the difference between stating a principle and showing how an action follows it. Recall that a Qur'anic verse and a historical account are different kinds of evidence. You do not need specialist history or science knowledge for this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Islam and education](./topic-01): why learning matters, reciprocal responsibilities, and knowledge with wisdom.
2. [Islamic culture and civilization](./topic-02): scholarship, institutions, and the relationship between science and education.
3. [Islam and contemporary issues](./topic-03): dignity, peace, coexistence, and responsible responses to school challenges.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Each starts with an invented classroom case located in Sindh. These are teaching examples, not reports about named schools. Follow the worked reasoning before doing the paired activity. Keep a notebook with three columns: principle, evidence, classroom decision. Return to it when the same principle appears in a new situation.

Try the three retrieval questions in each topic without looking back. There are nine formative questions across this unit, following its approved blueprint. Use the checklist to decide what needs another reading. The topic task develops your reasoning; the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) tests connections across the whole unit. Attempt its questions before reading the final public self-study guidance. The default assessment balance is 40% formative and 60% summative.

All scriptural references are paraphrases, not quotations. The linked English Qur'an pages display Mustafa Khattab's *The Clear Quran*. A comparison with a modern rights document shows selected parallels, not identical legal systems. Do not use a school activity to issue religious or legal rulings.
