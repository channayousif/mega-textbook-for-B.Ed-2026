# Topic 6.1 - Islam and education

## A real classroom situation

In an invented government school near Mirpurkhas, trainee teacher Nida asks pupils to explain why a puddle dries. One pupil says science belongs outside an Islamic Studies lesson. Another can repeat a definition of evaporation but cannot use it to explain the puddle. When a third pupil asks a question, classmates laugh. Nida must decide what counts as learning and how her conduct can support it.

## Explanation

### Importance of education in Islam

Education is the development of understanding, skills and conduct through learning. In Islamic education, the question is also what learning is for and how it affects one's responsibilities before Allah and towards people. A pupil may learn a rule, use it to solve a problem, and become more careful about the effect of a decision. These are related achievements. A lesson that produces accurate repetition but rewards cruelty has not joined understanding with good conduct.

The opening verses of Surah Al-'Alaq connect reading, teaching and the pen with the Creator (Qur'an [96:1-5](https://quran.com/96/1-5)). This reference supports the value placed on learning. It is not a modern science syllabus, nor does it settle the best method for every lesson. A teacher must still identify what pupils need to learn and how to check their understanding. Linking a principle to a practical choice requires reasoning. Naming the verse cannot replace that work.

**Misconception: Islam discourages worldly knowledge.** The contrast is misleading when it suggests that useful knowledge of health, number, language or the natural world has no place in a Muslim's education. Bhat (2019), reviewing K. Mohammad Ayirur's book, discusses Islamic education as linking knowledge with moral and spiritual formation, rather than reducing it to collecting information. His short book review provides an educational perspective, not a ruling on every field of study. The guide asks us to consider education broadly. Topic 6.2 will examine historical examples of scientific learning in Muslim societies.

Consider an invented class in rural Sindh learning how to read instructions on a seed packet. Reading the words is one goal. Understanding quantities is another. Explaining the instructions accurately to a neighbour brings a social responsibility. A teacher can value all three without pretending that the packet is a religious text. Equally, practical usefulness does not make every possible use of knowledge good. A person could use strong writing skills to mislead others. Educational purpose includes judgement about use.

A helpful distinction is between **access to information**, **understanding**, and **responsible action**. Access means that the learner can obtain an explanation or example. Understanding means that the learner can explain a relationship in their own words and apply it to a new case. Responsible action means that the learner considers other people and the limits of their knowledge before acting. These are working distinctions for this unit. They help you observe learning rather than judge a pupil's inner faith.

Here is a worked example. A pupil has copied instructions for storing drinking water. Nida first asks the pupil to explain why a container should be covered. This tests understanding beyond copying. She then asks how a household without the pictured container might adapt the advice safely. This tests transfer to another setting. Finally, she asks pupils to check the advice with their science teacher before making a health claim to families. This models responsibility and the limits of a trainee's expertise. The learning goal is useful understanding with care, not a public test of who is a better Muslim.

Now compare two possible assessment records:

| Observation | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Pupil repeats a sentence accurately | Recall of that sentence | Explanation of the relationship |
| Pupil explains a new example | Some transfer of understanding | Mastery of every related problem |
| Pupil checks uncertain advice | A responsible learning action | A judgement about personal piety |

The last column matters. Teachers often draw conclusions wider than the evidence permits. A quiet pupil may understand a concept but need more time or another language to explain it. A confident pupil may speak fluently while misunderstanding it. Give a short written, spoken or drawn explanation route where possible. Fair access does not require you to lower the goal; it requires you to find out whether the pupil can reach it.

### Role of teachers and learners in Islam

A teacher's authority carries duties. In this unit, the central duties are to prepare sound explanations, model honest inquiry, give fair attention and correct mistakes respectfully. Learners also have duties: listen carefully, ask relevant questions, practise, acknowledge help and treat other learners with respect. These are proposed classroom applications of the educational principles studied here. They are not a complete code of Islamic law.

Qur'an [16:125](https://quran.com/16/125) calls for wisdom and good counsel in inviting others, and for the best manner in discussion. Its immediate subject is religious invitation. Applying its concern for manner to teaching is an educational inference, which we should name as such. It suggests that a teacher should examine how an explanation is offered, not just whether the words are technically accurate. Humiliating a learner may silence the very question that would reveal a misunderstanding.

Respect does not mean that pupils must pretend to understand. It also does not make every interruption helpful. The teacher can arrange a question pause, ask pupils to write questions, or let pairs prepare one question together. Learners practise respect by asking about the idea rather than mocking the speaker. Teachers practise it by responding to the question rather than treating uncertainty as disobedience. Both sides need a way to repair a mistake.

Return to Nida's class. The learner who laughed should stop and hear the effect of the action. Nida should protect the questioner's dignity. She might say, 'We ask about ideas without laughing at people. Let us hear the question.' She then gives the pupil time to speak or write. The class resumes learning after the interruption has been addressed. Simply continuing the lesson would leave the pupil unprotected; cancelling all questions would punish the whole class for one harmful action.

The table below makes reciprocal responsibility visible. The reference to avoiding ridicule is Qur'an [49:11](https://quran.com/49/11). A teacher should not use it to accuse a child of being beyond improvement. The teaching move is to name and change the behaviour.

| Teacher responsibility | Learner responsibility | Evidence of the relationship in class |
|---|---|---|
| Explain the learning goal | Attempt the task and ask about difficulty | Pupil can describe what the task requires |
| Admit and correct an error | Revise an earlier response honestly | Both versions are kept with a reason for change |
| Give fair opportunities to speak | Leave space for others to speak | More than the quickest pupils contribute |
| Protect pupils from ridicule | Disagree without attacking identity | A mistaken claim is discussed without name-calling |

Suppose a teacher marks a calculation wrongly. A learner notices but fears that raising it will seem disrespectful. The teacher can invite a check of the steps and correct the mark if needed. The learner can point to the working without insulting the teacher. Authority remains, but accuracy and honesty also remain. A rule that the teacher can never be questioned would make the teacher's reputation more important than learning.

In a large class, reciprocal duties need practical arrangements. Rotate group roles so that the same fluent pupil does not always explain. Let one learner restate a partner's reasoning before adding a different view. Keep an unanswered-question space in the notebook or on the board. Return to it later with a checked explanation. If a question exceeds the teacher's knowledge, saying that it needs checking is stronger teaching than inventing a confident response.

There are also boundaries. Pupils do not carry the teacher's duty to manage safety. A learner who reports bullying should not be asked to negotiate alone with the pupil causing harm. A teacher should not ask children to disclose private family religious practices to prove respect. Use the observable actions in a learning task. This keeps a lesson about responsibility from becoming a judgement about homes, sects or personal worth.

### Knowledge and wisdom

**Ilm** means knowledge. In this topic, it includes knowing facts and understanding relationships. **Hikmah** means wisdom; here we use it for sound judgement about how knowledge should guide action. The terms have richer uses in Islamic scholarship. Our classroom distinction is a starting point, not an exhaustive definition. A learner may know a fact but still need help deciding when, how and with what limits to use it.

For example, a trainee knows that a pupil's written response contains errors. Knowledge allows the trainee to identify them. Wisdom concerns the next step: which error most blocks understanding, whether to correct privately, and what practice will help. Naming every error aloud may be accurate but unhelpful. Ignoring all errors may feel kind but leave the learner confused. Wise use seeks a response that is truthful, proportionate and useful.

Work through this invented case. A pupil in Hyderabad gives a wrong explanation of a shadow. Nida knows the explanation is mistaken. She has two minutes before the bell and sees that several pupils agree with it. She first asks the pupil to show the reasoning with a drawing. This reveals the shared misunderstanding without a personal attack. She then demonstrates the relationship with a torch and object, or a board sketch if no torch is available. She schedules a short check next lesson. The decision joins correct subject knowledge, attention to the learner and awareness of time.

Notice the reasoning sequence: identify what is known, identify what remains uncertain, consider who may be affected, choose a justified action, and check the result. This is a planning aid for the trainee. It does not guarantee a perfect decision. If Nida later finds that the sketch confused pupils, wisdom includes revising it. Refusing to reconsider because the lesson was well intended would block further learning.

Another invented case concerns peer assistance. A strong reader in Sukkur is asked to help a classmate. Reading the paragraph aloud shows skill, but completing the classmate's entire response removes the chance to learn. A wiser form of help is to explain one example, let the classmate attempt a new one and ask a question about the reason. The helper shares knowledge while preserving the other learner's agency, meaning their opportunity to think and act for themselves.

Use the distinction carefully. We cannot measure wisdom through one answer alone or label children as permanently wise or unwise. We can ask whether a particular decision used relevant knowledge, considered harm and gave a reason. A teacher who says 'use wisdom' without showing the reasoning has given a slogan rather than instruction. Model the judgement, then let learners try a related case.

There can be real disagreement between reasonable choices. One teacher may correct a misconception immediately because it affects safety. Another may postpone a minor detail to avoid overloading a beginner. To compare them, ask about the importance of the error, the available evidence, the learners' needs and the likely consequences. The aim is a reasoned account of the decision. Personal confidence alone is not evidence that one choice is wiser.

## Activity: Two versions of a lesson

**Work in groups of three. Allow about 20 minutes.** Use this invented case: a pupil copies a paragraph accurately but cannot explain it, another asks for a Sindhi explanation, and the teacher says that questions show poor respect. Assign a teacher, learner and observer; rotate after the first round.

First, each person recalls one idea about education, responsibility or wisdom without opening the text. Pool the ideas. Draft a four-minute exchange that improves understanding and respects the questioner. The observer must record a teacher action, a learner action and evidence that understanding was checked. Then replay the exchange with one constraint: no photocopies and only a board is available.

Exchange plans with another group. Ask them which action develops understanding rather than copying, and which action makes respect reciprocal. Revise one step in response to their evidence. Do not act out religious identities or imitate a pupil's accent. Discuss the decision rather than perform humiliation. Keep the first and revised plans so that the change in reasoning is visible.

## Summary

Islamic education connects learning with responsibility and conduct. Teachers and learners have reciprocal duties, and respectful questions can help fulfil them. Knowledge identifies what is understood; wise judgement concerns its responsible use in a particular situation. Neither fluent recall nor silent obedience alone establishes understanding.
