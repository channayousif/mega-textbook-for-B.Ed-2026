# Unit 3 Assessment: Worship and the Moral System of Islam

## Unit summary

This unit examined the inseparable unity between Islamic worship (*Ibadat*) and the Islamic moral system (*Akhlaq*). Far from being isolated ritual ceremonies or abstract ethical theories, worship and morality constitute an integrated spiritual and behavioural discipline that shapes both personal character and social order.

In Topic 3.1, we explored the four foundational acts of practical worship: Salat (prayer), Saum (fasting), Zakat (alms), and Hajj (pilgrimage). Each practice targets specific dimensions of human development: Salat establishes rhythmic spiritual remembrance and social egalitarianism; Saum cultivates self-restraint (*taqwa*) and direct empathy with the hungry; Zakat institutionalizes economic justice and prevents the concentration of wealth; and Hajj demonstrates universal human brotherhood by dismantling racial, ethnic, and status hierarchies. We corrected the misconceptions that worship is an exclusively private affair without social impact, and that Zakat is synonymous with discretionary charity.

In Topic 3.2, we investigated the moral architecture of Islam, rooted in divine accountability (*Akhirat*) and reverent mindfulness (*Taqwa*). We examined four cardinal virtues essential to professional educators: truthfulness (*sidq*), impartial justice (*adl*), sacred trustworthiness (*amanah*), and active perseverance (*sabr*). Drawing upon Sutomo's (2014) character education framework, we analyzed character building as a four-stage developmental cycle: moral knowledge (*ilm*), moral feeling (*hal* / *niyyah*), moral practice (*amal*), and formed habit (*istiqamah*), underpinned by living teacher role-modelling (*qudwah hasanah*). We exposed the critical flaw in moral education that treats ethics as mere rule-memorization detached from habitual school practice.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. What is the precise linguistic root and core theological meaning of *Ibadat* in Islamic scholarship? *(Remember)*
   - A) Derived from *ilm*, meaning the acquisition of theoretical knowledge.
   - B) Derived from *abd*, meaning willing submission, servitude, and devotion to Allah.
   - C) Derived from *adl*, signifying the administration of legal justice.
   - D) Derived from *amal*, denoting physical labour performed in the community.

2. According to Surah Al-Ankabut (29:45), what is the direct moral and behavioral outcome of regular, sincere Salat? *(Understand)*
   - A) It guarantees immediate worldly wealth and social status.
   - B) It exempts the worshipper from civic and financial duties.
   - C) It actively restrains the believer from shameful and unjust deeds.
   - D) It substitutes for the necessity of acquiring professional knowledge.

3. Surah Al-Baqarah (2:183) states that fasting (*Saum*) was ordained for believers in order to achieve which primary spiritual capacity? *(Understand)*
   - A) Physical endurance for agricultural labour.
   - B) Taqwa, or protective consciousness of God and moral self-restraint.
   - C) Total withdrawal from everyday family and community responsibilities.
   - D) Fasting replaces the legal requirement to pay annual Zakat.

4. A merchant in Hyderabad possesses gold and cash exceeding the nisab threshold continuously for one full lunar year. Under Islamic law, which action correctly fulfills his Zakat obligation? *(Apply)*
   - A) Donating used office furniture to a local community centre at his convenience.
   - B) Paying 2.5% of his surplus wealth to eligible categories specified in Qur'an 9:60.
   - C) Spending 10% of his gross annual revenue on street lighting in his commercial market.
   - D) Lending money without interest to his wealthy business partner.

5. How does the wearing of the unstitched white *ihram* during Hajj directly promote social equality among Muslims? *(Apply)*
   - A) It allows pilgrims to display their distinct national and tribal symbols openly.
   - B) It identifies which pilgrims belong to wealthy ruling dynasties.
   - C) It removes all visible indicators of wealth, social class, and ethnic rank.
   - D) It signifies that educated professionals are exempt from standing at Arafat.

6. In classical Islamic ethics, how did scholars such as Imam Al-Ghazali define a virtuous character trait (*khuluq*)? *(Remember)*
   - A) An occasional good deed performed under legal or social compulsion.
   - B) A public display of piety designed to secure community approval.
   - C) An ingrained internal disposition from which virtuous deeds proceed spontaneously.
   - D) The ability to recite moral poetry without understanding its meaning.

7. How does the Quranic command to "be with those who are true" (Surah At-Tawbah 9:119) apply directly to a school teacher's daily professional duties? *(Understand)*
   - A) By maintaining completely authentic attendance, grading, and examination records.
   - B) By agreeing with every policy proposed by senior administrators without question.
   - C) By ensuring that only students from affluent families receive praise in class.
   - D) By refusing to speak with parents regarding pupil academic difficulties.

8. A high school teacher in Sukkur is pressured by an influential relative to award top marks to a student whose test paper scored 52%. Which response best reflects the Quranic command of *Adl* (justice) in Surah An-Nisa (4:135)? *(Apply)*
   - A) Granting the student 90% to avoid family conflict and preserve family honour.
   - B) Lowering the student's mark to 40% out of resentment toward the relative's interference.
   - C) Impartially recording the earned 52% and offering after-school academic help to the student.
   - D) Letting the other students in class vote on whether the relative's request should be granted.

9. In Islamic pedagogy, why is *Sabr* considered an active, positive professional virtue rather than passive resignation? *(Understand)*
   - A) Because it allows educators to ignore disruptive classroom behavior without taking action.
   - B) Because it requires emotional self-regulation, perseverance in coaching struggling learners, and avoiding harsh anger.
   - C) Because it teaches students that academic failure is determined entirely by fate.
   - D) Because it replaces the need for thorough curriculum planning and instructional design.

10. A school headteacher notices that students score 100% on written Islamiat tests about honesty but routinely copy homework and cheat during tests. Applying Sutomo's (2014) character education framework, what is the primary diagnosis of this problem? *(Apply)*
    - A) The school has failed to make the written examinations sufficiently difficult.
    - B) The curriculum focused solely on cognitive moral knowledge (*ilm*) while neglecting affective conviction, deliberate practice, and habituation.
    - C) Character building in schools should be abandoned because moral qualities are purely genetic.
    - D) The students require physical punishment rather than values-based instruction.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. State the linguistic origin of *Ibadat* and explain in two sentences how this definition connects formal ritual worship to daily moral conduct. *(Understand)*

2. Identify two distinct benefits of performing Salat in congregation (*jama'ah*), one focusing on the individual worshipper and one focusing on social community relations. *(Understand)*

3. In two to three sentences, contrast the spiritual self-restraint (*taqwa*) developed through Saum with mere physical starvation. *(Analyze)*

4. Distinguish between obligatory Zakat and voluntary sadaqah across three specific legal or practical dimensions. *(Analyze)*

5. Explain in two sentences how the rites of Hajj (specifically the ihram and the gathering at Arafat) challenge racial and socioeconomic prejudice. *(Understand)*

6. Using the metaphor of a tree, explain in three sentences the functional relationship between faith (*Iman*), worship (*Ibadat*), and morality (*Akhlaq*). *(Understand)*

7. Explain in two sentences how the Islamic concept of *Amanah* (trusteeship) applies to an educator's responsibility toward classroom assessment and test confidentiality. *(Analyze)*

8. Contrast impartial justice (*Adl*) with nepotism in a school setting, citing the core principle established in Surah An-Nisa (4:135). *(Analyze)*

9. Identify the three classical dimensions of *Sabr* recognized by Islamic scholars, and give one concrete classroom example of how a teacher exercises patience. *(Understand)*

10. According to Sutomo (2014), why does teaching ethical rules as abstract cognitive facts fail to build stable moral character? State two additional pedagogical components required for genuine *akhlaq* formation. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Analyze how the regular practice of Salat (daily prayer) and Saum (fasting in Ramadan) functions as an integrated psychological and social training system. In your essay, examine how these two acts of worship cultivate internal self-regulation (*taqwa*) and evaluate how their communal observance breaks down socioeconomic barriers in Pakistani society. *(Analyze)*

2. Evaluate the economic and social significance of institutional Zakat as outlined in Qur'an 9:60 compared to discretionary voluntary charity (*sadaqah*). In your response, analyze how Zakat protects the dignity of recipients, prevents wealth hoarding, and establishes an institutional safety net for vulnerable citizens in a developing country. *(Evaluate)*

3. Analyze the four cardinal Islamic virtues - truthfulness (*sidq*), justice (*adl*), honesty (*amanah*), and patience (*sabr*) - in the context of professional education. Present a realistic scenario from a Pakistani school where two or more of these virtues are tested, and evaluate how an educator should navigate the dilemma while upholding professional ethics. *(Analyze)*

4. Critically evaluate the concept of teacher role-modelling (*qudwah hasanah*) in character education. Drawing upon Sutomo's (2014) character education framework, contrast the developmental impact of verbal moral instruction versus observed teacher conduct, and justify why institutional integrity is indispensable for cultivating *akhlaq* in learners. *(Evaluate)*

5. Develop a comprehensive, school-wide character education proposal for a Pakistani secondary school titled "From Rules to Living Akhlaq". Your proposal must integrate Sutomo's (2014) four-stage cycle - moral knowledge (*ilm*), moral feeling (*hal* / *niyyah*), moral practice (*amal*), and habituation (*istiqamah*) - into specific school routines, curriculum activities, and assessment practices across both academic and non-academic domains. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - *Ibadat* derives from *abd* (servant/slave), expressing total submission, conscious obedience, and loving servitude to Allah in every sphere of life.
2. C - Surah Al-Ankabut (29:45) explicitly states that genuine prayer restrains the worshipper from shameful and unjust deeds.
3. B - Surah Al-Baqarah (2:183) specifies that fasting is ordained so that believers may develop *taqwa* (God-consciousness and moral self-restraint).
4. B - Zakat is an obligatory 2.5% annual levy on qualifying surplus wealth held for one year, distributed strictly to the eight categories named in Qur'an 9:60.
5. C - The simple two-piece unstitched white cloth strips all outward symbols of wealth, tribe, nationality, and social rank, placing all human beings on an identical level before God.
6. C - Classical scholars defined *khuluq* as an ingrained, stable internal state of the soul from which moral actions proceed smoothly and spontaneously without artificial strain.
7. A - Surah At-Tawbah (9:119) demands total truthfulness in words, records, and professional deeds, requiring educators to maintain honest attendance and uncompromised grading.
8. C - Surah An-Nisa (4:135) commands believers to stand firm for justice even against parents or relatives, forbidding bias based on wealth, kinship, or social influence.
9. B - In Islamic thought, *sabr* is dynamic endurance and active self-regulation, enabling teachers to remain calm, avoid harsh retaliation, and persistently guide learners.
10. B - Sutomo (2014) highlights that cognitive knowledge (*ilm*) alone is insufficient; character requires moral feeling (*hal*), repeated deliberate practice (*amal*), and living role models.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model answer:** *Ibadat* derives from the root *abd* (servant), signifying willing submission and loving servitude to Allah. Because servitude to God requires obeying divine guidance in all affairs, any lawful, constructive action - such as honest teaching, caring for family, or helping a neighbour - becomes an act of worship when performed with sincere, righteous intention.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for correct linguistic root (*abd* / servant); 1 mark for core definition (submission and devotion to God); 1 mark for explaining how sincere intention transforms daily work into worship.

2. **Model answer:** For the individual, congregational Salat instills punctuality, mental focus, and personal humility before God. For the community, congregational prayer dissolves social hierarchies as rich and poor stand shoulder to shoulder in identical rows, cultivating mutual brotherhood and collective solidarity five times daily.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for individual spiritual/psychological benefit (humility, punctuality, focus); 1.5 marks for collective social benefit (egalitarian rows, removal of social rank, community solidarity).

3. **Model answer:** Physical starvation is merely physiological deprivation of nutrition, which can occur involuntarily or without ethical awareness. Saum, by contrast, is conscious, voluntary self-restraint undertaken purely for Allah, which trains the will (*dabt-e-nafs*), activates *taqwa* (God-consciousness), and awakens deep empathy for disadvantaged fellow human beings.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for defining physical starvation as mere biological deprivation; 1 mark for defining Saum as voluntary spiritual restraint; 1 mark for linking Saum to the cultivation of taqwa and empathy.

4. **Model answer:** First, Zakat is a mandatory religious pillar (*fard*) with legal obligation, whereas sadaqah is entirely voluntary (*nafl*). Second, Zakat has a fixed rate (2.5% on qualifying surplus wealth above the nisab threshold), while sadaqah has no prescribed rate or minimum amount. Third, Zakat must be distributed strictly among the eight categories designated in Qur'an 9:60, whereas sadaqah can be given freely to any person or charitable purpose.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for legal obligation difference (fard vs nafl); 1 mark for rate/threshold difference (fixed 2.5% above nisab vs flexible); 1 mark for recipient difference (strictly 8 categories in Qur'an 9:60 vs open).

5. **Model answer:** The universal wearing of the white *ihram* strips away all outward markers of caste, wealth, and nationality, ensuring that monarchs and labourers are visually indistinguishable. Standing together on the plain of Arafat reinforces that all human beings share equal dignity before their Creator, with superiority determined solely by personal righteousness.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for explaining the egalitarian symbolism of the ihram; 1.5 marks for explaining the gathering at Arafat as a demonstration of human equality before God.

6. **Model answer:** Faith (*Iman*) represents the root of the tree embedded deeply in the heart, providing conviction and purpose. Worship (*Ibadat*) represents the trunk and branches that draw regular spiritual nourishment through prayer, fasting, and charity. Morality (*Akhlaq*) represents the ripe fruit produced by the tree; without healthy roots and nourishment, no good fruit can grow, and a tree bearing toxic fruit reveals a diseased root.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for correctly assigning Iman as the root; 1 mark for assigning Ibadat as the trunk/branches providing nourishment; 1 mark for assigning Akhlaq as the visible moral fruit.

7. **Model answer:** *Amanah* requires educators to recognize that pupils, institutional resources, and evaluation records are sacred trusts placed in their care by families and society. Specifically in testing, *Amanah* demands absolute confidentiality of examination papers and uncompromised accuracy in recording marks, treating grading as a divine trust that cannot be compromised for personal favors.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for defining students and assessment as a sacred trust (*amanah*); 1.5 marks for connecting it to examination confidentiality and grading integrity.

8. **Model answer:** Nepotism awards grades, opportunities, or disciplinary exemptions based on family loyalty, wealth, or tribal affiliations. In contrast, *Adl* (justice), as commanded in Surah An-Nisa (4:135), demands that teachers evaluate every student with objective impartiality, standing firm for truth even when it conflicts with the personal interests of relatives or influential patrons.
   - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for defining nepotism as bias based on kinship or social connections; 1 mark for defining Adl as objective, principled fairness; 1 mark for citing Surah An-Nisa (4:135) as the basis for resisting bias.

9. **Model answer:** The three classical dimensions of *Sabr* are: patience in steadfastly performing righteous duties (*ta'ah*), patience in restraining oneself from sin and misconduct (*ma'siyah*), and patience during adversity and hardship (*musa'ib*). In the classroom, a teacher exercises *sabr* by remaining calm, patient, and constructive when helping a struggling learner master a difficult concept, deliberately refraining from sarcastic remarks or physical punishment.
   - **Marks (3):** 1.5 marks for naming the three dimensions (patience in obedience, in refraining from wrongdoing, and during adversity); 1.5 marks for providing a concrete classroom management example showing emotional self-regulation.

10. **Model answer:** Cognitive rule-memorization addresses only the intellect (*ilm*), leaving personal emotions, intentions, and behavioral habits untouched. According to Sutomo (2014), genuine character formation additionally requires: (1) moral feeling and sincere intention (*hal* / *niyyah*) to foster empathy and reverence for God, and (2) consistent practice (*amal*) and habituation (*istiqamah*) supported by authentic teacher role-modelling (*qudwah hasanah*).
    - **Marks (3):** 1 mark for explaining why cognitive knowledge alone fails; 1 mark for identifying moral feeling/intention (*hal* / *niyyah*); 1 mark for identifying habituation (*istiqamah*) and teacher role-modelling (*qudwah hasanah*).

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Integrated psychological and social mechanisms of Salat and Saum

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Doctrinal accuracy and textual foundations (0-3 marks):* Accurately cites Qur'an 29:45 and 2:183; clearly defines the individual spiritual objectives of both practices (dhikr, taqwa, self-control, spiritual renewal).
  - *Psychological analysis of self-regulation (0-3 marks, **Analyze**):* Critically analyzes how the rhythmic interruption of daily routine by Salat and the voluntary physical abstinence of Saum systematically retrain human willpower, emotional impulses, and moral mindfulness.
  - *Evaluation of social egalitarianism (0-3 marks):* Evaluates the specific mechanisms through which congregational prayer (shared rows, identical prostration) and Ramadan fasting (felt hunger, shared iftar, communal charity) dismantle socioeconomic and tribal hierarchies in Pakistani communities.
  - *Clarity and academic coherence (0-1 mark):* Written in clear, coherent academic prose with accurate terminology.

#### ERQ 2: Institutional Zakat versus discretionary voluntary charity

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Understanding of legal parameters (0-2 marks):* Accurately details the specific rules of Zakat (2.5% rate, nisab threshold, annual assessment) and cites the eight designated beneficiary categories in Surah At-Tawbah (9:60).
  - *Analysis of systemic poverty alleviation (0-3 marks, **Analyze**):* Analyzes how Zakat functions as an institutional mechanism that systematically circulates capital from surplus holders to marginalized groups, preventing wealth concentration.
  - *Comparative evaluation of human dignity (0-3 marks, **Evaluate**):* Evaluates how receiving Zakat as an established divine right (*haqq*) preserves the self-respect and psychological dignity of recipients, contrasting it with the patronising dynamics often accompanying discretionary voluntary charity (*sadaqah*).
  - *Contextual application to Pakistan (0-2 marks):* Applies the analysis to contemporary socioeconomic realities in Pakistan, proposing realistic insights for community and educational welfare.

#### ERQ 3: Applying cardinal virtues to resolve school ethical dilemmas

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Virtue definitions and Quranic foundations (0-2 marks):* Accurately defines *Sidq*, *Adl*, *Amanah*, and *Sabr* with corresponding Quranic textual references (9:119, 4:135, 4:58, 2:153).
  - *Scenario design and dilemma realism (0-2 marks):* Presents a authentic, complex Pakistani school scenario (such as examination integrity, mark inflation under administrative pressure, or resource allocation).
  - *Ethical analysis and conflict resolution (0-4 marks, **Analyze**):* Critically dissects the conflicting pressures (family loyalty, administrative coercion, career safety) and demonstrates how applying *Adl* and *Amanah* produces an uncompromised, principled professional decision.
  - *Pedagogical justification (0-2 marks):* Justifies how the resolved dilemma models moral courage and ethical consistency for students and the wider school community.

#### ERQ 4: Teacher role-modelling (Qudwah Hasanah) in character education

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Theoretical grounding in Sutomo's framework (0-3 marks):* Explains Sutomo's (2014) character education framework, explicitly articulating the distinction between cognitive knowledge (*ilm*) and internalised character (*khuluq*).
  - *Critical evaluation of verbal preaching vs observed action (0-4 marks, **Evaluate**):* Rigorously evaluates why lectures on morality fail when teachers exhibit contradictory behavior, analyzing the cognitive dissonance and cynicism created when educators act unjustly or dishonestly.
  - *Institutional and classroom strategy (0-2 marks):* Formulates concrete institutional guidelines (transparent grading, dignified discipline, respectful communication) that ensure teachers embody *qudwah hasanah* daily.
  - *Structure and coherence (0-1 mark):* Well-organized essay with professional, accessible vocabulary.

#### ERQ 5: Comprehensive school-wide character education proposal

- **Analytic rubric (10 marks total):**
  - *Curricular integration across Sutomo's 4 stages (0-3 marks):* Seamlessly weaves moral knowledge (*ilm*), moral feeling (*hal* / *niyyah*), deliberate practice (*amal*), and habituation (*istiqamah*) into a coherent multi-grade framework.
  - *Concrete school routines and behavioral norms (0-3 marks):* Proposes specific, realistic school routines (peer dispute mediation, anonymous test marking, collaborative community service, pupil-led assembly reflection) grounded in Islamic virtues.
  - *Analytical justification of institutional impact (0-3 marks, **Analyze**):* Analyzes how the proposed practices prevent common school ethical failures (cheating, bullying, favouritism) and justifies how values-based self-regulation replaces fear-based punitive discipline.
  - *Proposal feasibility and clarity (0-1 mark):* Structured as an actionable, professionally written policy document for a Pakistani school administrator.
