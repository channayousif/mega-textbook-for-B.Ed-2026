# Islamic Studies - Course Overview

This course gives you a full picture of the beliefs, teachings, moral values and social system of Islam. It asks you to apply Islamic principles in personal, social and professional life, and above all in your work as a teacher. It also looks at the part Islam plays in building character, keeping social harmony and supporting national development in Pakistan.

## Course objectives

The course guide states these objectives:

1. Understand the basic beliefs and teachings of Islam.
2. Explain the sources of Islamic law and guidance.
3. Apply Islamic ethical values in daily and professional life.
4. Understand the Islamic social system and its relevance today.
5. Promote tolerance, peace, and moral development through education.

## Units

The guide sets out sixteen weeks. This textbook groups the fifteen teaching weeks into seven units; Week 16 is for review and the final assessment.

| Unit | Title | Weeks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to Islamic Studies and the Fundamental Beliefs of Islam | 1-2 |
| 2 | Sources of Islamic Knowledge: the Qur'an and the Sunnah | 3-5 |
| 3 | Worship and the Moral System of Islam | 6-7 |
| 4 | The Social System and the State in Islam | 8-9 |
| 5 | The Economic and Political Systems of Islam | 10-11 |
| 6 | Islam, Education, Civilization and Contemporary Issues | 12-14 |
| 7 | Islamic Ethics in Professional Life | 15 |

Units are published as they are written.

## Assessment

The guide gives no assessment weights for this course, so the programme default applies: 40% formative and 60% summative. Week 16 includes student presentations and discussions. Your teacher will explain which work is collected.

## Reading

The guide recommends the Holy Qur'an with translation and tafseer, selected ahadith from *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*, *Islami Nazriya-e-Hayat*, and Abul A'la Maududi's *Towards Understanding Islam*. Qur'anic verses are cited by surah and ayah, and ahadith by collection and number, so that you can look each one up in the full text. Each unit lists the sources it draws on in its "Further reading" sections. Sources are cited by reference only and are never reproduced.

This course is for Muslim students. The Scheme of Studies offers GETH-401 Ethics as the alternative course for non-Muslim students.
