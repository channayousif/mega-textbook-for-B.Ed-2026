# Unit 4 - Assessment and review

## Unit summary

The Islamic social system concerns relationships of care and responsibility, within families and beyond them. Rights of people require action: returning property, hearing a person fairly or making information usable. A child's care is not a reward for good behaviour. Equal human dignity does not require identical provision where needs differ. A difference in support needs a relevant reason; wealth, family connection or personal liking does not establish such a reason.

The state topic extends the question of responsibility to public authority. Allah's sovereignty, khilafat as entrusted responsibility, consultation and justice provide concepts for examining authority. A modern procedure must be assessed by how it operates. Consultation requires more than a late announcement. Voting can help reach a shared decision without guaranteeing that every possible outcome is just.

When evaluating an argument about Islam and democracy, define the disputed feature, use relevant evidence and explain the link. Distinguish a passage from an interpretation and from your own application. Consider a serious objection and state what your conclusion does and does not establish. A school case can help explain reasoning, but one council decision cannot establish the character of an entire state.

## Summative assessment

Attempt the questions before reading the final guidance. MCQs have one best option. RRQs require bounded answers in the stated form. ERQs require a reasoned response, usually 400–500 words each. Use references from the topic pages; no direct quotation is required. Marks below support self-study and do not replace an institution's assessment rules.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which statement best describes a family system? *(Remember)*
   - A) A record of everyone using the same address.
   - B) A network of care and responsibilities among related people.
   - C) A ranking of households by income and property.
   - D) A school list restricted to fathers of pupils.

2. What does Huquq ul-Ibad refer to? *(Remember)*
   - A) The timetable for public meetings.
   - B) The selection of a council chair.
   - C) The rights of people.
   - D) The division of lesson periods.

3. A child returns a borrowed ruler but has damaged it. Which action best addresses the remaining responsibility? *(Apply)*
   - A) Help arrange a fair repair or replacement.
   - B) Treat returning it as settling the damage too.
   - C) Ask the owner to forget it because the borrower apologised.
   - D) Let the borrower's friendship with the teacher decide.

4. A school provides large print for a pupil with low vision. What best explains the difference in materials? *(Apply)*
   - A) A reward for the pupil's family contribution.
   - B) A change to the worth of the other pupils.
   - C) A preference for one household's status.
   - D) A response to a relevant access need.

5. Some pupils are cared for by grandparents. Which invitation process best supports family care? *(Apply)*
   - A) Invite fathers and ask children to explain other arrangements publicly.
   - B) Invite recorded carers and offer a way to clarify the message.
   - C) Invite donors and ask them to represent other households.
   - D) Invite the oldest male relative without checking care arrangements.

6. What does shura mean in this unit? *(Remember)*
   - A) Consultation about shared affairs.
   - B) Ownership of public resources by the chair.
   - C) Announcement of decisions after implementation.
   - D) Selection by family seniority alone.

7. Which distinction between state and government is most useful? *(Understand)*
   - A) The state is a meeting, while government is a vote.
   - B) The state is a household, while government is a council.
   - C) The state is a continuing public order, while government administers it at a given time.
   - D) The state is a private owner, while government is its family network.

8. A council chair asks for views only after an irreversible purchase. What change would most directly strengthen consultation? *(Apply)*
   - A) Announce the purchase in more colours.
   - B) Repeat the chair's reasons at the next assembly.
   - C) Count how many notices were delivered afterwards.
   - D) Hear affected groups while feasible choices remain open.

9. In the Islamic conception examined here, where does ultimate sovereignty lie? *(Remember)*
   - A) With the oldest officeholder.
   - B) With Allah.
   - C) With the highest donor.
   - D) With the best-known household.

10. A majority votes to exclude one class from a shared library without a relevant reason. Which response best applies the unit's distinction? *(Apply)*
   - A) Examine the exclusion's justice as well as the voting procedure.
   - B) Treat the vote count as sufficient evidence that exclusion is fair.
   - C) Treat the chair's title as sufficient evidence that exclusion is fair.
   - D) Replace the vote with the largest donor's preference.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In three sentences, explain two ways family responsibility differs from merely sharing a house. Use one example involving care across generations. *(Understand)*
2. In three or four sentences, explain why a dependent child's care should not be treated as a reward for obeying a teacher. Identify an age-appropriate responsibility the child can still practise. *(Understand)*
3. In four sentences, distinguish duties towards Allah and rights of people. Give one example of each and explain why these are not sealed areas of life. *(Understand)*
4. A school gives scarce reading-support places to donors' children rather than pupils with the greatest reading need. In four sentences, analyze the relevance of its selection criterion and propose one defensible alternative. *(Analyze)*
5. A trainee says that naming women's rights means every family entitlement must have an identical form. In three sentences, explain the flaw in that inference and identify a Qur'anic reference used in the topic to establish that women have claims. *(Understand)*
6. In three sentences, explain why describing authority as a public trust differs from treating office as private ownership. Apply the distinction to a library budget. *(Understand)*
7. In four sentences, explain how Allah's sovereignty and entrusted human responsibility differ. State one conclusion about a ruler that does not follow from the ruler's title. *(Understand)*
8. A school council hears every group before purchasing resources, but then favours the chair's relatives without reasons. In four sentences, analyze which principle remains unmet and why consultation alone does not establish it. *(Analyze)*
9. In four sentences, distinguish a principle, a procedure and an outcome using consultation, a council meeting and a library allocation. Explain why one cannot substitute for another. *(Analyze)*
10. In four sentences, explain why two speakers may disagree about "democracy" before discussing the same question. Distinguish a participation procedure from a claim about unlimited moral authority. *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A fictional school invites only fathers to learning discussions, although some recorded carers are mothers or grandparents. Its staff say that one invitation rule shows respect for all families. Analyze that reasoning and propose a fairer process. Use two relevant Qur'anic references, explain how your proposal supports care, and identify one fact the school should check before implementation. *(Analyze)*
2. A school has eight sets of reading materials and sixteen applicants. Its proposed rule favours children of donors. Create and defend an alternative allocation process using equal dignity, relevant need and correction of mistakes. Explain why your different treatment is justified and include one way to communicate the decision without exposing private family information. *(Create)*
3. Evaluate the argument, "Islam requires consultation, so any majority decision is automatically Islamic and just." Define its terms, use a Qur'anic passage and the mapped scholarly reading, consider a serious defence and objection, and reach a bounded judgement. Distinguish evidence from your own school application. *(Evaluate)*
4. A fictional public education committee spends shared funds on one member's preferred project, announces the decision afterwards and refuses questions because it claims to hold legitimate authority. Analyze the decision through public trust, entrusted responsibility, consultation and justice. Propose a realistic route for explanation and correction. State what further evidence would be needed before judging the whole institution. *(Analyze)*
5. Integrative task: a fictional school council consults only donor families, votes to reserve reading-support places for their children, and calls the result both socially just and democratic. Evaluate both descriptions using the two topics. Design an improved procedure that protects family participation and relevant learning needs, makes reasons available and allows correction. Explain one limit of treating this case as evidence about a state. *(Evaluate / Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

This section is intentionally public self-study support. It is separate from a restricted LMS question bank. Equivalent reasoning can earn credit. Assess the accuracy and relevance of reasons, rather than agreement with a party, a teacher's preference or a blanket slogan about religion and democracy. For the source passages, use the links in the topic pages and explain the connection in your own words.

### MCQ answer key

1. B - a family system concerns relationships, care and responsibilities, not only residence.
2. C - Huquq ul-Ibad identifies the rights of people.
3. A - repair or replacement addresses harm that returning the damaged item has not resolved.
4. D - large print supports access to the same learning purpose through a relevant difference in provision.
5. B - the process recognises actual care arrangements and checks whether communication is usable.
6. A - shura means consultation about shared affairs.
7. C - a continuing state is distinct from the government administering it at a particular time.
8. D - meaningful consultation occurs while relevant views can still affect feasible choices.
9. B - the Islamic conception discussed locates ultimate sovereignty with Allah, not an officeholder.
10. A - a majority procedure and the justice of its outcome require separate examination.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

Each response carries 4 marks. Award marks for distinct points, avoiding double credit for repeating one idea. An example may differ from the model while showing the same understanding. Do not require a direct quotation. For a four-mark response with a missing central distinction, credit only the accurate points actually demonstrated.

1. Family responsibility includes care and duties across generations, while shared residence describes a location. A grandparent may care for a child whose parents work elsewhere, and an adult child may help a parent living in another home. The relationships do not cease at the house boundary. **Marks (4):** 1 for distinguishing relationship from residence; 1 for care; 1 for a responsibility across generations; 1 for an example linking these ideas.

2. A dependent child continues to need care after misbehaviour. The adult can address conduct without making food, protection or belonging a prize. The child can practise returning materials or speaking respectfully within their capacity. **Marks (4):** 1 for the continuing claim to care; 1 for separating discipline from withdrawal of care; 1 for an age-appropriate responsibility; 1 for explaining the adult's different responsibility.

3. Duties towards Allah include worship; rights of people include respectful treatment or return of property. Returning a borrowed book respects its owner's claim and can also express obedience to Allah. The categories distinguish emphasis rather than isolating religious life from social life. **Marks (4):** 1 for each accurate category with an example, to a maximum of 2; 1 for the overlap; 1 for explaining it rather than merely naming it. Qur'an 4:36 is a relevant reference.

4. Donation is not evidence of a child's reading need. It gives an unrelated advantage in a programme intended to support learning. A recent reading task can identify need, with a fair method for ties and later review. **Marks (4):** 1 for identifying the programme's purpose; 1 for analyzing why donation is irrelevant; 1 for a relevant alternative criterion; 1 for explaining how the procedure can remain fair or be corrected.

5. Establishing that women possess claims does not establish that every legal entitlement has the same form for everyone. The topic uses Qur'an 4:7 because it names women as well as men as holders of inheritance shares. It does not teach a calculation of those shares. **Marks (4):** 1 for identifying the invalid inference; 1 for the distinction between dignity or having rights and identical entitlements; 1 for the reference; 1 for its relevant and limited use.

6. Public trust requires using resources for the purpose and people they serve. Private ownership would let the officeholder treat the budget as personal property. Library funds therefore need a reasoned allocation for the school, with explanation and possible correction. **Marks (4):** 1 for the entrusted purpose; 1 for the ownership distinction; 1 for a relevant budget application; 1 for accountability linked to that application.

7. Allah's sovereignty concerns ultimate authority; human responsibility concerns accountable use of entrusted power. A ruler remains a human decision-maker whose acts need examination. A title does not prove that every decision is just or divinely approved. **Marks (4):** 1 for ultimate authority; 1 for human responsibility; 1 for distinguishing them; 1 for identifying the unsupported inference about a ruler.

8. Justice remains unmet if the relatives receive an advantage without a relevant reason. Hearing people is part of consultation, but fair allocation needs defensible criteria too. The council should explain the choice and permit correction rather than point only to attendance at meetings. **Marks (4):** 1 for identifying justice; 1 for linking the defect to unjustified preference; 1 for distinguishing hearing from fair decision; 1 for an appropriate corrective step.

9. Consultation is a guiding principle about shared affairs. A council meeting is a procedure that may serve it. A library allocation is the resulting decision, which still needs examination for justice. **Marks (4):** 1 for each correctly classified element, to a maximum of 3; 1 for explaining that the occurrence of a meeting does not establish either meaningful consultation or a just allocation.

10. One speaker may mean participation in choosing officeholders, while another means unlimited power to make moral rules. These are different claims about democracy. Defining the word allows a focused comparison; it does not guarantee agreement about institutions or their limits. **Marks (4):** 1 for identifying the ambiguity; 1 for the procedure meaning; 1 for the unlimited-authority meaning; 1 for explaining the need for a narrower question.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task carries 12 marks, awarded through four criteria worth 0–3 each. Apply the performance descriptions below to each task's specific criteria. A response can earn a high score with a different conclusion when its reasoning, sources and treatment of objections are sound. Unsupported factual claims should not receive evidence credit.

| Score per criterion | Performance description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Missing, irrelevant, or based on a central misunderstanding. |
| 1 | Relevant idea named, but mainly asserted; little connection to the case. |
| 2 | Accurate idea explained with a case detail or relevant reason; one important link or qualification remains incomplete. |
| 3 | Accurate, specific reasoning joins the concept, case and evidence; it addresses the criterion's required qualification or practical test. |

1. **Family participation analysis:** identifies whose care role the rule overlooks and explains why "one rule" is insufficient. **Source reasoning:** uses two relevant references, such as 30:21, 4:36 or 49:13, and distinguishes the application from the passage. **Defensible alternative:** proposes inviting recorded carers with usable communication, explaining how this supports the child. **Evidence and limits:** identifies a relevant fact to check, such as the current carer contact, without assuming household roles. Full-credit analysis connects exclusion to care and participation rather than merely declaring the school wrong.

2. **Justice and dignity:** distinguishes equal worth from identical allocation and identifies why donation is unrelated to reading support. **Allocation design:** provides relevant need criteria, a defensible treatment of equally eligible applicants and a review point. **Communication and correction:** explains decisions without public family ranking and provides a route to correct mistaken information. **Justification:** weighs a possible objection, such as the apparent simplicity of a lottery, against the programme's purpose. Full-credit design explains why each step serves learning needs; it does not just list attractive procedures.

3. **Conceptual accuracy:** distinguishes consultation, majority procedure and moral limits. **Use of sources:** connects Qur'an 42:38 or 3:159 to consultation and uses Abdel Salam's mapped discussion as scholarship rather than universal agreement. **Evaluation:** gives a serious reason for a majority procedure, weighs its inability to guarantee every outcome's justice, and explains the inference being rejected. **Qualified judgement:** reaches a bounded conclusion with an example and identifies the example's limit. Full credit does not depend on accepting or rejecting democracy as an undefined whole.

4. **Public responsibility:** explains both entrusted purpose and why officeholders are not private owners. **Process analysis:** identifies missing prior consultation and lack of an explanation, using case details. **Justice and repair:** examines who benefits, what information is missing, and how reasons and correction could be obtained. **Bounded institutional judgement:** separates defects in this decision from a claim about the whole institution and names further evidence needed. A response should not assume that the preferred project has no merit simply because the process is defective.

5. **Integration:** connects family participation and learning needs with public responsibility instead of writing two unrelated topic summaries. **Evaluation of the claims:** tests both "socially just" and "democratic" against the specified exclusion and voting process. **Improved procedure:** includes usable invitations, relevant allocation criteria, recorded reasons and a correction route, explaining how each addresses a defect. **Evidence and limits:** uses relevant topic sources, considers an objection, and explains why one school council is not evidence of an entire state's character. Full-credit judgement acknowledges any value in a vote while examining the restricted participation and unjustified advantage.

For revision after any ERQ, underline one unsupported assertion in your response. Replace it with a reason linked to a source or a fact in the case. Then identify one conclusion you deliberately limited. These two checks reveal whether you have evaluated a decision or only attached a favourable or unfavourable label to it.
