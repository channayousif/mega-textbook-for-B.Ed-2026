# Introduction to Islamic Studies and the Fundamental Beliefs of Islam - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Islam means submission to Allah, freely chosen. A Muslim is a person who brings their moral choices into line with Allah's guidance, as the rest of creation follows the laws Allah has set for it. Islamic Studies is the organised study of Islam. Its branches include Qur'anic studies and tafsir, Hadith studies, Aqidah, Fiqh and Usul al-Fiqh, Seerah and history, Akhlaq, Islamic social, economic and political thought, and comparative religion. Its scope reaches from the beliefs of the heart to the public questions of law and government, so it is far wider than the rituals pupils often associate with school Islamiat.

Islam gives knowledge a very high place. The first revelation began with "Recite" (96:1), the Prophet (peace be upon him) was taught to pray for more knowledge (20:114), and the Qur'an asks whether those who know are equal to those who do not (39:9). Islamic education aims at the whole person: knowing Allah, building character and serving others, with the teacher as a role model. In Pakistan, Article 31 of the Constitution asks the state to make the teaching of the Qur'an and Islamiat compulsory.

Islam is a *din*, a complete code of life. Belief is its root, and its guidance reaches worship, morals, family and society, the economy and the state (2:208; 6:162; 5:3). "Complete" means fixed principles for every area of life, applied to new situations, not a separate rule for every case.

The beliefs of Islam rest on three foundations. Tauheed is the oneness of Allah in lordship, in His names and attributes, and in worship; it is more than believing that God exists, and its opposite is shirk. Risalat is Allah's guidance through messengers sent to every nation, ending with Muhammad (peace be upon him), the seal of the prophets and the pattern to follow (33:40; 33:21). Akhirat is the life after death, in which every deed is judged (99:7-8). The six articles of faith are belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day and the divine decree (*Sahih Muslim* 8a; 4:136). Belief in the decree does not remove responsibility (2:286; 13:11). Above all, belief is meant to show itself in conduct (2:177; 103:2-3). For a teacher, honest marking, punctuality and fair treatment of every pupil are where belief becomes visible.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All named pupils, teachers and schools below are invented teaching cases.

MCQs 1-5 and RRQs 1-5 address Topic 1.1; MCQs 6-10 and RRQs 6-10 address Topic 1.2. Extended tasks 1-2 address Topic 1.1, tasks 3-4 address Topic 1.2, and task 5 draws on both. Your course teacher decides which tasks to collect and how the course's 60% summative weighting is applied.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

Choose A, B, C or D. Each item is worth 1 mark.

1. In religion, the word *Islam* means: *(Remember)*
   - A) Following the customs of one's family
   - B) Submission to Allah, freely chosen
   - C) Membership of the Arab nation
   - D) Performing the five daily prayers
2. Which branch of Islamic Studies deals with rulings on worship and daily dealings? *(Remember)*
   - A) Seerah
   - B) Aqidah
   - C) Fiqh
   - D) Tafsir
3. The first words revealed to the Prophet (peace be upon him) were a command to: *(Remember)*
   - A) Pray
   - B) Fast
   - C) Migrate
   - D) Recite
4. A pupil asks, "Is a hadith I saw on Facebook really from the Prophet?" Which branch of Islamic Studies would answer this? *(Apply)*
   - A) Ulum al-Hadith
   - B) Comparative religion
   - C) Akhlaq
   - D) Islamic political thought
5. A teacher refuses a parent's gift offered in return for higher marks. Under the idea of Islam as a complete code of life, which area does this mainly show? *(Apply)*
   - A) Worship
   - B) Economic life: halal and haram earning
   - C) The state: consultation
   - D) Belief in the angels
6. *Tauheed al-Uluhiyyah* means: *(Remember)*
   - A) Allah alone creates and sustains everything
   - B) Allah's attributes are perfect and unlike anything in creation
   - C) Only Allah is to be worshipped and obeyed without limit
   - D) The Prophet is the last of the messengers
7. The Qur'an calls Muhammad (peace be upon him) "the seal of the prophets" in: *(Remember)*
   - A) 33:40
   - B) 112:1
   - C) 96:1
   - D) 2:30
8. Which of these is **not** one of the six articles of faith? *(Remember)*
   - A) Belief in the angels
   - B) Belief in the divine decree
   - C) Belief in the books
   - D) Belief in the Hajj
9. A pupil says, "I believe Allah exists and created the world, so I have full Tauheed." What does his statement leave out? *(Apply)*
   - A) Belief that Allah created the heavens
   - B) Worshipping and obeying Allah alone
   - C) Belief that the world had a beginning
   - D) Learning the Arabic of the *kalimah*
10. A teacher marks every test paper carefully at night, although no one will check her work. Which belief most directly explains her conduct? *(Apply)*
    - A) Belief in the books
    - B) Belief that every deed will be accounted for in the Akhirat
    - C) Belief that Islam began in Makkah
    - D) Belief that all prophets brought the same message

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

Answer each in the length stated. Each item is worth 3 marks.

1. In two or three sentences, explain the difference between Islamic Studies and school Islamiat as many pupils experience it. *(Understand)*
2. Explain, in three sentences, Maududi's point that the whole created world "submits" to Allah, and say what makes human submission different. *(Understand)*
3. Give two reasons, each with a Qur'anic reference, why Islam gives a high place to knowledge. *(Understand)*
4. A science teacher says, "Islamic education belongs only in the Islamiat period." In three or four sentences, analyse what an Islamic view of education would add to his lesson without changing its science. *(Analyze)*
5. Explain, in three sentences, what "complete" means and does not mean in the phrase "Islam is a complete code of life". *(Understand)*
6. Name the three aspects of Tauheed and give one effect of Tauheed on a person's conduct. *(Understand)*
7. Explain, in two or three sentences, why reason alone is not enough and Risalat is needed. *(Understand)*
8. A pupil says, "Belief is in the heart; copying in a test is a different thing." Using two Qur'anic references, explain in three or four sentences why this view is mistaken. *(Analyze)*
9. Explain, in three sentences, the difference between *Iman-e-Mujmal* and *Iman-e-Mufassal*. *(Understand)*
10. A pupil says, "If my result is already written, I need not study." Analyse this misunderstanding of the divine decree in three or four sentences, with two references. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

Answer in 400-600 words. Each task is worth 12 marks.

1. **More than rituals.** A Class 9 pupil says, "Islamiat is just rules for prayer and fasting." Analyse this view using the scope of Islamic Studies and the idea of Islam as a complete code of life. Use at least three branches, two Qur'anic references and one school example. *(Analyze)*
2. **The purpose of education.** Your school's head says, "Our job is to get pupils good board results. Character is the family's business." Evaluate this claim in the light of the Islamic view of the aims of education. Reach a clear judgement and justify it. *(Evaluate)*
3. **Belief at work.** Explain how belief in Akhirat shapes a Muslim teacher's everyday conduct. Analyse at least three specific situations from a teacher's week, showing in each the temptation, how the belief changes the choice, and the Qur'anic basis. *(Analyze)*
4. **Teaching the articles of faith.** Most pupils in a Class 8 can recite *Iman-e-Mufassal* but cannot explain it. Design a 35-minute lesson that teaches two articles of faith so that pupils understand them and can link them to conduct. Justify your design choices. *(Create)*
5. **Belief, knowledge and the whole of life.** "Tauheed is the root of a complete code of life." Analyse this statement, showing how Tauheed connects to at least four areas of life, and evaluate what it means for the way a Muslim teacher runs her classroom. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Islam means submission to Allah, freely chosen. A and C confuse the faith with family custom or ethnicity; D is one act of worship, not the meaning of the word.
2. **C.** Fiqh gives rulings on worship and daily dealings. Seerah is the Prophet's life, Aqidah is belief and Tafsir explains the Qur'an.
3. **D.** "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" (96:1) was the first revelation.
4. **A.** Checking whether a report really comes from the Prophet (peace be upon him) is the work of Ulum al-Hadith.
5. **B.** A gift in return for marks is a bribe; refusing it concerns halal and haram earning. It is also a moral matter, but of the options given, B fits best.
6. **C.** Uluhiyyah is oneness in worship. A describes Rububiyyah, B describes the names and attributes, and D is about Risalat.
7. **A.** 33:40 calls him "the Messenger of Allāh and seal [i.e., last] of the prophets". 112:1 is on Tauheed, 96:1 is the first revelation and 2:30 is on the human as *khalifah*.
8. **D.** Hajj is a pillar of Islam (an act of worship), not an article of faith.
9. **B.** He has accepted oneness in lordship but not oneness in worship and obedience. The Makkan idol-worshippers also accepted Allah as Creator (29:61).
10. **B.** Belief that every deed, even an atom's weight, will be seen and judged (99:7-8) gives her a reason to be careful when no human checks.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** School Islamiat often focuses on what to recite and how to perform rituals. Islamic Studies is the organised study of all of Islam: beliefs, sources, worship, morals, law, history and guidance for society. It also asks why, and what follows for the rest of life. **Marks (3):** 1 for school Islamiat's focus; 1 for the wider scope of Islamic Studies; 1 for the "why and what follows" point or an example.
2. **Model:** The sun, rivers and the cells of the body follow the laws Allah has set, so in that sense all creation submits. Human beings are free in their moral choices to obey or refuse. A person who freely chooses to obey Allah in these choices is a Muslim in the full sense. **Marks (3):** 1 for nature following Allah's laws; 1 for human moral freedom; 1 for free choice as the defining difference.
3. **Model:** The first revelation was a command to read (96:1). The Qur'an asks whether those who know are equal to those who do not (39:9), and teaches the prayer "My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (20:114). The human being is a *khalifah* (2:30) and must know how to care for what is entrusted. **Marks (3):** 1.5 for each reason with a correct reference (any two).
4. **Model:** The science stays the same, but the purpose widens: pupils learn the eye as one of the signs that invite reflection, not only for marks. Copying the diagram becomes a question of honesty and trust. Knowledge is to be used in service of others. So Islamic education changes the purpose, honesty and use of knowledge. **Marks (3):** 1 for keeping the science unchanged; 1 for at least two specific additions; 1 for the analytical point that what changes is purpose and values, not content.
5. **Model:** "Complete" means that the Qur'an and Sunnah give fixed principles for every area of life, such as justice, honesty and mercy (5:3). It does not mean a written rule for every new invention or case. Muslims apply the principles to new situations through methods scholars have developed. **Marks (3):** 1 for principles for all areas; 1 for "not a rule for every case"; 1 for application to new situations.
6. **Model:** Oneness in lordship (Rububiyyah), oneness in names and attributes (Asma wa Sifat), and oneness in worship (Uluhiyyah). One effect: a person who fears Allah alone is honest even when no one is watching, or treats all people as equal servants of one Lord (49:13). **Marks (3):** 2 for the three aspects (1 for two of them); 1 for a valid effect.
7. **Model:** Reason can show that the world has a Creator. It cannot tell with certainty how the Creator wants to be worshipped or what He has made lawful. The prophets bring this guidance and show it in their lives (16:36; 33:21). **Marks (3):** 1 for what reason can do; 1 for its limit; 1 for what the prophets supply.
8. **Model:** The Qur'an joins belief with action. 2:177 places belief in Allah and the Last Day beside keeping promises and patience, and 103:2-3 says people are in loss except those who believe and do righteous deeds. A pupil who believes Allah sees him and then copies has not let the belief do its work. **Marks (3):** 1 for each correctly used reference; 1 for applying the point to copying.
9. **Model:** *Iman-e-Mujmal* is faith in brief: belief in Allah as He is, with all His names and attributes, and acceptance of all His commands. *Iman-e-Mufassal* is faith in detail: it names the six articles one by one. Both state the same faith, one briefly and one in full. **Marks (3):** 1 for each correct description; 1 for the relationship between them.
10. **Model:** Belief in the decree means Allah knows and has measured all things (54:49). It does not remove human responsibility: each soul bears what it has earned (2:286), and change begins with what people do themselves (13:11). The right response is to study, pray and accept the result with patience. **Marks (3):** 1 for stating the belief correctly; 1 for the correction with two references; 1 for the conclusion about effort.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks; a fluent answer with weak reasoning cannot.

**ERQ 1: More than rituals**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope of Islamic Studies | Explains three or more branches accurately | Two branches accurate | One branch, or vague | Absent or wrong |
| Complete code of life | Explains *din* and several areas of life with two correct references | One reference, or areas only listed | Mentions the phrase without explanation | Absent |
| Analysis of the pupil's view (**Analyze**) | Shows exactly what the view includes and what it leaves out, using the school example | Shows what is left out in general terms | States the view is wrong without reasons | No analysis |
| Use of example | A specific, fitting school example used throughout | Example given but weakly linked | Generic example | None |

**ERQ 2: The purpose of education**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aims of Islamic education | States knowing Allah, building character and serving others, with support | Two aims explained | One aim | Absent |
| Qur'anic and scholarly support | Two or more fitting references, including the teacher as role model | One fitting reference | References misused | None |
| Evaluation of the claim (**Evaluate**) | Weighs what is right in the claim (results matter) against what it misses, and reaches a reasoned verdict | Mainly criticises or mainly supports, with reasons | Verdict with little reasoning | No evaluation |
| Practical consequence | Says clearly what the school should do differently | A general suggestion | A vague remark | None |

**ERQ 3: Belief at work**

**ERQ 4: Teaching the articles of faith**

| Link to conduct | Pupils are led to a specific link between each article and behaviour | Link for one article | Link stated by the teacher only | None |
| Justification | Each design choice justified by what pupils need | Some choices justified | Little justification | None |

**ERQ 5: Belief, knowledge and the whole of life**

| Connections to areas of life (**Analyze**) | Shows how Tauheed connects to four or more areas, with reasons | Three areas, or four listed without reasons | Two areas | None |
