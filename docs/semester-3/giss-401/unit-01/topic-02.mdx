# Topic 1.2 - The fundamental beliefs of Islam

## A real classroom situation

Saleem teaches Islamiat at a government boys' middle school in Larkana. In a Class 8 test he asks, "Write *Iman-e-Mufassal* and translate it." Almost every pupil writes the Arabic correctly and lists the articles of faith. Saleem is pleased, until he collects the papers. Two pupils have the same unusual spelling mistake in the same place. They sat next to each other.

The next day he asks the class, "What does believing in Allah mean?" A confident pupil, Bilal, answers, "It means believing that Allah exists. I believe that, so I have Tauheed." Saleem asks, "And when you copied in the test, did you believe that Allah saw you?" The class goes quiet. Bilal says, "Sir, belief is in the heart. The test is a different thing."

Saleem realises that his pupils can recite their beliefs without understanding them, and that they see no link between belief and behaviour. What do the fundamental beliefs of Islam actually say, and how should they change the way a person lives? That is the question of this topic.

## Explanation

The beliefs of Islam are called ***Aqaid*** (singular *aqidah*): firmly held convictions on which the rest of the religion rests. **Iman** means faith: accepting the truth with the heart and declaring it with the tongue. The Qur'an joins faith and conduct again and again. It says that all people are in loss except "those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience" (103:2-3). Belief is the root; righteous action is its fruit.

Scholars group the beliefs around three foundations: **Tauheed** (the oneness of Allah), **Risalat** (prophethood) and **Akhirat** (the life after death). The diagram below shows how they support each other.

<Figure id="fig-U1-4" src="/img/figures/giss-401/unit-01/fig-U1-4.svg" alt="Diagram of Tauheed, Risalat and Akhirat as three pillars standing on faith and holding up a believer's life and conduct, showing whom we worship, how we live and why it matters." />

Tauheed tells a person **whom** to worship. Risalat tells them **how** to live, through the guidance and example of the prophets. Akhirat tells them **why** it matters: every action will be accounted for. Remove any one pillar and the structure falls.

### Concept of Tauheed

#### What Tauheed means

**Tauheed** means the oneness of Allah. It is the first and central belief of Islam, declared in the first part of the *kalimah*: *La ilaha illallah*, "there is no god but Allah". Surah al-Ikhlas states it most briefly: "He is Allāh, [who is] One, Allāh, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent" (112:1-4). Another verse says, "There is nothing like unto Him" (42:11).

Scholars often explain Tauheed under three aspects. Together they show that Tauheed is more than accepting that God exists.

1. **Oneness in lordship** (*Tauheed al-Rububiyyah*). Allah alone creates, sustains and controls everything. No one shares this power with Him.
2. **Oneness in names and attributes** (*Tauheed al-Asma wa al-Sifat*). Allah's qualities, such as knowledge, mercy and power, are perfect and belong to Him alone. Nothing in creation is like Him (42:11).
3. **Oneness in worship** (*Tauheed al-Uluhiyyah*). Only Allah is to be worshipped, obeyed without limit and called upon in prayer. Every Muslim repeats this in each *rak'ah*: "It is You we worship and You we ask for help" (1:5).

The opposite of Tauheed is **shirk**: associating partners with Allah in any of these three aspects. The Qur'an calls it "a tremendous sin" (4:48).

#### The effects of Tauheed on a person's life

Tauheed is not only a statement. It changes how a person sees themselves and others.

- **Courage and self-respect.** A person who fears Allah alone need not bow to anyone out of fear or greed. A teacher with Tauheed does not flatter an officer to get a transfer.
- **Honesty when no one is watching.** Allah is All-Hearing and All-Seeing (42:11). A pupil who truly believes this has a reason not to copy, even when the teacher looks away.
- **Equality.** All people are servants of the same one Lord. The Qur'an says that "the most noble of you in the sight of Allāh is the most righteous of you" (49:13). A teacher with Tauheed does not favour the child of a landlord over the child of a labourer.
- **Hope and contentment.** Since provision and outcomes are in Allah's hands, a believer works hard but does not despair when results are poor.

#### Correcting a common misconception

A common belief is that **Tauheed only means believing that God exists**. This is Bilal's view. It is incomplete. Believing that Allah exists and created the world is the first aspect, oneness in lordship. The Qur'an points out that even the idol-worshippers of Makkah would say "Allāh" if asked who created the heavens and the earth (29:61). Yet they were not believers in Tauheed, because they worshipped others beside Him. Tauheed therefore also means accepting Allah's perfect attributes and worshipping and obeying Him alone. Bilal believes Allah exists, but in the test he acted as if Allah did not see him. Full Tauheed would have changed what he did.

#### Worked example: testing a statement against the three aspects

Here is how to check whether a statement shows full Tauheed. Take three statements a pupil might make.

| Statement | Which aspect is involved? | Does it show full Tauheed? |
|---|---|---|
| "Allah created the world, but my success depends only on pleasing my boss." | Lordship (who controls outcomes) and worship (whom one obeys without limit) | No. It accepts Allah as Creator but places a human being above His guidance |
| "Allah knows what I do even when I am alone." | Names and attributes (knowledge, seeing) | Yes, and it should lead to honest conduct |
| "I pray to Allah alone and ask Him alone for help in my exams, and I also study hard." | Worship and lordship | Yes. Asking Allah for help and making an effort go together |

When you test a statement, ask: does it accept Allah as the only Lord, does it respect His attributes, and does it give worship and final obedience to Him alone? You will use this method in the check questions.

### Risalat and Akhirat

#### Risalat: guidance through the prophets

**Risalat** means prophethood: Allah's sending of human messengers to guide people. A **rasul** (messenger) or **nabi** (prophet) receives revelation from Allah and conveys it. Reason can tell a person that the world has a Creator, but it cannot tell them, with certainty, how the Creator wants to be worshipped or what He has made lawful. The prophets bring that guidance and show it in their own lives.

The Qur'an teaches four points about Risalat.

1. **Every nation received guidance.** "We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying], 'Worship Allāh'" (16:36). All prophets brought the same core message of Tauheed.
2. **Muslims believe in all the prophets** and make "no distinction between any of His messengers" (2:285), including Ibrahim, Musa and Isa (peace be upon them).
3. **Muhammad (peace be upon him) is the last prophet.** The Qur'an calls him "the Messenger of Allāh and seal [i.e., last] of the prophets" (33:40). This belief in the finality of prophethood (*Khatm-e-Nabuwwat*) is an essential part of Muslim belief. No new prophet will come after him, and his guidance is for all people until the end of time. He was sent "as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107).
4. **The Prophet is the model to follow.** "There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allāh an excellent pattern" (33:21). Believing in Risalat means taking his life as the pattern for one's own.

For a teacher, the last point matters most. The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught with patience, mercy and personal example. Belief in Risalat asks a teacher to teach in the same way.

#### Akhirat: life after death and accountability

**Akhirat** means the Hereafter: the life that begins after death and does not end. Belief in Akhirat includes belief that this world will end, that all people will be raised again, that every person will be judged on the Day of Judgement, and that the result will be reward in Paradise or punishment in Hell.

The Qur'an teaches that nothing is lost in this judgement. "Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it" (99:7-8). It asks, "Did you think that We created you uselessly and that to Us you would not be returned?" (23:115). And it tells people, "Every soul will taste death, and you will only be given your [full] compensation on the Day of Resurrection" (3:185). The Qur'an also describes angels who record each person's deeds: "Noble and recording; they know whatever you do" (82:11-12).

Belief in Akhirat gives life a purpose and gives every action a weight. It is the strongest reason a believer has to be honest, fair and patient even when this world does not reward it.

#### Correcting a second misconception

A related belief is that **belief has no link to daily conduct**: "belief is in the heart; the test is a different thing", as Bilal said. The Qur'an rejects this separation. The verse on righteousness joins belief in Allah and the Last Day with giving to the needy, keeping promises and patience (2:177). Surah al-Asr joins belief with righteous deeds (103:2-3). The example of the Prophet (peace be upon him) is offered as a pattern "for anyone whose hope is in Allāh and the Last Day" (33:21). In each case belief is meant to show itself in action. A person who says "I believe in the Day of Judgement" and then cheats has not denied the words of the belief, but has not let the belief do its work.

#### Worked example: how belief in Akhirat shapes a teacher's day

Here is how to trace the effect of one belief on a teacher's conduct. Take belief in Akhirat and three ordinary moments in Saleem's week.

| Moment | What a teacher might be tempted to do | What belief in Akhirat changes | Qur'anic basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marking 60 test papers late at night | Mark quickly and guess the totals | Mark each paper carefully; each pupil's mark is a trust that will be accounted for | 99:7-8 |
| An officer's son has failed | Add marks to avoid trouble | Give the mark the work deserves; fear of the Day of Judgement outweighs fear of the officer | 3:185 |
| A tired Monday with no inspection due | Arrive late and give a reading task | Arrive on time and teach fully; the work is seen and recorded | 82:10-12; 9:105 |

Notice the pattern of the analysis. For each moment, name the temptation, name how the belief changes the choice, and name the evidence. Belief in Akhirat does not make the work easier. It changes the reason for doing it well: the teacher works for a judge who sees everything, not only for the inspector.

### Articles of faith

#### The six articles

The **articles of faith** are the beliefs every Muslim must hold. They are stated in a well-known hadith. The angel Jibril came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) in the form of a man and asked him about faith. The Prophet replied that faith is to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day, and to believe in the divine decree, its good and its evil (*Sahih Muslim*, hadith 8a). The Qur'an names the first five together: "whoever disbelieves in Allāh, His angels, His books, His messengers, and the Last Day has certainly gone far astray" (4:136).

Pakistani pupils learn these beliefs in two short Arabic statements. ***Iman-e-Mujmal*** (faith in brief) declares belief in Allah as He is, with all His names and attributes, and acceptance of all His commands. ***Iman-e-Mufassal*** (faith in detail) names the six articles one by one.

#### Understanding the divine decree

The sixth article is often misunderstood. Belief in **taqdir** (divine decree) means believing that "all things We created with predestination" (54:49): nothing happens outside Allah's knowledge and will. Some pupils take this to mean, "If my result is already written, why should I study?" This is a mistake. The same Qur'an says that a soul "will have [the consequence of] what [good] it has gained, and it will bear [the consequence of] what [evil] it has earned" (2:286), and that "Allāh will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves" (13:11). Belief in the decree is meant to give a believer patience when things go wrong and humility when they go right. It is never a reason to stop trying.

#### Worked example: from article to answer

Here is how a teacher can use the articles of faith to answer a real pupil's question, rather than only asking pupils to recite them.

**Pupil's question:** "Sir, if Allah has already decided whether I pass, why should I study for the board exam?"

1. **Name the article.** The question is about belief in the divine decree.
2. **State the belief accurately.** Allah knows and has measured all things (54:49).
3. **Add what the belief does not mean.** It does not remove human choice or responsibility; each person bears the result of what they earn (2:286), and change begins with what people do themselves (13:11).
4. **Link to conduct.** So the right response is to study hard, pray for success, and accept the result with patience.

The method works for any article: name it, state it accurately with a reference, correct any misunderstanding, and link it to conduct.

## Activity: From belief to behaviour

Work in groups of three. About 30 minutes.

1. Each group takes one of the six articles of faith, or one of the three foundations (Tauheed, Risalat, Akhirat). Make sure the whole class covers all of them.
2. Write the belief in one plain sentence, with its Qur'anic or hadith reference.
3. Choose one behaviour problem common in Pakistani schools, such as copying in tests, teasing a pupil from a poor family, arriving late, or wasting school property.
4. Plan a five-minute explanation for Class 8 that shows how your belief, properly understood, gives a reason to change that behaviour. Use the four-step method from the worked example.
5. Present your plan to the class. Other groups say whether the link between belief and behaviour is clear and accurate.

## Check your understanding

1. Name the three foundations of belief in Islam and state, in one line each, what each one means. *(Remember)*
2. List the six articles of faith in the order of *Iman-e-Mufassal*. *(Remember)*
3. Explain the difference between oneness in lordship and oneness in worship, using one Qur'anic reference for each. *(Understand)*
4. Explain why the idol-worshippers of Makkah were not counted as believers in Tauheed, even though they accepted Allah as the Creator (29:61). *(Understand)*
5. A pupil says, "My future is already written, so there is no point in working hard." Use the four-step method to write a short reply. *(Apply)*

## Summary

The beliefs of Islam (*Aqaid*) rest on three foundations. Tauheed is the oneness of Allah in lordship, in His names and attributes, and in worship; its opposite is shirk. It is far more than believing that God exists, and it gives a believer courage, honesty, a sense of equality and hope.

Risalat is Allah's guidance through the prophets, sent to every nation and ending with Muhammad (peace be upon him), the seal of the prophets and the model to follow. Akhirat is the life after death, in which every deed, however small, is judged. It gives every action weight and is the strongest reason to act well when no one is watching.

The six articles of faith are belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day and the divine decree. Belief in the decree does not remove responsibility. In every case, belief is meant to show itself in conduct: a teacher's marking, punctuality and fairness are where belief becomes visible.

## Try this at your practicum school

Observe one Islamiat lesson on belief at your practicum school. Note how much of the lesson is recitation and how much is explanation. Then ask three pupils, privately and politely, "What difference does believing in the Day of Judgement make to how you behave in school?" Write their answers in your journal without names. Reflect: do these pupils link belief and conduct? What would you change in the lesson so that they do?

## Summative task

Write 400-500 words in answer to this question: **How should belief in Akhirat shape a Muslim teacher's everyday conduct?** Your answer should:

1. Explain belief in Akhirat accurately, with at least two Qur'anic references.
2. Analyse three specific situations from a teacher's working week, showing for each the temptation, how belief in Akhirat changes the choice, and the evidence.
3. Explain how belief in Tauheed supports the same conduct.

*(Understand, Analyze)*

| Criterion | Strong | Partial | Missing (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy of belief (2 marks) | Accurate account of Akhirat with two correct references (2) | Partly accurate, or one reference (1) | Inaccurate or absent |
| Analysis of three situations (6 marks) | Each situation shows the temptation, the changed choice and the reason, with a reference (5-6) | Situations described but the link to belief is general (2-4) | Situations missing or unrelated (0-1) |
