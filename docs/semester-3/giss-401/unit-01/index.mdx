# Introduction to Islamic Studies and the Fundamental Beliefs of Islam

You studied Islamiat at school and college. You can probably recite the *kalimah*, name the five pillars of Islam and list the articles of faith. This unit asks a different question: what do these things mean, and what difference do they make to the way a person lives and teaches? A teacher who only knows the list can make pupils memorise it. A teacher who understands it can help pupils see why honesty, fairness and hard work matter even when no one is watching.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the meaning of Islam and of Islamic Studies, and name the main branches of Islamic Studies with what each one studies. *(Understand; SLO:GISS-401-1-1)*
- Explain why the Qur'an and the Sunnah give such importance to knowledge, and why Islamic education matters in Pakistan. *(Understand; SLO:GISS-401-1-1)*
- Analyse an ordinary school day to show how Islam guides belief, worship, morals, social life, economic life and public life. *(Analyze; SLO:GISS-401-1-1)*
- Explain Tauheed, Risalat and Akhirat with Qur'anic references, and state the six articles of faith. *(Understand; SLO:GISS-401-1-2)*
- Analyse how belief in Tauheed and Akhirat shapes a Muslim teacher's everyday conduct. *(Analyze; SLO:GISS-401-1-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This is the opening unit of the course. You need the Islamiat you studied up to HSC: the *kalimah*, the five pillars, the names of the main prophets and the main books, and a few short surahs. Before you begin, write three lines in your notebook: one belief of Islam you can explain well, one you can name but not explain, and one question about belief that a pupil might ask you.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each one begins with a real school situation, then explains the ideas and shows a worked example before asking you to use them. Qur'anic verses are cited by surah and ayah, for example (112:1-4) means Surah 112, verses 1 to 4. Short quotations are from the Saheeh International translation on Quran.com. Look up each verse in a full translation, and with a tafseer where you can, rather than relying on the short quotation alone.

Do the pair or group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions without looking back, then check your answers against the explanation. The [unit assessment](./unit-assessment) has practice questions and, at the end, a public self-study guidance section. Try the questions before you read that guidance. In formal course assessment the default weighting is 40% formative and 60% summative; your teacher will tell you which work is collected.
