# Quranic Syntax and Sentence Structure

In Unit 2 you learned how Qur'anic words are built and how nouns, verbs and particles behave. This unit looks at how those words are joined into sentences. Arabic has two main sentence types, and the type tells you where to look first for the meaning. You will learn how to find the parts of a sentence, how word order can change the emphasis, how conditional sentences link a condition to its result, and how the passive voice turns a verb around. Every idea is practised on short verses you can check on Quran.com and in the Quranic Arabic Corpus.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Identify whether a short Qur'anic sentence is nominal or verbal, and name its first word. *(Understand; SLO:GUHQ-301-3-1)*
- Describe the components of a nominal sentence (mubtada' and khabar) and of a verbal sentence (fi'l, fā'il and maf'ūl bihi), with an example of each. *(Understand; SLO:GUHQ-301-3-1)*
- Explain how word order can change the emphasis or restriction of a sentence, using one Qur'anic example. *(Analyze; SLO:GUHQ-301-3-1)*
- Break a conditional sentence into its condition word, condition clause and result clause, with an example. *(Apply; SLO:GUHQ-301-3-2)*
- Distinguish an active verb from a passive verb in a verse, and explain what the passive does to the doer. *(Analyze; SLO:GUHQ-301-3-2)*
- Use sentence structure to read a short verse more accurately. *(Apply; SLO:GUHQ-301-3-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

You need Unit 2: the parts of speech (ism, fi'l, harf), the root and pattern, and the basic nouns, verbs and particles you met there. You should be able to read the Qur'anic script with its vowel marks. No prior study of Arabic grammar (naḥw) is assumed; the few Arabic terms this unit uses are defined in plain words as they appear.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each begins with a real classroom situation, explains the ideas, shows a worked example, and then asks you to try. Keep Quran.com and the Quranic Arabic Corpus open. When the unit quotes an Arabic phrase, look it up word by word in the corpus: click each word to see its grammatical label. The corpus marks a passive verb "passive" and a conditional word "time adverb", which will help you see the patterns.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
