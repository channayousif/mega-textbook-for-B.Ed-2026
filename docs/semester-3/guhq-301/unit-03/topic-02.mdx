## Explanation

This topic answers three questions. How is a conditional sentence built? What does the passive voice do? And how can sentence structure help you read a long verse?

### Conditional sentences

A **conditional sentence** (جبملة شرطية, *jumlah sharṭiyyah*) joins two clauses. The first is the **condition** (*sharṭ*, شرط): the thing that must happen. The second is the **result** (*jawāb sharṭ*, جواب شرط): what follows from it. A **condition word** introduces the condition. It can be a particle, such as إِنْ (*in*, "if") or لَوْ (*law*, "if"), or a noun, such as مَنْ (*man*, "whoever"), مَا (*mā*, "whatever"), مَتَى (*matā*, "when") and أَيْنَمَا (*aynamā*, "wherever"). The result often begins with the linking particle فَ (*fa*, "so"), which joins it to the condition.

Take 2:282: إِذَا تَدَايَنتُم بِدَيْنٍ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى فَٱكْتُبُوهُ (*idhā tadāyantum bi-daynin ilā ajalin musamman fa-ktubūhu*, "When you borrow a debt for a fixed term, then write it down"). The condition word is إِذَا (*idhā*, "when"). The condition clause is تَدَايَنتُم بِدَيْنٍ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى ("when you borrow a debt for a fixed term"). The result clause is فَٱكْتُبُوهُ ("so write it down"), and it begins with the linking فَ.

A second example is 2:284: وَإِن تُبْدُوا۟ مَا فَىٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ يُحَاسِبْكُم بِهِ ٱللَّهُ (*wa-in tubdū mā fī anfusikum aw tukhfūhu yuḥāsib-kum bihi llāh*, "And if you show what is in your souls or hide it, Allah will bring you to account for it"). The condition word is إِنْ (*in*, "if"). The condition clause is تُبْدُوا۟ ... أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ ("whether you show it or hide it"). The result clause is يُحَاسِبْكُم بِهِ ٱللَّهُ ("Allah will bring you to account for it").

The Quranic Arabic Corpus treats إِذَا as a time adverb used as a conditional particle, and its grammar guide calls this a temporal condition: "when" the condition happens, "then" the result follows. The same shape works with إِنْ for a condition that may or may not happen.

**A worked example: find the skeleton.** Read 2:282 and stop at each step.

1. **Find the condition word.** The verse opens with إِذَا, a conditional word.
2. **Read the condition clause.** "When you borrow a debt for a fixed term" tells the situation.
3. **Read the result clause.** "So write it down" tells what to do in that situation. The فَ marks the join.
4. **Note the link.** The فَ is the sign that the command is the result of the condition, not a separate command.

### Passive voice in Quranic Arabic

A **passive verb** (فعل مبني للمجهول, *fi'l mabnī lil-majhūl*) is a verb whose doer is not named. The one the action happens to takes the doer's place. Arabic grammarians call this stand-in the **nā'ib fā'il** (نائب فاعل), and the Quranic Arabic Corpus labels the relationship the same way. The passive keeps the meaning but turns the sentence around, so the action or the person affected comes first.

Take 99:1: إِذَا زُلْزِلَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ زِلْزَالَهَا (*idhā zulzilati l-arḍu zilzālahā*, "When the earth is shaken with its earthquake"). The verb زُلْزِلَتِ (*zulzilat*, "is shaken") is a passive perfect verb. The word after it, ٱلْأَرْضُ (*al-arḍu*, "the earth"), is the nā'ib fā'il: the earth is the one the shaking happens to. The doer is not named, and the point is the earth and what happens to it.

Now compare 99:2: وَأَخْرَجَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ أَثْقَالَهَا (*wa-akhrajati l-arḍu athqālahā*, "And the earth brings forth its burdens"). Here أَخْرَجَتِ (*akhrajati*, "brings forth") is an active verb, and ٱلْأَرْضُ is the fā'il, the doer. The same word, ٱلْأَرْضُ, is the doer in one verse and the nā'ib fā'il in the other. The voice of the verb tells you which.

A third example is 2:183: كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ (*kutiba ʿalaykumu ṣ-ṣiyāmu*, "Fasting is decreed upon you"). The verb كُتِبَ (*kutiba*, "it is decreed") is passive, and the doer (Allah) is not named. The point is the duty and the people it falls on, not the one who decreed it.

**Correcting a common misconception: "The passive is rare in the Qur'an."** The passive is common, especially when the doer is Allah or when the point is the action and the person affected rather than the doer. The Qur'an says قِيلَ لَهُمْ ("it is said to them", 2:91), رُزِقُوا ("they are provided", 2:25) and أُنزِلَ ("it was revealed", 2:91) many times. A reader who expects an active verb in every sentence will be confused by these. Learning to spot the passive is part of reading the Qur'an well.

### Syntax-based comprehension exercises

Sentence structure is a reading tool. When a verse is long, find its skeleton before you translate it word by word. Ask four questions: What type of sentence is this? What are its parts? Is there a condition and a result? Is any verb passive?

**A worked example: 2:91.** Read the opening: وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ ءَامِنُوا۟ بِمَآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ (*wa-idhā qīla lahum āminū bi-mā anzala llāh*, "And when it is said to them, 'Believe in what Allah has revealed'").

1. **Sentence type.** It opens with وَإِذَا, a conditional word, so the first clause is a condition.
2. **Parts.** The condition word is إِذَا. The condition clause is قِيلَ لَهُمْ ("it is said to them"). The result clause is ءَامِنُوا۟ ... ("believe in what Allah has revealed").
3. **Voice.** قِيلَ (*qīla*, "it is said") is passive, so the doer is not named. أَنزَلَ (*anzala*, "he revealed") is active, and its doer is ٱللَّهُ (Allah).
4. **Meaning.** The verse describes a moment: whenever people say to them "Believe", they answer with pride. The passive قِيلَ keeps the focus on the people being addressed.

Now try the same four questions on 2:20: وَلَوْ شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَذَهَبَ بِسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَـٰرِهِمْ (*wa-law shā'a llāhu la-dhahaba bi-samʿihim wa-abṣārihim*, "And if Allah had willed, He would have taken away their hearing and their sight"). The condition word is لَوْ (*law*, "if"). The condition clause is شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ ("Allah had willed"). The result clause is لَذَهَبَ بِسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَـٰرِهِمْ ("He would have taken away their hearing and their sight"). Both verbs are active. The structure shows a hypothetical condition and its result.

The Quranic Arabic Corpus helps here. It marks each verb as active or passive, and it marks conditional words such as إِذَا as a time adverb. When you are stuck on a long verse, click the words one by one and let the labels guide your skeleton.

## Activity: Find the skeleton

Work in groups of three. About 25 minutes.

Each group takes one passage: 2:282 (the first sentence), 2:284 (the second sentence), 99:1-2, or 2:91 (the opening).

1. **Condition hunt (10 minutes).** Find the condition word, the condition clause and the result clause. If the passage has no condition, say so and name the sentence type instead.
2. **Voice hunt (10 minutes).** Find every verb in the passage and mark it active or passive. For a passive verb, name the nā'ib fā'il.
3. **Present (5 minutes).** Draw the skeleton on a slip: condition word, condition, result, and the voice of each verb. Present it to the class in one minute and answer one question.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name the three parts of a conditional sentence and give one example of a condition word.
2. *(Remember)* What does the passive voice do to the doer of a verb, and what takes its place?
3. *(Understand)* In 2:282, which word is the condition word, and which clause is the result? Answer in two sentences.
4. *(Apply)* Look at 99:1 and 99:2. Identify the passive verb in the first and the active verb in the second, and say what each means.

## Summary

A conditional sentence joins a condition (*sharṭ*) to a result (*jawāb sharṭ*), and a condition word such as إِنْ or إِذَا introduces it. The result often begins with the linking فَ, as in 2:282: "When you borrow a debt for a fixed term, so write it down." The passive voice turns a verb around so the doer is not named, and the one the action happens to becomes the nā'ib fā'il, as in 99:1: "When the earth is shaken." The passive is common in the Qur'an, not rare, and it keeps the focus on the action or the person affected. Sentence structure is a reading tool: find the type, the parts, the condition and result, and the voice, and a long verse becomes a skeleton you can follow.
