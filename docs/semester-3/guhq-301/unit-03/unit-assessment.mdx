# Quranic Syntax and Sentence Structure - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Arabic sentences come in two main types, and the first word tells you which type you are reading. A nominal sentence (jumlah ismiyyah) begins with a noun: the mubtada' opens it and the khabar completes it, as in "That is the Book" (2:2). A verbal sentence (jumlah fi'liyyah) begins with a verb: the fi'l comes first, and a fā'il and, where the verb takes one, a maf'ūl bihi follow, as in "So remember Me; I will remember you" (2:152). The doer may be hidden inside the verb, especially in an imperative. Word order is not fixed for effect: moving a word forward, as with "You alone" in 1:5, gives it emphasis or restriction.

A conditional sentence (jumlah sharṭiyyah) joins a condition (sharṭ) to a result (jawāb sharṭ). A condition word such as إِنْ or إِذَا introduces the condition, and the result often begins with the linking فَ, as in "When you borrow a debt for a fixed term, so write it down" (2:282). The passive voice (fi'l mabnī lil-majhūl) does not name the doer; the one the action happens to becomes the nā'ib fā'il, as in "When the earth is shaken" (99:1). The passive is common in the Qur'an, not rare, and it keeps the focus on the action or the person affected. Sentence structure is a reading tool: find the type, the parts, the condition and result, and the voice, and a long verse becomes a skeleton you can follow.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the Qur'an, not a list of definitions. Translations are Saheeh International (Quran.com).

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which word begins a nominal sentence? *(Remember)*
   - A) A verb
   - B) A noun
   - C) A particle
   - D) A pronoun suffix
2. In 2:2, the word *al-kitābu* ("the Book") is which part of the sentence? *(Remember)*
   - A) The mubtada'
   - B) The khabar
   - C) The fā'il
   - D) The maf'ūl bihi
3. Which word is the condition word in the opening of 2:282? *(Remember)*
   - A) فَٱكْتُبُوهُ
   - B) إِذَا
   - C) تَدَايَنتُم
   - D) بِدَيْنٍ
4. A verb that does not name its doer is called: *(Remember)*
   - A) Active
   - B) Nominal
   - C) Passive
   - D) Conditional
5. In 1:5, *iyyāka* ("You") is placed before the verb *naʿbudu* ("we worship") to show: *(Understand)*
   - A) The doer of the verb
   - B) Restriction: "You alone"
   - C) The object of another verb
   - D) A condition and its result
6. Which of the following is a verbal sentence? *(Understand)*
   - A) ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبُ
   - B) إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ
   - C) فَٱذْكُرُونِىٓ أَذْكُرْكُمْ
   - D) أُولَـٰٓئِكَ عَلَيْهِمْ صَلَوَٰتٌ
7. In 99:1, the word *zulzilat* ("is shaken") is: *(Understand)*
   - A) An active verb
   - B) A passive verb
   - C) A noun
   - D) A particle
8. Which pair shows the same verb in the active and the passive voice? *(Apply)*
   - A) قَالُوا / قِيلَ
   - B) كَتَبَ / كَتَبَ
   - C) ذَهَبَ / جَاءَ
   - D) عَلِمَ / جَهِلَ
9. In 2:284, which clause is the result of the condition? *(Apply)*
   - A) وَإِن تُبْدُوا۟
   - B) مَا فَىٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ
   - C) يُحَاسِبْكُم بِهِ ٱللَّهُ
   - D) أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ
10. Which statement best explains why the Qur'an uses the passive voice? *(Apply)*
   - A) To hide the meaning from the reader
   - B) To focus on the action or the person affected, often when the doer is known
   - C) Because the doer is always unknown
   - D) To make the verse shorter

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In two sentences, explain the difference between a nominal sentence and a verbal sentence, with one example of each. *(Understand)*
2. Name the two parts of a nominal sentence and give an example of each from 2:157. *(Understand)*
3. Explain, in two or three sentences, how the word order of 1:5 changes the meaning of "You we worship". *(Analyze)*
4. A trainee says every Arabic sentence must have an explicit doer. Using 2:25 or 2:152, explain what is wrong with this. *(Analyze)*
5. List the three parts of a conditional sentence and give one example of each from 2:282. *(Understand)*
6. Explain, with one example, what the passive voice does to the doer of a verb and what takes its place. *(Understand)*
7. In 99:1 and 99:2, compare the passive verb in the first verse with the active verb in the second, and say what each draws attention to. *(Analyze)*
8. Analyse how the conditional structure of 2:282 helps a reader find the command in a long verse. *(Analyze)*
9. Give two reasons a teacher might use sentence structure to explain a long verse to a Class 8 group. *(Understand)*
10. A pupil says 2:2 is a verbal sentence. In three sentences, explain the error and identify the correct sentence type and its parts. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A colleague says: "Arabic word order is fixed, so the first word of a verse always carries the main point." Using 1:5 and 2:148, evaluate this claim and explain how word order works in the Qur'an. *(Evaluate)*
2. Analyse how the nominal sentence of 2:2, "That is the Book", sets the theme of the opening of Surah al-Baqarah, and explain why beginning with this sentence matters more than beginning with a command. *(Analyze)*
3. Evaluate the usefulness of the passive voice in the Qur'an for a reader, using 99:1 and 2:183, and explain what is gained and what is lost when the doer is not named. *(Evaluate)*
4. Design a 20-minute lesson for a Class 8 Nazira Quran group that teaches conditional sentences using 2:282. Give the steps in order, justify each one, and include one check for understanding. *(Create)*
5. Choose one short verse from this unit or from Unit 2. Analyse its sentence type, its parts, and any conditional or passive structure, then explain how that structure shapes its meaning. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - A nominal sentence begins with a noun, which is the mubtada'.
2. B - *Al-kitābu* completes the mubtada' *dhālika*, so it is the khabar.
3. B - إِذَا (*idhā*, "when") is the condition word in 2:282.
4. C - A passive verb does not name its doer.
5. B - Putting *iyyāka* before the verb restricts the worship to Allah alone.
6. C - *Fa-dhkurūnī* begins with a verb, so the sentence is verbal. The others begin with a noun or a particle.
7. B - *Zulzilat* is a passive perfect verb; the earth is the nā'ib fā'il.
8. A - *Qālū* (they said) is active and *qīla* (it was said) is its passive.
9. C - *Yuḥāsib-kum bihi llāh* is the result of the condition "if you show or hide it".
10. B - The passive keeps the focus on the action or the person affected, and the doer is often known from the context.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: A nominal sentence begins with a noun (the mubtada'), as in "That is the Book" (2:2). A verbal sentence begins with a verb (the fi'l), as in "So remember Me" (2:152). **Marks (3):** 1 - distinguishes the two types; 1 - example of a nominal sentence; 1 - example of a verbal sentence.
2. Model: The two parts are the mubtada' and the khabar. In 2:157 the mubtada' is *ulā'ika* ("those") and the khabar is "ʿalayhim ṣalawātun min rabbihim wa-raḥmah" ("upon them are blessings from their Lord and mercy"). **Marks (3):** 1 - names both parts; 2 - correct example of each.
3. Model: The object pronoun *iyyāka* ("You") is placed before the verb *naʿbudu* ("we worship"), although the normal order puts the verb first. The forward object restricts the worship to Allah alone, so the meaning is "You alone we worship". **Marks (3):** 1 - identifies the forward word; 1 - states the normal order; 1 - explains the restriction.
4. Model: The doer is not always a separate word. In 2:25 the imperative *bashshiri* carries the hidden doer "you", and in 2:152 the waāw inside *udhkurū* is the doer. The doer is present in meaning but not always written out. **Marks (3):** 1 - states the doer can be hidden; 1 - a correct example; 1 - the explanation.
5. Model: The three parts are the condition word, the condition clause and the result clause. In 2:282: إِذَا; "when you borrow a debt for a fixed term"; "so write it down". **Marks (3):** 1 - names the three parts; 1 - correct condition word; 1 - correct condition and result.
6. Model: The passive does not name the doer, and the one the action happens to takes the doer's place as the nā'ib fā'il. In 99:1 *zulzilat* ("is shaken") is passive and *al-arḍu* ("the earth") is the nā'ib fā'il. **Marks (3):** 1 - the doer is not named; 1 - the nā'ib fā'il is named; 1 - a correct example.
7. Model: In 99:1 *zulzilat* is passive, so the earth is the one shaken and the doer is not named. In 99:2 *akhrajati* ("brings forth") is active, so the earth is the doer. The first draws attention to what happens to the earth, the second to what the earth does. **Marks (4):** 1 - identifies each verb; 1 - states its voice; 2 - compares the focus.
8. Model: The condition word إِذَا marks the situation, and the فَ marks the result. A reader can find the command "write it down" as the result of the condition without reading the whole long verse first. **Marks (3):** 1 - the condition word; 1 - the result marker; 1 - the explanation.
9. Model: It shows the skeleton of a long verse, and it helps pupils find the command or the main point in an orderly way. **Marks (2):** 1 per reason.
10. Model: 2:2 begins with *dhālika*, a demonstrative pronoun, so it is a nominal sentence, not a verbal one. The mubtada' is *dhālika* and the khabar is *al-kitābu*. The pupil looked for a verb and missed the first word. **Marks (3):** 1 - correct type; 1 - correct parts; 1 - explains the error.
