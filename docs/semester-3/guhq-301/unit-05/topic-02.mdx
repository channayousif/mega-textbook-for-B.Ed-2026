# Topic 5.2 - Interpreting selected verses and short surahs

## A real classroom situation

After a Class 9 exam in a school in Khairpur, a pupil stops Rabia in the corridor. "Miss, what is Surah al-Duha about?" Rabia can read it and translate it, but she wants to give the pupil more than a word-by-word gloss. She wants the pupil to see the theme, the message and the lesson it offers a student. This topic shows how to move from reading a verse or a short surah to a lesson, and how to check that the lesson really belongs to the text.

## Explanation

This topic gives you a method, a way to find a theme, and a way to draw a lesson.

### Interpretation of selected verses and short surahs

The method has five steps, shown in the flowchart above.

1. **Read.** Read the verse or surah aloud once, placing each letter, so that you are working from the text and not from a memory of it.
2. **Translate.** Give each word its meaning and its grammar, and then a meaning-based rendering, as Unit 4 taught.
3. **Check the context.** Read the verses around it and, where it is known, the occasion of its revelation. Ask what question or situation the verse answers.
4. **Consult tafsir.** See how the trusted commentaries read it, and notice which principle of interpretation they follow.
5. **Draw the lesson.** State the theme and the practical lesson it offers, and say which verse carries it.

Worked example: Surah al-Duha (93:1-8).

| Step | What you do | What the surah says |
|---|---|---|
| Read | Read the surah aloud once | It opens with two oaths and then comforts the Prophet |
| Translate | Give each word its meaning | "By the morning brightness... Your Lord has not taken leave of you, nor has He detested you" |
| Check the context | Read the occasion | The surah was revealed to console the Prophet in a hard time |
| Consult tafsir | See how the commentaries read it | The verses turn comfort into a lesson about the orphan and the poor |
| Draw the lesson | State the theme and the lesson | God sees the person in need; care for the orphan and the poor |

The lesson is not a new idea. It is the theme of the surah turned into a duty, and verses 6-8 carry it.

### Identifying main themes and messages

A theme is the message the text keeps returning to. To find it, look for the key words, notice what repeats, and read the passage as one unit rather than a string of separate verses.

In al-Duha the words of care repeat: God found the Prophet an orphan and gave him refuge, found him lost and guided him, found him poor and made him self-sufficient (93:6-8). The theme is God's care and the duty to care for the weak. In al-Kafirun (109) the phrase "I do not worship what you worship" and its paired forms repeat in verses 2-5. The theme is a clear boundary of faith, held with courtesy. The table below gives six short surahs with their main message and a practical lesson.

### Moral and practical lessons

A moral lesson is the theme turned into a concrete duty: be grateful, protect the weak, keep your worship sincere, turn to God for protection. A lesson must be traced to the theme, not bolted on. Worked example: 110:1-3 begins with the victory of God and the conquest, then says, "Then exalt Him with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him." The theme is that victory comes from God; the lesson is to thank God and seek forgiveness, not to boast. The lesson is carried by the command in verse 3.

Another example: 112 declares that God is One, the Eternal Refuge, who neither begets nor is born, and to whom there is no equivalent. The theme is the oneness of God; the lesson is to keep worship sincere and to treat people as equal before God. Again, the lesson follows from the wording.

**Correcting a common misconception: "A verse's meaning is fixed by the occasion of its revelation."** The occasion is a starting point, not a cage. The principle of generality means a general wording reaches beyond its first occasion, while a specific wording stays where the wording stays. A lesson drawn from a verse must be traced to the wording, so that a general warning is not confined to one event and a specific ruling is not stretched into a universal law.

## Activity: From verse to lesson

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Each pair takes one short surah from 93, 108, 109, 110, 112 or 113.

1. **Read (2 minutes).** Read the surah aloud once, placing each letter.
2. **Translate (5 minutes).** Give a word-by-word draft and then a meaning-based rendering.
3. **Find the theme (5 minutes).** List the key words, notice what repeats, and state the theme in one sentence.
4. **Draw the lesson (5 minutes).** State one moral or practical lesson for a student and say which verse carries it.
5. **Check (3 minutes).** Join another pair and test their lesson: does it follow from the theme, or was it bolted on? Say what you would change.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* List the five steps from a verse to a lesson, in order.
2. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain what a theme is and how repetition helps you find it.
3. *(Apply)* Take 112 and state its theme in one sentence, with two key words.
4. *(Analyze)* In three sentences, explain how a lesson you draw from a verse must be connected to its theme.
5. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "Surah al-Falaq is only about magic." Using the wording, what is one better reading?

## Summary

The path from a verse to a lesson runs through reading, translation, context and tafsir, and it ends in a lesson that can be lived. A theme is the message the key words keep returning to, and a moral lesson is the theme turned into a concrete duty. Context and the principle of generality keep the lesson honest, so that a verse is neither confined to its first occasion nor stretched beyond its words.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can list the five steps from a verse to a lesson.
- [ ] I can find the theme of a short surah from its key words and repetitions.
- [ ] I can draw a moral lesson and trace it to a verse.
- [ ] I can explain how the occasion and the wording work together.

## Try this at your practicum school

Take one short surah from 93 to 114 and plan a 15-minute Class 9 activity that moves pupils from reading to a one-sentence theme and one lesson. Note the surah, the theme, the lesson and one question you would ask the class.

## Summative task

Choose one short surah from 93, 108, 109, 110, 112 or 113. Write 250-300 words: give a word-by-word reading of two of its verses, state its theme in one sentence, and draw one moral or practical lesson for a student, showing how the wording carries it.

*(Analyze)*

**Mini-rubric (10 marks)**

| Criterion | Strong | Partial | Missing (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accurate reading and translation (3 marks) | Both verses read correctly, with meaning and grammar (3) | Most words correct, grammar thin (1-2) | Several words wrong or missing |
| Theme and key words (3 marks) | A clear one-sentence theme, with two key words and their meanings (3) | A theme is stated but the key words are thin (1-2) | No theme or key words |
| Lesson traced to the wording (3 marks) | A lesson is stated and tied to a specific verse or phrase (3) | A lesson is stated without a clear link to the wording (1-2) | No lesson, or one that does not follow from the text |
| Suitability for a student (1 mark) | The lesson is concrete and usable by a student (1) | The lesson is vague (0) | No lesson |

The lesson criterion asks you to show the link, not just to state a duty.
