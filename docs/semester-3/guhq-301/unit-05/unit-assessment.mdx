# Interpretation of Quranic Verses and Surahs - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Tafsir is the disciplined effort to understand what the Qur'an means. It is grounded in the text itself, the Sunnah, the Companions' usage and the Arabic language, and it uses the occasions of revelation as a tool rather than as a cage. Its principles keep it honest. The most important is the rule that the decisive point is drawn from the generality of the wording, not from the particularity of the occasion: a verse revealed about one event, whose wording is general, reaches every situation that wording covers. Context and grammar do not change the wording; they show how far its meaning reaches.

From that base, the unit taught a method for interpreting a verse or a short surah: read, translate, check the context, consult tafsir, and draw the lesson. A theme is the message the key words keep returning to, and a moral and practical lesson is the theme turned into a concrete duty. The short surahs of Juz 30 are ideal practice ground, from the comfort and care of al-Duha to the clear boundary of al-Kafirun, the gratitude of al-Kawthar and al-Nasr, the oneness of al-Ikhlas and the refuge of al-Falaq.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the verses, not a list of definitions. Translations are Saheeh International (Quran.com).

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which statement best defines tafsir? *(Remember)*
   - A) Reading the Qur'an aloud with correct pronunciation
   - B) The disciplined effort to understand what the Qur'an means, using accepted tools
   - C) A personal opinion about the meaning of a verse
   - D) Memorising the occasions of revelation
2. Which of the following is the first source of tafsir? *(Remember)*
   - A) The Qur'an itself
   - B) An Arabic dictionary
   - C) The opinions of later scholars
   - D) The local customs
3. The rule "al-ʿibrah bi ʿumum al-lafz la bi khusus al-sabab" means: *(Understand)*
   - A) The ruling is drawn from the general wording, not only the occasion of revelation
   - B) The occasion of revelation is more important than the wording
   - C) A verse revealed about a specific person applies only to that person
   - D) The wording of a verse may be ignored when the occasion is known
4. What are asbab al-nuzul? *(Remember)*
   - A) The grammatical rules of Arabic
   - B) The events or questions that occasioned the revelation of a verse
   - C) The chapters of the Qur'an
   - D) The rules of recitation
5. What does siyaq refer to? *(Understand)*
   - A) The arrangement and context of the text around a verse
   - B) The number of a verse in the mushaf
   - C) A type of Arabic verb
   - D) The pause marks in the text
6. A verse was revealed about a specific event, but its wording is general. How should a reader apply it? *(Apply)*
   - A) Only to that event
   - B) To that event and to every situation its wording covers
   - C) Only to people who lived at the same time
   - D) Only to the person it names
7. Which surah's theme is a clear boundary of faith? *(Remember)*
   - A) Surah al-Duha (93)
   - B) Surah al-Kafirun (109)
   - C) Surah al-Kawthar (108)
   - D) Surah al-Nasr (110)
8. Which words repeat in 109 and carry its message? *(Understand)*
   - A) The word *al-kaafirun* only
   - B) The phrase "I do not worship what you worship" and its paired forms
   - C) The word *al-kawthar*
   - D) The word *al-falaq*
9. A pupil reads 113:1-5 and notes that verses 2-5 each begin with "wa-min sharri" ("and from the evil of"). What does this show? *(Apply)*
   - A) The verses list different things that depend on the refuge asked for in verse 1
   - B) The verses are unrelated to each other
   - C) The surah has no theme
   - D) The word is a name of God
10. A teacher wants pupils to draw a moral lesson from a short surah. Which task best helps? *(Apply)*
    - A) Memorise the surah's first verse
    - B) State the theme and trace the lesson to a verse
    - C) Translate each verse word by word only
    - D) Count the number of verses

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In two sentences, define tafsir and name two tools it uses. *(Understand)*
2. Explain, in two or three sentences, why a translation alone is not a sufficient basis for interpreting a verse. *(Understand)*
3. State the rule "the decisive point is derived from the generality of the wording, not the particularity of the occasion", and illustrate it with 22:4. *(Understand)*
4. In three sentences, explain what asbab al-nuzul are and how they help interpretation. *(Understand)*
5. Analyse how the context of 93:1-8 shapes its message of comfort. *(Analyze)*
6. In three sentences, explain what a theme is and how repetition helps you find it. *(Understand)*
7. Analyse how the repeated phrases of 109 carry its message of a clear boundary. *(Analyze)*
8. Analyse how two verses of 110 support its message of gratitude and seeking forgiveness. *(Analyze)*
9. In three sentences, explain how a moral lesson is drawn from a theme and why it must be traced to the wording. *(Understand)*
10. Analyse how the wording of 112 supports the lesson that worship must be sincere. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. Two short commentaries are offered on 22:4. Commentary A says the verse applies only to al-Nadar ibn al-Harith. Commentary B says it warns every stubborn opponent of the truth, in every time. In about 250 words, weigh the two commentaries, explain which principle of interpretation each follows, and say which is stronger and why. *(Evaluate)*
2. Explain 92:17-18 to a Class 9 class. Some scholars read the verses as specific to Abu Bakr; others read them as general. In about 250 words, set out both readings, apply the rule of generality, and say which is better supported by the wording. *(Analyze)*
3. Choose one short surah from 93, 108, 109, 110, 112 or 113. In about 250 words, give its theme in one sentence, show how two of its verses support it, and explain how the wording carries the theme. *(Analyze)*
4. A pupil says: "The occasion of a verse is its meaning; a general lesson drawn from it is just opinion." In about 250 words, evaluate this claim using the rule of generality and one of 22:4 or 109, and say what is right in it and what it leaves out. *(Evaluate)*
5. Design a 20-minute Class 9 lesson that takes one short surah from 93 to 113 and leads pupils from accurate reading and translation to a theme and a moral lesson. Write about 250 words, giving each step and justifying it. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - Tafsir is the disciplined effort to understand the meaning; the other options name recitation, opinion or memorisation.
2. A - The first source of tafsir is the Qur'an itself; the other options are secondary tools.
3. A - The rule says the general wording decides, not only the occasion.
4. B - Asbab al-nuzul are the events or questions that occasioned a verse.
5. A - Siyaq is the arrangement and context of the text around a verse.
6. B - A general wording reaches beyond its first occasion, so it applies to every situation it covers.
7. B - Al-Kafirun (109) repeats "I do not worship what you worship" and ends with a clear boundary.
8. B - The paired phrases in verses 2-5 carry the message of a clear boundary.
9. A - Verses 2-5 list evils that hang on the refuge asked for in verse 1.
10. B - Stating the theme and tracing the lesson to a verse is the move the unit teaches.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: Tafsir is the disciplined effort to understand what the Qur'an means, using tools such as language, grammar, the Sunnah and the occasions of revelation. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines tafsir as understanding the meaning; 1 - names the discipline; 1 - names two correct tools.
2. Model: A translation is already one interpretation, and one word can carry more than one sense. A reader who has only the translation cannot see the grammar, the context or the reason behind the verse, so the explanation rests on sand. **Marks (3):** 1 - notes that a translation is an interpretation; 1 - notes a word can have more than one sense; 1 - names one thing the translation hides (grammar, context or occasion).
3. Model: The rule means that when a verse was revealed about a particular event but its wording is general, the ruling is drawn from the general wording, not only from the event. In 22:4 the wording warns every stubborn opponent of the truth, not only al-Nadar ibn al-Harith. **Marks (3):** 1 - states the rule; 1 - notes the wording is general; 1 - applies it correctly to 22:4.
4. Model: Asbab al-nuzul are the events or questions that occasioned the revelation of a verse. They explain the verse and give it a place, but the principle of generality means they do not confine a general wording to one event. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines asbab al-nuzul; 1 - says they explain the verse; 1 - says they do not confine a general wording.
5. Model: The surah was revealed to console the Prophet in a hard time, and the context turns the comfort into a lesson about the orphan and the poor. Verses 6-8 then carry the lesson. Without the context, the surah reads as a private consolation; with it, the comfort becomes a duty. **Marks (4):** 1 - names the occasion; 1 - notes the comfort; 2 - analysis of how the context turns comfort into a lesson about the orphan and the poor.
6. Model: A theme is the message the text keeps returning to. Repetition is the sign: when a phrase or a key word returns, it shows what the passage is about. In al-Duha the words of care repeat, so the theme is God's care and the duty to care. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines a theme; 1 - explains how repetition shows it; 1 - gives a correct example.
7. Model: Verses 2-5 repeat "I do not worship what you worship" and its paired forms. The repetition makes the boundary clear and courteous: the speaker disavows what the others worship, and the surah ends with "for you is your religion, and for me is my religion". **Marks (4):** 1 - names the repeated phrase; 1 - notes the paired forms; 2 - analysis of how the repetition carries the message of a clear boundary.
8. Model: Verse 1 names the victory of God and the conquest; verse 3 commands praise and seeking forgiveness. The movement from victory to thanks and forgiveness carries the message that help comes from God and that the proper response is gratitude, not pride. **Marks (4):** 1 - names the theme; 1 per verse analysed (2); 1 - shows how the verses support the theme.
9. Model: A moral lesson is the theme turned into a concrete duty, such as being grateful or protecting the weak. It must be traced to the wording, so that a general warning is not confined to one event and a specific ruling is not stretched into a universal law. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines a moral lesson; 1 - gives a correct example; 1 - explains why it must be traced to the wording.
10. Model: 112 says God is One, the Eternal Refuge, who neither begets nor is born, and to whom there is no equivalent. The wording makes worship exclusive and equal, so the lesson is to keep worship sincere and to treat people as equal before God. **Marks (4):** 1 - names the theme of oneness; 1 - notes the wording of exclusivity; 2 - analysis of how the wording carries the lesson of sincerity.

### ERQ rubrics

1. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Use of 22:4* (0-3): the verse is read correctly and its wording is quoted · *Principles identified* (0-3): Commentary A follows the particularity of the occasion, Commentary B follows the generality of the wording · *Evaluation* (0-4, **Evaluate**: credits what is right in A, that the occasion is real, and shows what it leaves out, that the wording is general, reaching a reasoned judgement).
2. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Both readings* (0-3): the specific and general readings are set out fairly · *Use of the wording* (0-4, **Analyze**: applies the rule of generality to the grammar of al-atqa and says which reading the wording supports) · *Judgement* (0-3): a justified statement on which reading is better.
3. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Theme* (0-3): a clear one-sentence theme, correct for the surah · *Key words* (0-2): at least two accurate key words with meanings · *Analysis of two verses* (0-4, **Analyze**: shows how each verse supports the theme, using the key words) · *Wording* (0-1): a justified sentence on how the wording carries the theme.
4. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Use of the rule* (0-3): the rule of generality is stated correctly · *Fair evaluation* (0-4, **Evaluate**: credits what is right in the claim, that the occasion matters, and shows what it leaves out, that a general wording reaches beyond it, reaching a reasoned judgement) · *Example used* (0-2): 22:4 or 109 is used accurately · *Clarity* (0-1).
5. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Steps* (0-4): accurate reading, translation, context, tafsir, theme and lesson, in a sensible order within 20 minutes · *Justification* (0-4, **Create**: each step is explained by how it helps pupils move from reading to meaning) · *Suitability for Class 9* (0-1) · *Use of one surah from 93-113* (0-1).
