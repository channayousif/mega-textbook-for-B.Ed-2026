# Reading and Understanding Quranic Text - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Reading the Qur'an accurately is the first step to understanding it. Give every letter its place, observe its sukun and shadda, stop where the meaning allows, and do not add or drop anything. Word-by-word translation then shows the skeleton of a verse through five steps: read, split, look up, order, check. It is honest about the words, but it is only the first half of understanding.

Meaning-based translation carries the message into natural English or Urdu. Al-Hilali (2026) places English translations on a spectrum between literal and interpretive, and shows that no translation is fully one or the other, so a careful reader compares translations. Key words, especially repeated ones, carry the theme of a passage, and consecutive verses connect through shared phrases, pronouns and movement, so a verse is understood best with its neighbours.

A ruku' is a paragraph-like semantic unit of the Qur'an, marked by ۝. Rafei and colleagues (2024) show that reading by ruku' keeps a passage's theme independent and its verses connected. The short surahs of Juz 30 are ideal practice: each is one or two ruku' and each carries one clear theme, from the gratitude and hope of al-Kawthar to the refuge of al-Falaq and the oneness of al-Ikhlas.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the verses, not a list of definitions. Translations are Saheeh International (Quran.com).

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. What best describes a ruku'? *(Remember)*
   - A) A single verse of the Qur'an
   - B) A paragraph-like semantic unit of the text, marked by ۝
   - C) A chapter of the Qur'an
   - D) A pause mark within a verse
2. In 103:1, the word إِنَّ (*innā*) is: *(Remember)*
   - A) A preposition meaning "in"
   - B) A noun meaning "mankind"
   - C) An emphasis particle that governs the noun after it
   - D) A verb meaning "to lose"
3. Which rendering is a meaning-based translation of 103:2? *(Understand)*
   - A) "Indeed, the mankind is in loss"
   - B) "Indeed, mankind is in loss"
   - C) "By the time, indeed"
   - D) "Except those who believed"
4. Al-Hilali (2026) describes English translations of the Qur'an as: *(Understand)*
   - A) All fully literal
   - B) All fully interpretive
   - C) A spectrum between literal and interpretive, with no translation at either extreme
   - D) Either word-by-word or tafsir
5. Which word repeats in 103:3 and signals the verse's paired structure? *(Remember)*
   - A) *Al-ʿaṣr*
   - B) *Al-insān*
   - C) *Tawāṣaw*
   - D) *Khusr*
6. A pupil reads 113:2-5 without verse 1. What does the pupil miss? *(Apply)*
   - A) The list of evils
   - B) The refuge in the Lord of daybreak that the list depends on
   - C) The word *wa-min sharri*
   - D) The name of the surah
7. A pupil merges the ع in *ʿaṣr* with alif. What is the best correction? *(Apply)*
   - A) Tell the pupil to read faster
   - B) Show the pupil the makhraj of ع and practise the sound slowly
   - C) Ask the pupil to skip the letter
   - D) Tell the pupil that ع and alif are the same letter
8. According to Rafei and colleagues (2024), neglecting ruku' divisions can lead to: *(Understand)*
   - A) Faster reading
   - B) Semantic discontinuity and reader confusion
   - C) Better memorisation
   - D) More accurate tafsir
9. Which pair best shows a meaning-based choice in 103:3? *(Apply)*
   - A) *Alladhīna āmanū* rendered as "those who believed"
   - B) *Bi-l-ḥaqqi* rendered as "to truth" after "advised each other"
   - C) *ʿAmilū* rendered as "did"
   - D) *Ṣ-ṣāliḥāt* rendered as "righteous deeds"
10. A teacher wants pupils to find the theme of a short surah. Which task best helps? *(Apply)*
    - A) Memorise the surah's first verse
    - B) Underline the key words and state the theme in one sentence
    - C) Translate each verse word by word only
    - D) Count the number of verses

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In two sentences, explain the difference between a word-by-word and a meaning-based translation, using 103:1 as an example. *(Understand)*
2. Explain, in two or three sentences, why a reader who stops after "Indeed, mankind" in 103:2 has not understood the verse. *(Understand)*
3. In three sentences, explain why giving each letter its *makhraj* matters, using one pair of letters that Urdu speakers tend to merge. *(Understand)*
4. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how the repeated word *tawāṣaw* in 103:3 shapes the meaning of the verse. *(Analyze)*
5. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how the shared phrase *wa-min sharri* connects the verses of 113:1-5. *(Analyze)*
6. In three sentences, explain Al-Hilali's (2026) spectrum of translation and why no translation is fully literal. *(Understand)*
7. Name three key words of 103:1-3 and, in two or three sentences, say what theme they carry. *(Understand)*
8. In three sentences, explain what a ruku' is and why Rafei and colleagues (2024) call it a semantic unit. *(Understand)*
9. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how two verses of Surah al-Kawthar (108) support its theme. *(Analyze)*
10. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how the verses of 102:1-8 move from warning to accountability. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A pupil says: "Word-by-word translation is the only accurate kind; a meaning-based translation is just opinion." Write a respectful reply of about 250 words. Use 103:1-3 and Al-Hilali's (2026) spectrum, and evaluate the claim fairly, saying what is right in it as well as what it leaves out. *(Evaluate)*
2. Compare a word-by-word and a meaning-based translation of 103:1-3. In about 250 words, give both renderings and analyse at least two places where they differ, explaining what the Arabic says and why the meaning-based rendering chose as it did. *(Analyze)*
3. Choose one surah from 105 to 114. In about 250 words, give its ruku' number, state its theme in one sentence, and analyse how two of its verses support that theme. *(Analyze)*
4. A colleague says: "A short surah can be understood verse by verse; a ruku' is only a marker in the mushaf." In about 250 words, evaluate this statement using Surah al-Kawthar (108) and Surah al-Falaq (113), and explain what a ruku' reading adds. *(Evaluate)*
5. Design a 20-minute Class 9 lesson that takes one short surah from 105 to 114 and leads pupils from accurate reading and word-by-word translation to a one-sentence theme. Write about 250 words, giving each step and justifying it. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - A ruku' is a paragraph-like semantic unit marked by ۝, not a single verse, a chapter or a pause mark.
2. C - *Innā* is an emphasis particle; it governs the noun after it, which is *al-insāna*.
3. B - "Indeed, mankind is in loss" is the natural, meaning-based rendering; A keeps the Arabic article awkwardly.
4. C - Al-Hilali finds a spectrum between literal and interpretive, with no translation at either extreme.
5. C - *Tawāṣaw* appears twice, in the paired clauses "advised each other to truth" and "to patience".
6. B - Verses 2-5 list evils that hang on the refuge asked for in verse 1; without it the list has no anchor.
7. B - The correction is to show the makhraj of ع and practise slowly; speed or skipping does not fix a merged letter.
8. B - Rafei and colleagues find that ignoring ruku' divisions can break the sense and confuse the reader.
9. B - *Bi-l-ḥaqqi* is literally "with/in the truth", but English idiom says "to truth" after "advised each other", a meaning-based choice.
10. B - Underlining key words and stating the theme in one sentence is the move the unit teaches; the others do not reach the theme.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: A word-by-word translation keeps the Arabic order and glosses each word, so 103:1 becomes "And the time, indeed mankind is in loss". A meaning-based translation carries the message into natural English, so it becomes "By time, indeed mankind is in loss". **Marks (3):** 1 - names word-by-word as keeping the Arabic order; 1 - names meaning-based as natural English; 1 - a correct example from 103:1.
2. Model: "Indeed, mankind" is only the subject of the sentence. The predicate "is in loss" is in the next words, and without it the reader has half a statement. The verse's serious point is the loss, so stopping early loses the meaning. **Marks (3):** 1 - notes the predicate is missing; 1 - explains the verse's point is the loss; 1 - states the consequence for understanding.
3. Model: Each letter is produced from a particular place in the mouth or throat. Urdu often merges ع and alif, so *ʿaṣr* (time) can sound like *aṣr*, and ق and ک can merge, turning *qulūb* (hearts) toward *kalb* (dog). Giving each letter its place keeps the word, and the meaning, intact. **Marks (3):** 1 - states the makhraj idea; 1 - a correct merged pair; 1 - explains the consequence for meaning.
4. Model: *Tawāṣaw* appears twice, in "advised each other to truth" and "advised each other to patience". The repetition makes the verse a pair and joins truth and patience as two things the believers hold together. Remove one clause and the pairing, and part of the message, is lost. **Marks (4):** 1 - notes the repetition; 1 - names the two things paired; 2 - analysis of how the pairing shapes the meaning.
5. Model: Verses 2-5 each begin with *wa-min sharri*, "and from the evil of". The shared phrase links every listed evil to verse 1, where the reader asks for refuge in the Lord of daybreak. The list is a prayer because it hangs on that refuge. **Marks (4):** 1 - names the shared phrase; 1 - notes it links verses 2-5; 2 - analysis of the dependence on verse 1.
6. Model: Al-Hilali places translations on a spectrum from literal to interpretive. A literal rendering keeps the Arabic words and order; an interpretive rendering carries the meaning into idiomatic English. No translation is fully one or the other, because every translation must choose between form and effect. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the spectrum; 1 - contrasts the two ends; 1 - explains why no translation is fully literal.
7. Model: Any three of *al-ʿaṣr* (time), *al-insān* (mankind), *khusr* (loss), *āmanū* (they believed), *ṣ-ṣāliḥāt* (righteous deeds), *tawāṣaw* (they advised each other), *al-ḥaqq* (truth), *aṣ-ṣabr* (patience). The theme is that mankind is in loss except the believers who hold to truth and patience. **Marks (3):** 1 per correct key word with meaning (up to 3).
8. Model: A ruku' is a division of the Qur'anic text into a paragraph-like unit, marked by ۝. Rafei and colleagues call it a semantic unit because its verses form one coherent passage with a topic sentence, supporting statements and a conclusion, as in 16:71-76. Reading by ruku' keeps the passage's meaning together. **Marks (3):** 1 - defines the ruku'; 1 - states the semantic-unit claim; 1 - gives the evidence from 16:71-76.
9. Model: Verse 1, "Indeed, We have granted you al-Kawthar", opens the theme of gratitude and hope. Verse 2, "So pray to your Lord and offer sacrifice", is the response the gift calls for. Together they show that the gift leads to worship, which is the theme. **Marks (4):** 1 - names the theme; 1 per verse analysed (2); 1 - shows how the verses support the theme.
10. Model: The passage begins with a warning about competing for increase, then repeats "No! You are going to know" to push the listener forward. It moves from "you will see the Hellfire" to "you will see it with the eye of certainty" and ends with "you will be asked that Day about pleasure". The movement from seeing to being asked carries it from warning to accountability. **Marks (4):** 1 - notes the repeated push; 1 - traces the movement; 2 - analysis of how it reaches accountability.

### ERQ rubrics

1. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Use of 103:1-3* (0-3): both renderings or a clear contrast, correctly read · *Use of Al-Hilali's spectrum* (0-3): explains literal and interpretive and that no translation is fully one · *Fair evaluation* (0-3, **Evaluate**: credits what is right in the claim, that word-by-word is honest about the words, and shows what it leaves out, that meaning also matters, reaching a reasoned judgement) · *Respectful tone* (0-1).
2. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Both renderings* (0-3): word-by-word and meaning-based renderings of 103:1-3, accurate · *Differences analysed* (0-4, **Analyze**: two differences, each showing the Arabic, the choice and the reason, for example *bi-l-ḥaqqi* as "to truth" or the order of *innā*) · *Conclusion* (0-3): a justified statement on what each kind of translation offers a reader.
3. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Ruku' and theme* (0-3): correct ruku' number and a clear one-sentence theme · *Key words* (0-2): at least two accurate key words with meanings · *Analysis of two verses* (0-4, **Analyze**: shows how each verse supports the theme, using the key words) · *Reading as one ruku'* (0-1): a justified sentence on what the ruku' view adds.
4. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Use of 108 and 113* (0-3): both passages read as units, with key words · *Fair evaluation* (0-4, **Evaluate**: credits that a verse can be translated alone and shows what the ruku' view adds, reaching a reasoned judgement) · *Explanation of what ruku' adds* (0-2): coherence, theme, connection · *Clarity* (0-1).
5. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Steps* (0-4): accurate reading, word-by-word translation, key words and theme, in a sensible order within 20 minutes · *Justification* (0-4, **Create**: each step is explained by how it helps pupils move from reading to meaning) · *Suitability for Class 9* (0-1) · *Use of one surah from 105-114* (0-1).
