## A real classroom situation

Nadia is a B.Ed trainee at a government girls' high school in Larkana. In a Class 9 Islamiat lesson the textbook asks: "What is the main theme of Surah al-Kawthar?" A pupil named Hina answers at once: "It is three verses, miss." The class laughs, but Nadia sees the real problem. Hina has read the surah as three separate lines, not as one short passage. She can recite it perfectly, but she has not seen what the three verses are doing together.

Nadia opens her mushaf to the symbol ۝ at the end of the surah. It marks a *ruku'*. By the end of this topic you will be able to read a ruku' as one unit, find the theme of a passage, and practise on the short surahs of Juz 30.

## Explanation

This topic moves from a single verse to a whole passage: how the Qur'an groups its verses, how a passage carries one theme, and how to practise on the short surahs.

### Paragraph-level comprehension (ruku')

**A ruku' is a paragraph-like unit of the Qur'anic text.** The symbol ۝ with a number marks its end in many mushafs. A ruku' gathers verses that belong together, so that a reader can hold one idea in mind before moving to the next.

Rafei Majoomerd, Zarezardini and Mirhoseini (2024) argue that the ruku' is a *semantic unit*: a unit of meaning, not only a division of the text. In a case study of the tenth ruku' of Surah al-Nahl (16:71-76), they show that the six verses form one coherent passage with a topic sentence, supporting statements, a transitional sentence and concluding sentences. They also find that many commentaries ignore the ruku' divisions, and that this neglect can break the sense, disrupt the coherence of the text and confuse the reader. Reading by ruku' keeps each passage's theme independent and its verses connected.

Take Surah al-Kawthar (108) as a worked example. It is three verses and one ruku' (ruku 552). Read it as one unit.

| Verse | Text (Saheeh International) | The job it does in the ruku' |
|---|---|---|
| 108:1 | Indeed, We have granted you, [O Muhammad], al-Kawthar. | Topic sentence: God gives a great gift |
| 108:2 | So pray to your Lord and offer sacrifice [to Him alone]. | Supporting command: the response to the gift |
| 108:3 | Indeed, your enemy is the one cut off. | Concluding statement: the enemy's loss |

The three verses are one movement: a gift, the gratitude and worship it calls for, and the fate of the one who opposes the Prophet. Read verse 1 alone and it is a statement; read it with verses 2 and 3 and it is the opening of a short sermon. That is paragraph-level comprehension.

**Correcting a common misconception: "A ruku' is only a numbered marker in the mushaf."** The number is a sign, not the meaning. The ruku' marks a unit of sense. In a Class 9 lesson, ask the pupils to find the ۝ at the end of a short surah and then to say in one sentence what that ruku' is about. They will find that the marker helps them group the verses.

### Thematic understanding of passages

**The theme of a passage is the main idea it develops.** You find it by joining the key words to the ruku' structure.

In al-Kawthar the key words are *al-Kawthar* (the abundance, a river in Paradise), *ṣalli* (pray), *nhar* (offer sacrifice), *shāni'aka* (your enemy) and *al-abtar* (the one cut off). Join them to the ruku' structure and the theme is clear: gratitude and hope. God has given abundance, so the believer prays and sacrifices, and the one who mocks the Prophet is the one who is cut off. The concept map above draws the three verses around that theme.

Surah al-Asr (103) gives a second example. Its key words are *al-ʿaṣr* (time), *al-insān* (mankind), *khusr* (loss), *āmanū* (they believed), *ʿamilū ṣ-ṣāliḥāt* (they did righteous deeds) and *tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqqi wa-tawāṣaw bi-ṣ-ṣabr* (they advised each other to truth and patience). The theme is time and the human condition: mankind is in loss, except the believers who hold to truth and patience. Rafei and colleagues (2024) describe this as the thematic independence of each ruku' together with its internal coherence. A passage has one main idea, and its verses work together to carry it.

A theme is not a title invented after reading. It is the idea the key words keep returning to. Ask: which word or phrase would I remove to change the passage? In al-Kawthar, remove *al-Kawthar* and the passage collapses. That is the theme word.

### Practice with selected surahs

The short surahs of Juz 30 are the best practice ground. Each is one or two ruku' and each has a clear theme. The table below lists ten of them with their main theme and key words.

<Figure id="fig-U4-6" src="/img/figures/guhq-301/unit-04/fig-U4-6.svg" alt="Table of ten selected short surahs (105 to 114) with each surah's main theme and its key words." />

Read the table, then choose one surah and do the three moves: read it as one ruku', find its key words, and state its theme in one sentence. For example, Surah al-Falaq (113) is one ruku' (ruku 557) about seeking refuge: its key words are *aʿūdhu* (I seek refuge), *rabbi* (the Lord), *al-falaq* (daybreak) and *wa-min sharri* (and from the evil of). Surah al-Ikhlas (112) is one ruku' (ruku 556) about the oneness of God: its key words are *aḥad* (One), *aṣ-ṣamad* (the Eternal Refuge), *lam yalid* (He begets not) and *kufuwan aḥad* (any equivalent).

A Class 9 group in Larkana can do this in one period. Give each pair one surah from the table, let them read it and find the key words, and ask each pair to say the theme in one sentence. The whole class then sees how ten short surahs each carry one idea.

## Activity: One surah, one theme

Work in groups of three. About 25 minutes.

Each group takes one surah from the table: 105, 106, 107, 109, 110, 111, 112, 113 or 114.

1. **Read (5 minutes).** Read the surah aloud together, once, then find its ruku' number in the mushaf or on Quran.com.
2. **Key words (10 minutes).** List four key words with their meanings. Mark any word that repeats.
3. **Theme (5 minutes).** Write one sentence that states the theme of the surah, and name the verse that carries it most clearly.
4. **Share (5 minutes).** Join another group. Teach them your surah in two minutes: its key words, its theme and the verse that carries it. Then listen to theirs.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* What is a ruku', and what does the symbol ۝ mark?
2. *(Remember)* Name the theme and two key words of Surah al-Kawthar (108).
3. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain how Rafei and colleagues (2024) show that a ruku' is a semantic unit, using their case study of 16:71-76.
4. *(Apply)* Choose Surah al-Ikhlas (112) or Surah al-Falaq (113). State its theme in one sentence and name two verses that support it.

## Summary

A ruku' is a paragraph-like semantic unit of the Qur'an, marked by ۝. Rafei and colleagues (2024) show that reading by ruku' keeps a passage's theme independent and its verses connected. The theme of a passage is the main idea its key words keep returning to, and the ruku' structure shows how the verses support it. The short surahs of Juz 30 are ideal practice: each is one or two ruku' and each carries one clear theme.

## Try this at your practicum school

In a Class 9 Islamiat period, ask the pupils to open a short surah such as 108, 112 or 113. Have them find its ruku' number, underline four key words and write one sentence on its theme. Collect three responses and note: the surah, the key words the pupils chose, and whether their one-sentence theme matched the verses. Write a short paragraph on what the task revealed about how pupils read a short surah.

## Summative task

Choose one surah from the table (105 to 114) and write 300-350 words. Give its ruku' number, list four key words with meanings, state its theme in one sentence, and analyse how two of its verses support that theme. Finish with one sentence on what a reader gains from reading the surah as one ruku' rather than as separate verses.

*(Analyze)*

**Mini-rubric (10 marks)**

| Criterion | Strong | Partial | Missing (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ruku' and key words (3 marks) | Correct ruku' number and four accurate key words with meanings (3) | Ruku' or key words partly wrong (1-2) | Ruku' and key words absent or wrong |
| Theme stated (2 marks) | One clear sentence that names the theme (2) | A theme implied but not stated (1) | No theme |
| Analysis of two verses (4 marks) | Shows how each of two verses supports the theme, using the key words (3-4) | Names verses without showing how they support the theme (1-2) | No analysis |
| Reading as one ruku' (1 mark) | A justified sentence on what the ruku' view adds (1) | A bare statement (0) | Absent |

The analysis criterion asks you to connect each verse to the theme. Writing "the verses are about the theme" without showing how does not meet it.
