# Quranic Vocabulary and Grammar - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

The Qur'an uses a compact vocabulary: a few hundred high-frequency roots and particles cover most of the text, and many of them are already part of Urdu. Every Arabic word is one of three kinds: a noun (*ism*), a verb (*fi'l*) or a particle (*harf*), each identified by its own signs. Nouns carry gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular, dual or plural). Feminine nouns usually show a marker such as tā' marbūṭa, but some are feminine by meaning. The dual, which Urdu does not have, marks exactly two. Plurals are either sound (a suffix is added) or broken (the internal pattern changes). Pronouns are detached (standing alone) or attached (joined to a noun, verb or particle), telling the reader who owns, does or receives.

Verbs mark three tenses: past (*māḍī*), present/future (*muḍāriʿ*) and imperative (*amr*). The past uses vowel endings; the present uses prefix letters (*ya-*, *ta-*, *a-*, *na-*); the imperative gives a command. The verb agrees with its subject in person, gender and number. Common verb forms such as Form I (basic) and Form II (doubled middle letter, causing or intensifying) add shades of meaning to one root.

Particles are small words that connect and modify. Prepositions such as *bi*, *min*, *fī* and *ʿalā* link nouns to sentences. Negation particles change a verb: *lā* negates the present, *lam* uses a present form to negate the past, *lan* strongly negates the future, and *mā* negates the past directly. Emphasis particles such as *inna* (indeed) and *qad* (certainly) add force. Grammar-based translation brings all these tools together, labelling each word's part of speech, gender, number, tense and meaning.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence from the Qur'an, not a list of definitions. Translations are Saheeh International (Quran.com).

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of these Arabic words appears most often in the Qur'an? *(Remember)*
   - A) كِتَاب (*kitāb*)
   - B) وَ (*wa*)
   - C) قَالَ (*qāla*)
   - D) يَوْم (*yawm*)
2. In the sentence ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ (1:2), the word *al-ḥamdu* is a: *(Remember)*
   - A) Verb (*fi'l*)
   - B) Particle (*harf*)
   - C) Noun (*ism*)
   - D) Pronoun
3. A noun that takes *al-* (the definite article) or *tanwīn* (a final -n sound) is classified as a(n): *(Remember)*
   - A) Particle
   - B) Verb
   - C) Noun (*ism*)
   - D) Preposition
4. A trainee reads the word مُسْلِمَات (*muslimāt*) and needs to decide its gender and number. Choose the best option: *(Apply)*
   - A) Masculine singular
   - B) Feminine plural (sound)
   - C) Masculine plural (sound)
   - D) Feminine dual
5. The word رَبُّهُمْ (*rabbuhum*) in 3:195 is made of: *(Apply)*
   - A) A verb and a subject pronoun
   - B) Two separate nouns
   - C) A noun (*rabb*) and an attached pronoun (*-hum*, their)
   - D) A particle and a noun
6. Which prefix letter marks "he" as the subject in an Arabic present-tense verb? *(Remember)*
   - A) نـ (*na-*)
   - B) تـ (*ta-*)
   - C) يـ (*ya-*)
   - D) أ (*a-*)
7. The verb عَلَّمَ (*ʿallama*) in 96:4 differs from عَلِمَ (*ʿalima*) because: *(Understand)*
   - A) It uses a different root
   - B) The doubled middle letter (Form II) changes "knew" to "taught"
   - C) It is in the present tense
   - D) It is a noun, not a verb
8. A pupil asks why لَمْ يَلِدْ in 112:3 uses a present-form verb to mean something in the past. The best explanation is: *(Apply)*
   - A) *Lam* changes a past verb to the present
   - B) *Lam* takes a present-form verb and negates it with a past meaning
   - C) *Lam* is the same as *lā* and both mean "not" in the present
   - D) The verb *yalid* is actually in the past tense
9. In the phrase فِي ٱلْأَرْضِ (2:30), what is the part of speech of فِي (*fī*)? *(Remember)*
   - A) Noun
   - B) Verb
   - C) Particle (preposition)
   - D) Pronoun
10. The particle إِنَّ (*inna*) at the start of a Qur'anic sentence adds: *(Understand)*
    - A) Negation
    - B) A question
    - C) Emphasis ("indeed, surely")
    - D) A command

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In two or three sentences, explain why learning root families helps a reader build Qur'anic vocabulary faster. Use one root as an example. *(Understand)*
2. Give the three signs for identifying a noun (*ism*) in Arabic, and show how one of them works with a Qur'anic word. *(Understand)*
3. Explain, in three sentences, the difference between a sound plural and a broken plural. Give one Qur'anic example of each. *(Understand)*
4. Break the word رَبَّنَا (*rabbanā*, 2:286) into its two parts. Name the pronoun, state whose Lord the verse is speaking of, and explain which set of pronouns (detached or attached) this pronoun belongs to. *(Apply)*
5. Identify the tense of the verb سَمِعْنَا (*samiʿnā*, 2:285). Name the root, the tense marker and the subject. *(Apply)*
6. In 112:3, the Qur'an says لَمْ يَلِدْ. In 109:2, it says لَا أَعْبُدُ. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how these two negation particles differ in what they negate. *(Analyze)*
7. The preposition مِن (*min*) means "from" and فِي (*fī*) means "in". In two sentences, explain why a reader cannot skip particles when translating. Use a Qur'anic phrase to support your point. *(Understand)*
8. Take the word يَكْتُبُ (*yaktubu*). State its part of speech, its tense, the prefix that shows the subject, and the subject. Then give its root. *(Apply)*
9. Explain, in three sentences, what إِنَّ (*inna*) and قَدْ (*qad*) each add to a sentence. Use one Qur'anic verse for each. *(Understand)*
10. Analyse, in three or four sentences, why the dual matters in Qur'anic Arabic. Give an example from the Qur'an where the dual changes the meaning a reader would get if they read the word as a plural. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A trainee says: "Qur'anic vocabulary is impossible to learn. There are too many words." In about 250 words, evaluate this claim. Use evidence about high-frequency words, root families and vocabulary techniques, and assess both what is right in the trainee's feeling and what strategies make the task manageable. *(Evaluate)*
2. Take 2:285 from وَقَالُوا۟ to ٱلْمَصِيرُ. Translate word by word, labelling each word's part of speech, gender/number (if a noun or pronoun), and tense/subject (if a verb). Then analyse how the grammar of the verse - its pronouns, tenses and particles - builds the believers' declaration. *(Analyze)*
3. Choose two of the following nouns: مَلَـٰٓئِكَتِهِ (His angels, 2:285), جَنَّتَانِ (two gardens, 55:46), مُسْلِمُونَ (Muslims, 3:52), كُتُبِهِ (His books, 2:285). For each, identify the gender, number, plural type and any attached pronoun. Then analyse how the grammatical form of one of them helps a reader understand what the verse is saying. *(Analyze)*
4. Using 96:1-5, identify every verb, name its root, tense and form (I or II), and explain how the difference between Form I (*ʿalima*, he knew) and Form II (*ʿallama*, He taught) changes the meaning. Then analyse how the tenses in these verses (commands and past statements) build the message of the Qur'an's first revelation. *(Analyze)*
5. Take the first sentence of 2:286: لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا. Translate word by word with grammatical labels. Then evaluate: what does each grammatical feature (the negation, the verb form, the gender of *nafs*, the attached pronoun *-hā*, the exception particle *illā*) contribute to the meaning? Justify your answer with evidence from the Arabic text. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - *wa* (and) appears over 20,000 times. The other three are common but far less frequent.
2. C - *al-ḥamdu* carries *al-* (the), which is a sign of a noun. It means "the praise".
3. C - *al-* and *tanwīn* are signs that identify a noun (*ism*).
4. B - The ending *-āt* is the feminine sound plural suffix. The singular is *muslimah*.
5. C - *Rabb* (Lord) is a noun, and *-hum* (their) is an attached pronoun.
6. C - *Ya-* marks "he" as the subject of a present-tense verb.
7. B - Both share the root ع ل م, but *ʿallama* doubles the middle letter (Form II), changing "knew" to "taught" - the subject makes someone else know.
8. B - *Lam* takes a present-form verb (*yalid*) and negates it with a past meaning ("did not beget"). *Lā* negates the present tense.
9. C - *Fī* is a particle (preposition) meaning "in". It has no meaning alone and cannot take *al-* or *tanwīn*.
10. C - *Inna* adds emphasis: "indeed, surely". It is not negation, a question or a command.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: Learning root families means learning one root that opens several words. For example, the root ع ل م gives *ʿalima* (knew), *ʿallama* (taught), *ʿilm* (knowledge), *ʿālim* (scholar) and *ʿalīm* (All-Knowing). One root unlocks five words, so the work is less than memorising each alone. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the strategy; 1 - gives a root with at least three words; 1 - explains the efficiency.
2. Model: A noun (*ism*) can take *al-* (the definite article), *tanwīn* (a final -n sound), or it names a person, place, thing or quality without tense. For example, *al-kitāb* in 2:2 takes *al-*, confirming it is a noun. **Marks (3):** 1 - two or three signs named; 1 - Qur'anic example; 1 - shows how the sign works.
3. Model: A sound plural adds an ending: masculine *-ūna/-īna* or feminine *-āt*. Example: *muslimūna* (3:52) from *muslim*. A broken plural changes the internal pattern. Example: *kutub* (books) from *kitāb* (2:285). **Marks (3):** 1 - sound defined with example; 1 - broken defined with example; 1 - both examples are Qur'anic.
4. Model: *Rabbanā* = *rabb* (Lord) + *-nā* (our). *-Nā* is a first-person plural attached pronoun. The verse speaks of the believers' Lord. It is an attached pronoun. **Marks (3):** 1 - two parts named; 1 - pronoun identified and meaning stated; 1 - type (attached) stated.
5. Model: Root س م ع (hearing); past tense; *-nā* is the ending for "we". Subject: we. So *samiʿnā* = "we heard". **Marks (3):** 1 - root; 1 - tense; 1 - subject with ending.
6. Model: *Lam* in 112:3 takes a present-form verb and shifts its meaning to the past: "He did not beget." *Lā* in 109:2 negates the present tense directly: "I do not worship." So *lam* negates past events using a present form, while *lā* negates present actions. The choice tells the reader whether the negation is about what never happened or what is not happening now. **Marks (4):** 1 - *lam* explained; 1 - *lā* explained; 2 - analysis of the difference.
7. Model: Particles show how nouns relate to the action. فِي ٱلْأَرْضِ means "in the earth" and مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ means "from the earth". The noun is the same, but the particle changes the meaning completely. Skipping it loses the relationship. **Marks (3):** 1 - names the role; 1 - Qur'anic phrase; 1 - explains the consequence of skipping.
8. Model: *Yaktubu* is a verb, present tense. The prefix يـ (*ya-*) marks "he" as the subject. Root: ك ت ب (writing). "He writes / he will write." **Marks (3):** 1 - part of speech and tense; 1 - prefix and subject; 1 - root.
9. Model: *Inna* means "indeed, surely" and adds emphasis. In 2:20, إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ ("Indeed, Allah is over all things competent") starts with *inna* to stress the certainty. *Qad* means "certainly/already" before a past verb. In 23:1, قَدْ أَفْلَحَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ ("Certainly, the believers have succeeded"), *qad* confirms the success has happened. **Marks (4):** 1 - *inna* explained with verse; 1 - *qad* explained with verse; 2 - distinction between the two.
10. Model: Arabic marks exactly two with the dual ending (-āni/-ayni), not with a plural. In 55:46, جَنَّتَانِ means "two gardens", not "gardens" in general. If a reader treats it as a plural, they miss that the verse promises exactly two, which is part of the surah's structure of pairs. The Qur'an uses the dual in many places, and without it the reader loses precision. **Marks (4):** 1 - dual explained; 1 - Qur'anic example; 2 - analysis of what is lost without the dual.
