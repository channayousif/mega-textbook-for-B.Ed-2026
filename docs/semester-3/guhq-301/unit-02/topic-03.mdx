## A real classroom situation

Ahmed is a B.Ed trainee at a government high school near Larkana. He is reading Surah al-ʿAlaq (96:1-5) with his Islamiat cooperating teacher. The first word is ٱقْرَأْ (*iqraʾ*), "Read!" The teacher says, "This is a command." A few verses later, verse 96:4 says عَلَّمَ (*ʿallama*), "He taught." Ahmed notices that one word feels like it happened already and the other is an order. He asks: "How do I know, just from the Arabic word, whether it is past, present or a command?" The teacher smiles and says, "That is what tenses are for." By the end of this topic you will be able to look at a Qur'anic verb and say which tense it is, who its subject is, and what pattern it follows.

## Explanation

This topic teaches three things about Qur'anic verbs: how to tell the tense, how the verb agrees with its subject, and how to recognise common verb patterns.

### Verbs: past (madi), present (mudari') and imperative (amr)

**Three tenses.** Arabic verbs mark time through their form:

1. **Past** (*māḍī*) - the action is finished. كَتَبَ (*kataba*), he wrote. عَلَّمَ (*ʿallama*), He taught (96:4). The past tense is built on the root's basic stem with vowel endings that show who did the action.
2. **Present/future** (*muḍāriʿ*) - the action is happening now or will happen. يَكْتُبُ (*yaktubu*), he writes / he will write. The present tense is recognised by a prefix letter: يـ (*ya-*) for "he", تـ (*ta-*) for "she/you", أ (*a-*) for "I", نـ (*na-*) for "we".
3. **Imperative** (*amr*) - a command. اكْتُبْ (*uktub*), write! اقْرَأْ (*iqraʾ*), read! (96:1). The imperative has no prefix letter; it speaks directly to "you".

**A worked example: three tenses in Surah al-ʿAlaq.** Look at the first five verses of Surah 96.

| Verse | Arabic word | Tense | Meaning | How you know |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96:1 | ٱقْرَأْ | imperative | Read! | A direct command to "you"; no prefix letter |
| 96:3 | ٱقْرَأْ | imperative | Read! | Same command repeated |
| 96:4 | عَلَّمَ | past | He taught | Ends in *-a*; no prefix; the action is complete |
| 96:5 | عَلَّمَ | past | He taught | Same form; "taught man what he did not know" |

Both verbs come from roots you learned in Unit 1: *iqraʾ* from the root ق ر أ (reading) and *ʿallama* from ع ل م (knowing, in the form that means teaching).

**Correcting a common misconception: "Arabic verbs have no tense."** Some trainees believe Arabic verbs only show completed or not completed, with no real time. It is true that the muḍāriʿ can mean either present or future, and context decides which. But the past tense (*māḍī*) is clearly about finished actions, and the imperative is about right now. The Quranic Arabic Corpus labels every verb's tense, so you can check.

### Subject-verb agreement

**The verb tells you who did the action.** In Arabic, the verb changes its ending (past) or its prefix and ending (present) to agree with the subject's person, gender and number. The table at the top of this topic shows how كَتَبَ changes.

**Key agreement patterns for a beginner:**

| Subject | Past ending | Present prefix + ending | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| he | *-a* (katab**a**) | *ya-...-u* (ya-ktub**u**) | he wrote / he writes |
| she | *-at* (katab**at**) | *ta-...-u* (ta-ktub**u**) | she wrote / she writes |
| they (masc.) | *-ū* (katab**ū**) | *ya-...-ūna* (ya-ktub**ūna**) | they wrote / they write |
| you (masc. sing.) | *-ta* (katab**ta**) | *ta-...-u* (ta-ktub**u**) | you wrote / you write |
| we | *-nā* (katab**nā**) | *na-...-u* (na-ktub**u**) | we wrote / we write |

**A worked example from the Qur'an.** In 2:285, the believers say: سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا (*samiʿnā wa aṭaʿnā*), "We heard and we obeyed."

| Word | Root | Tense | Subject ending | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| سَمِعْنَا | س م ع (hearing) | past | *-nā* (we) | we heard |
| أَطَعْنَا | ط و ع (obedience) | past | *-nā* (we) | we obeyed |

Both verbs end in *-nā*, which means "we". The past tense tells you these are finished actions - the believers are declaring what they have already done.

**A Pakistan-grounded example.** When a pupil recites Surah al-Kafirun (109), the verb *aʿbudu* appears three times. The *a-* prefix means "I", so *aʿbudu* means "I worship". In the same surah, *taʿbudūna* has the prefix *ta-* and the ending *-ūna*, so it means "you (all) worship". Spotting these markers helps the pupil see that the surah is a conversation between two sides.

### Common Quranic verb patterns

**Verb forms.** Arabic grammar lists verb forms numbered I to X (and beyond). Each form adds a shade of meaning. A beginner needs three:

| Form | Pattern | What it adds | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | *faʿala* | the basic action | *kataba* (he wrote), *ʿalima* (he knew) |
| II | *faʿʿala* (doubled middle letter) | causing or intensifying | *ʿallama* (he taught - made someone know), *nazzala* (he sent down) |
| IV | *afʿala* (added alif-hamza) | causing or bringing about | *aslama* (he submitted to Islam), *āmana* (he believed) |

**A worked example: Form I and Form II from one root.** From the root ع ل م:

- *ʿalima* (Form I) = he knew. The subject gained knowledge.
- *ʿallama* (Form II, doubled middle letter) = he taught. The subject made someone else know.

In 96:4-5, Allah says *ʿallama* (He taught): "who taught by the pen, taught man that which he knew not." The doubled middle letter shows that the action moves outward - from teacher to learner.

## Activity: Tense spotting in pairs

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Open Surah al-Kafirun (109:1-6) on Quran.com with the word-by-word display.

1. **List (5 minutes).** Write down every verb in the surah. The Corpus labels verbs with "V" in the part-of-speech tag.
2. **Identify (8 minutes).** For each verb, decide: is it past, present or imperative? Use the flowchart from this topic. Write the tense, the prefix or ending that told you, and the subject (I, you, they, etc.).
3. **Discuss (7 minutes).** Compare your answers with another pair. Agree on any differences. Then answer this together: "Why does this surah use mostly present-tense verbs?" Write your answer in two sentences.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Name the three verb tenses in Arabic and give the Arabic name of each.
2. *(Remember)* What prefix letter marks "he" in the present tense? What prefix marks "we"?
3. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain how the verb ending *-nā* works in the past tense, and give a Qur'anic example.
4. *(Apply)* Open 96:1 on the Quranic Arabic Corpus. Identify the verb, state its tense, and explain the sign that told you.

## Summary

Qur'anic verbs mark three tenses: the past (*māḍī*), the present/future (*muḍāriʿ*) and the imperative (*amr*). The past uses vowel endings, the present uses prefix letters (*ya-*, *ta-*, *a-*, *na-*), and the imperative gives a direct command. The verb agrees with its subject in person, gender and number. Common verb forms such as Form I (basic), Form II (doubled middle, causing) and Form IV (added hamza, causing) add shades of meaning to the same root. These tools let a reader identify who did what, when, and in what way.

## Try this at your practicum school

During a Nazira Quran period, choose a surah the class is reading and find one past-tense verb and one present-tense verb in it. Write both on the board with their meanings. Ask the class: "Which one already happened? Which one is happening now or will happen?" Note which tense the pupils found easier to understand and write a short reflection.

## Summative task

Write 250-300 words. Take Surah al-ʿAlaq, verses 96:1-5.

1. Identify every verb in these five verses. For each, state its root, its tense and the sign that told you.
2. Explain the difference between Form I (*ʿalima*, he knew) and Form II (*ʿallama*, He taught) using these verses.
3. Analyse how knowing the tenses helps a reader understand the message of these opening verses of the Qur'an's revelation.

*(Analyze)*

**Mini-rubric (10 marks)**

| Criterion | Strong | Partial | Missing (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbs identified with roots and tenses (3 marks) | All verbs correctly identified with root, tense and sign (3) | Most verbs correct but one or two errors (1-2) | Absent or mostly wrong |
| Form I and Form II explained (3 marks) | Shows the doubled middle letter and explains how it changes the meaning from knowing to teaching (3) | Names the forms without explaining the meaning change (1-2) | Absent |
| Analysis of tense and message (4 marks) | Connects the tenses to the message (commands to the Prophet, statements about Allah's past and ongoing teaching) and draws a conclusion (3-4) | Notes the tenses without connecting them to the message (1-2) | No analysis |

The analysis criterion asks you to connect grammar to the surah's message. Listing tenses without explaining what they tell the reader does not meet it.
