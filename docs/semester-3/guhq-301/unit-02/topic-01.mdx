## Explanation

This topic gives you three things: a starter vocabulary of the words you will meet most often, a way to sort every Arabic word into one of three kinds, and techniques for remembering what you learn.

### Common Quranic vocabulary (high-frequency words)

**A small number of words covers most of the text.** The Quranic Arabic Corpus (Dukes et al.) tags every word in the Qur'an by root, form and part of speech. Studies of this data show that the top 300 root words and particles account for roughly 70-80% of the entire text. In other words, a reader who knows a few hundred items can follow the gist of most verses.

The table above lists twenty words a beginner should learn first. They were chosen because each appears hundreds of times. Many are already familiar from Urdu or daily Muslim practice.

**Nouns you already know.**

|---|---|---|---|---|

| رَبّ | *rabb* | Lord | ربّ, as in "ya Rab" | over 950 |

The figures are rounded from the Quranic Arabic Corpus. The important point is not the exact number but the gap: *wa* alone accounts for over 20,000 occurrences. Learning it once means recognising it in every surah.

**Correcting a common misconception: "a dual is the same as a plural."** We will study the dual in Topic 2.2, but many trainees confuse it with the plural even before they meet it. For now, notice that Arabic keeps three numbers apart: one (singular), two (dual) and more than two (plural). Urdu uses only singular and plural, so the dual is new. Knowing that the dual exists helps you read a word's ending correctly.

### Parts of speech: noun, verb, particle

**Three kinds, not more.** Traditional Arabic grammar, which the Quranic Arabic Corpus follows, divides every word into exactly three kinds (Dukes, Atwell & Sharaf, 2010):

1. **Noun** (*ism*, اسم) - a word that names a person, place, thing, idea or quality. It does not carry tense. Examples: *Allāh*, *kitāb*, *yawm*, *ʿilm*.
2. **Verb** (*fi'l*, فعل) - a word that names an action or a state tied to a time (past, present or future). Examples: *kāna* (was), *qāla* (said), *āmana* (believed).
3. **Particle** (*harf*, حرف) - a word that has no complete meaning on its own but connects or modifies nouns and verbs. Examples: *wa* (and), *min* (from), *fī* (in), *lā* (not).

<Figure id="fig-U2-2" src="/img/figures/guhq-301/unit-02/fig-U2-2.svg" alt="Diagram showing the three parts of speech (ism, fi'l, harf) with two identifying signs and two example words under each" />

**How to tell them apart: the signs.** Arabic grammar gives each kind its own signs. Here are two signs for each that a beginner can use.

| Kind | Sign 1 | Sign 2 | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noun (*ism*) | It can take *al-* (the) | It can take *tanwīn* (a final -n sound: -un, -an, -in) | *al-kitābu*, *kitābun* |
| Verb (*fi'l*) | It shows tense (past, present, command) | It can take a subject pronoun ending | *kataba* (he wrote), *yaktubu* (he writes) |
| Particle (*harf*) | It cannot take *al-* | It cannot take *tanwīn* or a tense marker | *min*, *fī*, *wa* |

**A worked example: sorting the words of 1:2.** Take the verse ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ, "[All] praise is [due] to Allah, Lord of the worlds."

| Word | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *al-ḥamdu* | noun | It carries *al-* and means "the praise" |
| *li* | particle | It is a preposition meaning "for, to"; it has no meaning alone |
| *Allāhi* | noun | It is a proper name (a noun) |
| *rabbi* | noun | It means "Lord (of)" |
| *al-ʿālamīna* | noun | It carries *al-* and means "the worlds" |

This one verse has four nouns and one particle. No verb. The sentence is a nominal sentence (*jumlah ismiyyah*), a type you will study in Unit 3.

### Vocabulary memorization techniques

Knowing which words to learn is the first step. The second is remembering them. Three techniques work well for Qur'anic vocabulary.

1. **Root families (from Unit 1).** When you learn *kitāb* (book), add *kātib* (writer) and *maktūb* (written). The root ties them together, so you learn three words for the effort of one.
2. **Phrase spotting.** Find a word inside a phrase you already say. You know *al-ḥamdu lillāh* from daily life. That phrase contains two of the twenty high-frequency words: *al-ḥamd* and *Allāh*. When you notice a word inside a phrase, you have already memorised it.
3. **Daily verse method.** Read one short verse each day on Quran.com with the word-by-word display. Write the new words on a card. By the end of a month you have thirty verses and a growing word bank.

**A Pakistan-grounded example.** A trainee at a government school in Sukkur tried the daily-verse method with Surah al-Mulk (67). After ten days she had met *Allāh*, *al-ladhī*, *khalaq* (created), *samāwāt* (heavens) and *mawt* (death). She noticed that *khalaq* and *mawt* are words her pupils use in Urdu. She wrote them on the classroom board and three pupils said, "Miss, we know these!"

## Activity: Word-sort race

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Each pair opens Surah al-Fatiha (1:1-7) on Quran.com with the word-by-word display.

1. **Sort (8 minutes).** Write every word from the surah on a slip of paper (or in a notebook column). Sort each word into one of three columns: noun, verb, particle. Use the signs from the table above.
2. **Check (5 minutes).** Click each word on the Quranic Arabic Corpus to see its part-of-speech tag. Mark any word you placed in the wrong column.
3. **Explain (7 minutes).** For each word you got wrong, write one sentence explaining why you were mistaken and what sign you missed. Share your hardest case with another pair.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* From memory, list ten high-frequency Qur'anic words, give the meaning of each and say whether it is a noun, a verb or a particle.
2. *(Understand)* Explain, in two or three sentences, why learning root families helps a reader remember more words with less effort.
3. *(Apply)* Take the word مِن (*min*). Apply the two signs for a noun and the two signs for a verb. Show that *min* fails both tests, and state its part of speech.
4. *(Apply)* Open verse 2:3 on the Quranic Arabic Corpus. Pick three words from it, classify each as noun, verb or particle, and give the sign you used.

## Summary

A small number of high-frequency words covers most of the Qur'an, and many of them are already part of Urdu. Every Arabic word is a noun (*ism*), a verb (*fi'l*) or a particle (*harf*), and each kind has signs that identify it. Root families, phrase spotting and the daily-verse method help a learner remember new words. These three tools - vocabulary, classification and memory technique - prepare the ground for the grammar that follows.

## Summative task

Write 250-300 words. A colleague says: "Learning Qur'anic vocabulary is too hard. There are thousands of words and no system."

1. Explain how the high-frequency word list and root families reduce the task.
2. Classify the following three words as noun, verb or particle, giving the sign for each: رَبّ (*rabb*), قَالَ (*qāla*), فِي (*fī*).
3. Analyse how knowing one root (such as ع ل م or ك ت ب) gives a reader access to several words at once, with examples from the Qur'an.
