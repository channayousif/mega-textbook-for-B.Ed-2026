# Introduction to Quranic Arabic

Most of us learned to read the Holy Qur'an as children. We can read every line of the Arabic text, and many of us know several surahs by heart. Yet when a pupil asks, "Miss, what does this word mean?", many of us cannot answer. This unit begins the journey from reading the Qur'an to understanding it. It shows why that journey matters, what kind of Arabic the Qur'an uses, and the first tools a reader needs: the letters, the way words are built, and the expressions that come back again and again.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain, using verses of the Qur'an, why understanding the Qur'an matters for a Muslim and for a teacher. *(Understand; SLO:GUHQ-301-1-1)*
- Describe the main characteristics of Quranic Arabic, with an example of each. *(Understand; SLO:GUHQ-301-1-1)*
- Explain how Quranic Arabic relates to Classical Arabic, and how both differ from the Arabic spoken today. *(Understand; SLO:GUHQ-301-1-1)*
- Pronounce and tell apart the Arabic letters that Urdu speakers tend to merge, and explain why the difference matters. *(Apply; SLO:GUHQ-301-1-2)*
- Break a Qur'anic word into its root and pattern, and analyse how its meaning follows from both. *(Analyze; SLO:GUHQ-301-1-2)*
- Recognise and translate a set of frequently occurring Qur'anic expressions. *(Apply; SLO:GUHQ-301-1-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

You need to be able to read the Qur'anic script with its vowel marks (*nazira*), which most learners bring from home, mosque or school. No knowledge of Arabic grammar is assumed. Your Urdu or Sindhi will help more than you expect, because both languages borrowed thousands of words from Arabic.

## In this unit

1. [Why Quranic Arabic, and what makes it distinct](./topic-01) - the importance of understanding the Qur'an, the characteristics of Quranic Arabic, and how it differs from Classical Arabic.
2. [Letters, word formation and common expressions](./topic-02) - the alphabet and its pronunciation, how words are built from roots and patterns, and frequent Qur'anic expressions.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each begins with a real school situation, explains the ideas and shows a worked example before asking you to try. Keep two free tools open as you read: [Quran.com](https://quran.com), for the Arabic text with English and Urdu translations, and the [Quranic Arabic Corpus](https://corpus.quran.com), which shows the root of every word. Look up each verse the unit cites. This unit quotes the Saheeh International English translation on Quran.com; compare it with an Urdu translation you trust.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
