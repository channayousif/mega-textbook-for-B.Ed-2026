# Unit 5: Grading, Reporting and Interpreting Scores - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

This unit covered what a teacher does with assessment evidence after the test has been marked. Marking records how much of a task a pupil got right and preserves detail; grading converts marks into a category with a shared meaning and preserves a communicable judgement at the cost of detail. Percentages scale marks obtained against marks available, while a grade point average weights each course's grade by its credit hours, which is why the two can move in different directions. A report card serves pupils, parents, teachers and the school, and it serves them only when it carries a comparator and a next step alongside the mark.

A set of scores is described by its centre and its spread. The mean uses every value and is pulled by extremes; the median resists outliers; the mode names the most frequent value and may not exist. The standard deviation describes how far the marks sit from the mean, and it is the number that tells a teacher whether one class can be taught as a single group. Finally, the item difficulty index reports the proportion of pupils who answered an item correctly, and the item discrimination index reports whether the item separates stronger pupils from weaker ones in the way the rest of the test does. Read together, the two indices tell a teacher whether to keep an item, revise it or drop it.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which statement most accurately captures the difference between marking and grading? *(Understand)*  
   A. Marking converts marks into a grade, whereas grading records how many marks a task earned.  
   B. Marking records how much of a task a pupil got right, whereas grading converts marks into a category with a shared meaning.  
   C. Marking is carried out by external examination boards, whereas grading is carried out only by classroom teachers.  
   D. Marking always uses letters, whereas grading always uses percentages.

2. A Class 9 pupil obtains 605 marks out of 1100 in a board examination. Expressed as a percentage, this is: *(Remember)*  
   A. 50.5 percent  
   B. 55 percent  
   C. 56.5 percent  
   D. 60.5 percent

3. A school report card shows a pupil's marks in five subjects, but no class comparator, no teacher's remark and no attendance. Which reader is served worst by this card? *(Apply)*  
   A. The board, which needs the total mark  
   B. The parent, who needs to know what the child should practise next  
   C. The head teacher, who needs the promotion list  
   D. The clerk, who needs to file the register

4. In a grade point average, each course's grade points are multiplied by that course's: *(Remember)*  
   A. Total marks  
   B. Credit hours  
   C. Position in the timetable  
   D. Number of test items

5. A Class 5 section of eight pupils scores 4, 6, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 9 on a ten-mark quiz. Which measure of central tendency equals 8? *(Remember)*  
   A. The mean only  
   B. The median only  
   C. The mode only  
   D. The mean and the median

6. The mean mark of a 20-pupil class is 60. A pupil who had been absent joins the class and scores 100. What happens to the mean? *(Apply)*  
   A. It stays at 60, because the median is unchanged.  
   B. It rises, because the total increases by more than the class size does.  
   C. It falls, because the class now has one more pupil.  
   D. It stays at 60, because the mode is unchanged.

7. Two sections of the same class have the same mean mark of 55. Section A has a standard deviation of 3 and Section B has a standard deviation of 11. Which statement is correct? *(Understand)*  
   A. Section A has the higher average achievement.  
   B. Section B's marks are more spread out from the mean than Section A's.  
   C. Section A's marks are more spread out from the mean than Section B's.  
   D. The two sections have the same spread, because their means are equal.

8. The item difficulty index of a test item is: *(Remember)*  
   A. The number of pupils who answered the item incorrectly  
   B. The proportion of pupils who answered the item correctly  
   C. The difference between the upper group and lower group scores  
   D. The number of distractors a pupil considered before choosing

9. In a class of 30 pupils, 24 answer item 4 correctly. The difficulty index of item 4 is: *(Apply)*  
   A. 0.24  
   B. 0.40  
   C. 0.75  
   D. 0.80

10. An item has a discrimination index of -0.15. Which action should the teacher take first? *(Apply)*  
    A. Keep the item, because a negative index does not affect the rest of the test.  
    B. Check the scoring guide and the wording of the item.  
    C. Drop the item at once and re-score the whole paper without it.  
    D. Increase the marks the item carries, so that it discriminates better.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain the difference between marking and grading, and give one example of a decision a teacher makes that needs marks rather than a grade. *(Understand)*

2. A pupil scores 68 percent in one test and 68 percent in a second test on a different topic. Explain why these two identical percentages may not represent the same level of learning. *(Understand)*

3. A school report card carries marks and grades, but no class comparator, no remark and no attendance. Analyse two consequences of this for the pupil and one for the parent. *(Analyze)*

4. Define the mean, the median and the mode in one sentence each. *(Understand)*

5. A teacher reports only the class mean to parents at a meeting. Analyse two ways in which this can mislead a parent whose own child scored far above that mean. *(Analyze)*

6. Explain what a standard deviation of 3 marks means for a class whose mean mark is 60, and state what would change if the standard deviation were 12 instead. *(Understand)*

7. State the formula for the item difficulty index, and explain what a value of 0.90 means for a classroom test. *(Understand)*

8. An item has a difficulty index of 0.95 and a discrimination index of 0.05. Analyse why these two results together suggest that the item is not doing useful work. *(Analyze)*

9. Describe the three groups used in computing an item discrimination index, and explain how a remainder is handled when the number of pupils does not divide evenly by three. *(Understand)*

10. An item has a discrimination index of -0.20. Analyse two possible causes of this result, and state which check the teacher should make first. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Reporting scale in a Pakistani school:** A government elementary school in Sindh is planning to replace the percentages on its report cards with letter grades.  
   - (a) Evaluate whether this change would improve the school's communication with parents. *(Evaluate)*  
   - (b) Justify your judgement by explaining what each scale preserves and what each loses, and identify which group of readers would be most affected. *(Analyze)*

2. **Two sections, one average:** Two sections of Class 7 General Mathematics have the same mean mark of 52. Section A has a standard deviation of 4; Section B has a standard deviation of 13.  
   - (a) Analyse what the two standard deviations tell you about the readiness of the pupils in each section. *(Analyze)*  
   - (b) Propose two concrete adjustments the teacher of Section B should make to grouping and to feedforward, and explain why each follows from the spread. *(Apply)*

3. **Is this test fit for purpose:** A Class 6 teacher sets a ten-item objective test. Item analysis shows one item with a difficulty index of 1.00 and a discrimination index of 0.00, and a second item with a difficulty index of 0.20 and a discrimination index of -0.10.  
   - (a) Evaluate whether the test as a whole is fit for the purpose of reporting pupils' achievement to their parents. *(Evaluate)*  
   - (b) Propose one revision to the test and justify it using both indices. *(Analyze)*

4. **Designing a results summary (integrative):** Design an end-of-term results summary for one subject in Class 8 that a parent could read in two minutes.  
   - (a) Specify each element your summary contains, and justify it by saying what it tells the parent that the mark alone would not. *(Create)*  
   - (b) Explain how you would handle a pupil whose mark is far below the class mean, so that the summary stays honest without being discouraging. *(Analyze)*

5. **Item analysis of a short test (integrative blueprint item):** A Class 7 General Mathematics test is taken by 24 pupils. Ranked by total score, they form an upper group of 8, a middle group of 8 and a lower group of 8. Three items were answered as follows:

   | Item | Upper group correct (of 8) | Middle group correct (of 8) | Lower group correct (of 8) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | X | 8 | 5 | 2 |
   | Y | 4 | 4 | 4 |
   | Z | 2 | 3 | 6 |

   - (a) Calculate the difficulty index and the discrimination index for each of items X, Y and Z. *(Apply)*  
   - (b) Decide whether each item should be kept, revised or dropped, and justify each decision using both indices. *(Analyze)*  
   - (c) State one check the teacher should make before acting on the weakest of the three items. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Marking is the recording of how much of a task a pupil got right; grading is the conversion of marks into a category that carries a shared meaning.
2. **B** - (605 / 1100) x 100 = 55 percent.
3. **B** - A parent needs a comparator and a next step; a card carrying only marks gives neither.
4. **B** - Grade points are weighted by credit hours, so a course that takes more of a pupil's time counts more.
5. **C** - The mean is 7.125 and the median 7.5; only the mode equals 8.
6. **B** - The total rises by 100 while the class size rises by one, so the new mean is 1300 / 21, about 61.9.
7. **B** - A larger standard deviation means the marks sit further from the mean; it says nothing about which section has the higher achievement.
8. **B** - The difficulty index is the proportion of pupils who answer the item correctly.
9. **D** - 24 / 30 = 0.80.
10. **B** - A negative discrimination index signals a faulty item, so the scoring guide and the wording must be checked before anything is decided.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Marking and grading:**  
   - *Model answer:* Marking is the process of deciding how many marks a piece of work earns against an agreed scoring guide. Grading is the process of converting one or more marks into a category such as a letter, a band or a word. A decision that needs marks is diagnosing which items a pupil got wrong so that the next lesson can be planned; a decision that needs a grade is reporting whether the pupil has met the standard for promotion.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for defining marking; 1 mark for defining grading; 1 mark for a correct example of a decision that needs marks rather than a grade.

2. **Two identical percentages:**  
   - *Model answer:* The two tests may differ in difficulty, in content and in what the class achieved on each. A pupil who scores 68 percent on a paper that most of the class found hard has demonstrated more than a pupil who scores 68 percent on a paper that most of the class finished easily. A percentage describes performance on one paper only; it carries no information about the paper or the cohort.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for naming a difference between the two tests (difficulty, content or cohort); 1 mark for explaining that a percentage describes one paper and not the level of learning.

3. **Consequences of a bare mark card:**  
   - *Model answer:* For the pupil, the card gives no comparator, so the pupil cannot tell whether the mark is good or poor, and it gives no next step, so the pupil has nothing to act on. For the parent, the card gives no indication of what the child is good at or where help is needed, so the parent cannot support the child's learning at home.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for each of two consequences for the pupil; 1 mark for a consequence for the parent.

4. **Three measures of centre:**  
   - *Model answer:* The mean is the sum of all values divided by the number of values. The median is the middle value of the data once they are ordered, or the average of the two middle values when the number of values is even. The mode is the value that occurs most often.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for each correct definition.

5. **What the mean hides from a high-scoring parent:**  
   - *Model answer:* (1) The mean says nothing about spread, so a parent whose child scored far above it cannot tell whether the child is one of a few high flyers or one of many. (2) The mean says nothing about the shape of the distribution, so the parent cannot tell whether the child's result is typical of a small group at the top or an isolated one. In both cases the parent may read the child's position as more secure than it is.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for each distinct way the mean misleads, explained.

6. **Interpreting a standard deviation:**  
   - *Model answer:* A standard deviation of 3 means most pupils sit within about three marks of the mean of 60, so the class is working at a fairly uniform level. A standard deviation of 12 means the marks are spread over a range four times as wide, so the class contains pupils working at very different levels and the teacher needs more than one starting point.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for explaining what the standard deviation measures; 1 mark for interpreting 3; 1 mark for contrasting 12.

7. **Difficulty index formula and meaning:**  
   - *Model answer:* The difficulty index is the number of pupils who answer the item correctly divided by the number of pupils who attempted it. A value of 0.90 means that nine out of ten pupils got the item right, so the item was easy for that group.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for the formula; 1 mark for interpreting 0.90.

8. **A very easy item that does not discriminate:**  
   - *Model answer:* A difficulty index of 0.95 means almost every pupil got the item right, and a discrimination index of 0.05 means the upper and lower groups performed almost identically on it. An item that nearly everyone answers correctly cannot separate stronger pupils from weaker ones, so it adds nothing to the rank order the test is meant to produce while still costing time and space.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for reading the difficulty index; 1 mark for reading the discrimination index; 1 mark for explaining why the pair makes the item useless for ranking.

9. **The three groups and a remainder:**  
   - *Model answer:* The pupils are ranked by total score and divided into a top group, a middle group and a bottom group. If the number of pupils does not divide evenly by three, the remainder is assigned to the top and bottom groups in equal numbers, so the middle group is the one that is reduced.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for naming the three groups; 1 mark for describing the ranking; 1 mark for stating how a remainder is handled.

10. **A negative discrimination index:**  
    - *Model answer:* A value of -0.20 means the lower group answered the item correctly more often than the upper group. Two possible causes are a wrong key, where the option marked as the answer is not in fact right, and ambiguous wording, where stronger pupils reason their way to a defensible alternative while weaker pupils guess the keyed option. The first check is the scoring guide, because a wrong key is the simplest explanation and the cheapest to fix.  
    - *Mark scheme (4 marks):* 1 mark for explaining what the negative value means; 2 marks for two distinct plausible causes; 1 mark for naming the scoring guide as the first check and justifying it.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Reporting scale in a Pakistani school (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Evaluation of the change (Evaluate, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below standard (1-2 marks):* States a preference for grades or percentages without weighing the consequences for readers.  
  - *Meets standard (3 marks):* Evaluates the change from the parent's point of view and identifies at least one group of readers who would be better and one worse served.  
  - *Exceeds standard (4 marks):* Evaluates the change across pupils, parents, teachers and the board, and acknowledges a trade-off rather than declaring a winner.
- **Criterion 2: Use of unit concepts (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Uses the terms without explaining what each scale preserves or loses.  
  - *Score 2-3:* Explains what percentages preserve and lose, and what grades preserve and lose, with a Pakistan school example.  
  - *Score 4:* Explains the trade-off precisely and shows how it applies differently to a parent and to the board.
- **Criterion 3: Professional register and structure (2 marks):**  
  - 1 mark for a clear structure; 1 mark for a register appropriate to a school staff room.

#### ERQ 2: Two sections, one average (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Analysis of the spreads (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below standard (1-2 marks):* Restates the two standard deviations without saying what they imply for pupils.  
  - *Meets standard (3 marks):* Explains that Section B's marks are far more spread out and that its readiness is therefore uneven.  
  - *Exceeds standard (4 marks):* Explains the consequence for the range of marks a single lesson must cover in Section B.
- **Criterion 2: Proposed adjustments (Apply, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Proposes generic strategies unrelated to the spread.  
  - *Score 2-3:* Proposes two concrete adjustments to grouping and feedforward that follow from a wide spread.  
  - *Score 4:* Proposes two adjustments and explains the mechanism by which each addresses the spread.
- **Criterion 3: Feasibility in a government school (2 marks):**  
  - 2 marks for proposals that need no additional resources and fit a ordinary classroom.

#### ERQ 3: Is this test fit for purpose (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Evaluation (Evaluate, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below standard (1-2 marks):* Judges the test by one item or by the mean rather than by the evidence given.  
  - *Meets standard (3 marks):* Evaluates fitness for the stated purpose using both items and both indices.  
  - *Exceeds standard (4 marks):* Evaluates fitness and identifies what the test can and cannot support a parent to conclude.
- **Criterion 2: Proposed revision (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Proposes a change without linking it to the indices.  
  - *Score 2-3:* Proposes one revision and justifies it using both the difficulty and the discrimination index.  
  - *Score 4:* Proposes a revision and explains how it would change each index, including the risk of making the item too easy.
- **Criterion 3: Register and structure (2 marks):**  
  - 1 mark for structure; 1 mark for a register appropriate to a conversation with a colleague.

#### ERQ 4: Designing a results summary (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Elements and justification (Create, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below standard (1-2 marks):* Lists elements without saying what each communicates beyond the mark.  
  - *Meets standard (3 marks):* Specifies the elements and says what each tells a parent that the mark alone would not.  
  - *Exceeds standard (4 marks):* Specifies elements that work together to give a parent a comparator, a next step and a trend.
- **Criterion 2: Handling a low mark honestly (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Avoids the issue or proposes hiding the mark.  
  - *Score 2-3:* Explains how to report a low mark with a comparator and a next step, keeping the report honest.  
  - *Score 4:* Explains how to keep the report honest and actionable at the same time, with a concrete example.
- **Criterion 3: Feasibility (2 marks):**  
  - 2 marks for a summary a teacher could produce from an ordinary mark register.

#### ERQ 5: Item analysis of a short test (Total: 12 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Computation (Apply, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* The indices are missing or largely wrong.  
  - *Score 2-3:* The difficulty index is correct for all three items and the discrimination index for at least two.  
  - *Score 4:* Both indices are correct for all three items and the working is shown.
- **Criterion 2: Keep, revise or drop decisions (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Decisions are asserted without reference to the indices.  
  - *Score 2-3:* Each item receives a decision justified by both indices, with item Z correctly identified as the weakest.  
  - *Score 4:* Each decision is justified by both indices and the reasoning distinguishes an item that is merely easy from one that is actively misleading.
- **Criterion 3: First check on the weakest item (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Names a check without a reason.  
  - *Score 2-3:* Names the scoring guide as the first check and explains why it comes before rewriting the item.  
  - *Score 4:* Names the scoring guide first and explains what a wrong key would mean for every pupil's total, not only for this item.
