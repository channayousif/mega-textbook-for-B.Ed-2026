# Unit 5: Grading, Reporting and Interpreting Scores

Welcome to Unit 5 of Educational Assessment and Evaluation. In the preceding units you learned how to plan a test, write its items and gather evidence of learning. This unit deals with what a teacher does next: turning raw marks into grades, reporting those grades to pupils, parents and the school, and reading a set of scores honestly. It also introduces the two numbers that tell a teacher whether an individual test item is doing its work, the difficulty index and the discrimination index.

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, prospective teachers will be able to:

1. **Distinguish marking from grading and report results well (SLO:EFMP-408-5-1):** Explain what marking preserves and what grading preserves, convert between marks, percentages and grade point averages, and design a report card entry that tells a parent what the mark means.
2. **Describe a set of scores (SLO:EFMP-408-5-2):** Calculate and interpret the mean, median, mode and standard deviation of a small score set, and explain which measure gives the fairest picture of a class.
3. **Judge test items statistically (SLO:EFMP-408-5-3):** Compute the item difficulty index and the item discrimination index from a small score table, interpret both, and decide whether an item should be kept, revised or dropped.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds on two earlier bodies of work:

- **Unit 3, Constructing Tests (EFMP-408):** You already know how a table of specifications distributes items across content and cognitive levels, and how objective and subjective items are written and scored. Item analysis in Topic 5.3 only makes sense once a test has been built and marked in that disciplined way.
- **Quantitative Reasoning I (GQUR-300, Semester 1):** The arithmetic of this unit, addition, division, ordering numbers and square roots, is ordinary intermediate-level mathematics. If a formula feels unfamiliar, revisit the worked examples before moving on; every calculation here is done by hand on a small data set.

## In this unit

Unit 5 is organised into three topic cycles:

1. [Topic 5.1 - From marks to grades, and reporting them](./topic-01): Separates marking from grading, works through percentages and grade point averages, and shows what a report card must tell a parent before it can be called a report.
2. [Topic 5.2 - Describing a set of scores](./topic-02): Introduces the mean, median, mode and standard deviation, and shows why one average can hide more than it reveals about a real classroom.
3. [Topic 5.3 - Item analysis](./topic-03): Computes the difficulty and discrimination indices for a short quiz and uses them to decide which items to keep, which to revise and which to drop.

## How to use this unit

Each topic file is a self-contained learning cycle. Work through it in order:

- Read **A real classroom situation** and notice the tension the topic resolves. Every vignette is set in a Pakistani school you could be posted to.
- Study the **Explanation** section closely. Each `###` sub-heading teaches one sub-topic from the course guide, with a worked example before you are asked to perform the same move yourself.
- Do the **Activity** with a partner or a small group. These are discussion tasks, not private reflection, so agree on a time box and appoint someone to report back.
- Answer every item in **Check your understanding** without looking back at the Explanation. Retrieval is the point.
- Tick the **Self-assessment checklist** only when you can honestly say the statement is true of your own work.
- Carry out **Try this at your practicum school** during your placement. It is bounded so that one school visit is enough.
- Complete the **Summative task** and its mini-rubric, then work through the whole **Unit assessment** at the end of the unit.
