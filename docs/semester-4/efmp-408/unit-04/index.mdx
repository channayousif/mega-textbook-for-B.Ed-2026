# Unit 4: Performance-Based and Classroom Assessment

Welcome to Unit 4 of Educational Assessment and Evaluation. Units 1 to 3 built the foundations: what assessment is, how objectives and test items are aligned, and how a test is planned and judged. This unit turns to the assessments that ask pupils to do something rather than choose something. A written test can show what a pupil knows. It often cannot show whether the pupil can carry out an experiment, speak in front of a class, keep a record of their own progress, or judge the quality of their own work. Performance tasks, portfolios, projects, rubrics and the everyday techniques of observation, peer assessment, self-assessment and feedback are the teacher's tools for that wider evidence.

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, prospective teachers will be able to:

1. **Design tasks that assess what pupils can do (SLO:EFMP-408-4-1):** Plan practical tests, presentations, portfolios and projects that elicit genuine performance, and explain what each shows that a written test cannot.
2. **Build rubrics that make scoring public (SLO:EFMP-408-4-2):** Construct analytic and holistic rubrics with criteria, levels and descriptors, and use them to score pupil work consistently and to give useful feedback.
3. **Use everyday classroom techniques to gather and act on evidence (SLO:EFMP-408-4-3):** Use quizzes, classroom observation, peer assessment and self-assessment, and give feedback that moves learning forward rather than feedback that only judges.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit assumes the foundations laid in Units 1 to 3:

- **Unit 1 (Understanding Assessment and Evaluation):** The distinction between assessment and evaluation, and between formative and summative assessment, which decides how the techniques in this unit should be used.
- **Unit 2 (Objectives, Alignment and the Principles of Good Assessment):** Writing observable objectives and judging validity, reliability, practicality and fairness, because a performance task or rubric must be aligned with its objective and fair to every pupil.
- **Unit 3 (Constructing Tests):** Planning with a table of specifications and writing items, because a performance task is still a test that needs a plan and a scoring rule.

## In this unit

Unit 4 is organised into three interconnected topic cycles:

1. [Topic 4.1 - Assessing what pupils can do](./topic-01): Practical tests, presentations, portfolios and projects, and the kind of evidence each one produces.
2. [Topic 4.2 - Rubrics](./topic-02): Analytic and holistic rubrics, and how to build and use them without turning a rubric into a mark scheme.
3. [Topic 4.3 - Everyday classroom techniques and feedback](./topic-03): Quizzes, classroom observation, peer assessment, self-assessment and the feedback strategies that make them work.

## How to use this unit

Each topic file is a self-contained learning cycle designed for active study and collaborative discussion:

- Begin with **A real classroom situation**, reflecting on how a Pakistani teacher faces the tension between what a written test can show and what a pupil can actually do.
- Study the **Explanation** section carefully, paying attention to the worked examples, the common misconception and the featured figures.
- Take part in the collaborative **Activity** with your peers, practising the design of a task, a rubric or a feedback move in a simulated classroom context.
- Answer every item in **Check your understanding** to test your grasp before moving on.
- Review the **Self-assessment checklist** to monitor your progress against concrete competence statements.
- Complete **Try this at your practicum school** during your placement to connect the textbook to a real classroom.
- Finish the **Summative task** in each topic, then work through the complete **Unit assessment** at the end of the unit.
