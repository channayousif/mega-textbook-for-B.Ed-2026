# Unit 1: Understanding Assessment and Evaluation

<Figure id="fig-U1-4" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efmp-408/unit-01/fig-U1-4.webp" alt="A teacher in a Sindh government school classroom bends beside a student's desk to give supportive feedback on a lesson worksheet while other pupils work in pairs." width={1344} height={756} />

Welcome to Unit 1 of Educational Assessment and Evaluation. This unit introduces prospective teachers to the conceptual foundation of educational measurement, testing, assessment and evaluation. In Pakistani schools, assessment is frequently treated as identical to end-of-term examinations. This unit challenges that narrow perspective, demonstrating that assessment is an ongoing, multifaceted process of gathering evidence to inform and enrich teaching and learning.

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, prospective teachers will be able to:

1. **Explain the conceptual boundaries of assessment (SLO:EFMP-408-1-1):** Differentiate clearly between test, measurement, appraisal, assessment and evaluation, articulating why systematic assessment is essential for responsive pedagogy.
2. **Contrast formative and summative functions (SLO:EFMP-408-1-2):** Analyse how formative assessment shapes ongoing learning through descriptive feedforward, while summative assessment evaluates and reports cumulative achievement at terminal points.
3. **Select appropriate assessment purposes and reference frames (SLO:EFMP-408-1-3):** Distinguish diagnostic and placement assessment, and determine when to apply norm-referenced versus criterion-referenced interpretations of learner achievement.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This introductory unit builds upon foundational concepts established in Semester 1:

- **Educational Psychology (EFMP-301):** Understanding cognitive development, individual differences in learning readiness, motivation, and how constructive feedback affects student self-efficacy.
- **Teaching Profession (EFMP-302):** Familiarity with instructional planning, teacher professional standards in Pakistan, and the ethical responsibility of teachers to treat all learners equitably.

## In this unit

Unit 1 is organised into three interconnected topic cycles:

1. [Topic 1.1 - What assessment is, and what it is not](./topic-01): Clarifies the essential definitions of assessment, testing, measurement, appraisal and evaluation, examining the pivotal role assessment plays across the instructional cycle.
2. [Topic 1.2 - Shaping learning and reporting it](./topic-02): Explores formative assessment as an engine for learning alongside summative assessment as cumulative evaluation, showing how both functions support student growth.
3. [Topic 1.3 - Diagnostic, placement, and two reference frames](./topic-03): Examines diagnostic and placement tools for identifying prerequisite readiness, and contrasts norm-referenced with criterion-referenced interpretation frames.

## How to use this unit

Each topic file is structured as a self-contained learning cycle designed for active study and collaborative discussion:

- Begin with **A real classroom situation**, reflecting on how experienced Pakistani teachers encounter assessment dilemmas in everyday classrooms.
- Study the **Explanation** section carefully, paying close attention to the conceptual boundaries, worked examples, and the featured figures.
- Participate actively in the collaborative **Activity** with your peers, practicing assessment decisions in simulated classroom contexts.
- Answer all questions in **Check your understanding** to verify your grasp before advancing.
- Review the **Self-assessment checklist** to monitor your learning progress against specific competence statements.
- Complete the **Try this at your practicum school** assignment during your school placement visits to link textbook principles to authentic classroom realities.
- Finally, complete the **Summative task** in each topic and work through the complete **Unit assessment** at the end of the unit.
