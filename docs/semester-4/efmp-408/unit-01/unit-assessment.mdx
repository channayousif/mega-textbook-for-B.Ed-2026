# Unit 1: Assessment and Evaluation - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

This unit examined the conceptual architecture of educational assessment and evaluation. In professional teaching, assessment is the systematic, ongoing process of gathering evidence about student learning to make learner thinking visible and inform instructional moves. It is distinguished from testing (a specific measurement instrument), measurement (the quantitative assignment of numerals according to explicit rules), and appraisal (holistic qualitative estimation). Evaluation represents the broadest scope, delivering qualitative value judgments about educational adequacy, worth, and policy.

Assessment serves two complementary functions across instructional time: formative assessment (assessment for learning) operates during instruction to diagnose gaps and guide feedforward adjustments without punitive stakes, whereas summative assessment (assessment of learning) operates at terminal points to evaluate cumulative mastery, award certification, and report outcomes to stakeholders. Finally, assessment data can be gathered before instruction through placement and diagnostic tools, and interpreted through either a norm-referenced frame (comparing relative rank against peers for selection) or a criterion-referenced frame (evaluating performance against explicit competency standards for mastery learning).

   - (c) Classify this assessment practice along its **timing and placement** (diagnostic, formative, or summative). Explain how Mr Kamran's instructional moves reflect sound pedagogical alignment. *(Analyze)*

5. **Ethical and systemic evaluation of assessment in Pakistan:** NPSTP Standard 5 asks teachers to value diverse learner backgrounds, to believe that all students can achieve high standards, and to understand the ethical constraints on assessment.  
   - (a) Critically analyse how traditional rote-based examination practices in Pakistan often violate principles of fairness and validity for students from multilingual or rural backgrounds. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Assessment is the broader process of collecting learning evidence, while evaluation makes value judgments regarding worth or adequacy (HEC, 2012).
2. **D** - Assigning numbers according to explicit rules without passing a qualitative judgment is the technical definition of measurement.
3. **C** - A test is a specific instrument sampling behaviour, whereas assessment integrates observations, interviews, work samples, and tests.
4. **B** - NPSTP Standard 5 states that teachers use multiple assessment strategies to evaluate student learning and inform instruction, adapting daily teaching to student evidence (NPSTP, 2009).
5. **C** - An assessment is formative if and only if the generated evidence is actively used to modify ongoing instruction and learner practice.
6. **B** - Recording scores for grades without reviewing errors or adjusting subsequent teaching means the quiz functioned as a periodic summative audit.
7. **C** - Effective feedforward is descriptive, pointing out current achievement and concrete next steps rather than generic praise or letter marks.
8. **A** - Testing prior knowledge before instruction begins to identify readiness is placement and diagnostic assessment.
9. **D** - Norm-referenced interpretation compares an individual's score to the distribution of a defined peer group.
10. **C** - Evaluating performance against an absolute performance benchmark (70% cut score) independent of peer rankings defines criterion-referenced testing.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Definitions and relationship:**  
   - *Model answer:* A test is a specific instrument or formal procedure used to elicit a behaviour sample. Measurement is the quantitative process of assigning numerals to that performance according to explicit rules. Evaluation is the qualitative judgment of value, worth, or adequacy based on evidence. Assessment is the overarching umbrella that gathers, synthesises, and interprets multiple forms of evidence (including tests, measurements, observations, and work samples) to inform educational decisions.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for defining test and measurement accurately; 1 mark for defining evaluation; 1 mark for explaining their nested relationship within assessment.

2. **Harms of over-relying on terminal exams:**  
   - *Model answer:* (1) Terminal examinations encourage superficial rote memorisation rather than deep conceptual understanding, as students study solely to pass the test and rapidly forget material. (2) They provide delayed, autopsy-like data that arrives too late to remediate learning gaps for the current cohort of students.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for each distinct, pedagogically sound reason explained in the context of Pakistani schools.

3. **Immediate instructional modifications:**  
   - *Model answer:* (1) The teacher should pause the planned progression and provide explicit re-modelling using think-aloud strategies and visual sentence-diagramming on the chalkboard. (2) The teacher should organise structured peer-assisted practice, pairing students who mastered subject identification with those who struggled, followed by a rapid slate-board check.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for each realistic, low-resource instructional adjustment tailored to a primary classroom.

4. **Three questions of descriptive feedforward:**  
   - *Model answer:* (1) *Where am I going?* (Clarifying the learning objective and criteria for success). (2) *Where am I now?* (Analysing the current level of student work against the standard). (3) *How do I get there?* (Providing concrete, actionable steps to bridge the gap).  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for each question that is correctly identified and explained in terms of how it helps the learner improve.

5. **Stakes and consequences contrast:**  
   - *Model answer:* For an elementary student, formative assessment carries low stakes and zero penalty for errors, creating a psychologically safe environment that encourages intellectual risk-taking, questioning, and self-correction. In contrast, summative assessment carries high stakes, as scores directly determine official marks, report cards, class rankings, parental approval, and grade promotion.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for the formative perspective; 1 mark for the summative perspective.

6. **Why ungraded does not equal formative:**  
   - *Model answer:* Formative status is determined by how assessment evidence is used, not by the absence of marks. If an ungraded worksheet is collected, marked with ticks and crosses, and returned without discussion or instructional adaptation, it has not shaped learning. It becomes formative only when the teacher or student uses the evidence to adjust teaching moves or revise work.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for refuting the absence-of-marks fallacy; 1 mark for stating that instructional use defines formative status.

7. **Disadvantage of class rank reporting:**  
   - *Model answer:* Class ranks foster unhealthy peer competition and hide true academic achievement. A pupil ranked 3rd in an underperforming school may still lack basic grade-level reading skills, while a pupil ranked 35th in an exceptional class may have mastered 85% of the curriculum. Ranking demoralises lower-ranked students without providing diagnostic guidance on what skills to improve.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for identifying the motivational or diagnostic defect; 1 mark for explaining the distortion of achievement.

8. **Placement vs diagnostic assessment:**  
   - *Model answer:* Placement assessment occurs prior to instruction to determine entry level, prerequisite knowledge, and stream assignment (answering *Where should the learner begin?*). Diagnostic assessment can occur before or during instruction to identify persistent learning difficulties, error patterns, and underlying cognitive misconceptions (answering *Why is the learner struggling, and with what specific concept?*).  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for placement timing and purpose; 1 mark for diagnostic purpose and focus on root causes; 1 mark for distinguishing their educational outcomes.

9. **Medical college reference frame:**  
   - *Model answer:* The medical college uses a norm-referenced framework. It was selected because the resource is fixed (only 100 available physical seats), requiring a competitive ranking mechanism to select the top-performing applicants relative to the candidate pool, regardless of whether 500 applicants achieved high raw marks.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for identifying norm-referenced; 1 mark for justifying based on fixed institutional quota.

10. **Criterion referencing in licensing:**  
    - *Model answer:* Criterion referencing is mandatory in teacher licensing because professional certification serves as a public guarantee of safety and pedagogical competence. A licence must verify that every certified teacher has independently demonstrated mastery of core teaching standards (the criterion), rather than merely scoring higher than other poorly qualified applicants.  
    - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for connecting criterion referencing to competency standards; 1 mark for explaining the public assurance/safety mandate.

#### ERQ 3: Reference frames in educational policy (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Purpose classification and justification (Analyze, 3 marks):**

- **Criterion 2: Reference frame classification and justification (Analyze, 3 marks):**  
  - *Score 0-1:* Classifies as norm-referenced, or gives confused reasoning about ranking.
