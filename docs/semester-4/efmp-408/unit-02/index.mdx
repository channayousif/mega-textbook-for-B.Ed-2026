# Unit 2: Objectives, Alignment and the Principles of Good Assessment

Welcome to Unit 2 of Educational Assessment and Evaluation. Unit 1 established what assessment is and how it differs from measurement, testing and evaluation. This unit turns to the professional craft of designing assessment: writing objectives that say what learners should be able to do, aligning teaching and assessment with those objectives, and judging whether a test measures what it claims to measure, does so consistently, can actually be administered, and is fair to every learner. In Pakistani classrooms, a test is often treated as something a teacher simply hands out at the end of a chapter. This unit treats a test as a designed instrument that must earn the inferences teachers draw from it.

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, prospective teachers will be able to:

1. **Write and align instructional objectives (SLO:EFMP-408-2-1):** Write observable instructional objectives in the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains using Bloom's Taxonomy, and align teaching activities and assessment items with those objectives.
2. **Judge validity and reliability (SLO:EFMP-408-2-2):** Explain content, construct and criterion validity and test-retest and inter-rater reliability, and judge the validity and reliability of a given teacher-made test.
3. **Judge practicality and fairness (SLO:EFMP-408-2-3):** Assess whether a test is practical and usable in a resource-constrained classroom, and identify sources of bias that threaten fairness.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds directly on Unit 1 of this course:

- **Unit 1, Topic 1.1:** The distinctions between test, measurement, assessment and evaluation, and the idea that assessment gathers evidence to inform decisions.
- **Unit 1, Topic 1.2:** The difference between formative and summative assessment, and the role of feedback in closing learning gaps.
- **Unit 1, Topic 1.3:** Norm-referenced and criterion-referenced interpretation, which this unit applies when judging what a test score can and cannot support.

## In this unit

Unit 2 is organised into three interconnected topic cycles:

1. [Topic 2.1 - Objectives, domains and alignment](./topic-01): Shows how to write observable instructional objectives with Bloom's Taxonomy, how the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains differ, and how objectives, teaching and assessment must align.
2. [Topic 2.2 - Validity and reliability](./topic-02): Explains content, construct and criterion validity and test-retest and inter-rater reliability, using the target metaphor to show that a test can be reliable without being valid.
3. [Topic 2.3 - Practicality, fairness and bias](./topic-03): Examines whether a test can be administered and marked with the time, staff and materials a Pakistani school actually has, and identifies the sources of bias that make a test unfair.

## How to use this unit

Each topic file is a self-contained learning cycle designed for active study and collaborative discussion:

- Begin with **A real classroom situation**, reflecting on how Pakistani teachers meet the practical problems of objectives, alignment and test quality.
- Study the **Explanation** section carefully, paying attention to the worked examples, the figures, and the common misconception named and corrected in each topic.
- Work with peers on the **Activity**, practising objective writing, alignment, validity judgement and bias detection in realistic classroom contexts.
- Answer every item in **Check your understanding** to retrieve and apply what you have studied before moving on.
- Use the **Self-assessment checklist** to judge your own readiness against clear "I can" statements.
- Carry the **Try this at your practicum school** task into your school placement so the ideas meet a real timetable, a real staff room and a real group of pupils.
- Complete the **Summative task** in each topic, then work through the complete **Unit assessment** at the end of the unit.
