# Unit 2: Objectives, Alignment and the Principles of Good Assessment - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

This unit turned assessment from an event into a design problem. An instructional objective states what learners will be able to do, in observable language, at a stated level of Bloom's Taxonomy, and the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains remind a teacher that knowledge, attitudes and physical skills all deserve planned assessment. Alignment means that the objective, the teaching activity and the assessment item demand the same behaviour, so that a pupil's score measures what was taught rather than a mismatch.

A test must then earn the inferences drawn from it. Validity is the degree to which the evidence supports the intended inference, and it appears as content validity, which asks whether the test samples the taught content; construct validity, which asks whether it measures the intended skill; and criterion validity, which asks whether it agrees with an external criterion. Reliability is consistency, seen in test-retest stability and inter-rater agreement. Reliability is necessary but not sufficient for validity: a test can measure the wrong thing very consistently. Finally, a test must be practical, meaning it can be administered, marked and used with the resources a school has, and fair, meaning every pupil has a genuine opportunity to show the skill being assessed. Fairness is not sameness: removing barriers that are irrelevant to the skill protects the meaning of the score.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which command verb belongs to the Remember level of Bloom's Taxonomy? *(Remember)*  
   A. List  
   B. Justify  
   C. Design  
   D. Critique

2. Which statement best explains why "Students will learn about fractions" is a poor instructional objective? *(Understand)*  
   A. It is written in language that is too simple for secondary pupils.  
   B. It names content but no observable behaviour, so a teacher cannot tell whether it has been met.  
   C. It is too short to be entered in the lesson plan register.  
   D. It does not mention the textbook page numbers the class will use.

3. A teacher's objective is "pupils will be able to explain the water cycle", and her test item asks pupils to list the three states of matter. The mismatch is best described as: *(Apply)*  
   A. The objective and the item are both at the Understand level, so they are aligned.  
   B. The objective demands explanation while the item demands recall, so the item is below the objective.  
   C. The item is too difficult for the pupils who were taught the water cycle.  
   D. The objective is affective, while the item is psychomotor.

4. Which of the following objectives is written in the affective domain? *(Remember)*  
   A. Pupils will calculate the area of a rectangle.  
   B. Pupils will label the parts of a plant cell.  
   C. Pupils will show respect for a classmate's view during a group discussion.  
   D. Pupils will use a microscope to focus a prepared slide.

5. Validity is best defined as: *(Remember)*  
   A. The degree to which a test produces consistent scores on repeated occasions.  
   B. The degree to which the evidence supports the inference a teacher draws from the scores.  
   C. The number of marks a test carries and the time allowed to complete it.  
   D. The agreement between two teachers who mark the same scripts.

6. A Class 8 science test contains 22 items on one chapter and 8 items spread across the other four chapters taught in the unit. Which type of validity is weakest? *(Understand)*  
   A. Criterion validity  
   B. Construct validity  
   C. Content validity  
   D. Predictive validity

7. Two teachers mark the same ten Class 9 English essays and differ by as much as 8 marks out of 20 on the same script. Which kind of reliability is low? *(Apply)*  
   A. Test-retest reliability  
   B. Inter-rater reliability  
   C. Split-half reliability  
   D. Parallel-forms reliability

8. A weighing scale always reads two kilos too heavy. Which statement best explains why it is reliable but not valid? *(Apply)*  
   A. It is consistent, but the readings do not support the inference about true weight.  
   B. It is accurate, but it gives different readings each time it is used.  
   C. It is valid, but only when it is used by a trained person.  
   D. It is neither consistent nor accurate, so it cannot be used at all.

9. Practicality in assessment is best judged by: *(Understand)*  
   A. Whether the test looks thorough and covers every topic in the textbook.  
   B. Whether the school has the time, staff and materials to administer, mark and use the test.  
   C. Whether the test contains only objective items that can be marked quickly.  
   D. Whether the test has been used by a neighbouring school.

10. A Class 5 mathematics item asks pupils to calculate a percentage increase in ticket sales at a foreign football stadium, using a currency the class has never handled. What is the most appropriate first step for the teacher? *(Apply)*  
    A. Keep the item, because pupils must learn about the wider world.  
    B. Rewrite the item in a familiar local context while keeping the same mathematical structure.  
    C. Remove all percentage items from the test, because they are too difficult.  
    D. Give the pupils the exchange rate and teach the currency before the test.

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define an instructional objective in one sentence, and explain why an observable verb is essential. *(Understand)*
2. Explain the difference between the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains, and give one classroom example of each. *(Understand)*
3. A teacher's objective says "pupils will appreciate the importance of trees", her lesson has pupils copy a list of uses of trees, and her test asks pupils to design a poster campaigning to save trees. Analyse where the alignment breaks and what the pupil's score can and cannot support. *(Analyze)*
4. Explain the difference between content validity and construct validity, and give one example of a threat to each in a Pakistani classroom. *(Understand)*
5. Explain test-retest reliability and inter-rater reliability, and state one practical way a teacher can strengthen each. *(Understand)*
6. A Class 7 mathematics test has 30 items, of which 18 test only one of the five units taught. Identify the validity problem, explain its likely effect on the meaning of the scores, and state one step that would strengthen the test. *(Analyze)*
7. Define practicality in assessment and name three resources a teacher should check before choosing a test format. *(Understand)*
8. A Class 6 science item asks pupils to "explain how a refrigerator keeps food cold" in a school where no pupil has seen a refrigerator. Identify the source of bias, explain which pupils are disadvantaged, and rewrite the item so that it measures the same skill. *(Analyze)*
9. Explain why giving every pupil exactly the same paper under exactly the same conditions is not always fair, and give one example of a legitimate allowance. *(Analyze)*
10. A head teacher sets one two-hour written examination for every subject in Class 5, with no coursework and no practical tasks. Analyse one practical problem and one fairness problem with this decision. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Designing an aligned assessment set:** Choose a topic you are likely to teach in a named grade and subject.  
   - (a) Write one cognitive objective at Analyze level or higher, one affective objective and one psychomotor objective, each in observable language. *(Create)*  
   - (b) Describe one teaching activity that practises the cognitive objective, and write one assessment item that aligns with it. *(Apply)*  
   - (c) Explain how the objective, activity and item would be checked for alignment, and what a teacher should change if they do not align. *(Analyze)*

2. **Judging validity and reliability:** A Class 9 biology teacher in Larkana sets a 25-item test on "respiration and photosynthesis". Twelve items test recall of definitions, eight test labelled diagrams, and five test everyday applications. Several application items use formal English the class has not met, and the teacher has never compared marks with a colleague or re-marked a sample.  
   - (a) Judge the test for content, construct and criterion validity, giving the evidence for each judgement. *(Analyze)*  
   - (b) Judge the test for test-retest and inter-rater reliability, stating what is unknown and what is likely weak. *(Analyze)*  
   - (c) Recommend one step to improve validity and one to improve reliability, and explain why reliability alone would not make the test trustworthy. *(Evaluate)*

3. **Evaluating fairness and practicality:** A government primary school in Tharparkar has 55 Class 5 pupils, one teacher, no microscope and one hour of electricity a day. The teacher sets a paper with 40 essay items, an individual oral test for every pupil and a question about the wildlife of Canada.  
   - (a) Judge the paper for practicality and usability, given the school's resources. *(Analyze)*  
   - (b) Judge the paper for fairness, identifying at least two groups of pupils who could be disadvantaged and why. *(Evaluate)*  
   - (c) Propose a practical and fairer alternative that still gives the teacher useful information, and justify why a fairer plan can also be a more valid one. *(Evaluate)*

4. **Integrative critique of a supplied test (Blueprint integrative item):** Read the following short test and its stated objective carefully.  
   *Stated objective: "By the end of the unit, Class 6 pupils will be able to solve two-step word problems involving addition and subtraction."*  
   *The test has 20 items. Items 1 to 15 are one-step addition or subtraction problems. Items 16 to 20 are two-step problems, all set in a foreign airport with references to duty-free shops and luggage allowances the class has never met. The teacher marks all 20 items with one total and reports the rank order to parents. The school has 60 pupils and one teacher, and the paper takes 90 minutes to mark.*  
   - (a) Critique the test for validity, identifying at least two specific problems with evidence from the description. *(Analyze)*  
   - (b) Critique the test for reliability and practicality, explaining what each judgement rests on. *(Analyze)*  
   - (c) Critique the test for bias and fairness, and state one change that would remove an irrelevant barrier without lowering the mathematical demand. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Judging a test against its stated objectives (Blueprint Evaluate item):** A Class 7 General Science teacher states the objective "pupils will be able to design a simple fair test to compare two materials". The end-of-unit test asks pupils to recall the steps of the scientific method and label a diagram of apparatus.  
   - (a) Judge whether the test is valid for the stated objective, and justify your judgement with specific evidence. *(Evaluate)*  
   - (b) Judge whether the test is fair to pupils who were taught the objective but assessed on recall, and explain the consequence for the meaning of their marks. *(Evaluate)*  
   - (c) Redesign one section of the test so that it assesses the stated objective, and explain how you would check that the redesigned section is reliable to mark. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **A** - "List" recalls facts, which is the Remember level; justify, design and critique belong to Evaluate and Create.
2. **B** - The statement names content but no observable behaviour, so the teacher cannot tell whether the objective has been met.
3. **B** - The objective demands explanation (Understand), while the item demands recall (Remember), so the item is below the objective.
4. **C** - Showing respect for a classmate's view concerns attitude and values, which is the affective domain.
5. **B** - Validity concerns the support for the inference drawn from scores, not consistency, length or marker agreement.
6. **C** - Over-representing one chapter and under-representing the others weakens content validity, the match between the test and the taught content.
7. **B** - Widely different totals from two markers indicate low inter-rater reliability.
8. **A** - The scale is consistent, so it is reliable, but its readings do not support the inference about true weight, so it is not valid.
9. **B** - Practicality concerns whether the school can administer, mark and use the test with its available time, staff and materials.
10. **B** - Rewriting the item in a familiar context removes an irrelevant barrier while keeping the same mathematical demand.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Definition and the role of the verb:**  
   - *Model answer:* An instructional objective is a statement of what learners will be able to do after instruction, written in observable and measurable language. An observable verb is essential because it lets the teacher look at pupil work and decide whether the objective has been met; a verb such as "appreciate" names no action that can be seen.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for a correct definition; 1 mark for stating that the verb must be observable; 1 mark for explaining why, with a brief example.

2. **The three domains:**  
   - *Model answer:* The cognitive domain concerns knowledge and mental skills, for example "pupils can state the three conditions seed germination needs". The affective domain concerns attitudes and values, for example "pupils will show respect for a classmate's opinion in discussion". The psychomotor domain concerns manual and physical skills, for example "pupils will use a microscope to focus a prepared slide".  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for each domain correctly described, and 1 mark for a fitting example. (4 marks maximum; award 3 if descriptions are sound but examples are thin.)

3. **Alignment analysis:**  
   - *Model answer:* The objective is affective and vague ("appreciate"), the activity is passive copying, and the item demands a Create-level poster. The alignment breaks between the objective and the activity, and again between the activity and the item. The pupil's score can support a judgement about poster design, but it cannot support a judgement about appreciation of trees, because appreciation was never taught or assessed.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for identifying the objective-activity mismatch; 1 mark for identifying the activity-item mismatch; 1 mark for stating what the score can and cannot support.

4. **Content versus construct validity:**  
   - *Model answer:* Content validity asks whether the test adequately samples the taught content, protected by a table of specifications. Construct validity asks whether the test measures the intended skill or trait. A content threat is a paper that tests only one of five taught units. A construct threat is a science test written in formal English that ends up measuring reading rather than science.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for each correct definition; 1 mark for a fitting example of each threat.

5. **Two forms of reliability:**  
   - *Model answer:* Test-retest reliability is the consistency of scores when the same test is given to the same pupils on two occasions a short time apart. Inter-rater reliability is the agreement between two or more markers of the same work. A teacher can strengthen test-retest reliability by writing unambiguous items and keeping conditions stable, and inter-rater reliability by using an analytic rubric with clear descriptors.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for each correct definition; 1 mark for one practical strengthening step for each form.

6. **Validity problem and effect:**  
   - *Model answer:* The test has weak content validity because 18 of 30 items test only one of five units. Scores will overstate mastery of that unit and understate mastery of the others, so the total is a poor guide to the whole unit. Strengthening the test requires redistributing items across the five units, ideally with a table of specifications.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for identifying weak content validity; 1 mark for explaining the distortion of the total; 1 mark for a realistic strengthening step.

7. **Practicality and resources:**  
   - *Model answer:* Practicality is the capacity to administer, mark and use a test with the resources available. A teacher should check the time available for pupils and for marking, the number of staff who can supervise and mark, the materials and cost such as paper, printing and apparatus, and the conditions in the room.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for a correct definition; 1 mark for each of two further resources, or 2 marks for three resources described with brief effect.

8. **Bias identification and rewrite:**  
   - *Model answer:* The item carries cultural and socio-economic bias, because it assumes experience of a refrigerator that no pupil in the school has. Pupils are disadvantaged for a reason unrelated to the science being tested. A fair rewrite keeps the same skill in a familiar context, for example "explain how a matka (earthen pot) keeps water cool".  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for identifying the bias; 1 mark for naming the disadvantaged group and the reason; 1 mark for a rewrite that keeps the same skill.

9. **Fair is not identical:**  
   - *Model answer:* Identical treatment can produce unequal opportunity when a pupil faces a barrier that is irrelevant to the skill being assessed, such as low vision, slow writing or unfamiliar language. A legitimate allowance is a larger-print paper or extra time, which removes the barrier without changing the skill being measured.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for explaining the principle; 1 mark for a concrete barrier; 1 mark for a legitimate allowance and why it is not a lowering of the standard.

10. **Practical and fairness problems:**  
    - *Model answer:* Practically, a two-hour written examination in every subject multiplies marking and invigilation for a single teacher, and leaves no time for practical work. For fairness, it disadvantages pupils who write slowly, pupils who were absent for part of the teaching, and pupils with reading difficulties, because it offers one route to showing learning and no alternative.  
    - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for a practical problem; 1 mark for a fairness problem naming a group; 1 mark for explaining why the group is disadvantaged.

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Designing an aligned assessment set (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Objective quality (Create, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1-2 marks):* Objectives are headings or name no observable behaviour, or only one domain is present.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* One objective in each domain, observable and appropriately levelled.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Three precise, observable objectives, clearly distinguished by domain and level, with a realistic context.
- **Criterion 2: Activity and item alignment (Apply, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* The activity or item is unrelated to the objective.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* The activity practises the objective and the item demands the same behaviour.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* The activity and item are tightly aligned and the level is named and correct.
- **Criterion 3: Alignment check and repair (Analyze, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* States that alignment should be checked without saying how.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Names a concrete check and a realistic repair.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Explains how the check would detect a mismatch and how the repair protects the meaning of the score.

#### ERQ 2: Judging validity and reliability (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Validity judgement (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1-2 marks):* Confuses validity with difficulty or length; names no type correctly.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Judges content, construct and criterion validity with evidence from the described test.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Gives a precise, well-evidenced judgement of all three types and explains how they interact.
- **Criterion 2: Reliability judgement (Analyze, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Treats reliability as the same idea as validity.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Identifies test-retest and inter-rater reliability and states what is unknown or weak.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Analyses both forms and explains what evidence would settle each.
- **Criterion 3: Recommendations and reasoning (Evaluate, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Generic advice such as "improve the test".  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* One realistic validity step and one realistic reliability step.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Well-justified steps, including why reliability alone cannot deliver trust.

#### ERQ 3: Evaluating fairness and practicality (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Practicality analysis (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1-2 marks):* Describes the paper without judging resources or usability.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Judges practicality and usability against the school's real constraints.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* A detailed analysis of time, staffing, materials and usability, with the trade-offs named.
- **Criterion 2: Fairness evaluation (Evaluate, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Says only that the plan is unfair.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Identifies two disadvantaged groups and explains the reason.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Distinguishes fair from identical and cites legitimate allowances.
- **Criterion 3: Alternative plan and justification (Evaluate, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* A vague alternative such as "use different tests".  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* A practical, fairer plan with a brief justification.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* A well-justified plan and an explanation of why fairness strengthens validity.

#### ERQ 4: Integrative critique of a supplied test (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Validity critique (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1-2 marks):* Identifies one problem vaguely, or confuses validity with difficulty.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Identifies at least two validity problems with evidence, such as items 1-15 testing one step while the objective demands two steps, and items 16-20 using unfamiliar airport contexts that may measure reading rather than mathematics.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* A precise critique of content and construct validity, explaining how the total misrepresents the stated objective.
- **Criterion 2: Reliability and practicality critique (Analyze, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Mentions marking time without connecting it to practicality.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Explains that inter-rater reliability is unknown and that 90 minutes of marking for 60 pupils is a practical burden.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Analyses both judgements and states what evidence would settle them.
- **Criterion 3: Bias and fairness (Evaluate, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Says the airport context is unfair without explaining why.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Identifies the cultural and experiential bias and proposes a change that keeps the mathematical demand.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Explains how the change protects the meaning of the score and why a familiar context does not lower the standard.

#### ERQ 5: Judging a test against its stated objectives (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Validity judgement (Evaluate, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1-2 marks):* Says the test is invalid without evidence.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Judges the test invalid for the stated Create-level objective because it assesses recall and labelling, with specific evidence.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* A rigorous judgement explaining the construct mismatch and what the scores can and cannot support.
- **Criterion 2: Fairness judgement (Evaluate, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Says only that the pupils are unfairly treated.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Explains that pupils taught the objective are assessed on recall, so their marks misrepresent their learning.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Explains the consequence for the meaning of the marks and for motivation and trust.
- **Criterion 3: Redesign and reliability check (Create, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Suggests a new question without a way to mark it consistently.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Redesigns a section to assess designing a fair test, with a simple way to check marking consistency.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* A workable redesign with an analytic rubric or checklist that would raise inter-rater agreement.
