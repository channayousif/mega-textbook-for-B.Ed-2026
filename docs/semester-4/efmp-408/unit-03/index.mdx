# Unit 3: Constructing Tests

<Figure id="fig-U3-9" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efmp-408/unit-03/fig-U3-9.webp" alt="Two Pakistani teachers in a school staff room collaborate over a table of specifications and test draft papers spread across a wooden work table." width={1376} height={768} />

Welcome to Unit 3 of Educational Assessment and Evaluation. Building an effective classroom test is one of the most critical responsibilities of a professional educator. In many Pakistani schools, teachers assemble examinations hastily, copying familiar questions from old textbooks or writing items off the top of their heads without planning. Such unstructured tests often assess low-level memorisation, overlook important curriculum areas, and produce misleading scores.

This unit equips prospective teachers with the systematic knowledge and practical skills needed to construct valid, reliable, and fair classroom tests. You will learn how to design a two-dimensional Table of Specifications (test blueprint), balance subjective and objective item formats, write high-quality test questions that target higher-order thinking, and eliminate the common technical flaws that distort student performance.

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, prospective teachers will be able to:

1. **Plan tests using a Table of Specifications (SLO:EFMP-408-3-1):** Construct a two-dimensional test blueprint that systematically balances content topics, cognitive levels under Bloom's Taxonomy, item formats, and percentage weightings to guarantee content validity.
2. **Design and score subjective assessment items (SLO:EFMP-408-3-2):** Construct extended-response essays and structured short-answer questions, articulate what each assesses well, and develop analytic scoring rubrics to mitigate scoring subjectivity and marker bias.
3. **Construct diverse objective test formats (SLO:EFMP-408-3-3):** Write high-functioning multiple-choice questions (dissected into stem, key, and plausible distractors), true/false items, and matching exercises that assess both knowledge and applied reasoning.
4. **Apply item-writing rules and eliminate common flaws (SLO:EFMP-408-3-4):** Audit and refine draft items using professional review checklists, eliminating negative phrasing, grammatical giveaway clues, implausible distractors, and unintended cognitive barriers.

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds directly upon concepts mastered in earlier units:

- **Unit 1 (Understanding Assessment and Evaluation):** Distinction between measurement, testing, assessment, and evaluation, and the operational differences between formative and summative assessment.
- **Unit 2 (Objectives, Alignment, and Principles of Good Assessment):** Formulating instructional objectives using Bloom's Taxonomy, constructive alignment, and the essential test qualities of content validity, reliability, usability, and fairness.

## In this unit

Unit 3 is organised into four interconnected topic cycles:

1. [Topic 3.1 - Planning a test before writing it](./topic-01): Explores why test construction begins with a Table of Specifications, how to allocate curriculum weightage, and how to link Student Learning Outcomes directly to test questions.
2. [Topic 3.2 - Subjective items](./topic-02): Examines extended-response essays and short-answer items, analysing their cognitive strengths, sampling limitations, and the scoring guides needed to control marker bias.
3. [Topic 3.3 - Objective items](./topic-03): Dissects multiple-choice items into stems, keys, and distractors, and investigates the proper design and classroom role of true/false and matching exercises.
4. [Topic 3.4 - Writing items that work](./topic-04): Outlines essential item-writing guidelines, analyses common construction errors, and demonstrates step-by-step item sanitisation using professional quality checklists.

## How to use this unit

Each topic file is structured as a self-contained learning cycle designed for active study and collaborative professional practice:

- Begin with **A real classroom situation**, reflecting on how experienced Pakistani teachers encounter assessment dilemmas in everyday classrooms.
- Study the **Explanation** section carefully, paying close attention to the conceptual principles, worked examples, and the featured figures.
- Participate actively in the collaborative **Activity** with your peers, practicing test blueprinting and item construction in simulated school contexts.
- Answer all questions in **Check your understanding** to verify your grasp before advancing.
- Review the **Self-assessment checklist** to monitor your learning progress against specific competence statements.
- Complete the **Try this at your practicum school** assignment during your school placement visits to link textbook principles to authentic classroom realities.
- Finally, complete the **Summative task** in each topic and work through the complete **Unit assessment** at the end of the unit.
