# Market Research and Business Models - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Market research is the collection and analysis of data about the people a venture wants to serve. It begins with a question, uses primary data collected first-hand and secondary data that already exists, and confirms its findings through triangulation, which means checking a result in more than one way. Research should describe the target customer clearly: who they are, what they worry about, what they do now, what they can pay and where they look. Segmenting, targeting and positioning turn that description into a choice about whom to serve.

Competitor analysis turns the same habit outward. It names who else serves the customer, what they offer, what they charge and where they are weak, and it distinguishes direct rivals from indirect ones. A market with a few small competitors is often a better opportunity than an empty one, because rivals prove that customers exist and will pay. The value of a venture is stated in a value proposition: a short, testable promise of the benefit the customer can expect, built from the customer's pains, gains and job to be done.

A business model is the plan of how a venture creates value for customers and captures some of it back as income. It is not the same as a business plan, which is a longer document for a reader such as a bank; a model is meant to be redrawn as evidence arrives. Models differ in what they optimise: sales volume or profit margin, high price or low, market share or profitability, few choices or many, competition or cooperation, product or service. The Business Model Canvas lays the model out in nine blocks, and revenue streams are the distinct ways money comes in. A small venture usually does better with one or two streams that fit the customer's job than with many half-built ones.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit, market research is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) Asking friends whether they like your idea
   - B) The collection and analysis of data about the people who might buy from a business
   - C) Writing a long report about your product
   - D) Setting the lowest price in the market
2. Which of these is an example of secondary data? *(Remember)*
   - A) A questionnaire you hand out at the school gate
   - B) A focus group you run with parents
   - C) Government figures on school enrolment
   - D) Counting the customers at a rival shop
3. Hina asked forty pupils about their needs and counted customers at two shops. This is an example of: *(Understand)*
   - A) Triangulation
   - B) A target market
   - C) A value proposition
   - D) A direct competitor
4. A YouTube channel that teaches physics numericals for free is, for a pupil practice-sheet business, a/an: *(Understand)*
   - A) Direct competitor
   - B) Indirect competitor
   - C) Secondary source
   - D) Key partner
5. A trainee finds that three stationery shops in a town all sell the same guides at the same prices, and that none stocks practice sheets in the regional language. Which conclusion is best supported? *(Apply)*
   - A) There is no market, so she should drop the idea
   - B) The rivals have proved customers buy guides, and there is a gap she can serve
   - C) She should copy the guides at a lower price
   - D) Online videos mean nobody will buy sheets
6. A business model is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) A long document written for a bank
   - B) The plan of how a venture creates value for customers and captures some of it as income
   - C) A list of the products a business sells
   - D) The same thing as a business plan
7. In a multisided revenue model: *(Understand)*
   - A) Every customer pays the same price
   - B) One group uses the service free and another group pays to reach them
   - C) The business sells only through a marketplace
   - D) The business licenses its name to a rival
8. Which of these belongs in the "key resources" block of the business-model canvas? *(Remember)*
   - A) The people and things the venture needs to work, such as a kit box and a classroom
   - B) The money the venture earns
   - C) The rivals it competes with
   - D) The price it charges
9. A Saturday science club charges parents 200 rupees a month for four sessions and sells a worksheet pack for 50 rupees a term. These are two: *(Apply)*
   - A) Key partners
   - B) Revenue streams
   - C) Customer segments
   - D) Psychographics
10. Bilal's value proposition is "Saturday science practice for Class 8 pupils who missed school". Which statement best explains why it works? *(Apply)*
    - A) It is short and easy to remember
    - B) It names the customer's job and the gain, and a customer could say no to it
    - C) It mentions the price
    - D) It uses the word "science"

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define market research in one sentence, and explain why it is not the same as asking friends whether they like your idea. *(Understand)*
2. Explain the difference between primary and secondary data, and give one example of each. *(Understand)*
3. A trainee wants to sell mathematics practice sheets to Class 8 pupils. Describe two data-collection methods she could use, one primary and one secondary, and say what each would reveal. *(Apply)*
4. Read this case: "Sadiqa asked her five classmates whether they would buy her handmade greeting cards. All five said yes. She ordered 200 cards." Analyse two weaknesses in her evidence. *(Analyze)*
5. Analyse why a market with three small competitors may be a better opportunity than a market with none. *(Analyze)*
6. Explain, in three sentences, the difference between a business model and a business plan. *(Understand)*
7. A classmate says, "A value proposition is just a slogan." Explain two differences between a slogan and a value proposition. *(Understand)*
8. A trainee in Hyderabad runs an evening mathematics club for Class 9 pupils. Fill in four blocks of the business-model canvas for that club. *(Apply)*
9. Analyse why a venture with five revenue streams may serve its customers worse than one with two. *(Analyze)*
10. Read this case: "A tuition centre charges low fees and gets many pupils, but the owner earns little." Analyse which model choice the centre made, and what evidence would tell the owner whether to change. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Friends or evidence?** Hina's three friends all approved of her science-kit idea. Her talk with forty pupils and two science teachers showed that pupils wanted cheap practice sheets instead. In 300-400 words, evaluate whether Hina's research was better than her friends' approval, using the ideas of a target customer, primary and secondary data, and triangulation. Reach a reasoned judgement. *(Evaluate)*
2. **Before the first rupee.** A trainee in Shikarpur wants to start a small business selling learning materials to government schools. In 300-400 words, analyse how she should research customers and competitors before spending money, and judge which single research step matters most and why. *(Analyze)*
3. **A venture from a survey.** A short survey of 60 Class 9 pupils in a government school found that 42 worry about physics numericals, 35 would pay between 30 and 50 rupees for weekly practice, and 28 buy materials at the bazaar stationery shop. In 300-400 words, draft a value proposition and two revenue streams for a learning-materials venture serving these pupils, and explain what each revenue stream depends on. *(Create)*
4. **A model or a plan?** A speaker says, "A business model is only a plan for making money." In 300-400 words, evaluate this claim using the nine blocks of the business-model canvas and the difference between a business model and a business plan. Reach a reasoned judgement. *(Evaluate)*
5. **Three suggestions.** Bilal runs a Saturday science club. Imran says charge parents a monthly fee; Sajid says make a kit and rent it to the school; Bilal's uncle says write a business plan first. In 350-450 words, analyse the three suggestions, and judge which combination would best serve Class 8 pupils, using evidence from both Topic 3.1 and Topic 3.2. *(Analyze)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer, so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Market research is the collection and analysis of data about the people who might buy. A is the friends misconception; C and D are not definitions.
2. **C.** Government enrolment figures already exist, so they are secondary data. A, B and D are collected first-hand for your own purpose.
3. **A.** Hina checked her finding in more than one way: a survey and a count. That is triangulation. The other options are different ideas.
4. **B.** The videos meet the same need in a different way, so they are an indirect competitor. They do not sell the same product, so they are not direct.
5. **B.** Rivals prove that customers buy guides, and the missing regional-language sheets are a gap. A reads an empty market as proof of no demand; C and D ignore the evidence.
6. **B.** A business model is the plan of creating, delivering and capturing value. A describes a business plan; C and D are wrong.
7. **B.** In a multisided model one group is free and another group pays to reach them. The other options describe other models or blocks.
8. **A.** Key resources are what the venture needs to work. Money earned is a revenue stream, rivals are competitors, and price sits in the value proposition and revenue streams.
9. **B.** A monthly fee and a worksheet sale are two distinct ways money comes in, so they are revenue streams.
10. **B.** It names the customer's job and gain and could be tested. A and D describe a slogan; C is the weak draft from the topic.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Market research is the collection and analysis of data about the people who might buy from a business. Friends are a tiny, friendly sample who want to please you, so their approval is encouragement, not evidence. **Marks (3):** 1 for a definition that names data about potential customers; 1 for the difference from friends' approval; 1 for a reason, such as friends are few or biased.
2. **Model:** Primary data is collected first-hand for your own purpose, such as a questionnaire at the school gate. Secondary data already exists, collected for another purpose, such as government enrolment figures. **Marks (3):** 1 for each definition; 1 for each fitting example (2 marks total for examples, 1 for the pair of definitions).
3. **Model:** Primary: a short questionnaire to pupils asking which topic is hardest and what they would pay, which reveals what they say they need. Secondary: a count of what the bazaar stationery shop sells, or government enrolment figures, which reveals how many pupils there are and what is already bought. **Marks (4):** 1 for naming one primary method; 1 for what it reveals; 1 for naming one secondary source; 1 for what it reveals.
4. **Model:** Her sample is five classmates, too small and too friendly to represent buyers. She asked a leading question and got the answer she wanted, with no triangulation and no check of what people would actually pay. **Marks (4):** 2 for each weakness, with a reason.
5. **Model:** Competitors are evidence that customers exist and will pay. Three small rivals show demand and leave room for a different offer. A market with none may mean nobody can make it pay, or that the need is smaller than it looked. **Marks (4):** 1 for competitors as proof of demand; 1 for room to be different; 1 for the risk of an empty market; 1 for a reason, such as no proven demand.
6. **Model:** A business model is a working picture of how the venture creates, delivers and captures value, and it is redrawn as evidence arrives. A business plan is a longer document setting out goals, operations, market and finances for a reader such as a bank. One is a design; the other is a document built on that design. **Marks (3):** 1 for each definition; 1 for the distinction.
7. **Model:** A slogan is made to be remembered; a value proposition is made to be tested. A slogan need not name the customer's job, while a value proposition names the job and the gain. **Marks (4):** 2 for each difference (1 for the difference, 1 for the reason or example).
8. **Model:** Customer segments: Class 9 pupils who need mathematics support and their parents. Value proposition: a pupil who can solve problems without a tutor at home. Revenue streams: a monthly fee and the sale of practice sheets. Cost structure: printing, a room, the tutor's time. **Marks (4):** 1 for each block filled sensibly.
9. **Model:** Five streams split the venture's energy and attention, so each is built weakly. The owner spends time on five small incomes instead of making one or two steady. Two streams that fit the customer's job are easier to keep good. **Marks (4):** 1 for the split of energy; 1 for the risk of weak delivery; 1 for the fit with the customer's job; 1 for a reason or example.
10. **Model:** The centre chose volume over margin: a low price and many pupils. The warning sign is that revenue rises while profit stays flat, which means the price is too low for the cost of serving each pupil. Evidence would be the cost per pupil and the fee per pupil, compared with a rival's fee. **Marks (5):** 1 for naming volume over margin; 1 for the warning sign; 1 for the cost-per-pupil evidence; 1 for a comparison with a rival; 1 for a reason.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.

**ERQ 1: Friends or evidence?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Target customer | Describes Hina's pupils as the target customer and shows what she learned about them | Names the pupils with a partial description | Mentions pupils only | Absent |
| Primary and secondary data | Explains both, with Hina's talk as primary and the shop count or school records as secondary | Explains one well, or both loosely | Names the terms only | Absent |
| Triangulation (Analyze) | Shows how checking in more than one way changed her conclusion, with evidence from the case | Describes two checks without linking them to the change | Mentions checking | Absent |
| Judgement (Evaluate) | Weighs both and reaches a reasoned verdict on which evidence was better | Gives a verdict with partial reasons | States a preference only | No judgement |
