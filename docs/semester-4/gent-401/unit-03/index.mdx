# Market Research and Business Models

Unit 1 asked what an entrepreneur is. Unit 2 asked where opportunities come from. This unit asks the questions that come next. Who will actually buy, and how do you know? Who else is already serving them? And once you have an idea, how will the venture work: what value does it offer, and where does the money come from? Every later unit, from the business plan to funding and marketing, assumes you can answer these questions with evidence rather than hope.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Define market research, a target market and a customer segment, and explain why a venture needs them. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-3-1)*
- Choose suitable primary and secondary data-collection methods for a small venture and explain what each reveals. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-3-1)*
- Carry out a simple competitor analysis and use it to decide how a venture can be different. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-3-1)*
- Describe what a business model is, and distinguish it from a business plan. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-3-2)*
- Write a value proposition for a described venture. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-3-2)*
- Identify at least two revenue streams that could suit a described venture. *(Analyze; SLO:GENT-401-3-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 2. You should already be able to describe what a business opportunity is and how one is recognised. Bring your knowledge of small businesses around your own school or town: shops, tuition centres, canteens, tailors, repairers and learning-materials sellers. You will use these familiar examples throughout the unit.

## In this unit

1. [Researching customers, markets and competitors](./topic-01) - understanding customers and markets, data-collection methods, and how to analyse competitors.
2. [Business models, value and revenue](./topic-02) - types of business model, the value proposition, and revenue streams.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Topic 3.1 is about finding evidence before you spend money. Topic 3.2 is about shaping that evidence into a venture that can survive.

Each topic opens with a real situation from a town in Sindh, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before it asks you to use them. Do the group activity in each topic with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back, and use the self-assessment checklist honestly.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find sequencing help and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
