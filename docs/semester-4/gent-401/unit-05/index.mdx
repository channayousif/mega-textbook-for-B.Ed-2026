# Financial Planning and Funding

"Imran thinks 15 pupils will join his tuition centre. He has not counted the rent, the assistant's pay or the printing." A B.Ed trainee on practicum in Larkana hears this from her friend, and the answer is not as simple as "will 15 pupils come?". This unit shows how a new venture plans its money before it opens, works out how much it must sell to cover its costs, reads the three basic financial statements, and chooses where the money to start will come from. Every later decision in this course, from marketing to growth, depends on these numbers.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Define start-up costs, fixed costs and variable costs, and prepare a simple start-up budget. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-5-1)*
- Calculate a break-even quantity from fixed costs, selling price and variable cost, and explain what it means. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-5-1)*
- Read a simple income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement, and explain why profit and cash are not the same. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-5-1)*
- Compare bootstrapping, a bank loan, an angel investor, venture capital and crowdfunding. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-5-2)*
- Choose a funding source for a described venture, justifying the choice with control, risk and repayment. *(Analyze; SLO:GENT-401-5-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 4, Developing a Business Plan, gives you the venture idea and the plan this unit costs. From Semester 1, GQUR-300 Quantitative Reasoning I gives you the arithmetic you need: percentages, simple equations and reading a table. You also need ordinary school arithmetic and a willingness to work one number at a time.

## In this unit

1. [Costs, budgets, break-even and statements](./topic-01) - start-up costs and budgeting, break-even analysis, and the three basic financial statements.
2. [Where the money comes from](./topic-02) - bootstrapping, loans and investors, venture capital and angel investors, and crowdfunding.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Topic 5.1 teaches the numbers: a budget before the venture opens, the break-even quantity, and the statements that show how it is doing. Topic 5.2 uses those numbers to choose where the money should come from.

Each topic opens with a real situation from a town in Sindh and explains the ideas with one worked example. Do the group activity, answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory, and use the self-assessment checklist honestly. Take the practicum task into a real school or a local shop.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
