# Financial Planning and Funding - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

A new venture plans its money before it opens. Start-up costs are the one-time costs of opening, such as a rent deposit, furniture and registration. A budget is a forward plan of expected income and spending, and it lets the owner compare the plan with what actually happens. Costs are either fixed, which stay about the same each month, or variable, which rise and fall with sales. The contribution margin is the selling price minus the variable cost per unit, and the break-even quantity is the fixed costs divided by the contribution margin. Break-even is the level of sales at which revenue exactly covers costs: no profit and no loss. It is a floor, not a promise of success.

Three statements describe a running venture. The income statement shows revenue minus expenses over a period. The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity on one day, and the accounting equation must balance. The cash-flow statement shows the money actually moving in and out. Profit and cash are not the same: a business can be profitable and still run out of cash when customers pay late or a loan falls due.

The money to start can come from bootstrapping, debt or equity. Bootstrapping uses the founder's own savings and keeps control, but it grows slowly. A bank loan is debt that must be repaid with interest and often needs collateral. Equity financing sells part ownership: an angel investor is a wealthy individual who often backs early ventures, while a venture capitalist funds high-growth ventures and expects fast returns. Crowdfunding collects small sums from many people, usually online, and works best with a clear goal, a real network and a deliverable reward. Every source has a cost, so the founder weighs control, risk and repayment before choosing.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of these is a one-time start-up cost? *(Remember)*
   - A) Monthly rent
   - B) Furniture for the room
   - C) The assistant's monthly salary
   - D) The monthly electricity bill
2. A budget is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) A record of last month's sales
   - B) A plan for money before it arrives
   - C) A list of debts owed to the business
   - D) A bank statement
3. Fixed costs are costs that: *(Understand)*
   - A) Change with the number of units sold
   - B) Stay about the same each month
   - C) Are paid only once, at the start
   - D) Depend on how much the business sells
4. The contribution margin per unit is: *(Understand)*
   - A) Selling price minus variable cost per unit
   - B) Total revenue minus total cost
   - C) Fixed costs minus variable costs
   - D) Profit minus tax
5. A venture has fixed costs of Rs 40,000, a selling price of Rs 500 and variable costs of Rs 300 per unit. What is the break-even quantity? *(Apply)*
   - A) 80 units
   - B) 100 units
   - C) 200 units
   - D) 400 units
6. An income statement shows: *(Apply)*
   - A) What the business owns on one day
   - B) Revenue minus expenses over a period
   - C) The cash in the bank at the end of the month
   - D) Money owed to lenders
7. Bootstrapping means: *(Remember)*
   - A) Borrowing money from a bank
   - B) Self-funding the venture from personal savings
   - C) Selling shares to the public
   - D) Raising money online from many people
8. The main difference between debt and equity financing is: *(Understand)*
   - A) Debt must be repaid, while equity gives the investor part ownership
   - B) Debt is always cheaper than equity
   - C) Equity must be repaid, while debt does not
   - D) Only banks can provide equity
9. A wealthy private individual invests Rs 200,000 in a new venture in exchange for part ownership and offers advice. This is: *(Apply)*
   - A) A bank loan
   - B) Angel investment
   - C) Reward crowdfunding
   - D) Trade credit
10. A new school-supplies business wants to raise Rs 100,000 from many people, offering each supporter a small reward. This is: *(Apply)*
    - A) Venture capital
    - B) Equity crowdfunding
    - C) Reward crowdfunding
    - D) A soft loan

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In two sentences, explain the difference between a start-up cost and a monthly fixed cost, with one example of each. *(Understand)*
2. Explain in two sentences why a budget helps an entrepreneur before the business opens. *(Understand)*
3. Explain the difference between fixed costs and variable costs, with one example of each. *(Understand)*
4. A venture has fixed costs of Rs 25,000, a selling price of Rs 400 and variable costs of Rs 150 per unit. Calculate the break-even quantity and explain what it means. *(Apply)*
5. Explain why a business can show a profit on its income statement and still have no cash, in two or three sentences. *(Understand)*
6. Explain the difference between bootstrapping and a bank loan, with one advantage of each. *(Understand)*
7. Read this case: "Sajid wants to open a small printing unit. He has Rs 60,000 and needs Rs 120,000. A bank offers a loan at 15 percent interest. An uncle offers to invest Rs 60,000 for 30 percent ownership." Analyse which option better suits Sajid if he wants to keep control and grow slowly. Give two reasons. *(Analyze)*
8. Explain why a venture capitalist may not be interested in a small, slow-growing business. Give two reasons. *(Understand)*
9. A classmate plans a reward crowdfunding campaign for a school uniform service. Analyse which two factors will most affect its success, and why. *(Analyze)*
10. Analyse, in three or four sentences, why many new ventures use a mix of funding sources rather than one. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Is 15 pupils realistic?** Imran's tuition centre has fixed costs of Rs 20,000 a month, charges Rs 2,000 per pupil and has variable costs of Rs 400 per pupil. He hopes for 15 pupils in the first month. In 300-400 words, calculate the break-even quantity, then evaluate whether 15 pupils is a realistic target and whether the plan is financially sound. Reach a reasoned judgement. *(Evaluate)*
2. **The profit that is not cash.** A school canteen's income statement shows a profit of Rs 12,000, but the owner cannot pay Rs 8,000 for supplies. In 300-400 words, analyse the difference between profit and cash, using the income statement and the cash-flow statement, and explain what the owner should do. *(Analyze)*
3. **Which money?** A young woman in Sukkur wants to open a small phone repair and accessories shop. She needs Rs 150,000. She can borrow from a bank, ask an angel investor for part ownership, or run a reward crowdfunding campaign. In 300-400 words, compare two of these options and recommend one, justifying your recommendation with control, risk and repayment. *(Analyze)*
4. **Design a crowdfunding campaign.** Plan a reward crowdfunding campaign for a small venture that solves a problem at a school you know. In 300-400 words, give the funding goal, the reward levels, the audience, and explain how you would reach enough backers. Evaluate the biggest risk and how you would reduce it. *(Evaluate)*
5. **A plan on one page.** A friend wants to start a small venture (choose one: a tuition centre, a canteen, a printing unit or a repair shop). In 350-450 words, prepare a one-page financial plan: estimate the start-up costs, the monthly fixed and variable costs, the break-even quantity, and choose a funding source with reasons. Then evaluate whether the plan is realistic. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer, so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Furniture is bought once to open. Rent, salaries and electricity are monthly costs.
2. **B.** A budget is a forward plan for income and spending, not a record of the past.
3. **B.** Fixed costs stay about the same each month, whatever the sales.
4. **A.** The contribution margin is the selling price minus the variable cost per unit.
5. **C.** Contribution margin is 500 - 300 = 200. Break-even = 40,000 / 200 = 200 units.
6. **B.** The income statement shows revenue minus expenses over a period. A describes a balance sheet and C describes cash.
7. **B.** Bootstrapping is self-funding from personal savings and early income.
8. **A.** Debt is repaid; equity gives the investor part ownership.
9. **B.** A wealthy individual investing for part ownership and advice is an angel investor.
10. **C.** Raising small sums from many people with a reward is reward crowdfunding.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** A start-up cost is spent once to open, such as a rent deposit or furniture. A monthly fixed cost is paid each month, such as rent or an assistant's salary. **Marks (3):** 1 for the difference; 1 for an example of a start-up cost; 1 for an example of a monthly fixed cost.
2. **Model:** A budget lists the income the venture expects and the spending it plans, so the owner can see whether the plan is possible before spending the money. It also gives a plan to check against at the end of the month. **Marks (2):** 1 for a forward plan of income and spending; 1 for the check against actual results.
3. **Model:** Fixed costs stay about the same each month, such as rent or a salary. Variable costs rise and fall with sales, such as the cost of materials or printing. **Marks (3):** 1 for the difference; 1 for each correct example.
4. **Model:** Contribution margin = 400 - 150 = 250. Break-even = 25,000 / 250 = 100 units. At 100 units the venture covers its costs exactly; below 100 it loses money and above 100 it makes a profit. **Marks (3):** 1 for the contribution margin; 1 for the break-even quantity; 1 for explaining what it means.
5. **Model:** The income statement records revenue when the work is done, not when the cash arrives. A customer may owe money, so profit can exist on paper while the bank balance is low. The cash-flow statement shows the actual money in and out. **Marks (3):** 1 for revenue being recorded before cash arrives; 1 for a late-paying customer or a similar example; 1 for the cash-flow statement showing the difference.
6. **Model:** Bootstrapping uses the founder's own savings and keeps full control, but it grows slowly. A bank loan brings outside money quickly, but it must be repaid with interest and often needs collateral. **Marks (4):** 1 for each definition; 1 for each advantage.
7. **Model:** The bank loan better suits Sajid. It lets him keep full ownership and control, and the unit is planned to grow slowly, so the pressure of sharing profits and decisions with an uncle would be a cost. He must show the bank a plan that can repay 15 percent interest. Accept the uncle's offer if the answer argues he cannot repay the loan, but the reasoning must be clear. **Marks (4):** 2 for the choice with two reasons (control and slow growth); 1 for the repayment obligation; 1 for linking the choice to the case.
8. **Model:** A venture capitalist looks for high-growth ventures with large profit expectations and a quick return. A small, slow-growing business cannot offer that growth, and it is too small to interest most funds. **Marks (3):** 1 for the growth expectation; 1 for the size; 1 for the return or time horizon.
9. **Model:** The funding target and the number of backers matter most. A clear, realistic target tells supporters what the campaign needs, and a large network of backers spreads small contributions. The study by Wati and Winarno (2018) found that the target, the number of backers and the minimum sum affected success, while the length of the campaign did not. **Marks (4):** 2 for the two factors; 2 for explaining why each matters.
10. **Model:** No single source suits every stage. Bootstrapping keeps control but limits the money; a loan adds repayment pressure; equity brings money and guidance but shares ownership. A mix lets the founder match each need to the right source and avoid depending on one. **Marks (4):** 1 for each source's limit or strength (2); 1 for matching sources to needs; 1 for the risk of depending on one source.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.
