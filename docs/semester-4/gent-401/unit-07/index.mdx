# Growing a Business and Pitching It

"Miss, my uncle's tuition centre has forty pupils now, but he still teaches every class himself and keeps the fee book in a drawer. Is that a good business?" A Class 10 pupil in Dadu asked this, and the answer is not a simple yes or no. This unit looks at what happens after a venture starts working: how the owner leads and builds a team, how the venture grows without breaking, and how the owner tells the story to people who might fund or join it. Every earlier unit in this course prepared a plan; this one is about running and winning support for it.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain leadership and team building, and describe how a founder's role changes as a venture grows. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-7-1)*
- Compare at least four scaling strategies and choose one for a described venture, with reasons. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-7-1)*
- Identify business risks, place them on a simple risk matrix, and suggest ways to reduce them. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-7-1)*
- Structure a short pitch for a business idea using a clear sequence of parts. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-7-2)*
- Describe what an investor presentation needs, and what makes it credible. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-7-2)*
- Explain the communication skills a pitch depends on, and use them in a short delivery. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-7-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 4, 5 and 6 of this course: a business plan, a budget and break-even, and the legal and marketing basics. Bring one venture idea of your own, because you will pitch it at the end of the unit.

## In this unit

1. [Leading, scaling and managing risk](./topic-01) - leadership and team building, scaling strategies, and how to identify and manage risk.
2. [Pitching the idea](./topic-02) - how to pitch a business idea, what an investor presentation needs, and the communication skills behind both.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. The first is about running a venture that has outgrown one person; the second is about telling its story well. Each opens with a real situation, explains the ideas, shows a worked example, then asks you to use them.

Do the group activity in each topic with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
