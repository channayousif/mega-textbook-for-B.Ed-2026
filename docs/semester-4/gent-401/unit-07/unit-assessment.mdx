# Growing a Business and Pitching It - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

A venture that works at one person's hands soon meets the limits of those hands. Leadership decides where the venture is going and gets people to move there; team building chooses those people, gives each a clear task and helps them work together. As the team grows, the founder moves from doing tasks to arranging them, and from memory to written routines.

Scaling is a deliberate choice among entering a new market, adding a new product, franchising or selling online. Each has a benefit and a risk, and growth should be chosen because the venture can carry it. Fast growth can strain cash, so costs and payments must be watched. Risk management sorts each risk by likelihood and impact, reduces what can be reduced, and accepts that risk cannot be removed completely.

A pitch is a short, planned talk that asks for something. Its parts run from a hook through the problem, solution, market, model and team to a specific ask and a next step. An investor presentation is the same shape in a more formal setting, and it names the main risks and how the venture will handle them. Delivery decides much of the result: plain words, a carrying voice, eye contact, still hands and honest answers. A short pitch with a specific ask beats a long one with many slides.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. Keep to the length stated for restricted responses, and build an argument with evidence for extended ones. All names and places are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit, leadership is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) Doing every task in the venture yourself so nothing goes wrong
   - B) Deciding where the venture is going and getting people to move there
   - C) Keeping all the money and records in your own hands
   - D) Hiring as many workers as the venture can afford
2. Team building is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) Choosing people, giving each a clear task and helping them work together
   - B) Paying workers the lowest wage the market allows
   - C) Copying the organisation chart of a large company
   - D) Letting each worker decide their own task every day
3. Which of these is one of the three shifts as a venture's team grows? *(Understand)*
   - A) From written routines back to the founder's memory
   - B) From doing every task to arranging tasks and writing down routines
   - C) From a team with different skills to one person doing everything
   - D) From delegation back to the founder checking every detail
4. OpenStax describes adding a new market as a growth strategy that is: *(Remember)*
   - A) The least costly way to win more customers
   - B) A riskier path than selling more to current customers
   - C) Only possible once the venture has a franchise
   - D) Free of delivery and travel costs
5. A founder who grows fast but does not watch costs and customer payments is most likely to: *(Apply)*
   - A) Run short of cash even while sales rise
   - B) Lose the ability to delegate
   - C) Find that risk disappears
   - D) Need fewer staff
6. On a risk matrix, a risk with high likelihood and high impact should be: *(Understand)*
   - A) Accepted without any action
   - B) Ignored because it cannot be insured
   - C) Acted on now
   - D) Moved to the low-impact row
7. Which of these is an economic cause of business risk? *(Remember)*
   - A) A flood that damages stock
   - B) A key helper who leaves
   - C) A new competitor offering lower prices
   - D) A delivery accident on the road
8. A pitch is best defined as: *(Remember)*
   - A) A long report about the venture's history
   - B) A short, planned talk that asks for something
   - C) A slide deck sent without a speaker
   - D) A meeting to thank the team
9. In the pitch structure in this topic, the part that comes immediately after the solution is the: *(Understand)*
   - A) Ask
   - B) Market
   - C) Hook
   - D) Team
10. A presenter is asked about a risk the venture has not considered. The strongest answer is: *(Apply)*
    - A) "There is no risk in this venture"
    - B) "I do not know yet, but I would check it and come back to you"
    - C) "The risk is the panel's concern, not mine"
    - D) "Every figure I have given is certain"

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In two sentences, explain the difference between leadership and team building. *(Understand)*
2. A classmate says, "Delegating means the founder has lost control." Using a school-connected venture as an example, explain in three sentences why this is not true. *(Understand)*
3. Name two scaling strategies and, for each, give one benefit and one risk. *(Understand)*
4. A tailoring shop in Larkana wants to grow. Recommend one scaling strategy for it, with a reason drawn from its situation. *(Apply)*
5. Explain in two or three sentences why a venture can grow too fast for its cash. *(Understand)*
6. A risk with high impact but low likelihood is best handled by preparing and insuring rather than by acting at once. Explain why, in two sentences. *(Analyze)*
7. Read this case: "Asif runs a school canteen. He has started checking the stock himself each night because two workers have disagreed about missing items." Identify one human cause of risk in the case and say what Asif should do instead. *(Analyze)*
8. A pitch has seven parts. Name four of them and say what each part must do. *(Understand)*
9. In three sentences, explain why a twenty-slide pitch usually fails where a ten-slide pitch succeeds. *(Understand)*
10. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how a presenter should handle a question they cannot answer. Use the communication skills in this topic. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **The founder's dilemma.** Nadia runs a small uniform-mending service supplying two schools in Sukkur. She now has the chance to supply six schools. In 300-400 words, explain how her leadership and team building must change if she takes the chance, and analyse which tasks she should delegate first and how she should check them. *(Analyze)*
2. **Growth with care.** A tuition centre in Larkana has forty pupils and wants to open a second branch in a nearby town. In 300-400 words, evaluate two scaling strategies for it, recommend one, and analyse the risks of your choice using a risk matrix. *(Evaluate)*
3. **What could go wrong.** A women's group in Khairpur makes embroidered school bags and sells them to three shops. In 300-400 words, identify four risks it faces, place them on a risk matrix, and justify the placement of at least two. Then propose one way to reduce its most serious risk. *(Analyze)*
4. **A pitch that fails.** Hira has twenty slides and reads them one by one; she never states how her reading-corner venture will be paid for. In 300-400 words, analyse what is wrong with her pitch and rewrite its structure so that it carries a clear ask, then evaluate which change matters most. *(Evaluate)*
5. **Pitch your course project.** For the business plan you have built across Units 2 to 7, outline a three-minute pitch in 350-450 words. Give the hook, problem, solution, market, model, team and ask, and name one risk with its reduction. End with the first sentence you would say and one non-verbal habit you would keep. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer so you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Leadership is deciding the direction and getting people to move there. A and C describe control of details, and D is about numbers rather than direction.
2. **A.** Team building selects people, gives each a clear task and helps them work together. The other options describe pay, copying or leaving work unassigned.
3. **B.** Growth moves the founder from doing every task to arranging tasks and writing down routines. The other options reverse the direction of the shift.
4. **B.** OpenStax calls adding new markets a riskier path than selling more to current customers. A describes market penetration, and C and D are not in the text.
5. **A.** Growth raises cash coming in and going out at once; without close attention to costs and payments, a venture can run short of cash. B, C and D are not the risk the text names.
6. **C.** A high-likelihood, high-impact risk should be acted on now. Accepting it or moving it ignores the whole purpose of the matrix.
7. **C.** A new competitor is an economic cause. A is natural, and B and D are human causes.
8. **B.** A pitch is a short, planned talk that asks for something. The other options describe a report, a deck without a speaker, or a meeting.
9. **B.** The order is hook, problem, solution, market, model, team, ask. The ask comes last, the hook first and the team after the model.
10. **B.** Admitting a gap and saying how you would close it is stronger than denying risk or blaming the panel. A and D claim certainty that no venture has, and C refuses the question.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Leadership is deciding where the venture is going and getting people to move there. Team building is choosing those people, giving each a clear task and helping them work together. **Marks (2):** 1 for each sentence.
2. **Model:** Delegation means the founder hands a task to someone with a written routine, so the task still has an owner and a check. For example, a canteen owner asks a worker to record sales each day against a simple form and checks the total each week. The founder keeps control of the result without doing the task. **Marks (3):** 1 for delegation with a written routine; 1 for an example; 1 for the founder keeping control of the result.
3. **Model:** New market: benefit, more customers and steadier orders; risk, demand in the new area may fail and travel costs rise. Franchise: benefit, fast growth using another person's shop; risk, quality and your name are in their hands. Accept a new product or online sales with a correct benefit and risk. **Marks (4):** 1 for each strategy, 1 for each correct benefit and risk pair.
4. **Model:** A new market, because the tailoring shop can serve schools in a nearby town with the same skill and a delivery visit. Benefit: more orders without new equipment. Risk: travel and collection costs, and uncertain demand. **Marks (3):** 1 for the strategy; 1 for a benefit; 1 for a risk linked to the shop's situation.
5. **Model:** Growth raises cash coming in and going out at once: more stock, more workers and more delivery before customers pay. If costs and payments are not watched, the venture runs short of cash even while sales rise. **Marks (3):** 1 for the increase in spending; 1 for the timing gap; 1 for the cash-shortage result.
6. **Model:** A high-impact, low-likelihood risk is not expected to happen, so acting at once would spend money on something that may never occur. But if it does happen the damage is serious, so the venture prepares, insures or keeps a reserve. **Marks (2):** 1 for the low expectation; 1 for the serious damage and the preparation or insurance.
7. **Model:** Human cause: disagreement and possible mishandling of stock by workers. Asif should write a simple stock routine: who checks, how missing items are recorded, and a weekly count against the record, rather than checking everything himself each night. **Marks (3):** 1 for naming a human cause; 1 for the written routine; 1 for the founder checking the record rather than every item.
8. **Model:** Hook: a story, question or number that makes the listener care. Problem: the unmet need in one plain sentence. Solution: what you offer and why it is better. Market: who wants it, how many, and how you know. Accept model, team or ask with a correct description. **Marks (4):** 1 each.
9. **Model:** A long pitch hides its ask and makes the audience read rather than listen. A short pitch with few slides forces the speaker to choose what matters, and keeps the ask and the next step clear. **Marks (3):** 1 for the hidden ask; 1 for reading rather than listening; 1 for the clarity of a short pitch.
10. **Model:** Listen to the whole question, answer the question asked rather than the one you wished for, and if you do not know, say so and say how you would find out. This shows respect for the panel and keeps the speaker's credibility. **Marks (4):** 1 for listening to the whole question; 1 for answering the question asked; 1 for admitting the gap; 1 for saying how you would close it.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.

**ERQ 1: The founder's dilemma**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leadership change | Explains clearly how the founder's role shifts from doing to arranging | Describes the shift in general terms | Names the shift only | Absent |
| Team building | Identifies the skills and tasks the larger work needs | Names tasks without skills | Mentions hiring only | Absent |
| Delegation with a check (Analyze) | Names specific tasks to delegate first, with a written check for each | Delegates tasks without a check, or checks without a routine | Delegation mentioned without detail | None |
| Judgement on taking the chance | Weighs the chance against the venture's capacity to carry it | A reasoned preference with partial support | States a preference only | No judgement |

**ERQ 2: Growth with care**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two strategies | Two strategies named with benefit and risk each | One strategy, or benefits without risks | Strategies listed only | Absent |
| Recommendation | A clear recommendation with a reason drawn from the case | A recommendation with a partial reason | A preference only | None |
| Risk matrix (Analyze) | Places the main risks by likelihood and impact and justifies at least two | Places risks without justification | Lists risks only | None |
| Evaluation of the choice (Evaluate) | Weighs the recommended strategy against the alternative with evidence | A judgement with partial reasons | A bare preference | No judgement |

**ERQ 3: What could go wrong**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Four risks | Four distinct risks covering at least two causes | Three risks, or risks from one cause only | One or two risks | Absent |
| Risk matrix (Analyze) | Four risks placed and at least two placements justified by likelihood and impact | Placements present with weak justification | Risks listed without placement | None |
| Reduction of the main risk | A practical reduction linked to the risk and the venture | A reduction that is vague or unconnected | A general warning | None |
| Judgement on the most serious risk | A reasoned choice of the most serious risk, weighing impact and likelihood | A choice with partial reasons | A choice without reasons | No judgement |

**ERQ 4: A pitch that fails**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis of the failure (Analyze) | Identifies the structural faults and links each to its effect on the listener | Identifies faults without linking them | Describes the pitch only | Absent |
| Rewritten structure | All seven parts present in order with a specific ask | Four to six parts, or the ask vague | A partial structure | None |
| Use of evidence | Uses details from the case to support the analysis | Uses one detail | General advice only | None |
| Evaluation of the key change (Evaluate) | A reasoned judgement on which change matters most and why | A judgement with partial reasons | A bare preference | No judgement |

**ERQ 5: Pitch your course project**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook, problem and solution | A clear hook, a real problem and a solution that meets it | Two of the three clear | A vague idea only | Absent |
| Market, model and team | Each carries a concrete detail that could be checked | One or two details only | Claims without detail | None |
| Ask and next step (Create) | A specific ask with an amount or help and what it buys, plus a next step | An ask without a next step, or a vague ask | No clear ask | None |
| Risk and delivery | One named risk with a realistic reduction, and a delivery plan with a first sentence and a non-verbal habit | One of the two, or both general | Mentions risk or delivery only | None |
