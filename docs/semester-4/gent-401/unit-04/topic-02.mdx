## A real classroom situation

Rashid and his sister Maryam, both graduates of a teacher-training college in Sukkur, decided to establish an educational publishing venture called "Mehran Reader." They planned to write, illustrate, and distribute affordable Sindhi-language graded reading booklets for Class 1 to 3 children across northern Sindh.

During their first term, their marketing efforts met immediate success. By visiting private schools and attending a regional education fair in Larkana, they booked advance orders for 1,200 storybook packs from twenty schools. But within three weeks of launch, serious problems erupted in their daily operations.

Rashid spent all his time taking new telephone orders and promising custom delivery dates without checking stock. Maryam sat in a corner editing stories while two hired student helpers assembled printed sheets by hand without written instructions. One school received booklets missing pages 8 to 12. Another school in Shikarpur waited two weeks past their promised delivery date because nobody had purchased paper cartons for shipping. When the school head phoned to cancel their order, Rashid blamed the student helpers, the helpers blamed Maryam for not buying cartons, and Maryam pointed out that nobody had ever decided who was in charge of purchasing, quality inspection, or dispatch.

"We thought that because we are a small team, we did not need titles, rules, or a workflow chart," Maryam admitted. "We believed everyone would just pitch in wherever needed." By studying this topic, you will learn how to design a focused marketing plan, engineer daily operational workflows that prevent chaos, and build clear organizational structures.

## Explanation

A venture cannot survive on sales enthusiasm alone. A business plan must connect what is promised to the customer (the marketing plan) with how that promise is produced (the operational plan) and who is responsible for each task (the organizational structure).

### Marketing plan

The **marketing plan** is the operational blueprint that details how an enterprise will identify, reach, attract, and retain its target customers (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 8.1; Scott, 2017, p. 61). It translates the market analysis from earlier research into specific sales and promotional moves.

An effective marketing plan is built around the classic **4Ps marketing mix** (Product, Price, Place, and Promotion), tailored to the venture's target market segment:

1. **Product (or Service):** Exactly what is delivered, including its tangible design, packaging, reliability, and after-sales support. In educational ventures, this includes curriculum alignment, child safety, and teacher guidance materials.
2. **Price:** The pricing strategy adopted, supported by cost analysis and competitor price comparisons. Common strategies include:
   - *Cost-plus pricing:* Calculating total production cost per unit and adding a fixed profit margin.
   - *Value-based pricing:* Setting prices based on the perceived educational benefit and money saved by the school or parent.
   - *Penetration pricing:* Launching with an introductory discount to encourage rapid classroom adoption.
3. **Place (Distribution):** How the product moves from the venture to the buyer. Will the venture sell directly through school visits, through local bookshop agents, via direct mail order, or through an online portal?
4. **Promotion:** The communication channels used to inform and persuade decision-makers. In Pakistani education, head teachers and school owners respond strongly to peer recommendations, live classroom demonstrations, and free trial samples rather than expensive mass-media advertising (Scott, 2017, p. 64).

**A worked example: formulating a marketing mix for Mehran Reader.**

| Element | Strategic choice for Mehran Reader | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Target Customer** | Low-cost private primary schools and community schools in Sukkur and Khairpur | High unmet need for local-language early readers; public schools rely on state textbooks |
| **Product** | Pack of 6 graded Sindhi storybooks with colourful local art, gloss covers, and teacher prompts | High durability for classroom handling; culturally familiar stories increase pupil engagement |
| **Price** | PKR 600 per pack of 6 books (unit cost PKR 380; profit margin PKR 220 per pack) | Highly competitive against imported Urdu readers priced at PKR 1,200 or more |
| **Place** | Direct delivery to schools via local motorcycle courier; display table at teacher seminars | Eliminates middleman retail markups; allows direct relationship with head teachers |
| **Promotion** | Complimentary sample book given to head teachers during 10-minute demonstration visits | Builds immediate trust; lets teachers test the book with actual pupils before ordering |

### Operational plan

The **operational plan** details the physical engine of the enterprise: how inputs (raw materials, labour, equipment) are transformed into finished goods or services and delivered to customers on schedule (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 11.4; Scott, 2017, p. 69).

While the marketing plan generates orders, the operational plan fulfills them reliably. It addresses five operational dimensions:

- **Facilities and location:** The physical premises required (such as a workshop room, warehouse corner, or computer lab), lease costs, power backup (such as solar or UPS systems to counter load-shedding), and storage security.
- **Equipment and technology:** Tools, machinery, printing presses, computers, and vehicles needed for daily operations.
- **Supply chain and inventory:** Reliable suppliers for raw materials (such as paper mills, timber merchants, and box makers), minimum order quantities, delivery lead times, and inventory safety stock.
- **Production workflow:** The step-by-step sequence of actions required to assemble, package, and dispatch a finished unit.
- **Quality assurance and control:** Clear standards and physical inspection checkpoints before any package leaves the workshop.

The flowchart above maps the daily operational cycle of an educational manufacturing enterprise. Notice step 4: the **quality check diamond**. An operational workflow must have an explicit quality gate. If a package contains a printing defect or missing pieces, it is returned to assembly for correction rather than shipped to an unhappy buyer. Furthermore, step 6 creates a continuous feedback loop: teacher reviews and delivery records are used to adjust inventory ordering and refine kit designs.

### Organizational structure

The **organizational structure** formally defines how job tasks are divided, grouped, coordinated, and supervised (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 13.1; Scott, 2017, p. 75). It establishes the venture's chain of command, showing who reports to whom and who holds final decision-making authority for each business function.

**Correcting a common misconception: "A small business does not need an organisational structure."** Many founders believe that formal structures, job descriptions, and reporting charts belong only to large corporations. They assume that in a small team of three to five people, everyone should simply do whatever needs doing. This assumption is a frequent cause of small enterprise failure (Scott, 2017, p. 75).

When responsibilities are unassigned:
- Critical tasks fall between the cracks (such as verifying invoices, backing up customer lists, or inspecting outgoing stock).
- When mistakes occur, staff blame one another because nobody was explicitly in charge.
- Customer communication becomes chaotic when multiple people give contradictory promises.
- Founders become overwhelmed by minor operational crises instead of guiding business growth.

Even a sole entrepreneur has an organizational structure: the founder holds all four primary hats (Product, Marketing, Operations, Finance) but consciously manages them as distinct functional responsibilities. As the venture expands, generalist duties are divided into specialized roles.

<Figure id="fig-U4-4" src="/img/figures/gent-401/unit-04/fig-U4-4.svg" alt="Diagram showing three stages of organizational structure for an entrepreneurial venture: solo founder, five-person team, and twenty-person company with functional departments." />

Let us examine the operational characteristics of each stage:

1. **Stage 1: Solo Founder (1 person).** The entrepreneur performs all tasks. The key managerial challenge is time allocation: balancing immediate production with sales outreach and financial bookkeeping. The founder must establish structured daily routines so that non-urgent tasks like record-keeping are not neglected.
2. **Stage 2: Small Functional Team (5 people).** Tasks are divided into four dedicated roles: Product Design, Marketing and Sales, Assembly and Packaging, and Finance/Administration. The founder acts as the team coordinator. The key challenge is delegation: the founder must allow team members to execute their assigned workflows while enforcing clear standard operating procedures.
3. **Stage 3: Functional Departmental Hierarchy (20 people).** As volume reaches thousands of units across multiple districts, direct supervision by the founder becomes impossible. The enterprise establishes formal departments (Operations, Marketing, Finance/HR), each led by a specialized department manager. The Managing Director focuses on strategic partnerships, capital allocation, and long-term expansion.

**A worked example: assigning roles in Mehran Reader.** To resolve the crisis described in the opening scenario, Rashid and Maryam reorganized their venture using a clear functional division:

| Team Member | Functional Title | Key Responsibilities | Primary Performance Indicator |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maryam** | Director of Content & Quality | Authoring stories, commissioning local artists, reviewing proofs, and final quality inspection | Zero defect returns from schools |
| **Rashid** | Director of Marketing & Logistics | School visits, booking orders, managing delivery couriers, and customer support | On-time delivery rate above 95% |
| **Helper 1 (Ali)** | Materials & Assembly Specialist | Tracking paper and packaging inventory, binding booklets, and packing orders according to checklist | Daily assembly target of 100 packs |
| **Helper 2 (Fatima)** | Finance & Records Administrator | Invoicing schools, recording cash receipts, logging teacher feedback, and reconciling bank deposits | Daily balance reconciliation |

Within two weeks of implementing this structure, delivery delays dropped to zero, stockouts ended, and the founders could clearly evaluate each helper's performance.

## Activity: Mapping a school venture's workflow

Work in small groups of three or four. Allocate about 25 minutes for this task.

1. Review the scenario of "Sindh Science Sparks" (producing low-cost wooden earth-science kits for primary classrooms).

   - Order receipt and recording.
   - Raw material inspection (checking timber and non-toxic paint from Hala suppliers).
   - Component assembly and unit packaging.
   - A quality inspection checkpoint (specify three exact defect criteria that trigger a return to assembly).
   - Delivery method to rural schools.
   - Customer confirmation and teacher feedback logging.
3. Create an organizational chart for a 4-person team running this enterprise, assigning job titles, primary responsibilities, and reporting lines.
4. Present your workflow and chart to the class. Be prepared to defend how your structure prevents the operational breakdown experienced by Mehran Reader.

## Summary

A successful business plan balances external customer promises with internal operational capability and clear team governance. The marketing plan utilizes the 4Ps framework (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) to define how the venture will attract and retain customers within targeted educational segments, emphasizing direct demonstration and relationship-building in local Pakistani contexts.

The operational plan specifies the physical infrastructure, equipment, inventory controls, and step-by-step production workflows required to deliver quality goods reliably and on schedule. Embedding formal quality inspection checkpoints and customer feedback loops ensures dependable service and continuous improvement.

Organizational structure defines roles, responsibilities, and authority hierarchies. Refuting the misconception that small teams do not need structure, clear task allocation prevents neglected duties, eliminates blame-shifting, and enables scalable growth as an enterprise progresses from a solo founder to functional departmental teams.

## Summative task

Develop the operational, marketing, and organizational sections of a business plan for an educational venture in Sindh (you may use Mehran Reader, Sindh Science Sparks, or your own approved course project idea). Your submission must include:

1. **Target Marketing Strategy:** A detailed table applying the 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) specifically tailored to schools or families in your district, including a clear pricing justification (200-250 words).
2. **Operational Workflow Diagram & Standards:** A step-by-step workflow description from order intake to delivery, detailing:
   - Facilities and raw material suppliers.
   - Exact quality inspection criteria (at least three specific checks).
   - Packaging, storage, and courier logistics (200-250 words).
3. **Organizational Structure & Job Specifications:** An organizational chart for a 5-person team, accompanied by brief job specifications (title, key responsibilities, required qualifications, and primary performance metric) for each role (200-250 words).
4. **Vulnerability Analysis:** A short analytical paragraph identifying two operational risks (such as power outages or supplier delays) and explaining how your plan mitigates them.
