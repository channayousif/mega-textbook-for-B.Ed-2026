# Marketing, Legal and Ethical Issues - Notes for Teachers

The course guide specifies interactive discussions, lectures, case study analysis, question-and-answer sessions, and practical group research as recommended teaching strategies. In Unit 6, students transition from early conceptual planning to establishing market presence, legal compliance, and ethical operating policies. These notes guide teacher educators in sequencing the content across Weeks 12 and 13.

## Sequencing the two weeks

- **Week 12 (Topic 6.1 - Marketing a new venture):**
  - *Session 1:* Begin with the railway flyer vignette. Challenge students: "If you had 1,000 rupees to announce a new school transport service in your town, where would you spend it?" Contrast broad paid advertising with high-trust word-of-mouth and demonstration marketing.
  - *Session 2:* Teach market positioning using the two-axis positioning grid on the chalkboard. Have student pairs plot local tutoring academies and identify underserved spaces. End with the WhatsApp Business launch message drafting activity.
- **Week 13 (Topic 6.2 - Registering, protecting and acting ethically):**
  - *Session 1:* Contrast sole proprietorship, partnership, and private limited companies using a realistic liability case (for example, a delivery vehicle accident). Walk through the SECP eZfile digital incorporation flowchart.
  - *Session 2:* Explore the four branches of intellectual property through real school materials (textbooks, software, logos, apparatus). Conclude with a structured debate on deceptive advertising versus ethical marketing in Pakistani education.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"Marketing just means advertising."**
  - *How to handle:* Ask trainees: "If a local juice stall sells spoiled juice in dirty glasses, will spending 50,000 rupees on beautiful posters make it successful?" Guide students to see that marketing begins with product quality, fair pricing, and customer trust; advertising only communicates what the product already is.
- **"A small business does not need to register."**
  - *How to handle:* Present a scenario where an unregistered catering business seeks a 500,000 rupee school feeding contract. Ask: "Can the district government write a cheque to an unregistered name? Who is personally sued if food poisoning occurs?" Show how registration protects the owner and enables commercial expansion.
- **"An idea is protected as soon as you have it."**
  - *How to handle:* Ask a student: "Can you stop someone from opening a biryani shop just because you had the idea of selling biryani first?" Emphasize that intellectual property law protects original tangible expressions (copyright) or registered technical inventions and trademarks, never unspoken mental thoughts.

## Practical work: a startup registration and marketing simulation lab

In alignment with the course guide's requirement for practical work and group presentations, organize a 90-minute classroom simulation lab:

1. **Venture formulation:** Working in groups of three, trainees select one educational or community venture developed in earlier units (such as low-cost science kits, bilingual storybooks, or after-school remedial tutoring).
2. **Drafting promotional collateral:** Each team produces:
   - A 2x2 positioning map identifying their position relative to two competitors.
   - A one-page launch marketing strategy detailing their bootstrap promotional mix (word-of-mouth referral system, sample demonstration plan, and official WhatsApp Business catalog outline).
3. **Mock registration filing:** Teams complete a simplified SECP incorporation worksheet, specifying:
   - Three proposed company names in order of preference.
   - Principal line of business and Memorandum of Association object clauses.
   - Initial subscribed share capital and division among founders.
4. **IP and ethics statement:** Teams list their core intellectual property assets (trademark name, copyrighted manuals) and write a 100-word Ethical Code of Conduct governing customer truthfulness and fair pricing.
5. **Peer evaluation:** Each group presents its dossier to another team, which acts as the "Company Registration Office and Consumer Protection Panel", evaluating whether the application is complete, legally coherent, and ethically sound.
