## A real classroom situation

Tariq and Hina are two B.Ed trainees completing their practicum in Mirpurkhas. Both noticed that government middle school pupils struggled with science concepts because schools lacked laboratory apparatus. Tariq designed five low-cost, hands-on physics and chemistry experiment kits using locally sourced items such as measuring syringes, test tubes, wire coils, and simple magnets. Each kit cost only 350 rupees to assemble and could be used for dozens of practical demonstrations.

To sell the kits, Tariq spent his remaining savings of 4,000 rupees to print 1,500 glossy paper flyers. He spent an entire Saturday standing outside the main railway station in Mirpurkhas handing flyers to commuters stepping off morning trains. At the end of the day, his hands were empty, but only two people sent inquiries, and neither bought a kit.

When Tariq complained that "nobody in this town cares about education", Hina disagreed gently. "Tariq, handing flyers to railway commuters means you spoke to travelers in a rush who have no connection to local schools. The flyers had a bright picture, but they did not explain who we are, why teachers can trust us, or how this helps a child pass their exams. You spent money on advertising before we even defined our brand or spoke to headteachers." Hina suggested mapping the existing options in town and reaching science teachers directly through demonstration workshops.

## Explanation

Marketing is often misunderstood by new founders. Many believe that starting a business requires only making a good product and shouting loudly through advertisements. In practice, marketing begins long before any advertisement is created, by understanding what customers value and establishing a trustworthy identity.

### Branding and positioning

A **brand** is not merely a logo, a catchy slogan, or a business name printed on cardboard packaging. According to Laverty and Littel (2020, section 8.2), a brand represents the entire set of perceptions, emotions, and promises that customers associate with a venture. When parents see a particular school's crest or when teachers purchase a reputable textbook series, they are not buying paper or ink; they are buying a promise of quality, accuracy, and reliability. For an early-stage startup, branding answers a fundamental question: "Why should a customer trust you rather than someone else?"

A venture builds its brand through consistent customer experiences. If Tariq and Hina promise that their science kits are safe, durable, and aligned with the Sindh curriculum, but a plastic tube cracks during the first lesson, their brand promise is broken. Brand equity - the commercial value derived from consumer perception - grows through reliability, honesty, and consistent performance over time (Scott, 2017).

**Market positioning** is the deliberate strategic process of creating a distinct image and place for a product or service in the minds of target customers relative to competing alternatives (Scott, 2017). Positioning answers the customer's silent comparison: "In what specific way is this choice better or more suitable for my needs than the alternatives?"

To establish positioning, entrepreneurs frequently construct a **positioning map** (also called a perceptual map). A positioning map is a visual diagram that plots competing products or services along two key criteria that matter most to buyers, such as price and quality, or convenience and customisation.

**Correcting a common misconception: "Marketing just means advertising."** Many first-time entrepreneurs treat "marketing" and "advertising" as identical words. This confusion causes significant waste. Advertising is merely one specific tool within promotion, which itself is only one part of the broader marketing discipline (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 8.1). Marketing encompasses the complete process of:
1. Researching real customer needs and problems (market research).
2. Developing a product or service that effectively solves that problem (product design).
3. Setting an accessible and sustainable price that reflects customer value and covers costs (pricing).
4. Ensuring the offering reaches customers conveniently (distribution or place).
5. Communicating the value proposition truthfully through appropriate channels (promotion).

If an entrepreneur produces an overpriced, irrelevant item, the most creative advertising in the world cannot make it successful. Marketing creates value; advertising merely announces it.

**A worked example: constructing a positioning map.** Consider the market for supplementary learning support and study materials in a Sindh district headquarters such as Sukkur:

| Venture | Price level | Perceived quality / Academic depth | Market position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unregulated street-corner photocopier | Very low | Very low (blurry notes, factual errors) | Economy, unreliable baseline |
| Commercial testing academy chain | Very high | Moderate to high (rigid exam drills) | Premium commercial exam factory |
| Standard textbook bookstore | Moderate | Moderate (standard government textbooks) | Mass market standard |
| Tariq and Hina's Hands-on STEM Kit | Moderate | High (curriculum-aligned, hands-on, reusable) | High-value practical innovator |

By plotting these ventures on a grid with price on the horizontal axis and quality on the vertical axis (as shown in Figure 6.1), Tariq and Hina can clearly identify an unoccupied niche: high practical quality at an affordable, fair price. They do not need to compete with cheap photocopiers on rock-bottom price, nor do they need to charge the exorbitant fees of luxury private academies.

### Promotion strategies

Once positioning is clear, an entrepreneur selects a mix of **promotion strategies**. Promotion consists of all communication activities designed to inform, persuade, and remind target customers about a venture's offerings (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 8.3).

Large multinational corporations invest millions of rupees in television commercials, highway billboards, and celebrity endorsements. Early-stage entrepreneurs have strictly limited budgets and must practice **bootstrap marketing** - creative, low-cost techniques that leverage relationships, community presence, and direct demonstrations rather than paid media space (Scott, 2017).

<Figure id="fig-U6-2" src="/img/figures/gent-401/unit-06/fig-U6-2.svg" alt="Comparison table of four startup promotion channels: word of mouth, targeted print, digital and social media, and demonstrations with cost, reach, and suitable use cases." />

Four core promotional channels are especially effective for local educational and community ventures:

1. **Word-of-mouth marketing:** Recommendations passed directly from satisfied customers to their peers. It is a widely used bootstrap technique that costs little or no cash (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 8.3). When a headteacher in Khairpur tells a colleague at another school, "These experiment kits saved my teachers hours of preparation and our Class 8 pass rate improved", that personal endorsement carries far more credibility than any printed brochure. A venture can encourage such recommendations with referral incentives: for example, a tutoring academy might offer a 10 percent fee discount or a free workbook to any family whose recommendation brings a new enrolled pupil.
2. **Targeted print notices:** Rather than distributing flyers randomly to strangers on public roads, small ventures place concise, professional notices where target buyers naturally gather: school noticeboards (with permission), stationery shops, teacher union meetings, or community libraries.
3. **Digital and social channels:** Sharing a product catalogue, short teaching tips, or lesson clips through messaging apps and community groups reaches parents and teachers on their phones for a small data cost. The next section explains these tools.
4. **Product demonstrations and workshops:** Offering a free 20-minute demonstration lesson allows decision-makers to witness the product in action. When science teachers see pupils actively assembling electrical circuits with Tariq's kit, skepticism vanishes. Demonstrations eliminate buyer perceived risk.

**A worked example: selecting the right promotional mix.**
Suppose an entrepreneur in Nawabshah establishes an affordable after-school English phonics club for primary pupils:
- *Ineffective strategy:* Printing 2,000 flyers and slipping them under car windshield wipers in the commercial market. Cost: 5,000 rupees. Result: high litter, zero credibility with mothers.
- *Effective bootstrap strategy:* Conducting one free 30-minute interactive storytelling session at a local community hall on a Saturday morning, inviting mothers and children; distributing a two-page phonics guide with practical home tips; asking mothers who attended to share the invitation in their neighborhood WhatsApp groups. Cost: 600 rupees for printed activity sheets and tea. Result: high trust, direct contact with 18 interested parents, 12 immediate paid enrolments.

### Digital marketing basics

Digital marketing refers to the use of internet-connected digital platforms and communication tools to reach, engage, and convert prospective customers (Laverty & Littel, 2020, section 8.4). In Pakistan, the rapid spread of 4G mobile networks and affordable smartphones has transformed how consumers discover local services.

For a new venture in Sindh, digital marketing does not require complex software or expensive international ad agencies. It begins with mastering common, accessible tools:

1. **Messaging platforms (WhatsApp Business):** In Pakistan, WhatsApp is often the primary digital customer interface. WhatsApp Business allows an entrepreneur to set up an official business profile, showcase a product catalog with photos and prices, configure automated greeting and away messages, and organize customer inquiries using labels (such as "New Inquiry", "Order Placed", or "Payment Pending").
2. **Social media community pages (Facebook and Instagram):** Community groups and visual pages allow ventures to share authentic stories. A video showing middle-school pupils successfully lighting a bulb using a homemade battery kit communicates educational value instantly.
3. **Content marketing:** Creating and distributing helpful, educational content rather than aggressive sales pitches. An entrepreneur selling revision worksheets can publish weekly short study tips or past-paper solution guides. Parents and teachers follow the page because it provides genuine value, and when they need comprehensive workbooks, they buy from the creator they already trust.
4. **Local business search listings (Google Business Profile):** Registering a physical workshop, coaching centre, or shop on digital map services allows nearby residents searching for "science supplies near me" or "tuition centre Hyderabad" to find operating hours, contact numbers, and customer reviews.

**Digital marketing ethics and customer respect:** Digital tools carry significant ethical obligations. Sending unsolicited broadcast messages to private phone numbers without permission is invasive and damages a venture's reputation. Responsible entrepreneurs always obtain customer consent before adding them to messaging lists, protect client contact details from third parties, and provide accurate, unedited photographs of their actual products.

## Activity: Mapping competitor positions and designing a launch message

**Format:** Work in pairs. **Time:** Approximately 25 minutes.

1. **Select a venture:** Choose one simple educational or community venture you could start in your home district (for example: a low-cost mobile science lab, a Sindhi-language reading corner, or a recycled school desk repair service).
2. **Identify alternatives:** List three existing ways your target customers currently solve that problem in your town.
3. **Draw a positioning map:** Draw two perpendicular axes on a sheet of paper. Choose two dimensions that matter to your target buyers (for example: *Price: Low vs. High* and *Convenience: Low vs. High*, or *Material Durability: Basic vs. Long-lasting*). Plot the three existing alternatives and your proposed venture.
4. **Draft a launch message:** Write a 60-word WhatsApp launch broadcast message for your venture. The message must state:
   - The specific problem you solve for parents or teachers.
   - Your unique positioning promise.
   - One clear call to action (for example: "Reply to this message for a free demonstration sample").
5. **Exchange and critique:** Swap messages with another pair. Check whether their message sounds like an empty advertisement or communicates a genuine, distinct promise of value.

## Summary

Branding represents the total perception and promise of value that customers associate with a venture, built through repeated, dependable experiences over time. Positioning deliberately establishes where an offering sits in the mind of the target customer relative to competing alternatives, often mapped across dimensions such as price, quality, and service. Marketing is not merely advertising; it is the comprehensive process of creating, pricing, delivering, and communicating genuine customer value. For startups with limited capital, bootstrap promotional strategies - such as word of mouth, physical demonstrations, and targeted community notices - generate greater credibility than broad paid advertising. Accessible digital tools like WhatsApp Business and helpful content marketing enable entrepreneurs to build direct, respectful relationships with their audience.

## Try this at your practicum school

During your next practicum visit, observe the commercial and educational notices displayed on school noticeboards and outside nearby shops. 

Identify two distinct products or services marketed to pupils, teachers, or parents (such as an after-school academy, a school transport van, a stationery seller, or an educational publisher). For each, answer the following in your field notes:
1. What specific promise or value proposition does the seller communicate?
2. Which promotional channel was used (printed flyer, banner, word-of-mouth referral, or digital post)?
3. How effectively does the message address a real educational need, and how could its positioning be made clearer without increasing spending?

## Summative task

Write a 300 to 400-word Marketing and Positioning Plan for an innovative micro-venture serving government school pupils in Sindh (for example: low-cost laminated math manipulative sets, clean drinking water delivery for classrooms, or local-language remedial reading booklets). Your plan must:
1. Define the venture's target customer and articulate its core brand promise.
2. Outline a two-axis positioning strategy comparing your venture against at least two existing alternatives.
3. Select and justify a bootstrap promotional mix of at least two channels, explaining why these channels suit your customer's daily routines.
4. Include an explicit analysis of why this strategy will build long-term customer trust rather than temporary attention.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Emerging (1 mark) | Developing (2 marks) | Proficient (3 marks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brand and positioning clarity** *(Understand)* | Brand promise is vague or confused with product features; competitors are not clearly identified. | Brand promise is stated; positioning compares the venture to competitors along at least one clear dimension. | Articulates a compelling, realistic brand promise; clearly locates the venture on two distinct competitive dimensions. |
| **Promotional mix feasibility** *(Apply)* | Relies on unrealistic or high-cost advertising channels unsuitable for a micro-venture budget. | Selects low-cost channels, but does not fully adapt them to local community communication habits. | Recommends practical, low-cost bootstrap channels tailored to how local parents and teachers actually receive information. |
| **Customer trust analysis** *(Analyze)* | Simply asserts that customers will buy; does not analyze customer skepticism or trust factors. | Identifies why customers might hesitate, but offers only generic solutions. | Critically analyzes customer risks and trust barriers; justifies how transparent communication and demonstrations create durable trust. |
