# Entrepreneurship and the Entrepreneurial Mindset - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

An entrepreneur is someone who identifies and acts on a problem or an opportunity that others have not acted on, and accepts the risk that comes with it. Entrepreneurship is that process. The word comes from the French *entreprendre*, "to undertake". Owning a shop does not by itself make someone an entrepreneur: a small business owner may copy an existing model, while an entrepreneur brings something new, even if it is only a product taken to people who did not have it. Three questions test whether a person is acting as an entrepreneur: did they spot an unmet problem or opportunity, did they act on it, and did they accept a real risk?

Entrepreneurship is an engine of economic development. Land, labour and capital produce nothing until someone sees how to combine them, which is why Butler calls entrepreneurship the "unseen factor of production". It supports development through jobs, innovation, income, competition and community welfare, and these effects create new needs and new opportunities in turn. Successful entrepreneurs tend to be curious and alert, creative, persistent, confident, open to learning and willing to take calculated risks. No entrepreneur has all of these in equal measure, and they grow with experience and education, so entrepreneurs are made more than born.

Creativity is the ability to develop original ideas. Innovation is creativity put to use, a change that adds value. Invention is a leap to something truly new. A problem becomes an innovation through five steps: notice a problem, ask questions, generate ideas, test a small version, and improve, looping back when a test fails. Entrepreneurs face uncertainty, so risk-taking is part of their work, but they take calculated risks: they check carefully, start small and only risk what they can afford to lose. Many ventures therefore start with little money. Problem-solving turns each setback into information. Entrepreneurial thinking starts from an opportunity and finds resources to act on it; managerial thinking runs an existing system well with the resources it has. The two are different, not better or worse, and a growing venture needs both.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit, an entrepreneur is best described as someone who: *(Remember)*
   - A) Owns any shop or business
   - B) Has a degree in business studies
   - C) Acts on an unmet problem or opportunity
   - D) Works for a large company at a high salary
2. The French word *entreprendre*, from which "entrepreneur" comes, means: *(Remember)*
   - A) To undertake something
   - B) To buy and sell goods
   - C) To save money for later
   - D) To manage other workers
3. Butler calls entrepreneurship the "unseen factor of production" because: *(Understand)*
   - A) Entrepreneurs hide their profits to avoid paying tax
   - B) Most entrepreneurs work from home to save on rent
   - C) Governments do not count small businesses when they measure the economy
   - D) The factors of production need a person to combine them
4. A new juice shop in Dadu offers fresh juice at a fair price. Two older shops nearby then improve their hygiene and lower their prices. Which effect on economic development does this show most directly? *(Apply)*
   - A) Invention
   - B) Competition
   - C) Taxation
   - D) Community welfare
5. Which of these shows the characteristic "persistence"? *(Apply)*
   - A) Rewriting a product after the first version fails
   - B) Noticing that pupils have no cheap practice sheets
   - C) Explaining a product confidently to a head teacher
   - D) Spending all savings on the first idea
6. A Pakistani study of 805 university students (Saeed et al., 2014) found that entrepreneurship education and university support: *(Remember)*
   - A) Had no effect on students' plans
   - B) Made students more afraid of risk
   - C) Helped only students who came from business families
   - D) Raised students' intentions to start a business
7. In this unit, innovation is best defined as: *(Remember)*
   - A) Having an original idea in your mind
   - B) A completely new product that never existed before
   - C) Creativity put to use so that it adds value
   - D) Copying a successful product at a lower price
8. In the steps from a problem to an innovation, what should an entrepreneur do if a small test does not solve the problem? *(Understand)*
   - A) Give up the venture
   - B) Learn from the test and try new ideas
   - C) Spend more money on the same idea and keep going
   - D) Skip testing next time
9. Zainab wants to start a home tuition service in Thatta. Before renting a room, she teaches four pupils in her own house for a month to see whether parents will pay. This is an example of: *(Apply)*
   - A) A calculated risk
   - B) Gambling
   - C) Managerial thinking only
   - D) Invention
10. A school canteen manager says, "My job is to sell what the contract lists and keep costs down." This statement shows mainly: *(Apply)*
    - A) Entrepreneurial thinking
    - B) Creativity
    - C) Risk-taking
    - D) Managerial thinking

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define entrepreneurship in one sentence, and give one Pakistani example of a person acting as an entrepreneur. *(Understand)*
2. In three sentences, explain the difference between an entrepreneur and a small business owner, and show why the two can overlap. *(Understand)*
3. A carpenter in Khairpur starts making low-cost desks from recycled wood for village schools and hires two apprentices. Name three effects of this venture on economic development and explain each in one sentence. *(Apply)*
4. Explain in three sentences why Butler argues that innovation explains much of the long-run rise in living standards. *(Understand)*
5. Read this case: "Saima noticed that girls in her village had no safe way to reach the college in town. She arranged a shared van, asked parents to pay a small monthly fee, and when one driver left, she found another within a week." Identify two entrepreneurial characteristics in the case and give the evidence for each. *(Analyze)*
6. A classmate says, "My father has no business sense, so I can never be an entrepreneur." Give two pieces of evidence from this unit that challenge this view. *(Analyze)*
7. Explain the difference between creativity, innovation and invention, with one school-based example of each. *(Understand)*
8. A teacher notices that pupils forget their homework diaries. Show how she could use the five steps from a problem to an innovation to address this, in one sentence per step. *(Apply)*
9. In three or four sentences, explain why starting small is a way of taking a calculated risk, using one example. *(Understand)*
10. Analyse, in three or four sentences, why a growing venture needs both entrepreneurial and managerial thinking. Use one example. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Shopkeeper or entrepreneur?** In one bazaar in Sanghar, Asif runs a mobile phone shop like the ten others in the street. His cousin Farah opens the town's first shop that repairs phones and also trains young women as repair technicians. In 300-400 words, analyse whether each of them is acting as an entrepreneur, using the meaning of entrepreneurship and the three-question test, and evaluate which of the two ventures contributes more to economic development in the town. *(Evaluate)*
2. **Born or made?** A speaker at a school prize-giving says, "Entrepreneurs are born, not made. Schools cannot teach it." Evaluate this claim in 300-400 words. Use the characteristics of successful entrepreneurs and the evidence in this unit, and reach a reasoned judgement. *(Evaluate)*
3. **An idea under test.** Plan a small venture that could solve one real problem at a school you know. In 300-400 words, take it through the five steps from a problem to an innovation, describe the small test you would run, and carry out a risk check that shows how your risk is calculated. *(Create)*
4. **One problem, two responses.** A school-supplies business in Larkana finds that its sales of exercise books have fallen sharply because a large shop has opened nearby and sells them more cheaply. In 300-400 words, compare how an entrepreneur and a salaried manager of the business would each respond, using the four dimensions of goals, risk, resources and decisions. Then analyse which response is more likely to help the business survive, and whether combining the two would be better. *(Analyze)*
5. **Entrepreneurship in my school.** A head teacher in Shikarpur wants to start an "entrepreneurship club" for Class 8. In 350-450 words, design one club session of 40 minutes that helps pupils think like entrepreneurs. Give the learning aim, the steps of the session, and the problem pupils will work on. Explain how the session uses at least two ideas from Topic 1.1 and two from Topic 1.2. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer, so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **C.** An entrepreneur identifies and acts on an unmet problem or opportunity. A is the misconception that every shop owner is an entrepreneur; B and D are not part of the definition.
2. **A.** *Entreprendre* means to undertake something. The other options describe trading, saving and managing.
3. **D.** Butler's point is that the other factors need a human mind to combine them. The other options are not his argument.
4. **B.** The older shops improve because of the new rival, which is competition. Nothing truly new was invented, and the case is not mainly about taxes or welfare.
5. **A.** Persistence means keeping going after a setback. B is alertness, C is confidence, and D is an uncalculated risk.
6. **D.** Saeed et al. found that education and university support raised entrepreneurial intentions. This supports the view that entrepreneurship can be learned.
7. **C.** Innovation is creativity put to use so that it adds value. A is creativity and B is invention.
8. **B.** The flowchart loops back: a failed test is information for new ideas. Giving up or spending more on the same idea ignores what the test showed.
9. **A.** Zainab checks demand cheaply before committing money, which keeps the risk small. Gambling would mean renting the room with no check.
10. **D.** Following the contract and controlling costs is managerial thinking: running an existing system well.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying a problem or opportunity that others have not acted on, acting on it, and accepting the risk involved. Example: a retired teacher who writes and sells low-cost Sindhi worksheet packs because no one was meeting that need. **Marks (2):** 1 for a definition that includes acting on an unmet problem or opportunity; 1 for a fitting example.
2. **Model:** A small business owner runs a business that follows an existing model, such as one more stationery shop. An entrepreneur creates something new, such as a new product, service or method. The two overlap when a shop owner brings a product or service to an area where nobody offered it before. **Marks (3):** 1 for each sentence.
3. **Model:** Jobs: the two apprentices are employed and learn a skill. Community welfare: village schools get affordable desks. Innovation: recycled wood is used in a new way to make a useful product. Accept income or competition with a correct explanation. **Marks (3):** 1 for each effect correctly explained.
4. **Model:** In a competitive economy, entrepreneurs are under constant pressure to find cheaper, better and more useful products. Each improvement lets people get more value from the same resources. Over time, these steady improvements add up to most of the rise in living standards. **Marks (3):** 1 for competitive pressure; 1 for productivity or value; 1 for the long-run effect.
5. **Model:** Alertness to opportunity: Saima noticed that girls had no safe way to reach college. Problem-solving and persistence: when a driver left, she found another within a week instead of giving up. Accept creativity (shared van as a new solution) with evidence. **Marks (4):** 1 for each characteristic; 1 for each piece of evidence.
6. **Model:** Butler notes that no entrepreneur has all the characteristics in equal measure, and that experience can count for more than formal education, so the qualities can be built. Saeed et al. (2014) found that entrepreneurship education raised Pakistani students' intentions to start a business. Accept the OpenStax point that anyone can learn to notice opportunities. **Marks (4):** 2 for each piece of evidence (1 for the evidence, 1 for linking it to the claim).
7. **Model:** Creativity is having an original idea, such as a teacher thinking of a song to teach tables. Innovation is putting an idea to use so that it adds value, such as a school starting a reading corner that pupils actually use. Invention is a truly new product, such as the first electronic tablet. **Marks (3):** 1 for each term with a fitting example.
8. **Model:** Notice: many pupils forget their diaries. Ask questions: why, and which pupils? Generate ideas: a diary check at the gate, a class monitor, a diary hook in the classroom. Test: try the classroom hook with one class for two weeks. Improve: if forgetting falls, extend it; if not, try another idea. **Marks (5):** 1 for each step applied sensibly.
9. **Model:** Starting small means spending a little money and time to test whether an idea works before committing more. If the test fails, the loss is small and the entrepreneur has learned something. For example, selling 20 rolls on two mornings tells you whether pupils will buy, at the cost of a few hundred rupees. **Marks (3):** 1 for small spending; 1 for limited loss and learning; 1 for an example.
10. **Model:** Entrepreneurial thinking finds new opportunities, but a venture also needs someone to plan, keep records and control costs as it grows. Without managerial thinking, a promising venture can collapse through disorder; without entrepreneurial thinking, it can fail when customers change. For example, a breakfast-roll seller who expands to three schools must keep stock and accounts in order while still looking for new ideas. **Marks (4):** 1 for what each kind of thinking contributes (2); 1 for the risk of having only one; 1 for an example.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.
