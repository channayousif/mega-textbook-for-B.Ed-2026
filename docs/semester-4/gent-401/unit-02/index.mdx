# Types of Entrepreneurship and Opportunity Recognition

<Figure id="fig-U2-5" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/gent-401/unit-02/fig-U2-5.webp" alt="A banner scene of a Sindh market street with three adjoining ventures: a general store, a phone-repair shop and a tailoring centre where a woman teaches girls to sew, while a trainee teacher looks on." width={1200} height={675} />

Unit 1 asked what an entrepreneur is. This unit asks the two questions that follow. What kinds of entrepreneurship are there, and why does the difference matter? And how do you find a business opportunity, generate ideas for it, and judge which ideas are worth your time?

Not every venture is the same. A shop that feeds one family, a repair business that opens branches in three towns, and a centre that teaches out-of-school girls are all entrepreneurship, but they grow, fund themselves and measure success differently. This unit sorts them. It then turns to opportunity: where good business ideas come from, how to produce many of them, and how to screen out the ones that will not work before you spend your savings.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Distinguish a small business, a scalable start-up and a social enterprise, with Pakistani examples. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-2-1)*
- Explain what social entrepreneurship is and how a social enterprise differs from a charity. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-2-1)*
- Describe intrapreneurship and women's entrepreneurship and identify each in a described case. *(Understand; SLO:GENT-401-2-1)*
- Explain where business opportunities come from and apply at least two idea-generation techniques. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-2-2)*
- Screen and evaluate business ideas using a weighted checklist. *(Apply; SLO:GENT-401-2-2)*
- Evaluate which of several business ideas is most worth pursuing and justify the choice. *(Evaluate; SLO:GENT-401-2-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

You should have completed Unit 1. You need the meaning of entrepreneurship, the three-question test for deciding whether a person is acting as an entrepreneur, and the five effects of entrepreneurship on economic development. You will use all three in this unit.

## In this unit

1. [Kinds of entrepreneurship](./topic-01) - small businesses, scalable start-ups, social enterprises, and intrapreneurship and women's entrepreneurship.
2. [Finding and judging business opportunities](./topic-02) - where opportunities come from, idea-generation techniques, and how to screen and evaluate ideas.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each one begins with a real situation from a school or town in Sindh, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before it asks you to use them.

Do the group activity in each topic with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
