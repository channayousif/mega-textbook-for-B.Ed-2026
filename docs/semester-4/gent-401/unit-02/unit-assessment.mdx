# Types of Entrepreneurship and Opportunity Recognition - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Entrepreneurship takes several forms. A small business serves a local market and earns a livelihood for its owner. A scalable start-up is designed to grow quickly and to reach many customers, often with outside money. A social enterprise puts a social or environmental cause first and tries to pay its own costs through sales, which is why it is not the same as a charity. Intrapreneurship is entrepreneurial action inside an existing organisation, and women's entrepreneurship is entrepreneurship started and run by women. The forms overlap, and a small business is a deliberate choice rather than a failed start-up.

A business opportunity exists where real customer demand meets a feasible way to deliver. Opportunities come from deliberate search, chance, solving problems, work experience and customer feedback, and Schumpeter's five paths show that most of them do not require an invention. Brainstorming, crowdsourcing, storyboarding and the five-step creative problem-solving process help a group produce ideas. Screening then tests the ideas against demand, market structure and margins, using a weighted checklist so that the strongest idea is chosen for clear reasons. A good idea is only the start.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of the following best describes a scalable start-up? *(Remember)*
   - A) A business that stays small and serves its local market
   - B) A venture built to grow quickly and serve many customers, often with outside money
   - C) A charity that sells goods to raise funds
   - D) A government department that starts a new service
2. A social enterprise differs from a charity mainly because it: *(Understand)*
   - A) Has no employees
   - B) Cannot receive grant money
   - C) Sells goods or services and tries to pay its own costs
   - D) Is always run by women
3. Which of these is an example of intrapreneurship? *(Apply)*
   - A) A teacher who opens her own tuition centre after school
   - B) A shopkeeper who opens a second branch in another town
   - C) A woman who sells pickles through WhatsApp
   - D) A teacher who starts a reading programme inside her school with the head teacher's support
4. A woman in a village starts a home-based embroidery business and sells through a trader in the nearest town. This is best described as: *(Apply)*
   - A) A scalable start-up
   - B) A charity
   - C) Intrapreneurship
   - D) Women's entrepreneurship
5. A business opportunity exists when: *(Remember)*
   - A) Someone has a new invention
   - B) A bank agrees to give a loan
   - C) There is real customer demand and a feasible way to deliver
   - D) The idea is protected by a patent
6. Which of the following is a source of business opportunities? *(Remember)*
   - A) Work experience, where you notice a better way to do something
   - B) A government licence to start a business
   - C) A large amount of savings
   - D) A degree in business studies
7. In brainstorming, the most important rule while ideas are being produced is: *(Understand)*
   - A) Only the group leader may speak
   - B) Choose the best idea after every suggestion
   - C) Keep the list to five ideas
   - D) Do not criticise ideas
8. A group screens three ideas with a weighted checklist. Which criterion would usually carry the largest weight? *(Understand)*
   - A) Time to first sale
   - B) Demand
   - C) Matches our skills
   - D) Low start-up cost
9. A pupil's idea solves a real problem, but the school has no money to start it and no one with the skill. Which screening criterion does it fail most clearly? *(Apply)*
   - A) Demand
   - B) Market structure and size
   - C) Margins and resources
   - D) Timing
10. Schumpeter's five paths to new opportunities include: *(Remember)*
   - A) Inventing a new technology only
   - B) Copying a successful business at a lower price
   - C) Waiting for the government to create demand
   - D) Opening a new market for an existing product

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain the difference between a small business and a scalable start-up in two sentences, and give one Pakistani example of each. *(Understand)*
2. Give two reasons a social enterprise is not the same as a charity. *(Understand)*
3. A classmate says, "A small business is a failed start-up." Explain why this view is wrong. *(Understand)*
4. Analyse this case: a teacher in a government school in Sindh starts a weekend skills club for out-of-school girls, with the head teacher's permission and a small fee. Which form of entrepreneurship is it, and what evidence supports your answer? *(Analyze)*
5. Describe two barriers women entrepreneurs may face in Pakistan, and explain how each one affects the venture. *(Understand)*
6. Define a business opportunity in one sentence, and give one example that is not an invention. *(Understand)*
7. Explain two sources of business opportunities, with one example of each. *(Understand)*
8. A classmate says, "I have a great idea, so I have an opportunity." Explain what else must be true, using the definition from this unit. *(Analyze)*
9. Explain the difference between brainstorming and crowdsourcing, and give one example of each. *(Understand)*
10. Analyse which of the three screening criteria is most important for a small town, and justify your choice with an example. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Three ventures, three kinds.** Compare a small business, a scalable start-up and a social enterprise from Pakistan. In 300-400 words, analyse which of the three best suits a young B.Ed graduate with little savings, and justify your recommendation. *(Evaluate)*
2. **Intrapreneur or social entrepreneur?** A teacher in a government school in Sindh wants to start a weekend skills club for out-of-school girls. Analyse whether she should run it as an intrapreneur inside the school or as her own social enterprise, weighing at least three factors such as money, permission, time and risk. *(Analyze)*
3. **From idea to opportunity.** Take one everyday problem in a school or village you know. In 300-400 words, explain how you would turn it into a business opportunity, using at least two sources of opportunities and all three screening criteria. *(Create)*
4. **Which idea should we back?** Screen three business ideas for a small town using a simple weighted checklist and recommend one. In 300-400 words, show your weights, scores and totals, and evaluate your recommendation, including one reason it might still fail. *(Evaluate)*
5. **A venture for my district.** In 350-450 words, choose one venture for your district. Decide which kind of entrepreneurship it is, identify the opportunity it acts on, explain how you generated and screened the idea, and justify it with the three screening criteria. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer, so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** A scalable start-up is built to grow quickly and reach many customers. A describes a small business, and C and D describe other kinds of organisation.
2. **C.** A social enterprise sells goods or services and tries to pay its own costs. A charity depends on donations and cannot distribute profits.
3. **D.** The teacher acts entrepreneurially inside her school without leaving her job. A and C are independent ventures, and B is a growing business.
4. **D.** A woman who starts and runs her own venture is a woman entrepreneur. It is not a start-up, a charity or intrapreneurship.
5. **C.** An opportunity needs both real demand and a feasible way to deliver. An invention, a loan or a patent alone is not an opportunity.
6. **A.** Work experience is one of the sources named in the unit. A licence, savings and a degree may help, but they are not sources of opportunities.
7. **D.** Brainstorming asks for many ideas without judgement first. Choosing early or limiting the list kills the idea flow.
8. **B.** Demand carries the largest weight because an idea without customers cannot survive. The other criteria matter, but they come after demand.
9. **C.** Money and skills are resources, so the idea fails the margins and resources criterion. Demand is present in the case.
10. **D.** Opening a new market for an existing product is Schumpeter's first path. The other options are not in his list.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** A small business serves a local market and earns a living for its owner, such as a general store in a bazaar. A scalable start-up is built to grow quickly and reach many customers, such as a phone-repair business with branches in three towns. **Marks (4):** 1 for the difference in two sentences; 1 for each correct example.
2. **Model:** A social enterprise sells goods or services and tries to pay its own costs, while a charity depends on donations and grants. A charity cannot distribute profits to its members or investors, while a social enterprise can keep its surplus for its mission. **Marks (4):** 2 for each reason.
3. **Model:** A small business is a deliberate choice for a stable livelihood and a known market, not a failed attempt at growth. Many small businesses are profitable and employ local people for decades. Growth is one goal, not the only measure of success. **Marks (4):** 2 for the deliberate-choice point; 2 for the evidence or example.
4. **Model:** It is intrapreneurship, because the teacher acts inside an existing organisation with the head teacher's permission, rather than registering her own company. It also has a social aim, so it can be called a social enterprise as well. Evidence: she keeps her job, uses school permission, and charges a small fee. **Marks (4):** 1 for naming intrapreneurship; 2 for the evidence; 1 for noting the social-enterprise overlap.
5. **Model:** They may need family permission to travel or work outside the home, which limits where they can sell. They may have less access to bank loans, so they start with their own savings or family money. **Marks (4):** 1 for each barrier; 1 for each explanation of its effect.
6. **Model:** A business opportunity is a situation where there is real customer demand and a practical way to deliver what customers want. Example: a woman opens the first shop in her area selling ready-made school uniforms. **Marks (3):** 2 for the definition with both demand and feasibility; 1 for a fitting non-invention example.
7. **Model:** Solving a problem, such as a shopkeeper noticing that pupils have no safe way to reach school and starting a shared van. Customer feedback, such as a tailor changing a design after buyers ask for a different collar. Accept work experience or chance with a fitting example. **Marks (4):** 1 for each source; 1 for each example.
8. **Model:** An idea becomes an opportunity only when there is real demand and a feasible way to deliver. The classmate must check whether customers will pay and whether the venture can deliver at a cost that survives. An idea alone is not an opportunity. **Marks (4):** 2 for naming both demand and feasibility; 2 for linking the definition to the classmate's claim.
9. **Model:** Brainstorming is a group producing many ideas without judgement, such as a class listing twenty ways to give pupils clean water. Crowdsourcing asks a large group, usually online, to send solutions to a challenge, such as a company posting a prize for a new snack flavour. **Marks (4):** 1 for each technique; 1 for each example.
10. **Model:** Demand is usually most important for a small town, because if nobody wants to buy, the other criteria do not matter. For example, a water filter stall may have good margins and low cost, but if families already have clean water, it will not sell. Accept market structure or margins with a strong justification. **Marks (4):** 1 for naming the criterion; 2 for the justification; 1 for the example.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.

**ERQ 1: Three ventures, three kinds**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three forms compared | Compares all three forms on aim, growth and money | Compares two forms well | Describes the forms without comparing | Absent |
| Pakistani examples | Uses one fitting example for each form | Examples for two forms | Examples vague or unrelated | None |
| Suitability analysis (Analyze) | Analyses which form suits a graduate with little savings, with evidence | Partial analysis, one or two factors | Asserts suitability only | Absent |
| Justified recommendation (Evaluate) | A reasoned recommendation that weighs the alternatives | A recommendation with partial reasons | States a preference only | No recommendation |
