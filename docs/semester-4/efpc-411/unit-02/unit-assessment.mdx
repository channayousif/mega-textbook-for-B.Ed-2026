# Unit 2: School Placement Observation Tasks - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

Unit 2 turned the observation skills of Unit 1 towards the fifteen-day school placement. You learned to read a school as evidence: its environment, resources, rules, routines and daily schedule. You learned to read a classroom through interaction, seating and climate, using a seating map with T-P, P-P and P-T codes. You learned to observe teaching across three to four lessons in different subjects, recording objectives, methods, teaching aids and pupil participation with a proforma, and to track a lesson through its stages from opening to closure. You learned to observe management through discipline techniques, time management, behaviour and time on task, and to recognise that assessment lives in oral questions, quizzes and homework checks as well as in tests. Finally, you learned to observe a teacher's professional conduct, to look for inclusive practice that reaches every pupil, and to hold a whole lesson in view with a minute-by-minute timeline. Throughout, the standard is the same: record what can be seen and heard, keep the record low-inference, and use it to support a conversation rather than to pass a verdict.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which of the following is part of the school environment an observer records during the first placement week? *(Remember)*  
   A. The teacher's personality and character.  
   B. The building, grounds, water and sanitation, and learning resources.  
   C. The observer's own feelings about the school.  
   D. The salary and seniority of the staff.

2. Why are school routines useful evidence for an observer? *(Understand)*  
   A. They show what the observer personally prefers.  
   B. They are repeated and predictable, so they can be recorded exactly.  
   C. They are always written into the printed timetable.  
   D. They prove that the school is well managed.

3. An observer tallies 20 teacher-to-pupil moves in a period, 18 of them to the front two rows, and no pupil-to-pupil talk. What does this record most directly show? *(Apply)*  
   A. The teacher is unprofessional.  
   B. The lesson was silent, so it was well managed.  
   C. Interaction was concentrated at the front, and pupils did not talk to each other.  
   D. The pupils at the back had already learned the topic.

4. The course guide asks student-teachers to observe at least how many lessons in different subjects during the placement? *(Remember)*  
   A. One.  
   B. Two.  
   C. Three to four.  
   D. Ten.

5. What is the difference between a topic and a lesson objective? *(Understand)*  
   A. A topic is what pupils will do; an objective is what the teacher covers.  
   B. A topic names the content; an objective states what pupils will be able to do.  
   C. A topic is always longer than an objective.  
   D. A topic is written on the board; an objective stays in the plan book.

6. A teacher writes the objective on the board, explains with bundles of sticks, asks pairs to make numbers with their own sticks, and checks four pairs as they work. Which focus area is most clearly evidenced? *(Apply)*  
   A. Classroom discipline.  
   B. Lesson objectives, methods, aids and participation.  
   C. The daily schedule.  
   D. Professional conduct.

7. What does time on task measure? *(Understand)*  
   A. How long the teacher speaks.  
   B. The share of the lesson in which pupils are doing the learning activity.  
   C. The number of activities in a lesson.  
   D. The length of the timetable period.

8. A class is silent while pupils copy from the board. An observer tallies that fourteen of thirty pupils are not writing, not looking at the board and not responding. What is the best conclusion? *(Apply)*  
   A. The class is well managed because it is silent.  
   B. Silence is always a sign of disengagement.  
   C. The pupils are off task, so silence alone does not show good management.  
   D. The teacher should punish the fourteen pupils.

9. Which interaction with parents best reflects professional conduct? *(Understand)*  
   A. Telling a parent in front of the class that their child never works.  
   B. Receiving a parent courteously and sharing only what is needed.  
   C. Avoiding parents so that they do not disturb the school.  
   D. Asking parents to teach a period for the class.

10. In a lesson, every question goes to boys in the front two rows and a pupil in a wheelchair is never approached. Which observation is the most defensible? *(Apply)*  
    A. The teacher does not like the pupil in the wheelchair.  
    B. The lesson is not inclusive, because participation is concentrated and one pupil is not reached.  
    C. The lesson would be inclusive if the pupils sat in groups.  
    D. The teacher should be reported to the district education office.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Reading a school and a classroom:**  
   Consider this school. Government Boys Primary School in Khairpur has two classrooms, no boundary wall, a hand pump and a small courtyard. It has 96 pupils and 18 mathematics textbooks. The timetable gives six periods of 40 minutes, but assembly usually takes 20. In Class 4, 44 pupils sit in six rows. The teacher explains from the front for 30 minutes, asks 8 questions that all go to the front row, and at the end 26 of 44 pupils are on task. Evaluate which features of this school and classroom matter most for learning, and justify your judgement with specific evidence from the description. *(Analyze/Evaluate)*

2. **Designing a lesson-observation proforma:**  
   Design a six-item lesson-observation proforma for a Grade 5 science lesson. Three items must track objectives and methods, two must track teaching aids and pupil participation, and one must track questioning. For each item, write the observable indicator an observer would record. Then justify why your proforma would give a fair picture of a lesson to a mentor teacher who was not present. *(Analyze/Create)*

3. **Management and assessment in one lesson:**  
   Read this record of a Grade 4 mathematics lesson:  
   *"0 to 5 minutes: entry and attendance. 5 to 7: objective on the board. 7 to 19: teacher explanation. 19 to 24: transition to group work, which took 7 minutes. 24 to 30: group work, with 18 of 38 pupils on task. 30 to 33: summary. 33 to 35: the teacher collected the books and said she would mark them at home. The teacher asked 6 questions, all closed, and none to the back two rows."*  
   Evaluate the management and the assessment in this lesson, and propose two changes that would most improve learning. Justify each change with evidence from the record. *(Analyze/Evaluate)*

4. **Professional conduct and inclusion:**  
   Read this account. A teacher speaks sharply to a parent at the school gate, saying, "Your daughter never works, take her home if you want." In the staffroom she tells a colleague, "That class is hopeless, I have given up on them." In her Class 5 lesson, a pupil in a wheelchair sits at the back near the door with the same workbook as everyone else, but in 40 minutes the teacher never speaks to her, never moves towards her desk and never checks her work. Evaluate the teacher's professional conduct and her inclusive practice, and recommend one action her supervisor could take for each. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative placement observation plan:**  
   Synthesise the four topics of Unit 2 into a one-week observation plan for a placement school. Choose any week from Weeks 5 to 8. Your plan must include:  
   a. the focus of the week and the reason for choosing it;  
   b. the observation tools you would use (a school-environment sheet, a seating map with interaction codes, a lesson-observation proforma, a time-on-task tally, an assessment-method record or a whole-lesson timeline), with one tool per focus;  
   c. the order in which you would observe across the week, and why that order is sensible;  
   d. one ethical step you would take before you begin.  
   Write 300 to 400 words. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - The school environment is the physical and material setting, including the building, grounds, water, sanitation and learning resources.
2. B - Routines are repeated and predictable, so an observer can record exactly what is done and when.
3. C - The tally shows interaction concentrated at the front and an absence of pupil-to-pupil talk; it does not by itself show a motive.
4. C - The course guide requires at least three to four lessons in different subjects during the placement.
5. B - A topic names the content, while a lesson objective states what pupils will be able to do.
6. B - The objective, the use of bundles of sticks, the pair work and the checking of pairs all fall under objectives, methods, aids and participation.
7. B - Time on task is the share of the lesson in which pupils are actually doing the learning activity.
8. C - The tally shows the pupils are off task, so silence alone does not establish good management.
9. B - Receiving a parent courteously and sharing only what is needed reflects professional conduct.
10. B - The record shows participation concentrated by gender and location and one pupil not reached, which is an inclusion finding rather than a claim about motive.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Features of the school environment:**  
   - *Model answer:* Two features are the building and grounds, and the learning resources. The building and grounds show whether pupils can move safely and whether there is room to learn. The resources show whether the teacher has the materials needed to teach the lesson.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for naming two distinct features; 1 mark for explaining why at least one of them matters for learning.

2. **School routines:**  
   - *Model answer:* Two routines are taking the register at the start of the day and lining up for assembly. Taking the register protects learning time because the class settles quickly and the teacher knows who is present. Lining up for assembly protects learning time because pupils move to the next activity without waiting to be told.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for naming two routines; 1 mark for explaining how each protects learning time.

3. **Interaction and climate record:**  
   - *Model answer:* The record shows that interaction was heavily concentrated at the front of the room and that pupils did not talk to one another. This suggests a formal, teacher-led climate in which the teacher controls nearly all talk. One question it raises is whether pupils in the back rows had any opportunity to take part.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for identifying the concentration of interaction; 1 mark for identifying the absence of pupil-to-pupil talk; 1 mark for a relevant question the record raises.

4. **Three to four lessons:**  
   - *Model answer:* One lesson is a sample of one, because the topic, the time of day and the class mood all change from lesson to lesson. Observing three to four lessons in different subjects lets a trainee see a pattern rather than a single event, so the judgement is fairer.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for explaining the limits of a single lesson; 1 mark for explaining that several lessons reveal a pattern.

5. **Analysing a lesson record:**  
   - *Model answer:* The objective is present and the method is an explanation with a chart, which is one form of representation. Questioning is partly open-ended, which is a strength. Participation is limited to 21 of 34 pupils on slate paper. The weakest area is likely pupil participation, because fewer than two thirds of the class practised and no pupil-to-pupil work was recorded.  
   - *Mark scheme (4 marks):* 1 mark for each focus area addressed (objectives, methods, aids, participation); 1 mark for naming the weakest area with a reason grounded in the record.

6. **Time on task:**  
   - *Model answer:* Time on task is the share of a lesson in which pupils are actually doing the learning activity, not listening to the teacher or moving between activities. An observer can measure it with short snapshots of a few seconds, scanning the room and counting how many pupils are on task, or with a tally across five-minute blocks through the lesson.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for a correct definition; 1 mark for describing a practical measurement method.

7. **Two discipline approaches:**  
   - *Model answer:* Class B is more likely to work. Naming the expected behaviour tells the pupils what to do, and moving towards the group makes the expectation hard to ignore. Class A repeats a command without changing anything, so the pupils learn that the command has no consequence.  
   - *Mark scheme (3 marks):* 1 mark for choosing Class B; 1 mark for explaining the effect of naming the expected behaviour; 1 mark for explaining the effect of the teacher's movement.

8. **Formative and summative assessment:**  
   - *Model answer:* Formative assessment happens during teaching and changes it, for example a teacher asking oral questions and using the answers to re-teach a step. Summative assessment reviews learning at the end, for example a written end-of-unit test.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for a correct distinction; 1 mark for one correct example of each.

9. **Professional conduct:**  
   - *Model answer:* Two signs are receiving a parent courteously and sharing only what is needed, and speaking about a class or a pupil without naming or shaming them in front of others. Both show that the teacher treats pupils, parents and colleagues as people who deserve respect.  
   - *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for each sign, provided it is observable rather than a personality judgement.

10. **Inclusion record:**  
    - *Model answer:* The record shows that interaction never reached Desk 12 and that the pupil's workbook stayed closed, which suggests the pupil was excluded from participation. One change is to plan a question or a task for that pupil in every lesson. Another is to check the pupil's work during the lesson rather than at the end of the day.  
    - *Mark scheme (4 marks):* 1 mark for identifying the exclusion; 1 mark for identifying the closed workbook as evidence of non-participation; 2 marks for two specific changes (1 mark each).

### ERQ rubrics

#### ERQ 1: Reading a school and a classroom (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Identification of relevant features (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* Lists features without connecting them to learning.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Identifies at least four features from the description and links most of them to learning.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Selects the features that matter most and explains the chain from each feature to a learning consequence.
- **Criterion 2: Justification with evidence (Evaluate, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Asserts a judgement without evidence.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Supports the judgement with at least two specific details from the description.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Weighs competing features and ranks them with evidence and a reason for the ranking.
- **Criterion 3: Register and clarity (Apply, 3 marks):**  
  - *Meets Standard (2 to 3 marks):* Stays descriptive, uses low-inference language and is respectful of the school.

#### ERQ 2: Designing a lesson-observation proforma (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Allocation across the focus areas (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* Items are unevenly allocated or the focus areas are confused.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Six items cover objectives and methods (three), aids and participation (two) and questioning (one).  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* The allocation is deliberate and the reason for each item is clear.
- **Criterion 2: Observable indicators (Create, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* Indicators are vague or evaluative.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Indicators describe what an observer could see or hear.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Indicators are countable or verbatim, with no judgement words.
- **Criterion 3: Justification (Understand, 2 marks):**  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Explains why a mentor who was not present could trust the proforma.

#### ERQ 3: Management and assessment in one lesson (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Analysis of management and assessment (Analyze, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* Describes the lesson without identifying management or assessment issues.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Identifies at least two management issues and one assessment issue, with evidence from the record.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Explains how the seven-minute transition, the closed questions and the unmarked homework connect to one another.
- **Criterion 2: Proposed changes (Evaluate, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* Proposes changes that are vague or unrelated to the record.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Proposes two specific changes, each linked to evidence in the record.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Proposes changes that a teacher could make in the next lesson, with a way to tell they worked.
- **Criterion 3: Use of evidence (Understand, 2 marks):**  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Uses specific details consistently and separates observation from inference.

#### ERQ 4: Professional conduct and inclusion (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Evaluation of professional conduct (Evaluate, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* Calls the teacher unprofessional without analysing the behaviour.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* Identifies the sharp words to the parent and the negative comment about the class as conduct concerns, with their likely effect.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* Explains the ethical principles at stake and how the behaviour affects trust in the school.
- **Criterion 2: Evaluation of inclusion (Analyze, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Notes only that the pupil uses a wheelchair.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Identifies the absence of teacher-to-pupil moves and the closed workbook as evidence of exclusion.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Analyses the exclusion as a pattern of participation rather than an oversight about one pupil.
- **Criterion 3: Recommendations (Apply, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Advises the supervisor to warn the teacher.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Recommends one actionable step for conduct and one for inclusion.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Recommends steps that are specific, supportive and checkable.

#### ERQ 5: Integrative placement observation plan (Total: 10 marks)

- **Criterion 1: Focus and rationale (Understand, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Names a week without a reason.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Names a week and gives a reason that relates to the unit's topics.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Justifies the week by showing how its focus follows from the earlier weeks.
- **Criterion 2: Tools matched to focus (Apply, 3 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 mark):* Lists tools without matching them to a focus.  
  - *Meets Standard (2 marks):* Matches at least three tools to a focus area.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (3 marks):* Matches one tool per focus and explains why each is the right instrument.
- **Criterion 3: Sequence and ethics (Analyze/Create, 4 marks):**  
  - *Below Standard (1 to 2 marks):* The order is arbitrary and no ethical step is given.  
  - *Meets Standard (3 marks):* The order is sensible and one ethical step is stated.  
  - *Exceeds Standard (4 marks):* The sequence builds from the school to the lesson and the ethical step is practical and specific.
