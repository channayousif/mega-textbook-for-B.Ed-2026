# Unit 2: School Placement Observation Tasks

<Figure id="fig-U2-9" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efpc-411/unit-02/fig-U2-9.webp" alt="A student-teacher observes a Sindh government school classroom with a clipboard while a mentor teacher helps pupils working in groups." width={1344} height={756} />

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, student-teachers will be able to:

- `SLO:EFPC-411-2-1`: Observe and describe a school's environment, rules, routines and daily schedule, and one classroom's interaction, seating arrangement and general climate.
- `SLO:EFPC-411-2-2`: Observe three to four lessons in different subjects and record their objectives, teaching methods and strategies, teaching aids and pupil participation.
- `SLO:EFPC-411-2-3`: Observe classroom discipline techniques, time management, pupil behaviour and engagement, and the assessment methods in use.
- `SLO:EFPC-411-2-4`: Observe a teacher's professional conduct and interaction with colleagues and parents, the use of inclusive practices, and one complete lesson from start to end.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 2 builds directly on Unit 1 of this course, where you learned what classroom observation is, the roles of supervisors, mentors and observers, the ethics of observing, and the main observation tools. Two earlier courses also prepare you for this placement unit:

- `EFPC-411` Unit 1 (Foundations of Classroom Observation, Semester 4): observation types, focus areas, checklists, rating scales, anecdotal records, bias and feedback.
- `EFMP-305` (Inclusive Education, Semester 2): the principles of inclusive education, individual differences and the classroom practices that include every learner.

## How to use this unit

This unit is your working guide for Weeks 5 to 8, the fifteen-day school placement. Read the topic for the week before you go, carry the observation sheets and proformas into school, and complete the activities with your fellow student-teachers and your mentor teacher. Record only what you can see and hear, in the low-inference way Unit 1 taught you, and keep every note confidential. Each topic ends with a task for your practicum school and a summative task for your portfolio. The unit assessment at the end brings the four weeks together for the seminar assessment.
