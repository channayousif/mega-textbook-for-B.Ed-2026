# Topic 1.3 - Aims and objectives of teaching science

## A real classroom situation

Kiran is a B.Ed student on placement at a government elementary school in Hyderabad. She shows her lesson plan for Grade 6 to her cooperating teacher, Madam Shazia. Under "Objective" Kiran has written: "To teach pupils about the digestive system."

Madam Shazia reads it and asks two questions. "When the bell rings, what will the pupils be able to *do* that they could not do before?" And then a harder one: "Why do we teach the digestive system at all? Most of these girls will not become doctors." Kiran realises she has mixed up two things: why science is taught in school, and what one lesson should achieve. This topic separates the two.

## Explanation

### Aims and objectives of teaching science

**Aims and objectives are different.** An **aim** is a broad, long-term purpose. It describes what science education should achieve over years of schooling, and no single lesson can complete it. An **objective** is a specific, short-term result of one lesson or unit. It says what pupils will be able to do by the end, in words that can be checked. "Develop scientific literacy" is an aim. "Label the main parts of the digestive system on a diagram" is an objective.

Aims tell you *why* you teach science. Objectives tell you *what* this lesson will achieve towards those aims. A teacher needs both: aims without objectives lead to vague lessons; objectives without aims lead to lessons that tick boxes but go nowhere.

**The aims of teaching science.** Older methods books on the course reading list, such as Das (1985), are one place to read about these aims. In Pakistan today, the clearest statement of them is the National Curriculum of Pakistan 2022-23. Reading its Science progression grid for Grades 4-8, six aims stand out (National Curriculum Council [NCC], 2023). Figure fig-U1-5 lists them with the part of the curriculum that shows each.

1. **Understanding key concepts.** The National Curriculum says it aims to move classrooms "from rote learning to concept-based learning" (NCC, 2023, p. 4). Pupils should understand big ideas in life science, physical science, and earth and space science, not just recite them.
2. **Enquiry skills.** Through the Scientific Enquiry strand, pupils learn to ask questions, run fair tests, record results and reason to a conclusion.
3. **Scientific attitudes.** Curiosity, honesty and open-mindedness, built every time pupils check their results or suggest improvements to an experiment.
4. **Applying science to daily life.** Pupils should "recognise the importance of science and technology to solve everyday problems" (NCC, 2023, p. 35), from clean water to saving electricity.
5. **Science in society and the environment.** The Science in Context strand asks pupils to discuss how science and technology can have positive and negative effects on the environment, locally and globally.
6. **Preparing for further study and work.** Pupils identify people in their own area who use science in their jobs, such as the lady health worker, the pharmacist or the electrician.

Together these aims add up to **scientific literacy**: enough understanding of science, and of how science works, to make sense of everyday life and take part in decisions as a citizen. Pakistan's earlier science curriculum for classes one to eight already named scientific literacy as an aim of teaching science (Halai, 2003). It is the aim behind the answer to Madam Shazia's second question. Most pupils will not become doctors, but all of them will choose what to eat, whether to boil water and whether to trust a health rumour.

**A note on "secondary level".** The course guide speaks of the aims of teaching science "at secondary level". You are preparing to teach Grades 1 to 8, and the aims above apply across all these grades. What changes from Grade 4 to Grade 8 is the depth: a Grade 5 pupil runs a fair test the teacher has planned, while a Grade 8 pupil plans her own and judges whether a hypothesis is testable (NCC, 2023).

**Objectives.** Objectives turn aims into lessons. A good objective names what pupils will *do*, using an action verb you can observe: *label*, *describe*, *compare*, *measure*, *predict*, *explain*. Verbs such as *know*, *understand* or *learn about* cannot be observed, so they are weak in objectives. Objectives can target knowledge ("describe the role of saliva"), skills ("measure the temperature of water every minute") or attitudes ("record results honestly, even when they differ from the group's"). You will learn to write objectives in detail in Unit 5.

**Worked example: rewriting Kiran's objective.** Kiran's "To teach pupils about the digestive system" fails in two ways: it describes what the *teacher* will do, and "about" cannot be measured. Here is how she can rewrite it. Grade 6 includes "Human digestive system" (NCC, 2023).

- Step 1. Ask what pupils should be able to do. Answer: name the organs, follow the path of food, explain one process.
- Step 2. Choose observable verbs: *label*, *trace*, *explain*.
- Step 3. Write objectives beginning "By the end of the lesson, pupils will be able to...":
  - label the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine on a diagram;
  - trace the path of a piece of roti from the mouth to the large intestine;
  - explain in one sentence why chewing helps digestion.
- Step 4. Link to an aim. These objectives serve aim 1 (understanding a concept). Adding "test whether chewing speeds up the breakdown of a biscuit in water" would also serve aim 2 (enquiry skills).

> **Common misconception:** "The aim of teaching science is to finish the syllabus and get pupils through the exam." Exams matter, but they are a way of checking progress towards the aims, not the aims themselves. A lesson that prepares pupils only to recall definitions may raise marks for a term, yet miss five of the six aims above.

## Activity: Aim or objective?

Work in pairs. About 15 minutes.

1. Your teacher gives each pair ten statements, such as "Pupils will develop curiosity about the natural world", "Pupils will measure the length of a shadow at three times of day", "Pupils will appreciate the role of science in Pakistan's development", and "Pupils will classify ten animals as vertebrates or invertebrates".
2. Sort them into aims and objectives. For each objective, underline the observable verb.
3. Take two of the aims and, together, write one objective for a Grade 5 lesson that would move pupils towards each.
4. Join another pair. Read each other's objectives and check: could a teacher see, at the bell, whether each one was achieved?

## Check your understanding

1. Define an aim and an objective in one sentence each. *(Remember)*
2. List four aims of teaching science found in Pakistan's National Curriculum 2022-23 for Science. *(Remember)*
3. Explain why "Pupils will understand magnetism" is a weak objective. *(Understand)*
4. Rewrite "To teach the water cycle" as two measurable objectives for a Grade 5 lesson. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Aims are the long-term purposes of teaching science; objectives are the short-term, measurable results of a lesson. Pakistan's National Curriculum 2022-23 points to six aims: understanding concepts, enquiry skills, scientific attitudes, applying science to daily life, understanding science in society and the environment, and preparing for further study and work. Together they build scientific literacy. Good objectives use observable verbs and connect each lesson to these aims.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can explain the difference between an aim and an objective with an example of each.
- [ ] I can name the main aims of teaching science and show where the National Curriculum states them.
- [ ] I can rewrite a vague lesson statement as measurable objectives.
- [ ] I can judge which aims a lesson serves and which it leaves out.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask to see two science lesson plans or diary entries written by teachers at your placement school. Copy the objectives exactly. At the seminar, mark each objective as observable or not, and note which of the six aims it serves. Do not name the teacher.

## Summative task

A Grade 7 teacher writes this plan for a lesson on "Immunity and diseases":

> Objective: To make pupils aware of diseases. Activities: read pages 54-56 aloud; pupils copy the list of diseases and their causes; homework: learn the list.

Write 200-250 words. First, explain why the objective is weak. Second, analyse which of the six aims the lesson serves and which it misses. Third, rewrite the plan with three measurable objectives, including at least one that builds an enquiry skill or an attitude.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1) | Secure (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critique of the objective | Says it is weak without reasons | Gives one clear reason, such as the unobservable verb | Gives two reasons and links each to the meaning of an objective |
| Analysis of aims *(Analyze)* | Lists aims without linking them to the lesson | Identifies aims served and missed with some evidence | Analyses each aim against specific activities in the plan and explains why it is served or missed |
| Rewritten objectives *(Create)* | Objectives remain vague | Three measurable objectives | Three measurable, grade-appropriate objectives covering knowledge and at least one skill or attitude |
