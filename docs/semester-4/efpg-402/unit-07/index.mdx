# Unit 7: Assessment and Evaluation in Science

<Figure id="fig-U7-11" src="/img/figures/efpg-402/unit-07/fig-U7-11.webp" alt="A secondary science teacher in Sindh holds an observation clipboard while assessing Grade 8 students conducting a filtration experiment in the school laboratory." />

## Unit learning outcomes

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

- `SLO:EFPG-402-7-1`: Explain the meaning and purpose of evaluation in science education, distinguish clearly between formative and summative assessment, and select valid tools and techniques to evaluate cognitive understanding and laboratory performance.
- `SLO:EFPG-402-7-2`: Construct a balanced achievement test using a table of specifications, interpret assessment data to diagnose student misconceptions, and design targeted remedial teaching strategies.

## Prerequisite knowledge

You need the pedagogical and curriculum foundations from Unit 2 (Science Curriculum and Textbook), Unit 3 (Methods and Approaches of Teaching Science), and Unit 5 (Lesson Planning and Teaching Skills). You should also recall general assessment concepts from EFMP-408 Educational Assessment and Evaluation, which this unit applies directly to school science teaching.

## How to use this unit

This unit spans weeks 13 and 14 of the course. Each topic forms an integrated, nine-part learning cycle designed to move you from classroom realities in Sindh schools to practical mastery:

- A concrete classroom situation setting up an assessment challenge.
- An evidence-based explanation linking national curriculum standards with practical examples and common misconceptions.
- A collaborative pair or small-group activity.
- Formative retrieval questions to check your grasp immediately.
- A summary of enduring understandings.
- A self-assessment metacognitive checklist.
- A transfer task to trial during school placement.
- A summative task with an analytic rubric demanding higher-order thinking.
- Further reading citations.

Read each topic ahead of your methods seminar, practice drafting test items and rubrics during workshops, and test these assessment strategies in your practicum school.
