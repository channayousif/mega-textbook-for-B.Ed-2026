# Topic 6.6 - The science teacher's role in laboratory management

## A real classroom situation

Madam Bilquees is the science teacher at a government high school in Badin. When she was first assigned laboratory responsibilities, she treated practical classes identically to standard textbook lectures, walking into the laboratory only when the period bell rang and leaving immediately when the bell dismissed the class.

During those early months, practical sessions were chaotic: reagent bottles were empty when experiments were scheduled to begin; pupils spent twenty minutes of a forty-minute period searching for matches and dropper pipettes; and sinks were left clogged with filter paper and sediment. After three months, half the glass test tubes were broken, stock was unaccounted for, and the headteacher expressed concern about wasted instructional time.

Madam Bilquees realized that successful laboratory teaching depends on rigorous management that occurs *before* pupils arrive and *after* they depart. She instituted a systematic operational protocol: conducting pre-lesson trials, active circulating supervision during inquiry, and structured student cleanup and security routines at dismissal.

## Explanation

### Role and responsibilities of the science teacher in laboratory management

The science teacher serves not merely as an instructor delivering content, but as the primary manager, safety officer, curator, and custodian of the school science laboratory (Kochhar). The quality of practical learning directly reflects the teacher's planning, organizational discipline, and foresight (National Curriculum Council, 2023).

As illustrated in Figure fig-U6-11, the teacher's professional responsibilities span three distinct instructional phases:

1. **Pre-laboratory preparation phase (Before class):**
   - *Curriculum planning and risk assessment:* The teacher selects experiments aligned with student learning outcomes, reviews potential physical and chemical hazards, and designs safety mitigations.
   - *Trial testing of experimental procedures:* The teacher personally conducts a trial run of the experiment 24 to 48 hours prior to class to verify that reagents are fresh, burners provide adequate heat, and expected reactions occur within the class time box.
   - *Material staging:* Reagents and apparatus are pre-measured and arranged on trays for each student group, eliminating student queueing and congestion at the central chemical cupboard.
   - *Safety inspection:* Verifying that wash sinks have water, the eyewash bottle is filled with fresh distilled water, emergency exits are unbolted, and the first-aid kit is fully stocked.
2. **During-laboratory supervision phase (Live instruction):**
   - *Clear briefing and safety contract:* The teacher delivers a concise 5-minute pre-lab orientation highlighting key procedural moves, hazard pictograms, and disposal expectations.
   - *Active circulating supervision:* The teacher does not remain seated at the front desk. Active, continuous movement among student benches allows the teacher to observe manipulative techniques, correct unsafe habits immediately, ask probing questions, and guide struggling pairs.
   - *Pacing and time management:* Keeping groups on schedule so that data collection concludes with sufficient time reserved for cleaning and debriefing.
3. **Post-laboratory management phase (After class):**
   - *Supervising student cleanup:* Ensuring that student pairs wash their glassware, wipe down bench worktops with clean cloths, and deposit chemical residues in designated waste containers.
   - *Apparatus inventory and audit:* Accounting for all issued apparatus before dismissing the class to identify breakages or missing items immediately.
   - *Facility security:* Personally verifying that all bench gas taps are closed, master electrical switches are turned off, water taps are tightly closed, and the chemical storeroom is locked securely.

#### Weekly operational maintenance routines

To prevent deterioration of facilities and maintain steady instructional readiness, the science teacher establishes a structured weekly maintenance routine, as detailed in Figure fig-U6-12:

- **Monday (Facility safety audit):** Inspect the main gas shutoff valve, electrical breaker box, and ensure both emergency exits open freely without binding.
- **Tuesday (Chemical stock checks):** Check reagent container caps for tightness, wipe acid trays, replace damaged or faded labels, and note depleted reagents for requisitioning.
- **Wednesday (Apparatus inspection):** Examine glassware for hairline cracks or chipped rims, discard damaged items into the designated glass bin, and record condemnations in the Breakage Register.
- **Thursday (First-aid and emergency gear):** Refresh eyewash squeeze bottles with clean distilled water, check sterile gauze supplies, and verify that sand buckets are dry and uncompacted.
- **Friday (Security lockup and ledger reconciliation):** Reconcile consumable usage logs, secure all storage cabinets, inspect window latches, and obtain weekly administrative sign-off with the school headmaster.

#### Worked example: Executing a 10-minute pre-lab staging routine

Madam Bilquees prepares for a Grade 7 inquiry investigating the neutralisation of dilute hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide using phenolphthalein indicator:

1. *24 hours prior:* She conducts a trial test, confirming that 10 mL of 0.1 M acid requires approximately 10 mL of 0.1 M base to turn pale pink.
2. *10 minutes before class:*
   - She prepares four identical plastic staging trays for the four student bench islands.
   - Each tray contains: four clean test tubes in a rack, two plastic dropper pipettes, one small dropper vial of 0.1 M HCl labelled with a corrosive warning, one dropper vial of 0.1 M NaOH labelled with an irritant warning, and one small dropper bottle of phenolphthalein.
   - She checks the wash sink water tap and places a labelled plastic waste beaker on each bench.
3. *Outcome:* When pupils enter, they go directly to their assigned benches and begin purposeful inquiry immediately, completely eliminating crowd bottlenecks at the chemical cupboard.

> **Common misconception:** "The science teacher's instructional job begins when the period starts and ends when the bell rings." Many beginning teachers assume that preparation and cleanup are purely custodial duties. In science education, pre-lab staging and post-lab security are core pedagogical responsibilities: a poorly staged laboratory wastes valuable learning time, while neglected post-lab management breeds catastrophic accidents.

## Activity: Creating an operational laboratory checklist

Work in pairs. About 25 minutes.

1. Select one of the following practical laboratory units:
   - Practical A: Grade 7 investigation of boiling points and thermal changes.
   - Practical B: Grade 8 preparation and observation of plant epidermal cells under microscopes.
2. Create a comprehensive, chronological teacher responsibility checklist divided into three clear columns:
   - Column 1: Pre-Lab Actions (Tasks done before pupils enter the room).
   - Column 2: Live Supervisory Moves (Specific teacher observations and prompts during the 30-minute practical).
   - Column 3: Post-Lab Security and Reconciliation (Tasks done before locking the room).
3. Include at least four specific checkpoints in each column.
4. Review your checklist with another pair and assess whether any safety or administrative task was omitted.

## Summary

The science teacher is the professional custodian, instructional facilitator, and safety manager of the school science laboratory. Effective laboratory management encompasses a rigorous tripartite cycle: pre-lab preparation (risk assessment, trial runs, material staging, and safety checks); live circulating supervision (active monitoring, immediate corrective coaching, and time pacing); and post-lab accountability (student cleanup, equipment reconciliation, and facility lockdown). By instituting systematic weekly maintenance schedules, the science teacher maintains instructional readiness, ensures administrative transparency, and protects pupil well-being.

## Summative task

A secondary school with 400 pupils has a single science laboratory that has fallen into severe disrepair, with teachers reporting frequent apparatus shortages, chaotic student classes, and dirty benches. The school principal appoints you as the lead laboratory coordinator to establish an operational management system. Write a 250-300 word comprehensive laboratory management manual for the science faculty. In your manual, analyze the operational causes of the current disorder, formulate a mandatory tripartite checklist (pre-lab, live, post-lab) for teachers, and establish a weekly facility maintenance schedule to ensure continuous safety and apparatus readiness.
