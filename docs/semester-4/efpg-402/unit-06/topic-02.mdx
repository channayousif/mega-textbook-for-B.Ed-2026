# Topic 6.2 - Layout and organisation of a science laboratory

## A real classroom situation

Sir Jameel is appointed as the senior science teacher at a newly upgraded secondary school in Sukkur. The school administration assigns him a large rectangular hall to establish the new combined science laboratory. When he enters the room, he finds wooden desks pushed closely against the walls, single electrical extension cords trailing across the central floor, and only one narrow inward-opening wooden door serving as both entrance and exit.

During the first practical session with forty Grade 8 pupils, chaos ensues. Pupils carrying boiling water from the single corner sink collide with classmates returning to their seats. When a spirit burner accidentally tips over on a middle desk, pupils surge toward the single door, blocking each other in panic. Sir Jameel quickly smothers the flame with a damp cloth, but the near-disaster leaves him shaken.

He realizes that poor spatial arrangement, blocked gangways, and missing emergency exits transform minor classroom mishaps into life-threatening emergencies. He immediately resolves to re-plan the laboratory floor layout according to established architectural and safety standards.

## Explanation

### Layout and organisation of a science laboratory

A school science laboratory is a specialised instructional space that demands careful architectural and pedagogical planning (Kochhar). Unlike a conventional classroom where pupils remain seated at uniform desks facing forward, a laboratory requires dynamic movement: pupils retrieve glassware, collect reagents, consult reference charts, wash equipment, and conduct experiments in small collaborative teams (CPSC & NIOSH, 2006).

Figure fig-U6-3 illustrates a model floor plan for a secondary school science laboratory accommodating 24 to 32 pupils. Key spatial and functional zones include:

1. **Teacher demonstration bench and instructional zone:** Positioned at the front of the room on a low raised platform (15 cm), this bench enables all pupils to observe teacher demonstrations clearly. It includes master shutoff switches for electricity and gas, a deep sink, and immediate access to the chalkboard or display screen.
2. **Student workstation islands:** Central rectangular benches arranged with wide perimeter and cross gangways. Each bench accommodates 6 to 8 pupils working in collaborative pairs. Worktop surfaces should be constructed of seasoned hardwood treated with acid-resistant polyurethane or chemical-resistant composite material.
3. **Perimeter service and washing sinks:** Sinks, water taps, and drying racks are placed along outer walls rather than in central student aisles. This perimeter arrangement prevents trailing water puddles in high-traffic walking corridors.
4. **Safety and emergency station:** Located adjacent to the primary exit, containing an eye-wash station, first-aid cabinet, fire blanket, and sand buckets.
5. **Preparation and chemical storage room:** A separate, secure room accessible only by the teacher and laboratory attendant, featuring lockable cupboards, ventilated reagent shelving, and a preparation bench.

#### Architectural standards and safety rationales

Every structural feature in a laboratory plan directly serves a safety or instructional function, as detailed in Figure fig-U6-4:

- **Dual outward-opening emergency exits:** Every school laboratory must have at least two independent exit doors located at opposite ends of the room. Doors must open outward toward the direction of egress and remain completely unlocked and unobstructed during classes. If a fire or chemical spill blocks one door, pupils can evacuate safely through the second exit without crushing.
- **Wide gangways (minimum 1.5 metres):** Aisles between student benches must provide at least 1.5 metres of clear space. This width allows two pupils carrying glassware to pass each other comfortably and gives the teacher room to circulate and monitor experimental techniques.
- **Master utility shutoffs:** Central isolation switches for gas and electrical lines allow the teacher to immediately cut power or fuel supply to the entire room during an emergency without searching for individual bench valves.
- **Adequate ventilation and illumination:** Natural cross-ventilation through large windows supplemented by ceiling exhaust fans prevents the accumulation of irritating fumes. Large windows also provide high natural light, ensuring pupils can read precise graduations on burettes and thermometers.

#### Worked example: Auditing and re-planning a cramped laboratory space

Sir Jameel audits his 10 metre by 7 metre room in Sukkur. Initially, benches are placed in three long rows with only 0.8 metres between them, and utility wires run across the floor. To correct this hazard:

1. *Demarcation:* He rearranges the tables into four distinct island benches placed across the width, creating a central 1.8-metre thoroughfare.
2. *Egress:* He requests the school administration to install a second exit door at the rear wall opening into the open courtyard.
3. *Utility routing:* Trailing extension cords are removed; electrical sockets are mounted onto the walls adjacent to benches with protective residual current circuit breakers (RCCB).
4. *Safety station:* He mounts a first-aid kit and clean eyewash bottle on the wall beside Exit 1, clearly marked with a green cross.

This re-organization eliminates tripping hazards, ensures rapid evacuation routes, and gives Sir Jameel unobstructed visibility across all pupil groups.

> **Common misconception:** "Laboratory organisation is purely a matter of neatness and appearance." Many novice teachers treat laboratory arrangement as superficial housekeeping. In practice, layout is a primary safety system: correct spacing, clear sightlines, and isolated storage directly prevent fatal chemical burns, fires, and stampedes.

## Activity: Evaluating laboratory floor plans

Work in pairs. About 25 minutes.

1. Examine the following school scenario:
   - A secondary school in Mirpurkhas has a laboratory measuring 8 metres by 6 metres with 36 enrolled pupils.
   - The room has one inward-opening door, benches positioned 0.9 metres apart, and chemical storage bottles kept on open shelves above the student wash sink.
2. Identify three critical architectural or safety hazards in this setup.
3. Sketch a revised floor plan on grid paper, incorporating:
   - Dual exit pathways.
   - At least 1.5-metre aisle clearances.
   - A dedicated safety corner and isolated chemical cupboard.
4. Present your revised plan to another pair, justifying your spatial modifications.

## Check your understanding

1. State the minimum recommended width for gangways between student laboratory benches and explain why this clearance is necessary. *(Remember)*
2. Explain the safety necessity of requiring dual, outward-opening emergency exits in a secondary school science laboratory. *(Understand)*
3. A headteacher proposes converting a windowless storage room into a science laboratory to save classroom space. Apply architectural layout standards to analyze three severe risks of this proposal and suggest necessary structural prerequisites before it can be approved. *(Apply)*

## Summary

The physical layout of a school science laboratory must integrate instructional visibility with rigorous safety engineering. Functional zoning requires a raised teacher demonstration bench, spacious student island workstations with at least 1.5 metres of aisle clearance, perimeter wash sinks, a dedicated safety station, and a locked preparation room. Dual outward-opening emergency exits and master utility controls provide essential safeguards against fire and chemical hazards. Effective organization ensures that every pupil can work without collision, while the teacher maintains continuous supervision.

## Try this at your practicum school

Inspect the physical layout of the science laboratory at your practicum school using a measuring tape or paces. Measure the width of the aisles between student benches, note the number and swing direction of exit doors, and check whether sinks have working drainage. Check if master electrical breakers or gas shutoffs are easily accessible to the teacher. Prepare a concise architectural audit report with two concrete recommendations for improving pupil safety and circulation.

## Summative task

A government school receives a grant to furnish a new General Science laboratory measuring 11 metres by 8 metres for classes of 30 pupils. Write a 250-300 word technical layout proposal for the school management committee. In your proposal, specify the dimensions and placement of student benches, detail the location of utility services and safety fixtures, analyze how the design prevents common physical accidents, and justify the placement of the preparation and chemical storage room.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Below expectations (0-1) | Meets expectations (2-3) | Exceeds expectations (4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical layout specifications** | Mentions generic tables without clear dimensions or zoning. | Specifies realistic bench dimensions, aisle clearances, and functional zones (demo, student, sinks). | Delivers precise, professional spatial specifications including gangway widths (>= 1.5 m), dual exits, and utility routing. |
| **Safety analysis of spatial design** *(Analyze)* | Fails to link layout choices to accident prevention. | Analyzes how wide aisles and exit locations reduce crowding and collision risks during practical work. | Critically evaluates specific accident vectors (fire, corrosive spills, stampedes) and justifies architectural mitigations. |
| **Storage and prep room integration** | Omits preparation room or treats it as an open cupboard. | Locates the prep room logically and explains the need for locked, ventilated chemical storage. | Thoroughly integrates the preparation room into the operational flow, balancing security, ventilation, and teacher accessibility. |
