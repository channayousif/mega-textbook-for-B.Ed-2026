# Topic 8.4 - Environmental education and sustainable development

## A real classroom situation

Following the catastrophic 2022 floods in Sindh, pupils at a government elementary school in Dadu return to a rehabilitated school building. During a Grade 5 science lesson on the water cycle, a pupil named Farzana raises her hand: "Madam, our textbook diagram shows rain falling, flowing into rivers, and evaporating into clouds peacefully. But last year the flood destroyed our village, drowned our cattle, and washed away our crops. Why did the rain become so destructive, and can we stop it?"

Madam Hameeda realizes that teaching the standard water cycle as an isolated, mechanical process ignores the profound environmental realities shaping her pupils' lives. The floods were exacerbated by unprecedented monsoon rains linked to global climate change, combined with local deforestation and poor drainage infrastructure. How can science teachers integrate environmental education and sustainable development into everyday lessons so that pupils understand the environmental crises around them and take action?

## Explanation

### Environmental education and sustainable development

Environmental education (EE) and Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) represent essential dimensions of contemporary science pedagogy. Far from being luxury topics, they are critical survival literacies for Pakistani learners living on the frontlines of global climate vulnerability (NCC, 2023; UNESCO, 2017).

#### Definitions and Core Principles

- **Sustainable Development:** Defined classically by the Brundtland Commission and reaffirmed by the United Nations, sustainable development is *development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs* (UNESCO, 2017). It balances three interdependent pillars: economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection.
- **Environmental Education (EE):** A multidisciplinary learning process that increases individuals' knowledge and awareness about the environment and its associated challenges, develops necessary skills to address challenges, and fosters attitudes, motivations, and commitments to make informed decisions and take responsible action.
- **Education for Sustainable Development (ESD):** UNESCO (2017) defines ESD as an educational approach that empowers learners to take informed decisions and responsible actions for environmental integrity, economic viability, and a just society, for present and future generations.

#### The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in School Science

Adopted by all United Nations Member States in 2015, the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development comprises 17 Global Goals. In elementary and middle school science (Grades 4-8), several SDGs connect directly to core curriculum themes (NCC, 2023; UNESCO, 2017):

1. **SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being:** Linked to human body systems, nutrition, sanitation, disease prevention, and air/water quality.
2. **SDG 6: Clean Water and Sanitation:** Directly connects to lessons on mixtures, solutions, filtration, water conservation in the Indus basin, and protecting groundwater from contamination.
3. **SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy:** Links to topics on forms of energy, energy transfer, electricity, and the transition from fossil fuels to renewable solar, wind, and biogas resources in Pakistan.
4. **SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production:** Connects to material properties, waste management, and the principles of Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (the 3Rs).
5. **SDG 13: Climate Action:** Intertwined with lessons on weather, climate, the greenhouse effect, global warming, carbon footprints, and disaster risk reduction.
6. **SDG 14 & 15: Life Below Water and Life on Land:** Found in ecosystem units, biodiversity studies, food chains, deforestation, habitat destruction, and protecting endangered native species (such as the Indus river dolphin and snow leopard).

#### Infusion Model: Weaving EE into the Existing Curriculum

**Common misconception to avoid.** A very common misconception among school administrators and teachers is that *environmental education is a separate, additional subject that requires a dedicated timetable slot and a new textbook*. Because teachers already struggle with tight schedules, this misconception leads them to neglect environmental topics altogether.

In professional practice, environmental education is taught through an **infusion model**. Rather than adding a new subject, the teacher weaves environmental and sustainability concepts into existing science units:

- When teaching *States of Matter* in Grade 4, incorporate how plastic pollution affects local waterways and wildlife.
- When teaching *Plant Biology* in Grade 5, investigate local native trees (neem, babul) versus thirsty exotic species (eucalyptus), discussing groundwater conservation.
- When teaching *Energy Transformations* in Grade 7, compare the environmental costs of coal-fired power plants with solar mini-grids installed in rural Sindh.

**Worked example: Developing an ESD Learning Unit on Water Scarcity and Quality.**

Figure fig-U8-8 illustrates how Madam Hameeda integrates environmental education into a Grade 6 science unit on "Water: Sources and Purification" in Dadu:

1. *Curriculum standard:* National Curriculum SLO on identifying sources of water and methods of water purification (filtration, boiling, chlorination).
2. *Local sustainability issue:* Salinization of local tube wells and microbial contamination after floodwaters recede.
3. *Classroom learning activities:*
   - Cognitive domain: Pupils test water turbidity and total dissolved solids using simple filter paper and evaporation dishes.
   - Socio-emotional domain: Pupils interview elderly community members about how river levels and canal water quality changed over the last 30 years.
   - Behavioral domain: Pupils construct a three-tier low-cost sand-gravel-charcoal biofilter for school hand-washing stations and plant a small drought-resilient kitchen garden using recycled greywater.
4. *ESD outcome:* Pupils do not merely memorize the definition of "potable water"; they master practical skills to filter water and advocate for responsible water management in their households.

## Activity: Curriculum Green Audit

**Structure:** Pairs or small groups.  
**Time box:** 20 minutes.

1. Select one unit from the National Curriculum of Pakistan for General Science (Grade 4, 5, or 6), such as *Living Organisms and Environment*, *Matter and Materials*, or *Energy*.
2. Conduct a "Green Audit" of the unit by answering these three prompts on a worksheet:
   - *Target science topic:* What is the core topic and textbook SLO?
   - *Mapped SDG:* Which Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 3, 6, 7, 12, 13, 14, or 15) connects most meaningfully to this topic?
   - *Infused student action project:* Design one practical, hands-on sustainability activity that pupils can complete using zero-cost materials (e.g., composting school lunch waste, making seed balls, or mapping energy waste in the school).
3. Share your audited unit with another pair and discuss how the infusion enhances, rather than burdens, normal curriculum delivery.

## Summary

Environmental education and Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) prepare learners to understand and respond to critical ecological challenges. In Pakistan, science teachers must move beyond abstract definitions by infusing sustainability principles across all science strands. By linking classroom science to the Sustainable Development Goals, teachers empower pupils with the scientific knowledge, values, and practical skills needed to protect local ecosystems and build resilient communities.

## Summative task

Develop an "Infused Environmental Science Lesson Plan" (400 to 550 words) for a Grade 6 or Grade 7 science class in Pakistan. Your plan must include: (1) the core science curriculum topic and SLO, (2) the explicitly mapped Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) and targets, (3) a three-domain learning objective breakdown (cognitive knowledge, socio-emotional values, behavioral action per UNESCO 2017 guidelines), (4) an inquiry-based learning activity utilizing local resources, and (5) an authentic assessment rubric evaluating both scientific understanding and environmental action.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1) | Proficient (2) | Exemplary (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curriculum & SDG alignment** | Mentions environment vaguely without clear curriculum or SDG linkage. | Accurately maps a specific science curriculum SLO to an appropriate Sustainable Development Goal. | Seamlessly integrates a core science standard with targeted SDG indicators, articulating a compelling local ecological rationale. |
| **Pedagogical integration (Analyze)** | Treats the environmental component as an afterthought or lecture topic. | Analyzes how environmental concepts enrich the scientific inquiry without detracting from required content. | Critically analyzes how the infusion model bridges theoretical science and socio-ecological action across cognitive, socio-emotional, and behavioral domains. |
| **Authentic action design** | Proposes impractical activities requiring unavailable funding or technology. | Designs realistic, student-centered inquiry activities utilizing low-cost or recycled materials. | Develops an empowering, culturally relevant student action project that produces measurable school or community ecological benefits. |
