# Topic 8.1 - Role of the science teacher in society

## A real classroom situation

In a government middle school in Badin district, monsoon floods have left standing pools of stagnant water around the village. Several families report children falling ill with fever and diarrhea. During an assembly, some community elders suggest that the illness is caused by bad air or evil spirits. Inside the classroom, Grade 7 pupil Bilawal asks his science teacher, Sir Tariq: "Sir, people in our street say that drinking the canal water is fine if it looks clear, and that the sickness comes from the night wind. Is that true?"

Sir Tariq recognizes that his responsibility as a science teacher extends beyond the classroom walls. If he only teaches textbook definitions of waterborne pathogens and ignores the community crisis, his pupils will memorize terms for tests while their families continue to drink contaminated water. How can a science teacher act as an agent of rational enlightenment, public health, and social welfare in the community?

## Explanation

### Role of the science teacher in society

Science education does not exist in an ivory tower. In Pakistan, where communities face critical challenges in public health, clean drinking water, agricultural productivity, and climate vulnerability, the science teacher serves as an indispensable bridge between scientific knowledge and community well-being (National Curriculum Council [NCC], 2023; Yadav, 2002).

**The science teacher as a community educator and health advocate.** Everyday decisions in a community require scientific understanding. When waterborne diseases like cholera and typhoid spread, or when vector-borne diseases like dengue and malaria surge after rains, the science teacher provides accurate, evidence-based guidance. By explaining how boiling kills microscopic pathogens or how covering water containers halts mosquito breeding, the teacher translates biological concepts into life-saving civic practices.

**Cultivating a scientific temper and dispelling superstitions.** A major societal role of the science teacher is fostering what Yadav (2002) identifies as a scientific temper: an open-minded, evidence-seeking disposition that questions unfounded dogmas, superstitions, and misinformation. In many rural and peri-urban areas, superstitions regarding illnesses, lunar eclipses, or crop failures persist. The science teacher gently helps learners and community members examine empirical evidence rather than accepting untested claims.

**Environmental stewardship and local problem-solving.** Pakistan is among the countries most affected by climate disasters, such as extreme heatwaves and flash floods. The National Curriculum of Pakistan 2022-23 explicitly embeds the "Science in Context" strand, emphasizing that pupils must recognize human impacts on ecosystems and participate in conservation (NCC, 2023). Science teachers lead tree plantation campaigns, promote water conservation in irrigated areas, and model safe disposal of plastic waste.

**Inspiring future scientific careers.** The science teacher identifies potential in diverse learners, including girls and children from low-income families, encouraging them to pursue higher studies in medicine, engineering, agriculture, and environmental sciences. In rural schools, where pupils may have never met a professional scientist or engineer, the teacher serves as the primary gateway to scientific careers.

**Common misconception to avoid.** A widespread misconception is that *the science teacher's professional duty ends at the school gate, limited strictly to preparing pupils to pass board examinations*. In reality, science education is fundamental civic education. An effective science teacher recognizes that preparing scientifically literate citizens who can evaluate evidence, protect public health, and steward local natural resources is the ultimate goal of science teaching in society.

**Worked example: Turning a community health crisis into a science learning project.**

Here is how Sir Tariq connects classroom science with community action in Badin:

1. *Classroom investigation:* In Grade 7 science, Sir Tariq guides pupils to take water samples from an open canal, a covered hand pump, and boiled water. Pupils examine the samples under simple microscopes or observe settled sediment, testing pH and discussing microbial contamination.
2. *Connecting to evidence:* Pupils learn that clear water can still harbor deadly invisible bacteria and amoebae, directly refuting the myth that "clear water is always safe".
3. *Community action plan:* Under Sir Tariq's guidance, pupils create simple Sindhi and Urdu pictorial pamphlets demonstrating the three-step water safety rule: settle, filter, and boil for at least one minute.
4. *Societal impact:* Pupils present their findings at a parent-teacher meeting, helping village households adopt water purification practices that significantly reduce local gastrointestinal infections.

## Activity: Community Science Audit

**Structure:** Small groups (3 to 4 participants).  
**Time box:** 25 minutes.

1. In your group, identify three pressing environmental or public health challenges in the community surrounding your school or university (for example, standing sewage water, open burning of crop residue or plastic, unboiled drinking water, or excessive pesticide use).
2. For each challenge, complete the following three columns on chart paper:
   - *Community belief or practice:* What do local residents currently believe or do?
   - *Underlying science concept:* What concept from the elementary science curriculum (Grades 4-8) explains this phenomenon?
   - *Teacher-led community action:* What concrete, realistic action can an elementary science teacher organize with pupils to address this challenge?
3. Pin your chart on the classroom wall and participate in a 5-minute gallery walk to compare solutions.

## Check your understanding

1. State two distinct ways in which an elementary science teacher acts as a public health resource in a rural Pakistani community. *(Remember)*
2. Explain why cultivating a "scientific temper" is essential for democratic citizenship and community development. *(Understand)*
3. A local community believes that spraying standing rainwater to kill mosquito larvae will poison the soil and ruin surrounding vegetable gardens. Describe how you, as the local science teacher, would address this concern using scientific evidence. *(Apply)*

## Summary

The science teacher occupies a vital societal position in Pakistan, bridging formal classroom learning with community welfare. Beyond teaching textbook concepts, the teacher promotes scientific literacy, dispels harmful superstitions, advocates for public health, models environmental stewardship, and inspires future generations toward scientific careers. Science teaching is fundamentally a social mission that empowers citizens to make rational, evidence-based decisions for community well-being.

## Try this at your practicum school

During your next school placement visit, interview your cooperating science teacher or headteacher. Ask them: "In what ways has our school's science teaching helped address problems in the surrounding neighborhood, such as clean water, hygiene, or plantation?" Record their responses in your reflective journal, noting one specific opportunity where pupils could share their science learning with their families.

## Summative task

Write an action proposal (350 to 500 words) titled "The Science Teacher as a Community Resource". In your proposal, choose one specific local issue (such as drinking water quality, seasonal dengue outbreaks, or heatwave safety in Sindh) and outline a school-community outreach plan. Your proposal must include: (a) the scientific principles involved, (b) the targeted community misconceptions, and (c) a sequential plan for involving Grade 6-8 pupils in educating the wider community.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1) | Proficient (2) | Exemplary (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scientific grounding** | Mentions the issue with vague or superficial science concepts. | Clearly explains the accurate biological or physical science principles underlying the problem. | Thoroughly integrates curriculum-aligned scientific concepts with authentic local epidemiological or environmental context. |
| **Misconception analysis (Analyze)** | Identifies a community practice without analyzing the underlying misconception. | Analyzes the root causes of the community misconception and contrasts it with empirical evidence. | Critically analyzes why the misconception persists culturally and demonstrates how evidence-based instruction dismantles it without alienating community members. |
| **Community outreach design** | Proposes unrealistic or passive activities (such as lectures only). | Outlines a realistic, sequenced student-led outreach plan with actionable steps. | Designs an innovative, highly feasible, student-led inquiry plan that fosters active civic participation and measurable community impact. |
