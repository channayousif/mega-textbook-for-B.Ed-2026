# Topic 2.2 - Aims and objectives of the school science curriculum

## A real classroom situation

Madam Fareena is a head teacher at a girls' primary school in Hyderabad. At a staff meeting she asks her science teachers: "What is the aim of teaching science in our school?" One teacher says, "To help pupils pass the board examination." Another says, "To cover the syllabus before March." A third says, "To make pupils good citizens." All three answers feel partly right, but Madam Fareena knows that Pakistan's national curriculum says something more specific. She opens the NCP 2022-23 for Science and finds six broad aims - and she realises that her school's practice covers only one of them.

This topic explains what the national science curriculum aims to achieve, how those aims are turned into measurable objectives, and why a school that teaches only for the exam misses most of the picture.

## Explanation

### Aims and objectives of the secondary science curriculum

**The aims of the national science curriculum.** The National Curriculum of Pakistan 2022-23 for Science, Grades 4-8, sets out to build scientific literacy for every pupil (National Curriculum Council, 2023). Its foreword states the shift "from rote learning to concept-based learning". Six broad aims can be drawn from the curriculum's structure and benchmarks:

1. **Understand key scientific concepts** across three domains: life sciences, physical sciences and earth and space sciences.
2. **Develop enquiry (process) skills** - asking questions, planning fair tests, recording observations and drawing conclusions - through the Scientific Enquiry strand.
3. **Develop scientific attitudes** - curiosity, honesty, open-mindedness and willingness to revise ideas.
4. **Apply science to daily life** - recognising the importance of science and technology to solve everyday problems.
5. **Understand science in society and the environment** - analysing both benefits and harms of science and technology.
6. **Prepare for further study and work** - identifying people who use science professionally and building a foundation for higher study.

Figure fig-U2-3 shows the three content domains and the enquiry strand with a sample standard each.

**From aims to objectives.** An aim is a broad, long-term direction ("develop scientific literacy"). An objective is a specific, measurable outcome for one lesson or unit ("by the end of the lesson, pupils will be able to label five organs of the digestive system on a diagram"). You met this distinction in Topic 1.3 (Unit 1). In the NCP 2022-23, each domain is broken into topics, and each topic carries Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs) that are measurable objectives. The progression grid shows how the same concept grows from Grade 4 to Grade 8.

**The Sindh adoption.** The Directorate of Curriculum, Assessment and Research (DCAR) Sindh adopts the national curriculum and may add or adapt content for the province (DCAR Sindh). Textbooks approved by the Sindh Textbook Board must align with these SLOs. When a textbook's content does not match the SLOs, the teacher - not the textbook - must fill the gap.

<Figure id="fig-U2-4" src="/img/figures/efpg-402/unit-02/fig-U2-4.svg" alt="Diagram showing four levels from national curriculum to provincial adoption to textbook to classroom lesson, with a feedback arrow from the classroom back to the curriculum level." />

**Worked example: turning an aim into an objective.** Take aim 4, "Apply science to daily life." A Grade 6 teacher in Larkana wants to teach a lesson on clean water. She writes this objective: "By the end of the lesson, pupils will be able to explain two reasons why boiling water makes it safer to drink, using the idea of germs." This objective is specific (two reasons), measurable (the teacher can check), and tied to daily life (every family in Larkana boils or filters water). It moves the aim from a poster on the wall to something the teacher and pupils can actually do and check.

> **Common misconception:** "A thicker textbook is a better textbook." Some teachers and parents judge a textbook by its size. But the NCP 2022-23 measures quality by how well the content matches the SLOs, how many activities it includes and whether it builds process skills - not by page count. A short textbook with good activities may serve pupils better than a thick one filled with definitions to memorise.

## Activity: Aims on the wall vs. aims in action

Work in groups of three. About 20 minutes.

1. Your teacher gives each group a list of four school aims taken from real school notice boards in Sindh (for example: "To produce responsible citizens", "To prepare pupils for the board examination", "To develop critical thinking", "To cover the full syllabus").
2. For each school aim, the group matches it to one or more of the six NCP aims listed above, or notes "not in the NCP aims" if it does not match.
3. If a school aim is not in the NCP aims, the group writes one sentence explaining what the school is missing.
4. Each group presents its most surprising finding to the class.

## Check your understanding

1. List the six aims of Pakistan's national science curriculum in your own words. *(Remember)*
2. Explain, in two or three sentences, the difference between an aim and an objective, using an example from science teaching. *(Understand)*
3. A teacher's only stated goal for the year is "to finish the textbook before the annual exam." Use two of the six NCP aims to explain what this goal misses. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Pakistan's NCP 2022-23 for Science aims to build scientific literacy through six directions: understanding concepts, developing enquiry skills, building scientific attitudes, applying science to daily life, understanding science in society and preparing for further study. These broad aims are turned into measurable Student Learning Outcomes for each grade. The national curriculum flows through provincial adoption and textbooks to the classroom. A school that teaches only for the exam covers only a fraction of the curriculum's aims.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask the science teacher which aim of science teaching is most important to them. Write down their exact words. Then compare their answer with the six NCP aims. Note which aims the teacher mentioned and which they did not. Bring your notes to the next seminar.

## Summative task

A school's annual science plan states one aim: "Pupils will pass the annual science examination with at least 50% marks." Write 200-250 words. Evaluate this aim against the six aims of the NCP 2022-23 for Science, Grades 4-8. Identify at least three aims that the school's plan ignores, explain why each matters, and write one measurable objective for one of the missing aims.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1) | Secure (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identification of missing aims | Names aims without explaining why they matter | Identifies three missing aims with a reason for each | Identifies three or more missing aims, each with a reason linked to what pupils lose |
| Evaluation judgement *(Analyze)* | Gives an opinion with no evidence | Judges the plan with reasons from the NCP aims | Weighs what the plan achieves against what it misses and reaches a balanced judgement |
| Measurable objective | Vague or not measurable | A relevant objective with a measurable verb | A specific, grade-appropriate objective tied to a named missing aim |
