# Topic 2.4 - Textbook analysis and evaluation

## A real classroom situation

Madam Zubaida teaches Grade 6 General Science in Thatta. The school has just received a new science textbook. She opens the chapter on "Food and Digestion." The chapter begins with two pages of definitions ("digestion is the process of breaking down food"), followed by a labelled diagram of the digestive system copied from an Indian textbook, with labels such as "esophagus" (American spelling) and no Urdu equivalents. The chapter ends with ten questions that all begin with "Define..." There are no activities, no experiments and no connection to what pupils eat at home.

Madam Zubaida feels something is wrong, but she does not have a clear set of criteria to explain what. This topic gives you a checklist for evaluating a science textbook - so you can say exactly what is good, what is weak and what to do about it.

## Explanation

### Textbook analysis and evaluation

A *textbook* is a teaching resource, not the curriculum itself (Topic 2.1). A good textbook turns the curriculum's SLOs into readable lessons, activities and assessments. A poor textbook may contain wrong facts, miss important SLOs, use language pupils cannot follow, lack activities or show only city-based examples. A teacher who can evaluate a textbook can fill its gaps and can advise the school on which resources to choose.

**A six-criterion checklist.** The following six criteria, drawn from the literature on textbook evaluation (Kochhar; Aggarwal) and adapted for the Pakistani context, give you a systematic way to judge a science textbook. Figure fig-U2-7 sets out the checklist.

**1. Accuracy.** Every fact, diagram and formula must match current science. An outdated classification of living things or a wrong chemical formula teaches pupils something they will have to unlearn later. Check the textbook against the NCP 2022-23 SLOs and against a reliable reference.

**2. Curriculum alignment.** Each chapter should cover the SLOs assigned to that grade and topic. If the NCP asks pupils to "plan a fair test with one variable changed," the chapter must include a fair-test activity, not only a description of what a fair test is.

**3. Language.** The text should be clear and suited to the age of the pupils. Key terms should be defined when they first appear. Unnecessary jargon makes science feel like a foreign language. In bilingual classrooms, Urdu equivalents for technical terms help pupils who think in Urdu.

**4. Activities.** A science textbook without experiments or investigations is like a cooking book without recipes. Activities should use materials that are available in the school or can be found at low cost. A Grade 6 experiment that requires a spectrophotometer is useless in most Pakistani schools; one that uses sunlight, a glass of water and a mirror is better.

**5. Illustrations.** Diagrams should be clear, correctly labelled, accurately drawn and culturally appropriate. A diagram of the human body should use Pakistani school uniforms, not foreign clothing. Labels should match the terms used in the text.

**6. Inclusivity.** The textbook should show girls and boys doing science, rural and urban settings, and a variety of communities. If every scientist in the textbook is a foreign man, Pakistani girls may conclude that science is not for them.

**Worked example: evaluating Madam Zubaida's chapter.** Apply the six criteria to the "Food and Digestion" chapter described above.

- *Accuracy:* We cannot judge from the description alone, but the diagram uses American spelling ("esophagus") rather than the British spelling ("oesophagus") used in Pakistani exams - a small but confusing inconsistency.
- *Curriculum alignment:* If the NCP asks pupils to "describe the functions of the main organs of the digestive system," the chapter may partly cover this. But if the NCP also asks pupils to plan an investigation on food (such as testing foods for starch), the chapter misses that SLO entirely because it has no activities.
- *Language:* Two pages of definitions with no context make the terms hard to understand. No Urdu equivalents are given.
- *Activities:* None. This is a serious gap.
- *Illustrations:* The diagram is from an Indian source and may not match the terminology or context of a Sindh classroom.
- *Inclusivity:* Not enough information to judge, but the lack of local context is a concern.

Summary: the chapter is *weak* on activities, *adequate* on accuracy (pending a full check) and *weak* on language and illustrations. A teacher using this textbook would need to add an activity (such as testing foods for starch using iodine solution), define terms in Urdu and replace the diagram.

> **Common misconception:** "The textbook is the curriculum." (This misconception appeared in Topic 2.1, but it matters most here.) If you believe the textbook *is* the curriculum, you will never evaluate it - you will just follow it page by page. Once you understand that the curriculum stands above the textbook, you can judge the textbook against the curriculum's SLOs and fill its gaps.

## Activity: Evaluate a chapter together

Work in pairs. About 25 minutes.

1. Each pair receives one chapter from a Sindh General Science textbook (Grade 5, 6 or 7).
2. List the NCP SLOs the chapter should cover (use the progression grid).
3. Score the chapter on each of the six criteria: strong (the criterion is fully met), adequate (partly met) or weak (not met). Write one sentence of evidence for each score.
4. Identify the weakest criterion and suggest one specific improvement.
5. Share your evaluation with another pair and compare.

## Check your understanding

1. Name the six criteria for evaluating a science textbook. *(Remember)*
2. Explain why "curriculum alignment" is important. In two sentences, say what happens when a textbook does not cover the NCP SLOs. *(Understand)*
3. A textbook chapter on magnets asks pupils to "observe the behaviour of iron filings near a magnet," but the school has no magnets or iron filings. Which criterion is weakest, and what would you do as a teacher? *(Apply)*

## Summary

A science textbook is a resource, not the curriculum. Teachers should evaluate textbooks using six criteria: accuracy, curriculum alignment, language, activities, illustrations and inclusivity. Applying these criteria systematically helps a teacher find the gaps in a textbook and decide what to add or change. The worked example shows that even a widely used textbook can be weak on activities, language or relevance.

## Self-assessment checklist

- [ ] I can name and explain the six criteria for evaluating a science textbook.
- [ ] I can apply the six criteria to score a textbook chapter.
- [ ] I can identify the weakest criterion in a chapter and suggest a specific improvement.
- [ ] I can explain why a textbook should be judged against the NCP SLOs, not taken at face value.

## Try this at your practicum school

Choose one chapter from the science textbook used at your practicum school. Score it on each of the six criteria (strong, adequate or weak) and write one sentence of evidence for each. Identify the weakest criterion and suggest one change. Bring your evaluation to the next seminar.

## Summative task

Your head teacher asks you to evaluate one chapter of a Sindh Grade 6 General Science textbook against the six-criterion checklist in figure fig-U2-7 and the matching NCP standard. Write 200-250 words. Score each criterion with evidence, judge the chapter overall, and suggest two improvements. At least one improvement should address a gap between the textbook and the NCP SLOs.

### Mini-rubric

| Criterion | Developing (1) | Secure (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application of criteria | Names criteria without scoring them | Scores each criterion with a reason | Scores each criterion with specific evidence from the chapter |
| Overall judgement *(Analyze)* | States an opinion with no reasons | Judges the chapter with reasons from the criteria | Weighs the evidence across criteria and reaches a balanced judgement |
| Suggested improvements | Vague or unrelated | Two relevant improvements | Two specific improvements, at least one tied to a gap between the textbook and the NCP SLOs |
