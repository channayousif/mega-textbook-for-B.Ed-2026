# Unit 1: Philosophy of Education - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

Education has a narrow meaning, deliberate teaching in schools, and a broad meaning, every experience that shapes a person's mind, character or body. Schooling is one part of education, not all of it. Western thinkers such as Pestalozzi and Dewey, and Muslim scholars with the terms *tarbiyah*, *ta'lim* and *ta'dib*, define education differently, but most agree that it is growth in a good direction, and the Muslim view insists that knowledge must show in conduct.

Philosophy is the reasoned search for wisdom. Its three branches ask what is real (metaphysics), what is true and how we know (epistemology), and what is good (axiology, with ethics and aesthetics). Every teaching decision rests on answers to these questions, whether the teacher notices or not.

A philosophy reaches the classroom through a chain: a view of reality, knowledge and values shapes the aims, which shape the curriculum, the method and the roles of teacher and pupil. Classroom results then test the philosophy. Education covers formal, non-formal and informal learning across a whole life, and philosophy of education asks what education is for, what to teach, how and by whom. Education has individual, social, vocational, moral and spiritual aims, which compete and must be balanced. Examinations check progress towards the aims; they are not the aim.

## Summative assessment

Answer every question before you read the final section of this page.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* The Latin verb *educere*, one of the roots of "education", means:
   - A) to bring up or rear
   - B) to lead out or bring out
   - C) to teach with the pen
   - D) to test and examine
2. *(Understand)* Which of these is education in its **narrow** meaning?
   - A) A girl learns to bargain by helping in her family's shop
   - B) A boy learns patience by fasting in Ramzan
   - C) Class 7 pupils follow a science syllabus taught by a trained teacher
   - D) A child learns to swim in a canal from her cousins
3. *(Remember)* Which Arabic term for education stresses knowledge that shows itself in right conduct?
   - A) *Tarbiyah*
   - B) *Ta'lim*
   - C) *Ta'dib*
   - D) *Iqra*
4. *(Remember)* Which branch of philosophy asks "What is true, and how do we know?"
   - A) Metaphysics
   - B) Epistemology
   - C) Aesthetics
   - D) Ethics
5. *(Apply)* A pupil admits that he broke a window. His teacher wonders whether to reward his honesty or punish the damage. Which branch of philosophy is the teacher's question mainly about?
   - A) Metaphysics
   - B) Epistemology
   - C) Axiology
   - D) Logic
6. *(Remember)* In the chain from a philosophy to the classroom, what comes directly after "a view of reality, knowledge and values"?
   - A) Curriculum
   - B) Aims
   - C) Method
   - D) Roles of teacher and pupil
7. *(Apply)* A teacher believes that knowledge is a fixed set of correct statements held by the textbook. Which method is she most likely to use?
   - A) Pupils test ideas with simple experiments
   - B) Dictation followed by memorising
   - C) Group inquiry into a local problem
   - D) A field visit to observe and record
8. *(Understand)* Which of these is an example of **non-formal** education?
   - A) A matric class in a government high school
   - B) An adult literacy centre with flexible evening timings
   - C) A mother teaching her daughter to cook at home
   - D) A B.Ed programme at a university
9. *(Apply)* A policy aims "to enable an individual to earn his/her livelihood honestly through skills". Which aim of education does this express most directly?
   - A) Individual
   - B) Social
   - C) Vocational
   - D) Spiritual
10. *(Apply)* A school spends the whole of Class 10 drilling past papers. Games, art and class discussions about right and wrong are dropped. Which statement best describes the problem?
    - A) The school serves the spiritual aim too strongly
    - B) The school treats a tool for checking progress as the aim of education
    - C) The school follows the broad meaning of education
    - D) The school balances all five aims of education

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the two pictures of education suggested by the Latin roots *educare* and *educere*.
2. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain why "education means schooling" is a misconception. Include one example from Sindh.
3. *(Understand)* State one thing that Pestalozzi's definition of education stresses and one thing that Curren's definition stresses. Use one sentence for each.
4. *(Apply)* A teacher is planning a Class 5 lesson on honesty. Write one question she might ask herself for each of the three branches of philosophy.
5. *(Analyze)* Test this definition with the three questions (who is educated, who or what educates, towards what): "Education is the transfer of the textbook from the teacher to the pupil." Say which question it fails most badly, and why. Use no more than five sentences.
6. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain how the relationship between philosophy and education runs in both directions.
7. *(Understand)* List the three settings of education (formal, non-formal and informal), with one Pakistani example of each.
8. *(Understand)* Give two practical reasons why philosophy matters to a classroom teacher. Explain each in one sentence.
9. *(Apply)* Write one aim and one objective for a Class 4 lesson on cleanliness. Then explain in one sentence how the objective serves the aim.
10. *(Analyze)* A school rule says pupils must sit silently in rows and speak only when the teacher asks a question. In four sentences, analyse what this rule assumes about knowledge and what it assumes about pupils.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Evaluate)* Suppose a district report counts a person as "educated" only if they have passed Class 5. In 300-400 words, evaluate this way of counting. Use the narrow and broad meanings of education and the terms *tarbiyah*, *ta'lim* and *ta'dib* to show what the count captures and what it misses. End by proposing a better way to describe how educated a community is, and justify it.
2. *(Evaluate)* A teacher says, "I have no philosophy. I just teach." In 300-400 words, evaluate this claim. For each of the three branches of philosophy, describe one decision this teacher must make in an ordinary week and the philosophical answer hidden inside it. Reach a reasoned judgement about the claim.
3. *(Analyze)* Two Class 5 teachers in Nawabshah teach fractions. Teacher A asks pupils to learn the rules for adding fractions by heart and drills them with board exercises. Teacher B cuts chapatis into halves and quarters, asks pupils to share them fairly, and then builds the rules from what they found. In 300-400 words, trace each lesson back through the chain from philosophy to classroom (view of knowledge and of the pupil, aim, curriculum, method, roles). Then analyse which aims of education each lesson serves well and which it serves poorly.
4. *(Analyze)* Read three statements by Pakistani policy-makers.
   - **Statement A** (Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah, message to the All Pakistan Educational Conference, 27 November 1947): there is an "immediate and urgent need" to train people in scientific and technical education "in order to build up our economic life".
   - **Statement B** (National Education Policy 2009, aim 4): "To promote national cohesion by respecting all faiths and religions and recognise cultural and ethnic diversity."
   - **Statement C** (National Education Policy 2009, aim 8): "To aim at nurturing the total personality of the individual: dynamic, creative and capable of facing the truth as it emerges from the objective study of reality."

   In 300-400 words, identify the aim of education each statement implies. For each, quote the key words that show the aim and say what the statement leaves out. Then analyse which of the five aims is least represented across all three statements, and suggest why a policy might give it less space.
5. *(Create)* Write your own short philosophy of education for the classroom you hope to teach in (250-300 words). It must include: your definition of education; your view of how pupils come to know; how you will balance at least three aims of education; and one teaching method that follows from these. Then, in 100-150 words, check your statement against one misconception from this unit and explain how your statement avoids it.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - *educere* means to lead out or bring out; *educare* (A) means to bring up or rear.
2. C - the narrow meaning is deliberate teaching in an institution; A, B and D are education only in the broad meaning.
3. C - *ta'dib* is training in *adab*, knowledge shown in good action; *tarbiyah* stresses nurturing growth and *ta'lim* stresses passing on knowledge.
4. B - epistemology studies the nature and extent of human knowledge; metaphysics asks what is real.
5. C - weighing honesty against damage is a question of value and right conduct, which is axiology (ethics).
6. B - the view of reality, knowledge and values leads first to aims; curriculum, method and roles follow from the aims.
7. B - if knowledge is a fixed set of statements held by authority, the natural method is to transmit and memorise it; A, C and D assume knowledge is built from evidence.
8. B - an adult literacy centre is organised but flexible, which is non-formal; A and D are formal, C is informal.
9. C - earning a livelihood through skills is the vocational aim.
10. B - examinations check progress towards aims; drilling past papers at the cost of other aims makes the tool the aim.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: *Educare* (to bring up or rear) suggests the teacher shapes and fills the child, as a gardener shapes a plant. *Educere* (to lead out) suggests the teacher draws out abilities already inside the child. **Marks (2):** 1 for each picture correctly linked to its root.
2. Model: Schooling is deliberate teaching in an institution, but education also includes everything that shapes a person's mind, character and skills. A person can attend school for years and learn little, or leave early and keep learning. For example, a girl in Tando Allahyar who left school after Class 5 but keeps shop accounts and reads the Qur'an with meaning is still being educated. **Marks (3):** 1 for defining schooling as only one part; 1 for the reason; 1 for a fitting Sindh example.
3. Model: Pestalozzi stresses the natural, harmonious development of powers the child already has. Curren stresses that education promotes *desirable* learning and development, so it has a direction we judge to be good. **Marks (2):** 1 for each accurate point.
4. Model: Metaphysics: "Is a child born good, or must goodness be taught?" Epistemology: "How will pupils come to know that honesty matters: from a rule, a story or their own experience?" Axiology: "Is honesty always more important than loyalty to a friend?" **Marks (3):** 1 for each question that genuinely belongs to its branch.
5. Model: Who is educated? Only pupils in classrooms, which leaves out adults and children out of school. Who educates? Only the teacher, which leaves out family, community and the pupil's own effort. Towards what? Only having the textbook's content, with no purpose beyond it. It fails "towards what" most badly, because it gives education no aim: a pupil could hold the whole textbook and still not understand, act well or use it in life. **Marks (4):** 1 for each of the three questions applied; 1 for a reasoned choice of the worst failure (accept another question if the reason is sound).
6. Model: Philosophy guides education, because a view of knowledge and values shapes aims, curriculum and method. Education also tests philosophy, because classroom results show whether the view works. Without education philosophy stays in books, and without philosophy teaching becomes routine. **Marks (3):** 1 for each direction; 1 for the consequence.
7. Model: Formal: a government high school with a syllabus and board examination. Non-formal: an adult literacy centre or a non-formal basic school. Informal: a child learning manners from grandparents at home. **Marks (3):** 1 for each setting with a fitting example.
8. Model: Philosophy makes a teacher's aims clear, so lessons are planned with purpose. It gives questions for judging a new method or policy before adopting it. (Also accept: it brings consistency; it helps weigh clashing values.) **Marks (2):** 1 for each reason explained.
9. Model: Aim: to develop healthy habits that pupils carry into family and community life. Objective: by the end of the lesson, pupils will be able to show the five steps of handwashing. The objective builds one concrete habit that moves pupils towards the broad aim of healthy living. **Marks (3):** 1 for a broad aim; 1 for a specific, checkable objective; 1 for the link.
10. Model: The rule assumes that knowledge comes from the teacher and is passed to pupils by listening. It assumes that talk among pupils is a distraction rather than a way to learn. It treats pupils as receivers rather than active thinkers. It also assumes that order is a value worth more than discussion. **Marks (4):** 2 for the assumption about knowledge with a reason; 2 for the assumption about pupils with a reason.

### ERQ rubrics

1. **ERQ 1 (10 marks).** *Use of the two meanings* (0-3): 3 shows that the count measures only the narrow meaning and explains what the broad meaning adds; 1-2 names the meanings without applying them. *Use of the Muslim terms* (0-2): 2 shows which of *tarbiyah*, *ta'lim* and *ta'dib* the count could and could not capture. *Evaluation* (0-3, **Evaluate**): 3 reaches a balanced judgement, noting what the count is good for (simple, comparable) as well as what it misses, with reasons; 1-2 only criticises; 0 states an opinion. *Better indicator* (0-2): 2 proposes a practical alternative and justifies it.
2. **ERQ 2 (10 marks).** *Metaphysics* (0-2), *Epistemology* (0-2), *Axiology* (0-2): for each, 2 describes a real weekly decision and the hidden answer to the branch's question; 1 names the branch with a weak or unrelated example. *Judgement* (0-4, **Evaluate**): 4 concludes, with reasons drawn from the three examples, that the teacher does follow a philosophy, unexamined; 2-3 reaches the conclusion with partial reasoning; 1 asserts it without reasons.
3. **ERQ 3 (10 marks).** *Tracing Teacher A* (0-3) and *tracing Teacher B* (0-3): 3 covers view of knowledge and pupil, aim, curriculum, method and roles accurately; 1-2 covers some steps. *Analysis of aims* (0-4, **Analyze**): 4 identifies, with evidence from each lesson, which aims each serves well and poorly (for example, B serves the individual aim and links to fair sharing, A may secure exam marks but neglects understanding); 2-3 identifies aims without evidence; 1 lists aims with no link to the lessons.
4. **ERQ 4 (12 marks; the unit blueprint's Analyze item).** *Aim identified* (0-3): 1 per statement - A vocational (with a social element: building the nation's economy), B social, C individual. *Key words* (0-3): 1 per statement for quoting the words that show the aim, such as "technical education" and "economic life"; "national cohesion" and "respecting all faiths"; "total personality of the individual". *What is left out* (0-3): 1 per statement for a fair note of what the statement does not address, without claiming the speaker rejected it. *Cross-statement analysis* (0-3, **Analyze**): 3 identifies the moral or spiritual aim as least represented across the three statements, with evidence, and offers a reasoned explanation (for example, these statements address the economy and unity; the 2009 policy treats moral and spiritual aims in other aims and in its chapter on Islamic education); 1-2 names an aim without evidence or reasoning.
5. **ERQ 5 (12 marks).** *Definition* (0-2): clear and consistent with the unit's meanings. *View of knowledge* (0-2): states how pupils come to know, linked to epistemology. *Balance of aims* (0-3): names at least three aims and explains how they will be balanced, not just listed. *Method* (0-2): a method that follows logically from the definition, view and aims. *Self-check* (0-3, **Evaluate**): 3 tests the statement honestly against a named misconception and shows with reasons how it avoids it, or revises it; 1-2 names the misconception without a real test.
