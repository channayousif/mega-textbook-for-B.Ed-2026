# Philosophy of Education

"Is my sister educated?" "Why do we need philosophy if we only teach the syllabus?" "What is school for, if not the exam?" Teachers in Pakistan hear questions like these from pupils, parents and colleagues. This unit gives you the ideas to answer them with reasons. It also opens the whole course: every later unit, from Plato to Pakistan's education policies, builds on the meanings and aims you meet here.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain what this course covers and why a teacher needs its five foundations. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-1-1)*
- Define education in its narrow and broad meanings, and compare Western and Muslim definitions. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-1-1)*
- Define philosophy and link each of its three branches to a decision a teacher makes. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-409-1-1)*
- Trace how a philosophy reaches the classroom through aims, curriculum, method and roles. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-409-1-2)*
- Describe the scope and importance of education and of philosophy of education. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-1-2)*
- Analyse which aims of education a policy statement implies, and how the aims compete. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-409-1-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

EFMP-302 Teaching Profession (Semester 1) is useful background, especially its discussion of what makes teaching a profession. No study of philosophy is needed. Bring your own memories of school: the teachers you learned from, the rules you followed and the examinations you sat. This unit asks you to look at them again.

## In this unit

1. [What we mean by education and by philosophy](./topic-01) - an introduction to the course, the meanings and definitions of education, and the meaning and branches of philosophy.
2. [How philosophy shapes education and its aims](./topic-02) - the relationship between education and philosophy, the scope and importance of both, and the aims of education.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each one begins with a real school situation, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before asking you to try the same move yourself. Keep a notebook for the questions you collect at your practicum school; both topics use them.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
