# Education Reports and Policies

Pakistan has produced education reports and policies since 1947. Each one promises universal schooling, better teachers, a fairer system or a modern curriculum. Few of those promises have been kept in full, and many have been repeated by the next policy with new dates. This unit asks why. You will learn what a report is and what a policy is, how each is written and launched, and what stands between a promise on paper and a change in a classroom. You will study three documents closely: the Sharif Commission report of 1959, the National Education Policy of 1979 and the National Education Policy 1998-2010. By the end you should be able to read a policy target, ask what it would take to meet it, and judge whether a new policy is really the answer.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Distinguish an education report from an education policy, and give an example of each. *(Remember; SLO:EFMP-409-5-1)*
- Explain how reports and policies are framed, launched and applied in Pakistan. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-5-1)*
- Identify the main factors that hinder the application of reports and policies. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-5-2)*
- Summarise the main points of the Sharif Commission (1959) and of the National Education Policy 1979. *(Remember; SLO:EFMP-409-5-2)*
- Summarise the main points of the National Education Policy 1998-2010 and analyse its strengths and weaknesses. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-5-3)*
- Judge whether Pakistan needs a new education policy, and defend your judgement with evidence. *(Evaluate; SLO:EFMP-409-5-3)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

EFMP-303 Educational Policies and Plans of Pakistan (Semester 2) is the direct preparation: it describes the sequence of Pakistan's education plans and policies in detail. This unit keeps the foundations angle and links to EFMP-303 for that detail rather than repeating it. Unit 2 of this course taught you the historical foundation of education, which helps you see why a policy promise can be older than the government that makes it. Bring your own school experience with you: every promise in this unit has a classroom consequence, and you have seen some of them.

## In this unit

1. [Reports and policies: how they are made and applied](./topic-01) - the difference between a report and a policy, how each is framed, and how each is launched and applied.
2. [Why policies falter; the Sharif Commission and the 1979 policy](./topic-02) - the factors that hinder application, the Sharif Commission of 1959 and a critical review of the 1979 policy.
3. [The 1998-2010 policy and the case for a new one](./topic-03) - the main points of the 1998-2010 policy, an analysis of its record, and the argument over a new policy.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Each opens with a real school situation, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before asking you to make the same move yourself. Keep a notebook for the promises you collect from policies and from your practicum school; the third topic asks you to compare them.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish all three topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions, with answers and marking guidance at the end of that page.
