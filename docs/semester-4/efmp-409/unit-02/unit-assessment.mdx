# Unit 2: The Foundations of Education - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

Education has five foundations, and each asks a different question. The philosophical foundation asks what is real, true and good, and demands reasons. The ideological foundation asks what kind of people a nation wants to raise. The sociological foundation asks how society and schooling shape each other. The psychological foundation asks how learners grow and learn. The historical foundation asks how education developed and why the past still shapes the present.

The philosophical and ideological foundations differ in source, question and classroom role. Philosophy asks what is true and good and is open to question; an ideology is a shared commitment about who a group is and what it should become. The sociological foundation offers three main lenses: functionalism, which sees schools holding a society together; conflict theory, which sees schools reproducing inequality; and interactionism, which sees a pupil's identity made in everyday classroom talk. The psychological foundation offers four: behaviourism, constructivism, social cognitivism and humanism. The historical foundation explains present arrangements, such as the medium of instruction, as the results of past decisions.

An ideology is not the same as a religion. An ideology is a system of shared ideas about the life a group wants; a religion is a tradition of belief and practice oriented toward the sacred. In Pakistan they overlap. The Ideology of Pakistan draws on the Two-Nation Theory, the Lahore Resolution of 1940 and the Objectives Resolution of 1949, and it appears in national aims, in Article 25A and in what a school expects a pupil to become. The Islamic foundation asks what education means in the light of Islam: knowledge sought as a duty, character formed by conduct, and the equal worth of all. A school assembly can carry both foundations at once.

## Summative assessment

Answer every question before you read the final section of this page.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Which foundation of education asks how education has developed over time and why the past still shapes the present?
   - A) The philosophical foundation
   - B) The sociological foundation
   - C) The psychological foundation
   - D) The historical foundation
2. *(Remember)* Which sociological perspective holds that schools pass on shared culture and sort pupils into the roles society needs?
   - A) The conflict perspective
   - B) The functionalist perspective
   - C) The interactionist perspective
   - D) The humanist perspective
3. *(Understand)* Which psychological perspective treats learning as a change in observable behaviour shaped by reinforcement?
   - A) The constructivist perspective
   - B) The humanist perspective
   - C) The behaviourist perspective
   - D) The social cognitivist perspective
4. *(Apply)* A teacher ticks each correct sum and writes "Well done!" beside it. Which psychological perspective most directly explains why the pupils repeat the method?
   - A) The behaviourist perspective
   - B) The constructivist perspective
   - C) The humanist perspective
   - D) The social cognitivist perspective
5. *(Apply)* A government primary school in Tharparkar has one teacher for four grades and no science equipment, while a private school in Karachi has laboratories and trained teachers. Which sociological perspective best explains this difference?
   - A) The functionalist perspective
   - B) The interactionist perspective
   - C) The conflict perspective
   - D) The humanist perspective
6. *(Remember)* Which document, adopted in 1949 and now part of the Constitution as Article 2A, sets out the principles on which Pakistan's Constitution is framed?
   - A) The Lahore Resolution
   - B) The Objectives Resolution
   - C) The National Education Policy
   - D) The Two-Nation Theory
7. *(Understand)* Which statement best describes the difference between an ideology and a religion?
   - A) An ideology is always religious, and a religion is always political
   - B) An ideology is a system of shared ideas about the life a group wants, while a religion is a tradition of belief and practice oriented toward the sacred
   - C) An ideology is a personal opinion, while a religion is a public institution
   - D) An ideology has scripture, while a religion does not
8. *(Apply)* A school's mission says it will "raise loyal citizens who serve the nation and respect all faiths". Which foundation does this serve most directly?
   - A) The psychological foundation
   - B) The historical foundation
   - C) The ideological foundation
   - D) The philosophical foundation
9. *(Remember)* In the Islamic view used in this unit, which Arabic term for education stresses knowledge that shows itself in right conduct?
   - A) *Tarbiyah*
   - B) *Ta'lim*
   - C) *Ta'dib*
   - D) *Iqra*
10. *(Apply)* A school begins the day with a recitation from the Qur'an and then the national anthem. Which answer best distinguishes the two?
    - A) Both belong to the ideological foundation
    - B) Both belong to the Islamic foundation
    - C) The recitation belongs to the Islamic foundation, and the anthem to the ideological foundation
    - D) The anthem belongs to the Islamic foundation, and the recitation to the ideological foundation

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the difference between the philosophical foundation and the ideological foundation of education.
2. *(Understand)* List the three sociological perspectives named in this unit and give one sentence on what each says about schooling.
3. *(Understand)* State two ways education affects society, and give one Pakistani example of one of them.
4. *(Analyze)* A teacher says, "I do not need psychology; I teach my subject." Analyse which psychological perspective is already hidden in that claim, and what that perspective cannot explain about a real classroom.
5. *(Analyze)* A government school teaches in the regional language up to Class 5 and then switches to English as the medium. Analyse this arrangement through the historical foundation, using one historical reason.
6. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain why ideology and religion are not the same thing.
7. *(Understand)* Name two founding texts that express the Ideology of Pakistan, and give one idea from each.
8. *(Understand)* Explain in two sentences what the Islamic foundation asks of education.
9. *(Analyze)* A school rule requires all pupils to stand for the national anthem, and it also allows each faith's festival to be celebrated in class. Analyse how this rule combines the ideological and Islamic foundations.
10. *(Analyze)* Analyse one risk of treating the Ideology of Pakistan as identical with Islam in a school curriculum.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Analyze)* A Class 5 teacher in Larkana praises only the three highest scorers each week, gives extra practice to pupils she calls "weak", and teaches mostly by dictation from the textbook. In 300-400 words, analyse this practice through two of the three sociological perspectives, and argue which one explains it better, using evidence from the description.
2. *(Evaluate)* A trainee says, "Educational psychology is only useful for pupils with difficulties." In 300-400 words, evaluate this claim. Use the four psychological perspectives to show what each would say about an ordinary Class 4 lesson, and reach a reasoned judgement about the claim.
3. *(Analyze)* Compare the Objectives Resolution (1949) and the Constituent Assembly address of 11 August 1947. In 300-400 words, analyse what each text asks of citizens and of the state, and explain what each implies for a school.
4. *(Evaluate)* A classroom includes Muslim and non-Muslim pupils. In 300-400 words, evaluate whether the Ideology of Pakistan or the Islamic foundation should decide what the school teaches about values. Argue one position and defend it against the strongest objection you can think of.
5. *(Analyze)* A Pakistani school has ten features: its timetable, its uniform rule, its morning assembly, its examination system, its language of instruction, its wall poster of a national leader, its Islamiyat lesson, its science laboratory, its parent-teacher meeting, and its annual sports day. Sort the ten features by the foundation each one reflects (philosophical, ideological, sociological, psychological or historical). Then choose two features and explain, in 250-350 words, why the foundation you assigned to each is the best fit and what another foundation would miss.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. D - the historical foundation asks how education developed and why the past still shapes the present; the others ask different questions.
2. B - functionalism sees schools socialising pupils, transmitting culture, keeping order and placing people into roles.
3. C - behaviourism studies changes in observable behaviour shaped by reinforcement.
4. A - praise and ticks reinforce the method, which is the behaviourist account of why the pupils repeat it.
5. C - conflict theory explains how schools reproduce inequality by class, region and resources.
6. B - the Objectives Resolution was adopted in 1949 and inserted into the Constitution as Article 2A.
7. B - an ideology is a system of shared ideas about the life a group wants; a religion is a tradition of belief and practice oriented toward the sacred.
8. C - "loyal citizens who serve the nation" is a statement about the kind of people the nation wants to raise, which is the ideological foundation.
9. C - *ta'dib* is training in *adab*, knowledge shown in good conduct; *tarbiyah* stresses nurturing growth and *ta'lim* passing on knowledge.
10. C - the recitation is worship and belongs to the Islamic foundation; the anthem speaks about the nation and belongs to the ideological foundation.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: The philosophical foundation asks what is real, true and good and demands reasons. The ideological foundation asks what kind of people a nation wants to raise and is held as a shared commitment. **Marks (2):** 1 for each accurate difference in question or role.
2. Model: Functionalist: schools socialise pupils, transmit culture, keep order and place people into roles. Conflict: schools reproduce inequality by class, gender, language and region. Interactionist: labels and expectations in everyday classroom talk shape who a pupil becomes. **Marks (3):** 1 for each perspective with an accurate statement of what it says about schooling.
3. Model: Education raises literacy, improves health, delays marriage, opens work and builds civic participation (any two). Example: a girl who completes Class 8 near Sukkur is more likely to delay marriage and to read health information to her family. **Marks (3):** 1 for each of two ways; 1 for a fitting Pakistani example.
4. Model: The claim hides a behaviourist or transmission view of learning: the teacher delivers the subject and the pupil receives it, so only a breakdown needs psychology. It cannot explain how pupils build understanding, how observation and self-efficacy shape effort, or how a pupil's needs and feelings affect learning. **Marks (4):** 2 for naming the hidden view (transmission or behaviourist) with a reason; 2 for explaining what it misses, with a concrete classroom consequence.
5. Model: The arrangement has a colonial and post-colonial history. English became the language of government and employment under British rule, and later policies kept English as an official language and a medium in many schools, so a switch at Class 6 continues a long-standing pattern rather than a natural fact. **Marks (4):** 2 for a correct historical reason (colonial employment and administration, or later policy); 2 for explaining how the past shapes the present arrangement.
6. Model: An ideology is a system of shared ideas about the life a group wants and is held as a commitment. A religion is a tradition of belief and practice oriented toward the sacred, with scripture and worship. They differ in source, scope and role, though they can overlap. **Marks (2):** 1 for each accurate difference.
7. Model: The Lahore Resolution (1940) demanded autonomous Muslim-majority states; the Objectives Resolution (1949) set out the principles of the new constitution, including sovereignty belonging to Allah and the principles of democracy, freedom, equality, tolerance and social justice as enunciated by Islam. (Also accept the Two-Nation Theory: Muslims were a separate nation.) **Marks (3):** 1 for each text named accurately; 1 for a correct idea from either.
8. Model: The Islamic foundation asks that knowledge be sought as a duty and that it shape conduct. It nurtures the whole person (*tarbiyah*), passes on knowledge (*ta'lim*) and forms right conduct (*ta'dib*), and it values the equal worth of all learners. **Marks (2):** 1 for knowledge as a duty or *ilm*; 1 for conduct or the three terms.
9. Model: Standing for the anthem serves the ideological foundation: it builds a shared national identity and equal citizenship. Allowing each faith's festival to be celebrated serves the Islamic foundation and the constitutional promise to minorities: it protects worship and religious practice. The rule combines a national commitment with protection for religion. **Marks (4):** 2 for the ideological side with a reason; 2 for the Islamic or minority-protection side with a reason.
10. Model: If the two are treated as identical, a teacher may present a political vision as a religious duty, and pupils may not learn to tell the difference. It can also make minority pupils feel that the school's religious language is not theirs, and it weakens the critical study of either foundation. **Marks (4):** 2 for a clearly stated risk; 2 for explaining its consequence for teaching or for pupils.

### ERQ rubrics

1. **ERQ 1 (12 marks).** *Functionalist analysis* (0-3): 3 shows how praise for the top three and dictation serve order, placement and shared standards, with evidence from the description; 1-2 names the perspective with a weak link. *Conflict analysis* (0-3): 3 shows how labelling some pupils "weak" and rewarding the already-advantaged reproduces inequality, with evidence; 1-2 names the perspective only. *Interactionist analysis* (0-3): 3 shows how the teacher's expectations and labels shape the pupils' identity and participation, with evidence; 1-2 names it only. *Judgement* (0-3, **Analyze**): 3 argues, with reasons drawn from the description, which perspective explains the practice best and what the chosen one misses; 1-2 states a preference without reasons.
2. **ERQ 2 (12 marks).** *Behaviourist* (0-2), *constructivist* (0-2), *social cognitivist* (0-2), *humanist* (0-2): for each, 2 shows what that perspective would say about an ordinary Class 4 lesson, with a fitting example; 1 names the perspective with a thin example. *Judgement* (0-4, **Evaluate**): 4 concludes, with reasons drawn from the four, that the claim is wrong because psychology explains how every pupil learns, not only those with difficulties; 2-3 reaches the conclusion with partial reasoning; 1 asserts it.
3. **ERQ 3 (12 marks).** *Objectives Resolution* (0-3): 3 identifies what it asks of the state and of citizens (sovereignty belonging to Allah, principles of democracy, freedom, equality, tolerance and social justice as enunciated by Islam; minorities protected); 1-2 gives a partial account. *Jinnah's address* (0-3): 3 identifies equal citizenship and freedom of worship, with a short accurate quotation; 1-2 gives a partial account. *Comparison* (0-3, **Analyze**): 3 shows how the two texts differ in what they ask of citizens and of the state, with reasons; 1-2 lists differences without analysis. *Implication for a school* (0-3): 3 draws a reasoned implication for a school from both texts; 1-2 states an aim without linking it to the texts.
4. **ERQ 4 (12 marks).** *Position* (0-3): 3 states a clear position and defines the foundation it rests on; 1-2 states a position vaguely. *Argument* (0-4): 4 builds a reasoned case with at least two supporting reasons; 2-3 gives one reason; 1 asserts. *Strongest objection* (0-3, **Evaluate**): 3 states the strongest opposing argument fairly and answers it with a reason; 1-2 names an objection without answering it. *Conclusion* (0-2): a conclusion that follows from the argument.
5. **ERQ 5 (12 marks; the unit blueprint's Analyze item).** *Sorting* (0-4): 4 sorts all ten features into defensible foundations, with at most one or two arguable choices; 2-3 sorts most features but with several unsupported choices; 1 sorts a few only. *Explanation of two features* (0-5, **Analyze**): 5 chooses two features, names the best-fit foundation for each with a reason, and says what another foundation would miss; 2-4 names foundations with partial reasons; 1 labels without reasons. *Use of the unit's vocabulary* (0-3): 3 uses the foundations accurately and consistently; 1-2 uses them loosely.
