# The Foundations of Education

<Figure id="fig-U2-5" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efmp-409/unit-02/fig-U2-5.webp" alt="A head teacher and a B.Ed trainee talk in a Sindh school courtyard while pupils gather in rows and small groups behind them, with a playing field and mango tree in the morning light." width={1376} height={768} />

A school looks simple from the gate: classrooms, a timetable, a bell, examination papers. Ask why it is arranged the way it is, and the simple view falls apart. Why is the morning assembly held? Why are some subjects given more periods? Why is the answer to almost every question "because that is how schools are"? This unit gives you five pairs of eyes for looking at a school. Each comes from a foundation discipline and asks a different question about the same building. By the end you will be able to name the foundation behind a rule, a timetable or a wall poster, and to tell the difference between an ideology and a religion.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Name the five foundations of education and the question each one asks. *(Remember; SLO:EFMP-409-2-1)*
- Compare the philosophical and the ideological foundations of education, and explain how each reaches a school. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-2-1)*
- Explain the functionalist, conflict and interactionist views of schooling, with a Pakistani example of each. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-2-1)*
- Explain the behaviourist, constructivist, social cognitivist and humanist perspectives on learning, and apply one to a described lesson. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-409-2-1)*
- Explain what the historical foundation asks, and trace how one present-day arrangement in Pakistan came about. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-2-1)*
- Distinguish ideology from religion on source, scope and role in education. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-2-2)*
- Explain how the Ideology of Pakistan and Islamic teachings appear in national aims and in a school. *(Apply; SLO:EFMP-409-2-2)*
- Analyse which foundation a described school feature reflects, and justify your answer. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-409-2-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 of this course is the direct preparation: it introduced the five foundations and taught you the meanings of education and philosophy, the three branches of philosophy, and the aims of education. EFMP-301 Educational Psychology (Semester 1) and GSOS-301 Social Science (Semester 2) are useful background, because Topic 2.1 revisits psychological and sociological ideas at a deeper level. Bring your memories of your own school: the assembly, the timetable, the rules and the prizes. This unit asks you to explain them.

## In this unit

1. [Philosophical, sociological, psychological and historical foundations](./topic-01) - the five foundations, the three sociological perspectives, the four psychological perspectives, and what the historical foundation asks.
2. [Ideology, religion and the Islamic foundations](./topic-02) - ideology versus religion, the Ideology of Pakistan, and the Islamic foundations of education.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Each opens with a real school situation, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before asking you to make the same move yourself. Keep a notebook for the school features you collect at your practicum school; both topics use them.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions, with answers and marking guidance at the end of that page.
