# Unit 2: The Foundations of Education - Teacher Notes

## Purpose and sequencing

This unit widens Unit 1. Unit 1 taught the meaning of education and philosophy; this unit asks what the foundation disciplines add, and then separates the ideological foundation from the religious one. Most student teachers arrive believing that sociology is about social problems, that psychology is for pupils with difficulties, and that ideology and religion are the same thing. The unit does not attack these beliefs head on. It lets student teachers apply each foundation to a school they recognise until the beliefs loosen.

The course guide gives two weeks of three hours each.

- **Week 3 (Topic 2.1).** Session 1: the five foundations and the philosophical and ideological foundations, with the morning-assembly worked example. Session 2: the three sociological perspectives and the "Five lenses on one school" activity. Session 3: the four psychological perspectives and the historical foundation, ending with the one-lesson-four-lenses worked example.
- **Week 4 (Topic 2.2).** Session 1: ideology versus religion, with the comparison table and the two founding texts. Session 2: the Ideology of Pakistan, from the Two-Nation Theory to Article 25A, using the diagram. Session 3: the Islamic foundation and the "Two texts, one school" activity. Return to the Week 3 school description at the end and let student teachers re-read it through the ideological and Islamic foundations.

## Teaching strategies from the course guide

- **Lecture.** Keep each segment to about 15 minutes, and end each with one question for pairs to discuss.
- **Discussion.** Use the classroom situations at the start of each topic as openers. Ask "What would you say?" before teaching the concept.
- **Question-answer.** Use the "Check your understanding" items orally at the start of the next session, with books closed.
- **Brainstorming.** Use it to surface everyday examples of each foundation, such as what a school does for society before naming functionalism.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"Sociology of education is only about social problems."** Ask the class what a school does that holds a country together, and let them name the functionalist answer themselves.
- **"Psychology matters only for pupils with difficulties."** Ask the class to explain why a lesson that "worked" for most pupils failed one. The four perspectives give four answers, and the class sees that psychology is needed for ordinary teaching.
- **"Ideology and religion are the same thing."** Put the Objectives Resolution and Jinnah's 1947 address side by side and ask which sentences speak about God and which about citizens. The distinction emerges from the texts.
- **"An ideology is just a slogan on a wall."** Ask the class to trace one line from the Objectives Resolution to a right in the present, such as Article 25A.

**One good discussion prompt:** "If a school could keep only one of the five foundations, which should it keep?" It forces the class to compare what each foundation explains.

## Practical work

The course guide lists four kinds of practical work. Suggested tasks:

- **Group work.** The "Five lenses on one school" and "Two texts, one school" activities.
- **Group assignment.** Each group collects ten features of a real school, sorts them by foundation, and presents the pattern, noting the features that two foundations both explain.
- **Individual assignment.** The Topic 2.1 or Topic 2.2 summative task.
- **Presentation.** Each student teacher presents, in three minutes, one school document and the foundation it speaks from, using one founding text as evidence.

Handle the Islamic material with care and accuracy: quote verses by reference, name the translation used, and invite student teachers to check the text themselves. Where a question touches a matter of religious interpretation, refer it to a qualified scholar rather than settling it in class.
