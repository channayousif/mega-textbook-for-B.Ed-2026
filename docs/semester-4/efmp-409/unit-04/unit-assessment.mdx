# Unit 4: Islamic Perspectives on Education - Unit Assessment

## Unit summary

The first revelation commands a person to read in the name of the Lord who created, and it names the pen as an instrument of teaching. The Qur'an calls believers to seek knowledge, to reflect on creation and to pray for more understanding. The Hadith honour the seeker of knowledge, join learning to teaching, and treat education as a lifelong duty for every Muslim, male and female. Together, the sources do not separate religious from worldly knowledge.

The Charter of Medina organised a plural community in 622 CE around shared rules, religious freedom, justice and mutual defence. It is called the foundation stone of education because those values are the foundation of a school as well as a state. The Last Sermon, delivered at Arafat in 632 CE, sums up the moral teaching of Islam in a public address: equality, the sanctity of life and property, the return of trusts, the rights of women, justice over custom, and holding to the Qur'an and the Sunnah.

As-Suffa was a shaded platform beside the Prophet's Mosque where the Ashab al-Suffa lived and learned. It is called the first institute of learning in Islam because it joined boarding, structured study of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, and preparation to teach others. Its method was a small circle of listening, memorising, questioning, example and practice. Its curriculum joined the sources, literacy and the duties of daily life. A present-day teacher cannot copy the model, but can transfer its principles.

## Summative assessment

Answer every question before you read the final section of this page.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* The first word of the first revelation, Surah Al-Alaq 96:1, is:
   - A) Pray
   - B) Read
   - C) Write
   - D) Teach
2. *(Remember)* Which instrument does Surah Al-Alaq 96:4 name as a means of teaching?
   - A) The pen
   - B) The board
   - C) The book
   - D) The tongue
3. *(Understand)* Which verse asks whether those who know and those who do not know are equal?
   - A) Surah Al-Alaq 96:1
   - B) Surah Az-Zumar 39:9
   - C) Surah Al-Baqarah 2:269
   - D) Surah Al-Ghashiyah 88:17
4. *(Remember)* "Seeking knowledge is obligatory upon every Muslim" is reported in:
   - A) Sahih al-Bukhari
   - B) Sahih Muslim
   - C) Sunan Ibn Majah
   - D) Sunan al-Tirmidhi
5. *(Understand)* The Charter of Medina was drawn up after which event?
   - A) The first revelation
   - B) The Hijrah
   - C) The Battle of Badr
   - D) The Last Sermon
6. *(Apply)* A school lets two communities of pupils keep their own festivals while sharing one school council. Which principle of the Charter does this follow most directly?
   - A) Freedom of conscience
   - B) Usury
   - C) Blood-revenge
   - D) Sanctuary for one group only
7. *(Remember)* The Last Sermon was delivered at:
   - A) Makkah
   - B) Medina
   - C) Arafat
   - D) Khaybar
8. *(Understand)* Who were the Ashab al-Suffa?
   - A) The teachers of Makkah
   - B) The learners of the platform beside the Prophet's Mosque
   - C) The rulers of Medina
   - D) The scribes of the Charter
9. *(Apply)* A Class 5 teacher splits forty pupils into circles of five, gives each circle one task, and asks faster pupils to help slower ones. Which feature of As-Suffa does this follow most directly?
   - A) Residential boarding
   - B) A small study circle
   - C) Writing official letters
   - D) Teaching only adults
10. *(Apply)* A pupil says, "Islam only values religious knowledge." Which response best answers that claim from this unit?
    - A) The first revelation commands reading, and the Qur'an asks people to reflect on creation
    - B) Religious knowledge is the only knowledge mentioned in the sources
    - C) The Charter of Medina forbids the study of nature
    - D) As-Suffa taught only for examinations

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* In two sentences, explain the educational idea in Surah Al-Alaq 96:4, "Who taught by the pen."
2. *(Understand)* Give two Qur'anic verses from this unit that call a learner to reflect, and say in one line what each asks.
3. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain why "Islam values only religious knowledge" is a misconception.
4. *(Apply)* Write one classroom activity that follows the Qur'anic call to reflect on creation, and say which verse it follows.
5. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain why the Charter of Medina is called the foundation stone of education.
6. *(Understand)* State two principles of the Last Sermon and say what each asks of a community.
7. *(Apply)* A school rule says pupils must return anything they borrow from the library. Which principle of the Last Sermon does this follow, and how would you explain it to Class 6?
8. *(Understand)* In three sentences, describe what As-Suffa was and who learned there.
9. *(Apply)* Describe one feature of the As-Suffa teaching method and one way to use it in a class of forty pupils.
10. *(Analyze)* Analyse which principle of As-Suffa would transfer best to a present-day Pakistani primary classroom and which would transfer worst, giving a reason for each.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Evaluate)* A pupil says, "The Qur'an separates religious knowledge from the study of the world, so a science lesson has nothing to do with faith." In 300-400 words, evaluate this claim. Use Surah Al-Alaq, at least one other verse from this unit and one reporting from the Hadith. Say what the claim gets right, what it misses, and reach a reasoned judgement.
2. *(Analyze)* In 300-400 words, analyse how two principles from the Charter of Medina and the Last Sermon could shape a school's treatment of pupils from different neighbourhoods or faiths. Name the principles, describe one school practice each would change, and justify your choices.
3. *(Analyze)* Compare the As-Suffa model with a present-day Pakistani primary classroom on method, curriculum and the teacher's role. In 300-400 words, explain which principle (for example, small study circles) would transfer and why, and which would not.
4. *(Evaluate)* In 300-400 words, evaluate which of the two documents in Topic 4.2, the Charter of Medina or the Last Sermon, gives a present-day teacher the more useful foundation for moral education. Use evidence from both and reach a reasoned judgement.
5. *(Create)* Design a one-page classroom charter for a Class 5 in Sindh that draws on two principles from this unit. In 300-400 words, write the charter, explain how you would introduce it in the first week, and describe how you would check after one month whether it is working.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - the first word of the first revelation is "Read" (*iqra*).
2. A - verse 96:4 names the pen as the instrument through which God teaches.
3. B - Surah Az-Zumar 39:9 asks whether those who know and those who do not know are equal.
4. C - the reporting is found in Sunan Ibn Majah (224).
5. B - the Charter was drawn up after the Hijrah of 622 CE.
6. A - letting each community keep its own practice while sharing one council follows freedom of conscience.
7. C - the Last Sermon was delivered at Arafat during the Farewell Pilgrimage.
8. B - the Ashab al-Suffa were the learners of the platform beside the Prophet's Mosque.
9. B - small circles with a task and peer help follow the study-circle feature of As-Suffa.
10. A - the first revelation commands reading and the Qur'an asks people to reflect on creation, so the claim is too narrow.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: The verse names the pen as the tool through which God teaches, so writing and recording carry knowledge across distance and time. A pupil who keeps notes is using the tool the verse names. **Marks (2):** 1 for naming writing or recording as the tool; 1 for the consequence, that knowledge is carried beyond the teacher.
2. Model: Surah Al-Ghashiyah 88:17-20 asks people to look at the camels, the sky and the mountains. Surah Al-Imran 3:190-191 says there are signs in the creation of the heavens and the earth for people of understanding. **Marks (2):** 1 for each verse with a fitting description of what it asks.
3. Model: The first revelation commands reading and points to creation, and the Qur'an asks people to reflect on the world, so the study of nature is invited rather than forbidden. The Hadith honour the seeker of knowledge without naming a subject. The early community sought literacy wherever it could find it. **Marks (3):** 1 for the first revelation; 1 for the Qur'anic call to reflect; 1 for the Hadith or the historical point.
4. Model: A Class 6 teacher takes pupils to look at a plant or a pond, asks them to write three questions, and then reads the textbook lesson. It follows Surah Al-Ghashiyah 88:17-20, which asks people to look at creation. **Marks (3):** 1 for a concrete activity; 1 for the link to the verse; 1 for the reason the activity follows from it.
5. Model: The Charter organised a plural community around shared rules, religious freedom, justice and mutual defence. Those values are also what a school needs, because a school is a small society with differences. **Marks (3):** 1 for naming two features of the Charter; 1 for the link to a school; 1 for the reason.
6. Model: Equality: the noblest are the most God-conscious, so no group has merit by birth. Sanctity of life and property: blood and property are sacred, so a community must keep its members safe and honest. **Marks (4):** 2 for each principle with what it asks.
7. Model: It follows the principle that trusts must be returned to their owners. A library book is a trust, and the borrower is answerable for it. Explain to Class 6 that a trust is anything given into your care, not only money. **Marks (3):** 1 for the principle; 1 for applying it to the book; 1 for a fitting explanation.
8. Model: As-Suffa was a shaded platform beside the Prophet's Mosque in Medina. The Ashab al-Suffa were companions who had left Makkah and had no family in the city. They lived there and learned the Qur'an and the Sunnah. **Marks (3):** 1 for the place; 1 for who they were; 1 for what they learned.
9. Model: The halaqah, a small circle where the teacher questions and the learners answer. In a class of forty, use circles of five with one task each and ask faster pupils to help slower ones. **Marks (3):** 1 for the feature; 1 for a concrete adaptation; 1 for the reason it works.
10. Model: Best transfer: small study circles, because they let a teacher see who is lost and let pupils teach each other, and a large class can be split into groups. Worst transfer: residential boarding with the teacher, because a present-day school cannot house its pupils and the law and norms do not allow it. **Marks (4):** 2 for the best transfer with a reason; 2 for the worst with a reason.

### ERQ rubrics

1. **ERQ 1 (10 marks).** *Use of Surah Al-Alaq* (0-3): 3 shows how the first revelation joins reading, creation and the pen; 1-2 names the verses without applying them. *Use of a second verse and a reporting* (0-3): 3 uses one of each accurately to test the claim. *Evaluation* (0-4, **Evaluate**): 4 reaches a balanced judgement, noting what the claim gets right (religious knowledge is central) and what it misses (the study of the world is also commanded), with reasons; 1-2 only criticises or only agrees; 0 states an opinion.
2. **ERQ 2 (10 marks).** *Two principles named* (0-3): 1 each for a principle correctly drawn from the Charter or the Sermon, up to 2, and 1 for a fair second. *School practice described* (0-3): 1 each for a concrete practice each principle would change. *Justification* (0-4, **Analyze**): 4 explains, with evidence from the documents, why each practice protects the value; 2-3 explains one side only; 1 lists practices without reasons.
3. **ERQ 3 (10 marks; the unit blueprint's Analyze item).** *Method* (0-2): 2 compares the halaqah with a present-day classroom method accurately. *Curriculum* (0-2): 2 compares the subjects and their purpose. *Teacher's role* (0-2): 2 compares the teacher as model and questioner with the present-day role. *Transfer* (0-4, **Analyze**): 4 names a principle that would transfer and one that would not, with reasons tied to a real classroom constraint; 2-3 names a principle with a weak reason; 1 asserts without reasons.
4. **ERQ 4 (10 marks).** *Use of the Charter* (0-2): 2 draws a principle or feature that fits moral education. *Use of the Sermon* (0-2): 2 draws a principle or feature that fits moral education. *Evaluation* (0-6, **Evaluate**): 6 reaches a reasoned judgement with evidence from both, and handles the strongest objection to the chosen document; 3-5 reaches a judgement with partial evidence; 1-2 asserts a preference.
5. **ERQ 5 (10 marks).** *Charter content* (0-3): 3 gives a clear, usable charter with at least two principles from the unit and language a Class 5 can understand. *Introduction* (0-3): 3 describes a realistic first-week plan, including how pupils help make the charter. *Check after one month* (0-4, **Create**): 4 describes a concrete, checkable way to see whether the charter is working, with a way to revise it; 1-2 gives a vague check.
