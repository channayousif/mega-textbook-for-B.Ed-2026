## A real classroom situation

Sajid is a B.Ed trainee on practicum at a government boys' high school in Larkana. After a Class 9 biology lesson on the human heart, a pupil named Imran stays behind. He says, "Sir, my uncle is a religious scholar. He says Islam only wants us to learn the Qur'an. Science is worldly, and too much of it pulls you away from faith."

Sajid has heard this before. The same week, a colleague in the staff room told him, "Our job is to finish the syllabus and get them through the board. Reading and thinking are extras." Imran is waiting for an answer. He is not being rude. He wants to know whether his faith and his science book can sit together.

By the end of this topic you will be able to answer Imran from the sources themselves, and you will see why the first word of the revelation was a command to read.

## Explanation

This topic answers three questions. What does the first revelation say about reading and knowledge? What does the Qur'an ask of a learner? And what do the Hadith say about the person who seeks knowledge?

### Explaining Surah Al-Alaq

**The setting.** Surah Al-Alaq is the 96th chapter of the Qur'an. It has five short verses and is also called Surah Iqra, after its first word. Muslims hold that it is the first passage revealed to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), in the cave of Hira near Makkah, around the year 610 CE (Higher Education Commission [HEC], 2012/2013). The chapter opens with a command, not a suggestion.

**The five verses in plain words.**

| Verse | What it says | The educational idea |
|---|---|---|
| 96:1 | Read, in the name of your Lord who created | The first word of the revelation is a command to read |
| 96:2 | He created man from a clinging clot | Learning begins with the wonder of how life begins |
| 96:3 | Read, and your Lord is the most Generous | Reading is tied to gratitude, not pride |
| 96:4 | Who taught by the pen | The pen, and so writing and recording, is an instrument of teaching |
| 96:5 | He taught humanity what they did not know | Revelation itself is an act of teaching |

Three ideas matter for a teacher. First, the command to read comes before any command to pray in the order of revelation, so knowledge is not an extra added to faith. Second, the pen is named. A community that reads and writes can keep knowledge alive after a teacher is gone. Third, the passage says that God taught people what they did not know. In Islam, knowledge has a source beyond the teacher, and the teacher is a means, not the final authority.

**A worked example: reading a verse for its educational idea.** Take verse 96:4, "Who taught by the pen." A learner might say, "It means God writes." That is not the teaching point. The verse names the pen as the tool through which God teaches. The educational idea is that writing carries knowledge across distance and time. A pupil in a government school in Sindh who keeps notes in a notebook is using the tool this verse names. Now you try the same move in the activity below.

**Correcting a common misconception: "Islam values only religious knowledge."** Many people, like Imran's uncle, use "religious knowledge" to mean only the rules of worship. The first revelation does not support that narrow reading. It commands reading, points to the natural world, and names the pen. The Qur'an asks people to look at what God has created, which is an invitation to study it. The narrow reading also forgets history: for centuries Muslim scholars in Baghdad, Cordoba and Delhi studied mathematics, astronomy, medicine and optics because they saw the study of God's creation as an act of devotion (Curren, 2025, discusses how a philosophy of education judges which knowledge is worth teaching).

### The emphasis on the acquisition of education in the Qur'an

**Knowledge is named again and again.** The Qur'an speaks of knowledge, *ilm*, and its forms many hundreds of times. It is not a marginal topic. It links knowledge to faith, to humility and to action.

**Four verses that teach a learner how to learn.**

- **Surah Az-Zumar 39:9** asks whether those who know and those who do not know can be equal. The question assumes the answer: they are not equal, and knowledge makes the difference. For a teacher, this verse is a reason to care about every pupil's understanding, not only about covering the syllabus.
- **Surah Al-Mujadilah 58:11** says that God will raise those who believe and those who have been given knowledge by degrees. Knowledge brings rank, and the rank is tied to faith and action, not to birth or wealth.
- **Surah Taha 20:114** is a prayer: "My Lord, increase me in knowledge." The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) is told to ask for more knowledge. A learner who asks for more is following a Qur'anic prayer.
- **Surah Al-Baqarah 2:269** says that God gives wisdom to whom He wills, and that the one who receives wisdom receives an overflowing benefit. Wisdom here is knowledge that is used well, not knowledge that is stored.

**Reflecting, not only memorising.** The Qur'an repeatedly calls people to think and to look. In Surah Al-Imran 3:190-191 it says that in the creation of the heavens and the earth there are signs for people of understanding. In Surah Al-An'am 6:10-12 it asks people to look at the world and reflect. In Surah Al-Ghashiyah 88:17-20 it asks, "Do they not look at the camels, how they were created, at the sky, how it was raised, at the mountains, how they were set up?" The questions invite observation. A science lesson that begins with looking at a plant, a river or the sky is doing something the Qur'an asks for.

**A Pakistan-grounded example.** A Class 6 teacher in Sukkur takes her class to the bank of the Indus. She asks them to observe the water, the boats, the birds and the farmers' wells, and to write three questions. The pupils then read a lesson on rivers. She is teaching reading, observation and writing together, in the order of the first revelation: read, look, write. Her head teacher may call it a waste of time, but the Qur'anic call to reflect gives her a reason to do it.

**What this means for a school.** The Qur'an does not divide knowledge into "sacred" and "secular" the way a timetable sometimes does. It treats the study of the world and the study of the revelation as two ways of answering the same call to know. A school that teaches only for an examination, and never asks pupils to observe, question or connect, is not following the first revelation.

### The emphasis on the acquisition of education in the Hadith

**What the Hadith are.** The Hadith are the recorded sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). They are the second source of guidance after the Qur'an, and they are where the Qur'an's call to knowledge becomes daily practice.

**Four reportings that honour the seeker of knowledge.**

- **"Seeking knowledge is obligatory upon every Muslim."** This reporting, found in Sunan Ibn Majah (224) and reported in other collections, makes knowledge a duty, not a hobby. The HEC course guide records it in the same words and applies it to men and women (HEC, 2012/2013).
- **"Whoever treads a path in search of knowledge, God makes easy for him a path to Paradise."** This reporting is in Sahih Muslim (2699). It honours the effort of seeking, not only the moment of finding.
- **"The best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it."** This reporting is in Sahih al-Bukhari (5027). It joins learning to teaching: the best learner is the one who passes it on.
- **"When a person dies, his deeds come to an end, except for three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for him."** This reporting is in Sahih Muslim (1631). It says that knowledge taught to others outlives the teacher.

**Knowledge has no borders.** The HEC course guide records three short sayings that the Prophet used to teach the scope of seeking knowledge (HEC, 2012/2013):

| Saying | What it teaches |
|---|---|
| Seek knowledge from the cradle to the grave | Education is lifelong |
| Seek knowledge even if it is as far as China | Distance is no excuse |
| Seeking knowledge is a duty of every Muslim | It is for everyone, male and female |

These sayings are short and memorable, which is why teachers have used them for centuries. Treat them as the guide presents them: as the Prophet's emphasis on seeking knowledge, in the guide's own wording.

**Writing and literacy in practice.** After the Battle of Badr, some prisoners of war who could read and write taught Muslims to read and write in return for their freedom. This report is widely told in the historical sources. Whether or not a teacher repeats every detail, the point stands: the early Muslim community treated literacy as a skill worth gaining, and it used the knowledge of others to gain it.

**A worked example: naming the teaching in a saying.** Take "Seek knowledge even if it is as far as China." The saying is not mainly about travel. It teaches that no distance is too great for knowledge, so a pupil in a village school in Tharparkar has as much right to learn as a pupil in Karachi. Now you try the same move in the activity below.

**Correcting the misconception again.** If Islam valued only religious knowledge, the seeker of knowledge would be praised only for learning law and worship. Instead, the reportings praise the seeker without naming a subject, and the early community sought literacy wherever it could find it. The duty is to seek knowledge, and the Qur'an tells the learner to look at the world while doing it.

## Activity: Which kind of knowledge?

Work in pairs. About 20 minutes.

Each pair receives these six short statements. For each one, decide together:

1. "Read, in the name of your Lord who created." (Qur'an 96:1)
2. "My Lord, increase me in knowledge." (Qur'an 20:114)
3. "Do they not look at the camels, how they were created?" (Qur'an 88:17)
4. "Seeking knowledge is obligatory upon every Muslim." (Sunan Ibn Majah 224)
5. "Seek knowledge even if it is as far as China." (recorded in the HEC guide)
6. "The best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it." (Sahih al-Bukhari 5027)

- Say whether the statement is mainly about **seeking**, **reflecting**, **teaching** or **acting**.
- Give one classroom move that follows from it. For example, "start the lesson with a question" or "ask pupils to explain to a partner".
- Then rank the six statements: which one would you put on the wall of your classroom, and why?

Share your ranking with another pair. If your choices differ, explain the reason, not just the preference.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Without looking back, give the first word of the first revelation and the two things the passage says God taught by or through.
2. *(Remember)* Name two reportings that honour the seeker of knowledge, and give the collection each is found in.
3. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain why "Islam values only religious knowledge" is too narrow, using one verse and one reporting.
4. *(Apply)* A Class 7 teacher in Mirpurkhas wants to teach the Qur'anic call to reflect in a science lesson on plants. Describe two activities she could use, and say which verse each follows from.
5. *(Analyze)* A school rule says pupils must copy the board notes silently and may not ask questions during the lesson. Analyse what this rule assumes about knowledge and about pupils, using two ideas from this topic.

## Summary

The first revelation commands a person to read in the name of the Lord who created, and it names the pen as an instrument of teaching. The Qur'an asks believers to seek knowledge, to reflect on creation and to pray for more understanding. The Hadith honour the seeker of knowledge, join learning to teaching, and treat education as a lifelong duty for every Muslim, male and female. Taken together, the sources do not separate religious from worldly knowledge; they invite a learner to read, look, write, question and pass learning on.

## Try this at your practicum school

Ask three people at your practicum school, for example a teacher, a pupil and a support staff member, to finish this sentence: "A person who seeks knowledge is someone who ..." Write down their exact words. At home, decide which of the four ideas from this topic (seeking, reflecting, teaching, acting) each answer is closest to. Bring your notes to the next class.

## Summative task

Write 250-300 words. Use the three answers you collected in "Try this at your practicum school", or three statements from this topic if you could not collect them.

1. For each answer, say whether it is mainly about seeking, reflecting, teaching or acting, and give a reason.
2. Analyse what each answer leaves out, using at least one verse and one reporting from this topic.
3. Write one sentence for your own classroom wall that captures the Qur'anic and Hadith teaching about knowledge, and explain why it is stronger than the three answers you analysed.
