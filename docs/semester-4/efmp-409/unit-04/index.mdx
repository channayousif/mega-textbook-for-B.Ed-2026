# Islamic Perspectives on Education

<Figure id="fig-U4-7" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/efmp-409/unit-04/fig-U4-7.webp" alt="A teacher sits in a circle with primary school children on a carpet in a Sindh village courtyard, and a child points at a slate." width={1376} height={768} />

"Islam cares about faith, not about school." "The Qur'an only tells us to pray." "A madrasa is not a school." Student teachers in Pakistan meet these ideas all the time, in staff rooms, in families and in the comments on a news story. This unit asks what the sources of Islam actually say about knowledge, learning and teaching. It reads the first revelation, the Qur'anic call to seek knowledge, the hadith that honour the seeker of knowledge, the Charter of Medina, the Last Sermon and the learning circle of As-Suffa. Each of these is a foundation for how Muslims have thought about education for fourteen centuries, and each speaks to a teacher in Sindh today.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the place of reading and knowledge in the first revelation, Surah Al-Alaq. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-4-1)*
- Describe the emphasis the Qur'an places on seeking knowledge, with two verses and what each asks of a learner. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-4-1)*
- Explain the emphasis the Hadith place on seeking knowledge, with two reportings and what each teaches. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-4-1)*
- Explain the educational relevance of the Charter of Medina and the Last Sermon. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-4-2)*
- Analyse the educational principles in the Last Sermon and say how one of them could shape a classroom rule. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-409-4-2)*
- Describe As-Suffa, its method of teaching and its curriculum. *(Understand; SLO:EFMP-409-4-3)*
- Compare the As-Suffa model with a present-day primary classroom and justify which principle would transfer. *(Analyze; SLO:EFMP-409-4-3)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 2 of this course, especially Topic 2.2 on ideology, religion and the Islamic foundations of education, is the main background. GISS-401 Islamic Studies (Semester 3) will have introduced the Qur'an, the Hadith and the life of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). You do not need to be a scholar of the sources. Bring your own experience of school and of any madrasa or mosque class you attended, because this unit keeps asking how those ideas reach an ordinary classroom.

## In this unit

1. [Knowledge in the Qur'an and Hadith](./topic-01) - the first revelation in Surah Al-Alaq, the Qur'anic call to seek knowledge, and what the Hadith say about the seeker of knowledge.
2. [The Charter of Medina and the Last Sermon](./topic-02) - the Charter as a foundation for a learning community, and the educational principles of the Farewell Sermon.
3. [As-Suffa, the first institution of learning](./topic-03) - the learning circle beside the Prophet's Mosque, its teaching method and its curriculum.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Each one opens with a real school situation, explains the sources, and shows a worked example before asking you to make the same move yourself. The unit speaks about sacred texts and a sacred person, so read it with care and accuracy: quote by reference, name the translation or collection you use, and do not treat a paraphrase as a quotation.

Do the group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish all three topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first.
