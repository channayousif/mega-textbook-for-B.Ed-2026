# Foundations of Education - Course Overview

This Semester 4 professional course looks at the ideas and forces that sit under every school: its philosophical, sociological, psychological, ideological and historical foundations. It asks future teachers how great educators of the East and the West have shaped education, and how these foundations help explain the strengths and problems of education in Pakistan today.

## Course outcomes

The University of Sindh B.Ed course guide says this course will enable student teachers to:

1. Understand the basic purpose of education from the social, political, historical, economic, psychological and philosophical perspectives.
2. Relate philosophy to education.
3. Highlight the role of Western and Eastern philosophers in education.
4. Trace the historical development of education.
5. Analyze the prevailing issues and problems of education from the disciplines that constitute the foundations of education.

## Begin with Unit 1

[Philosophy of Education](./unit-01/) covers the first two teaching weeks. It has two topics, a unit assessment and notes for teachers. Other units, on the foundations of education, schools of thought and educationists, Islamic perspectives, and education reports and policies, are added through separate authoring jobs.

## Teaching strategies and assessment

The course guide suggests lectures, discussions, question-answer sessions and brainstorming. Practical work includes group work, group assignments, individual assignments and presentations.

The course guide lists class tests, a mid-term test, evaluation of assignments, attendance and class participation, but gives no weights, so the programme default applies: 40% formative and 60% summative. Your teacher will explain which work is collected. Unit 1 includes retrieval questions, group activities and summative tasks with self-study guidance.

## Readings

Each topic lists its own readings. The course guide recommends anthologies of foundational readings by Canestrari and Marlowe, Provenzo and Semel. Unit 1 also draws on open sources you can read free: the Higher Education Commission's *Foundations of Education* course guide, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on philosophy of education, the OpenStax *Introduction to Philosophy*, and Pakistan's National Education Policy 2009. Read both topics of Unit 1 before attempting the unit assessment.
