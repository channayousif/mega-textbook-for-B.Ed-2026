# State, Government and Civil Society

"Miss, now that the government has changed, is Pakistan a new country?" A Class 8 pupil in Mirpurkhas asked this the week after a general election. The answer is no, and the reason is the starting point of this unit. Unit 1 showed what civics and citizenship are. This unit looks at the institutions citizens live under, the state and its government, and at the space where citizens organise themselves, civil society.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Name the four elements of a state and explain why the state is not the same as the government. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-2-1)*
- Describe the federal, provincial and local tiers of government in Pakistan, and trace how a federal bill becomes law. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-2-1)*
- Match a public problem to the tier and branch of government responsible for it. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-2-1)*
- Define civil society and explain why it is more than NGOs. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-2-2)*
- Explain how democracy and civil society depend on each other. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-2-2)*
- Argue why political participation and representation matter, and judge with evidence how far a civil-society organisation strengthens representation. *(Evaluate; SLO:GCCE-400-2-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds on Unit 1: the meaning of civics and citizenship, the forms of civic engagement, and the rights of citizens in Articles 16, 19 and 51 of the Constitution. If you are unsure of these, revise Topics 1.1 and 1.2 first. Bring what you know about your own union council, your provincial assembly member and the last general election.

## In this unit

1. [The state and the government of Pakistan](./topic-01) - the concept of the state, the structure of government in Pakistan, and the functions of government.
2. [Civil society, democracy and participation](./topic-02) - the meaning of civil society, its relationship with democracy, and the importance of political participation and representation.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order; each covers one teaching week. Each topic begins with a real situation from a school or community in Sindh, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before it asks you to use them.

Keep a copy of the Constitution, or its online text, open as you read Topic 2.1. Do the group activity in each topic with classmates, answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
