# State, Government and Civil Society - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

A state is a political community with four elements: a permanent population, a defined territory, a government and sovereignty. The Montevideo Convention of 1933 states the same idea, naming the capacity to deal with other states where civics books say sovereignty. The government is only one of the four elements. It is the part through which the state acts, and it changes at elections while the state continues. The Constitution itself, in Article 7, treats the Federal and Provincial Governments, Parliament, the Provincial Assemblies and taxing local authorities as "the State" for the purpose of fundamental rights.

Pakistan is a federal republic (Article 1) with three tiers of government. At the federal tier, Parliament is the President, the National Assembly of 336 seats and the Senate of 96 members; the Prime Minister, elected by the National Assembly, leads the Federal Government; and since 2025 the Federal Constitutional Court sits beside the Supreme Court. At the provincial tier, the Provincial Assembly (168 seats in Sindh) elects the Chief Minister, and the Governor is appointed by the President. At the local tier, Article 140A requires every province to set up elected local governments, which Sindh does through its Local Government Act of 2013. Article 142 gives Parliament the Federal Legislative List and the provinces everything else, since the Eighteenth Amendment of 2010 removed the Concurrent List. A federal bill passes both Houses, with a joint sitting if they disagree, and becomes law when the President assents. Governments make laws, carry them out and settle disputes through separate branches, the separation of powers, and they serve both basic purposes, such as order and justice, and welfare purposes, such as the education promised in Article 25A.

Civil society is the space where citizens join together by choice, outside the family, the market and the state. It includes associations, unions, welfare bodies, media and community groups; registered NGOs are only one part of it. Democracy and civil society depend on each other. Civil society watches the government, carries citizens' voices between elections, teaches democratic skills and checks elections. Democracy, through the rights to association (Article 17), speech (Article 19) and peaceful assembly (Article 16), protects civil society. Political participation takes many forms, from voting and contacting a representative to petitions, peaceful protest and standing for election. Representation works best when every group takes part, which is why the Constitution reserves seats for women and non-Muslims. In 2024 turnout was 47.6 percent. One vote rarely decides a result alone, but the votes and silence of many single citizens always do.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which set lists the four elements of a state used in this unit? *(Remember)*
   - A) Population, territory, government, sovereignty
   - B) Population, religion, language, government
   - C) Territory, army, parliament, currency
   - D) Government, constitution, elections, courts
2. After a general election, a new Prime Minister takes office. What has changed? *(Understand)*
   - A) The state, because its government is new
   - B) The government, while the state stays the same
   - C) Both the state and its territory
   - D) Neither, because the President has not changed
3. Under Article 142, on which matters can a Provincial Assembly make laws? *(Remember)*
   - A) Only matters in the Federal Legislative List
   - B) Only matters in the Concurrent List
   - C) Matters not in the Federal Legislative List, and criminal law shared with Parliament
   - D) Only matters the President sends to it
4. A federal bill passed by the National Assembly is not passed by the Senate within 90 days. What can happen next under Article 70? *(Understand)*
   - A) The bill is dropped for five years
   - B) The President decides alone
   - C) The bill goes to a joint sitting of both Houses at the request of the House where it started
   - D) The Supreme Court votes on the bill
5. A Hyderabad court decides a dispute over who owns a plot of land. Which branch of government is acting? *(Apply)*
   - A) The legislature
   - B) The executive
   - C) The judiciary
   - D) The local council
6. Which description best fits civil society? *(Remember)*
   - A) Every organisation that is registered as an NGO
   - B) Groups that citizens join by choice, outside the family, the market and the state
   - C) The government departments that deliver services
   - D) Businesses that give part of their profit to charity
7. Which of these is part of civil society? *(Apply)*
   - A) A district education office
   - B) A shop that sells textbooks
   - C) A mohalla committee that organises a street clean-up
   - D) A family planning a wedding
8. Which constitutional right most directly allows citizens to form a teachers' association? *(Understand)*
   - A) Article 1, Pakistan as a federal republic
   - B) Article 17, freedom of association
   - C) Article 51, seats in the National Assembly
   - D) Article 142, subjects of laws
9. Why does the Constitution reserve seats in the National Assembly for women and non-Muslims? *(Understand)*
   - A) To reduce the number of general seats over time
   - B) To make sure groups that might be left out have a voice in Parliament
   - C) Because only reserved members may become ministers
   - D) Because women and non-Muslims cannot vote for general seats
10. In a ward, Candidate A wins by 30 votes, and 900 registered voters did not vote. Which conclusion is best supported? *(Apply)*
    - A) The non-voters could not have changed the result
    - B) The result was decided by one voter
    - C) A small share of the non-voters could have changed the result
    - D) Turnout does not matter in local elections

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Name the four elements of a state and give the Pakistani example of each, in a short list. *(Understand)*
2. In three sentences, explain why a province such as Sindh is not a state, using the elements of a state. *(Analyze)*
3. Describe the three tiers of government in Pakistan, naming the main law-making body or council at each tier. *(Understand)*
4. In four steps, trace how a federal bill becomes an Act of Parliament, including what the President may do. *(Understand)*
5. A village near Larkana has a closed government school, a broken street light and a land dispute over the school building in court. In three sentences, name the tier or branch responsible for each problem and give a reason. *(Analyze)*
6. Define civil society in one sentence, and explain in two more sentences why it is wrong to say civil society means only NGOs. *(Understand)*
7. Give one way civil society strengthens democracy and one way democracy protects civil society, with a Pakistani example of each. *(Understand)*
8. Explain, in three sentences, why Batool et al. (2019) describe civil society in Pakistan as still developing, and give one reason a teacher should care. *(Analyze)*
9. Name three forms of political participation other than voting, and say what each asks of the citizen. *(Understand)*
10. In 2024, turnout was 47.6 percent although more people voted than in 2018. In three sentences, explain how both facts can be true and what this means for representation. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. "The government is the state." Evaluate this claim in about 350 words. Use the four elements of a state, Article 7 of the Constitution, and at least two Pakistani examples. *(Evaluate)*
2. A parent in Thatta wants a school building repaired, a new law on school fees, and a decision on a dispute with a private school. Write a guide of about 350 words telling the parent which tier and which branch of government to approach for each matter, and why. Use the Constitution articles from Topic 2.1. *(Create)*
3. "Civil society is just NGOs, and it does nothing for democracy." Analyse this claim in about 350 words. Use the meaning of civil society, at least three kinds of civil-society group, and both directions of its relationship with democracy. *(Analyze)*
4. Choose one named Pakistani civil-society organisation, such as the Free and Fair Election Network (FAFEN), a press club, a bar association or a local welfare trust. In about 400 words, judge with evidence how far it strengthens representation, and name one limit of its work. *(Evaluate)*
5. Write a short talk of about 400 words for Class 9 pupils titled "Why your voice matters in Pakistan's democracy". Explain the state and its tiers of government, the role of civil society, and why participation and representation matter, and answer the claim that one vote makes no difference. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. Short, plain answers can earn full marks when the idea is accurate and supported.

### MCQ answer key

1. A - population, territory, government and sovereignty are the four elements; the others mix in features a state may have but does not need.
2. B - the government changes at an election; the population, territory and sovereignty of the state do not.
3. C - Article 142 gives provinces every matter outside the Federal Legislative List, and shares criminal law, procedure and evidence with Parliament; the Concurrent List no longer exists.
4. C - Article 70(3) sends such a bill to a joint sitting at the request of the House in which it started.
5. C - interpreting law and settling disputes is the judiciary's function.
6. B - civil society is voluntary, non-state and non-profit; NGOs are only one part of it.
7. C - the committee is voluntary, not part of the state and not for profit; the others belong to the state, the market and the family.
8. B - Article 17 gives every citizen the right to form associations or unions.
9. B - reserved seats give a voice to groups that might otherwise be left out of Parliament.
10. C - 31 of the 900 non-voters, about 3 percent, could have reversed the result by voting for the losing candidate.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Population, the people of Pakistan; territory, the four provinces and the Islamabad Capital Territory (Article 1); government, the federal, provincial and local governments; sovereignty, Pakistan's supreme power to make its own laws and deal with other states. **Marks (4):** 1 for each element with a fitting example.
2. **Model:** Sindh has a population, a territory and a government. It is not sovereign, because it works under the Constitution of Pakistan and cannot deal with other states as an equal. Without sovereignty it lacks one of the four elements, so it is a province, not a state. **Marks (3):** 1 for the three elements it has; 1 for the missing element; 1 for the conclusion.
3. **Model:** Federal: Parliament (the President, National Assembly and Senate). Provincial: the Provincial Assembly. Local: elected local councils set up under Article 140A, such as union councils in Sindh. **Marks (3):** 1 for each tier with its body.
4. **Model:** (1) The bill is introduced and passed in one House. (2) It is sent to the other House and passed there. (3) If the second House rejects it or does not pass it in 90 days, a joint sitting can pass it. (4) The President assents within ten days or, except for a Money Bill, returns it once for reconsideration. **Marks (4):** 1 for each step in order.
5. **Model:** The closed school is a provincial matter, because school education is not in the Federal Legislative List and belongs to the province. The street light is a local council matter, as municipal services are given to local governments. The land dispute belongs to the judiciary, which settles disputes. **Marks (3):** 1 for each problem matched with a reason.
6. **Model:** Civil society is the space where citizens join together by choice, outside the family, the market and the state. A registered NGO is only one kind of civil-society group. Unregistered community groups, associations, unions and the press are also civil society, because they are voluntary, non-state and non-profit. **Marks (3):** 1 for the definition; 1 for NGOs as one kind; 1 for other examples or the three features.
7. **Model:** Civil society strengthens democracy by watching the government, for example a press club reporting on a school without a teacher. Democracy protects civil society through rights such as freedom of association in Article 17, which lets teachers form an association. **Marks (4):** 1 for each direction (2); 1 for each fitting example (2).
8. **Model:** Batool et al. (2019) argue that civil society in Pakistan was weakened from the start by how the state was built, and has grown stronger only in recent years. It is still developing because groups do not always have the space or capacity to act. A teacher should care because schools build the habits of joining, discussing and speaking up that civil society needs. **Marks (3):** 1 for the cause; 1 for the present state; 1 for a reasoned link to teaching.
9. **Model:** Contacting a representative asks the citizen to know who the representative is and state the problem clearly. A petition asks the citizen to gather facts and signatures and follow up. Peaceful protest asks the citizen to organise peacefully and within the law. Other acceptable forms: joining a campaign, standing for election. **Marks (3):** 1 for each form with what it asks.
10. **Model:** More people voted in 2024, about 60.6 million, but the number of registered voters grew much faster, to 128.6 million. So the share who voted fell to 47.6 percent. This means more than half of registered voters did not take part, so the assemblies were chosen by a minority and some groups' views may count for less. **Marks (3):** 1 for the growth in registered voters; 1 for the falling share; 1 for the effect on representation.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.

**ERQ 1: Is the government the state?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elements of a state | Explains all four and places government as one of them | Explains three, or all four without placing government | Names elements only | Absent |
| Use of Article 7 | Explains that the State acts through many bodies and what this shows | Mentions Article 7 with a weak link | Names the article only | Absent |
| Evaluation of the claim (Evaluate) | Reaches a reasoned judgement that weighs what is true and false in the claim, with evidence | Gives a judgement with partial reasons | States agreement or disagreement only | No judgement |
| Pakistani examples | Two or more apt, specific examples | One apt example | General examples | None |
