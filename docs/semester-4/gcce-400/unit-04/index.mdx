# Ethics and Community Engagement

"Everyone wants the hand pump fixed. Why do we keep fighting about how?" A trainee teacher near Sukkur heard a parent say this at a stormy school meeting. This unit takes that question seriously. The first topic asks what makes civic action right, and not only effective. The second asks what a community is, how people can work together to improve it, and how a school can become a real partner of the families it serves.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain accountability and show how it applies to officials and to citizens' own groups. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-4-1)*
- Explain non-violence as an active method of civic action, with a South Asian example. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-4-1)*
- Apply five ethical considerations to judge and improve a plan for civic action. *(Apply; SLO:GCCE-400-4-1)*
- Describe the characteristics and nature of a community, and explain community development. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-4-2)*
- Compare levels and approaches of community engagement, and explain how engagement can build social cohesion. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-4-2)*
- Analyse why the Orangi Pilot Project succeeded, and what its limits were. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-4-2)*
- Explain how school and community support each other, and plan ways to involve parents. *(Create; SLO:GCCE-400-4-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds on Units 1 to 3 of this course. You should already know what civic engagement is and its main forms, how government and civil society work in Pakistan, and the rights and duties of citizens under the Constitution, including peaceful assembly and freedom of speech. Bring your own experience of your mohalla, village or school committee.

## In this unit

1. [Ethics in civic engagement](./topic-01) - accountability, non-violence, and the ethical considerations every citizen should weigh before acting.
2. [Communities, engagement and the school](./topic-02) - the concept and development of community, approaches to engagement and social cohesion, the Orangi Pilot Project, and the school's relationship with parents and the community.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in order. Topic 4.2 uses the ethical ideas from Topic 4.1, especially accountability. Each topic opens with a real situation from Sindh, explains the ideas, and works through an example before it asks you to try.

Do each group activity with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back, and use the self-assessment checklist honestly. The practicum tasks ask you to look closely at how your practicum school works with parents and the community; ask your cooperating teacher first, and do not record anyone's name.

When you finish both topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help and two practical activities in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
