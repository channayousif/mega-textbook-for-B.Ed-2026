# Ethics and Community Engagement - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Civic engagement is judged by its methods as well as its aims. Accountability means being answerable for duties and decisions: giving an account, accepting responsibility and putting things right. Citizens hold officials to account using rights such as freedom of speech (Article 19) and peaceful assembly (Article 16), and citizens' own groups must be accountable too, especially for money. Non-violence is an active method that avoids harm to people and property; the Khudai Khidmatgar movement (1929-1948) is one South Asian example. But a method can be non-violent and still unethical. Honesty, respect, inclusion, doing no harm to others, and consent and privacy all matter. A four-question check helps: Is it true? Is it fair? Does it harm anyone? Could we explain it openly? A good aim never excuses a harmful method.

A community is a group of diverse people linked by social ties, sharing something and acting together in a place or setting. Living near one another is not enough, and a community need not share one place. Communities change over time and contain disagreement and unequal power. Community development improves living conditions and builds people's own ability to solve problems; it is shared work between communities and government, not the government's job alone.

Engagement ranges from informing people, through consulting, involving and collaborating with them, to empowering them to decide. Effective engagement starts from the community's own priority, meets people where they are, includes every group affected, shares information honestly, builds local leadership, and follows through. It follows a cycle of identifying a need, mobilising, planning together, acting and reviewing. Done well, it builds social cohesion: a society that works for the well-being of all its members, creates belonging and builds trust.

The Orangi Pilot Project in Karachi, set up in 1980, showed what organised residents can do. Lane by lane, residents formed organisations, chose lane managers, and with technical help built and paid for their own sewers, while government was asked to build the large trunk sewers. Residents built sewers in 5,479 lanes, at a fraction of the government's estimated cost, and infant mortality fell sharply. Finally, the school is itself a community institution. Through communication, learning at home, volunteering, decision-making in bodies such as school management committees, and links with local organisations, school and community support each other. Parents who seem uninterested have often not yet been engaged at a time, place and language that suit them.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. *(Remember)* Which description best defines accountability?
   - A) Following every order given by a person in authority
   - B) Being answerable for your duties and decisions, and putting things right
   - C) Punishing officials who make mistakes
   - D) Keeping public decisions private until they are complete
2. *(Understand)* Which action is an example of non-violent civic action?
   - A) Threatening an official's family until he agrees to act
   - B) Damaging the office of the water board
   - C) A peaceful, unarmed delegation that presents a signed petition
   - D) Forcing shops to close by throwing stones
3. *(Apply)* A parent posts a video saying the head teacher stole the repair money, although there is no evidence. Which ethical consideration does this break most directly?
   - A) Inclusion
   - B) Honesty
   - C) Consent
   - D) Non-violence
4. *(Apply)* A youth group in Larkana collects money from families to buy benches for a school. Which step best shows that the group is accountable?
   - A) Buying the benches quickly without telling anyone the price
   - B) Asking the families to trust the group leader
   - C) Showing receipts and reporting the spending at a public meeting
   - D) Collecting more money than needed for future projects
5. *(Apply)* Passengers on a bus from Hyderabad to Karachi share a journey. Which characteristic of a community do they most clearly lack?
   - A) A setting
   - B) Diversity
   - C) A common route
   - D) Social ties and joint action
6. *(Understand)* Which statement best describes community development?
   - A) Government building facilities for people, without their involvement
   - B) A charity giving gifts to poor families
   - C) Community members working together, often with government, to improve conditions and build their own ability to solve problems
   - D) A community refusing all help from government
7. *(Apply)* A union council asks residents to comment on two possible sites for a water tank, and then decides by itself. Which level of public participation is this?
   - A) Inform
   - B) Consult
   - C) Collaborate
   - D) Empower
8. *(Remember)* In the Orangi Pilot Project, which work did residents themselves finance and build?
   - A) Trunk sewers and treatment plants
   - B) Latrines in homes and sewers under their lanes
   - C) The main roads of Karachi
   - D) New government schools
9. *(Understand)* Which situation shows community engagement weakening social cohesion?
   - A) A meeting held in a place where women of the village cannot attend
   - B) A committee that reports its spending openly
   - C) A plan made with every biradari in the village
   - D) Neighbours of different faiths cleaning a park together
10. *(Apply)* Only six parents came to a school's weekday morning meeting, which was announced by an Urdu notice. Most parents are farm workers who speak Sindhi. What should the head teacher do first?
    - A) Conclude that parents are not interested and stop holding meetings
    - B) Fine the families of absent pupils
    - C) Hold the next meeting at a time and place that suit parents, and invite them in Sindhi
    - D) Send the same notice again with stronger words

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. *(Understand)* Accountability runs in two directions in civic life. Name both directions and give one Pakistani example of each, in no more than four sentences.
2. *(Understand)* Give two reasons why non-violent methods often work better over time than violent ones.
3. *(Analyze)* A group plans to get a road repaired by blocking it for two days and sharing photos of local schoolchildren standing in the mud, taken without asking their parents. Identify two ethical considerations the plan breaks, and explain each in one sentence.
4. *(Understand)* In two or three sentences, explain what the historian Sruti Bala argues about the Khudai Khidmatgar movement and non-violence.
5. *(Understand)* Explain in three sentences why two hundred families who have just moved into a new apartment block in Hyderabad may not yet be a community.
6. *(Analyze)* Compare the "involve" and "collaborate" levels of public participation. State one similarity and one difference.
7. *(Understand)* Explain the Orangi Pilot Project's division of sanitation into internal and external work, and why this division mattered.
8. *(Analyze)* Using evidence from the Orangi Pilot Project, give two reasons it succeeded, each linked to a practice of effective community engagement.
9. *(Understand)* List three reasons, other than lack of interest, why parents in a low-income area may not come to school meetings.
10. *(Apply)* Name three roles of the school management committee described by Retallick (2005), and explain which one shows accountability most clearly.

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. *(Evaluate)* The only dispensary in a village near Mirpurkhas has been closed for six months. Residents have tried three things: a signed application to the district health office, a peaceful sit-in outside the office every Monday, and a social media post naming a doctor as corrupt without any proof. Evaluate each method using accountability, non-violence and at least two other ethical considerations. Judge which method should continue and which should stop, and justify your judgement. (About 300 words)
2. *(Analyze)* "If the cause is good, the method does not matter." Analyse this claim. Use at least two examples, one from this unit and one of your own from Pakistan, and explain what the methods we choose teach children who watch us. (About 300 words)
3. *(Analyze)* A village in Tharparkar wants a boundary wall and a washroom for its girls' primary school. Analyse whether the Orangi approach of internal and external work, lane organisations and community finance could be used here. Identify what would transfer, what would need to change, and what risks the village should watch for. (About 350 words)
4. *(Evaluate)* A town committee in Dadu plans a new park. It holds one meeting in the committee office at 11 a.m., announces its decision in an Urdu newspaper, and invites no women's groups. Evaluate the committee's engagement using the levels of public participation and the six practices of effective engagement. Judge its likely effect on social cohesion, and recommend specific changes. (About 300 words)
5. *(Create)* Plan a small parent-and-community engagement activity for a government primary school you know, for example a reading morning, a cleanliness day or a meeting about attendance. Your plan should: (a) state the need and how you found it out; (b) follow the five steps of the engagement cycle; (c) explain how you will involve parents who usually do not come; and (d) name at least three ethical safeguards, drawn from Topic 4.1, and how you will keep each one. (About 400 words)

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. B - accountability means being answerable for duties and decisions, including putting things right. Option C confuses accountability with punishment, and A describes obedience.
2. C - a peaceful, unarmed delegation with a petition avoids harm and threats. Options A, B and D all use threats or damage.
3. B - an unproven accusation breaks honesty first, and it also breaks respect. It is not violent, so D does not fit.
4. C - receipts and a public report give an account to the people who paid. Options A, B and D keep the families in the dark.
5. D - a crowd shares a setting for a short time but has no social ties and takes no joint action.
6. C - community development both improves conditions and builds people's own ability. Options A and D each leave out one partner.
7. B - asking for comments at a set point and then deciding alone is consultation.
8. B - residents built the internal work: home latrines and lane sewers. Trunk sewers and treatment plants were external work for government.
9. A - excluding women from a meeting deepens division. Options B, C and D build trust and belonging.
10. C - the barriers are time, place and language, so the first step is to remove them. Option A repeats the misconception.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. Model: Citizens hold those in power to account, for example parents asking the union council chairman to report on a hand pump repair at a public meeting. Citizens' own groups must also be accountable, for example a lane committee showing how it spent the money households gave. **Marks (4):** 1 for each direction named; 1 for each fitting example.
2. Model: Non-violent methods protect people, so nobody is hurt, and they keep the support of neutral people who will not join a violent cause. They also keep the community together, because people who disagree must live side by side afterwards. **Marks (2):** 1 for each valid reason, explained. Accept "they are lawful under Article 16" as one reason.
3. Model: Blocking the road for two days breaks "do no harm", because patients, workers and pupils who are not part of the dispute lose out. Sharing photos of children without permission breaks consent and privacy. **Marks (4):** 1 for each consideration named; 1 for each explanation tied to the plan. Accept "non-violence" only if the answer explains a threat or harm.
4. Model: Bala argues that the Khudai Khidmatgar (1929-1948) was a movement of radical non-violent action that drew on Islamic principles and had its own local roots. She rejects the stereotype that non-violence was out of place among the Pashtuns, and the view that the movement was only an offshoot of Gandhi's. **Marks (3):** 1 for non-violent action; 1 for Islamic or local roots; 1 for rejecting the stereotype or the "offshoot" view.
5. Model: Living near one another is only one characteristic, a shared setting. The families may not yet know one another, share common concerns or act together. They become a community when social ties form and they begin to take joint action, for example on security or water. **Marks (3):** 1 for saying place alone is not enough; 1 for naming missing characteristics; 1 for how they could become a community.
6. Model: In both, the public takes part from early on and has many chances for input, and the agency still makes the final decision. The difference is that collaboration works towards a shared solution, often by consensus, while involvement only gathers input. **Marks (2):** 1 similarity; 1 difference.
7. Model: Internal work (home latrines, lane sewers and neighbourhood collector sewers) was built and paid for by residents, while external work (trunk sewers and treatment plants) was left to government. This mattered because residents could do the smaller work cheaply and quickly, and government kept its duty for the large works that only it could build. **Marks (3):** 1 internal; 1 external; 1 why it mattered.
8. Model: It started from the community's own priority, because participatory research showed residents saw sanitation as their main need. Money stayed with the people who paid it, because lane committees collected and spent their own funds, so accountability and trust were strong. **Marks (4):** 1 for each piece of evidence; 1 for each link to a practice. Accept local leadership through lane managers, or partnership with government.
9. Model: Parents may work for daily wages or on farms during school hours; they may not be able to read the notice or may feel uncomfortable in a school; the meeting may be in a language they do not speak. **Marks (3):** 1 for each valid reason. Accept feeling unwelcome and gender norms.
10. Model: The SMC maintained the building, raised funds and paid community teachers, visited the homes of absent pupils, and checked teachers' punctuality and pupils' results. Checking teachers' punctuality and results shows accountability most clearly, because it makes the school answer to the community for its work. **Marks (4):** 1 for each of three roles; 1 for a justified choice. Accept home visits as accountability if the answer explains it.

### ERQ rubrics

1. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Each method described accurately* (0-2) · *Ethical considerations applied to each method* (0-3) · *Judgement justified* (0-3, **Evaluate**: decides which methods to continue and stop, with reasons drawn from the scenario, and recognises that the application and the sit-in are ethical while the post breaks honesty and respect) · *Clarity and organisation* (0-2).
2. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Claim stated and understood* (0-2) · *Two relevant examples analysed* (0-3, **Analyze**: shows how each method breaks or keeps specific ethical considerations) · *Argument about what methods teach children* (0-3) · *Clarity* (0-2). A strong answer reaches a reasoned conclusion that methods matter as much as aims.
3. Analytic rubric (12 marks) - *Orangi approach explained accurately* (0-3) · *Transfer analysis* (0-4, **Analyze**: identifies what transfers, such as community finance of small works and local leadership, and what must change, such as the role of the education department as the "external" partner and the scattered settlement pattern of Tharparkar) · *Risks identified* (0-3, for example exclusion of poorer families, misuse of funds, or government shifting its duty onto the village) · *Clarity* (0-2).
4. Analytic rubric (10 marks) - *Level of participation identified* (0-2, the committee only informs; accept "inform, at most a token consult") · *Practices applied with evidence* (0-3) · *Judgement on social cohesion* (0-3, **Evaluate**: explains how excluding women and Sindhi speakers could weaken belonging and trust) · *Specific, realistic recommendations* (0-2).
5. Analytic rubric (14 marks) - *Need and how it was found* (0-2) · *Five-step engagement cycle applied* (0-4) · *Strategies for parents who usually do not come* (0-3, **Create**: changes to time, place, language and invitation fitted to the named school) · *Ethical safeguards* (0-3: at least three, such as consent for photographs, inclusion of mothers and minority families, open accounts of any money, each with a practical way to keep it) · *Coherence and feasibility* (0-2).
