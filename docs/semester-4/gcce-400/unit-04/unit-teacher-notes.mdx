# Ethics and Community Engagement - Notes for Teachers

The course guide lists no teaching strategies for this unit, but it suggests practical activities for the course. Two of them, community event planning and service-learning, fit this unit well. These notes offer one way to plan the two weeks.

## Sequencing the two weeks

- **Week 8 (Topic 4.1).** Start from the hand pump meeting before any definition. Ask trainees to list every method the parents proposed and to sort them into "would work" and "would not work". Only then introduce accountability, non-violence and the ethical considerations. Trainees usually discover that "would work" and "is right" are different questions.
- **Week 9 (Topic 4.2).** Start from the Thatta meeting and the claim "parents here do not care". Return to the claim at the end of the week, after the Orangi case and the section on parental involvement, and ask trainees whether they still agree with it.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"If the aim is good, any method is acceptable."** Ask: "Would you accept the same method if it were used against your own family's shop or school?" Most trainees then see the problem for themselves.
- **"A community is just the people who live in one place."** Ask how many trainees know the names of the people living three houses away. Then ask which groups they belong to that are not based on where they live.
- **"Community development is the government's job alone."** A likely wrong answer is, "Poor people should not have to pay for sewers." Accept the concern about fairness, then show the Orangi division of internal and external work: the community did what it could do cheaply and well, and government kept its duty for trunk sewers.
- **"Parents in low-income areas are not interested in their children's schooling."** Do not argue. Ask trainees who say it to list the reasons a working mother in a village might miss a 10 a.m. meeting. The list usually changes their view.

**One good discussion prompt.** "Was blocking the highway non-violent?" Expect a split. Use it to show that non-violence is necessary but not sufficient, and to introduce the "do no harm" consideration.

## Practical work: community event planning or service-learning

The guide suggests community event planning and service-learning with a local organisation as practical activities for the course. Choose one.

1. **Community event planning.** In groups, trainees plan a small event with a nearby school or community group, such as a cleanliness drive, a health talk for mothers or a reading morning for parents. They use the engagement cycle: identify a need with the community, mobilise, plan together, act and review. Each group writes down its ethical safeguards (consent, inclusion, honest use of any money) before the event.
2. **Service-learning.** Trainees give a fixed number of hours to a local organisation, such as a literacy centre, a school's SMC or a welfare trust, and keep a short reflective log that links what they see to the ideas of accountability, community development and social cohesion.

Follow your institution's consent rules. Get permission before photographing anyone, never photograph children without their parents' consent, and leave out names in written work. The activity is not marked against a fixed model answer; judge whether trainees apply the ideas of the unit to what they planned and saw.
