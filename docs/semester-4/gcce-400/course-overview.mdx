# Civics and Community Engagement - Course Overview

This Semester 4 course is about how a society is run and how ordinary people take part in it. You will study civics, citizenship, the state, government and civil society, rights and responsibilities, and the ways people work together to improve their communities. As a future teacher, you will also prepare to help pupils become informed, fair and active members of their own communities.

## Course outcomes

The University of Sindh B.Ed course guide gives three outcomes. After this course you should be able to:

1. Demonstrate fundamental understanding of civics, government, citizenship and civil society.
2. Understand the concept of community and recognise the significance of community engagement for individuals and groups.
3. Recognise the importance of diversity and inclusivity for societal harmony and peaceful coexistence.

## Begin with Unit 1

[Introduction to Civics and Citizenship](./unit-01/) covers the first three teaching weeks. It has three topics, a unit assessment and notes for teachers. Other units are added through separate authoring jobs.

## Practical work and assessment

This course carries one practical credit. The guide suggests four practical activities: community storytelling, planning a community event, service-learning with a local organisation, and cultural exchange activities. Your teacher will choose one or more of them. Any activity with pupils or community members follows your school's consent rules.

The guide gives the course marks as follows: participation in discussions 10, short reflection papers on readings 10, a mid-term essay 30, and a final paper of three to four extended essays 50. In this course, the essays are on civic topics. Participation and reflection papers (20 marks) are formative work; the mid-term and final papers (80 marks) are summative.

## Readings

Each topic lists its own readings. The guide recommends several books, including *Civics Today* and *Citizenship in Diverse Societies*, which may be available in your library. Unit 1 also uses free sources you can open online, such as UNESCO's guide to global citizenship education and a study of citizenship education in Pakistani schools. Read every topic before you attempt the unit assessment.
