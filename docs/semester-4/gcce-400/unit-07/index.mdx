# Diversity, Inclusion and Social Justice

"Miss, if we all speak different languages at home, how can we be one country?" The Class 6 pupil who asked this in Sukkur was not being difficult. He had noticed something real, and he had drawn the wrong conclusion from it. Unit 6 looked at digital citizenship. This unit looks at the society that citizenship is lived in: how diverse it is, who gets left out of it, and what citizens and governments can do about that.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Describe the ethnic, cultural, economic and political diversity of Pakistani society, with examples. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-7-1)*
- Explain what a minority group is and why numbers alone do not define one. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-7-1)*
- Explain how young people, women and minorities take part in social development, and what barriers they face. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-7-2)*
- Identify forms of social inequality and injustice in Pakistan, and the indicators used to measure them. *(Apply; SLO:GCCE-400-7-3)*
- Analyse a described injustice and choose a civic response, from evidence to advocacy to legal remedy. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-7-3)*
- Evaluate how far a practice promotes inclusive citizenship and equal rights, and design one routine that includes every pupil. *(Evaluate, Create; SLO:GCCE-400-7-4)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

This unit builds on Unit 3, especially fundamental rights and the Constitution's equal-protection guarantee in Article 25, and on Unit 4's work on ethics and community engagement. It also assumes the inclusive-education vocabulary from `EFMP-305` Inclusive Education in Semester 2, particularly the difference between equality and equity. If you are unsure of those ideas, revise the equality and equity entries in the glossary before you start. Bring what you know about your own school: which languages your pupils speak, and who takes part in school decisions.

## In this unit

1. [Diversity in Pakistani society](./topic-01) - what diversity means, its dimensions in Pakistan, and why it is not a threat to national unity.
2. [Youth, women and minorities in social development](./topic-02) - who takes part, what stops them, and the constitutional safeguards for equal participation.
3. [Inequality and injustice, and responses to them](./topic-03) - how inequality shows up in education, income, gender and region, and how citizens can respond.
4. [Inclusive citizenship and equal rights](./topic-04) - equality, equity and the practices that build an inclusive classroom, home and community.

## How to use this unit

Read the four topics in order. Each one is a single teaching section of Week 12, so each is shorter than a full week's topic in Units 1 to 4. Each topic opens with a real situation from a school or community in Sindh, teaches the ideas, and shows a worked example before it asks you to use them.

Keep the Constitution open as you read. Articles 25, 26, 27, 28, 33, 34, 36 and 37 are named in this unit, and the text of each one is quoted or paraphrased. Do the group activity in each topic with classmates, answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish the four topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help, likely misconceptions and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
