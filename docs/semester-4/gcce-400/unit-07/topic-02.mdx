## A real classroom situation

In a colony on the outskirts of Hyderabad, a group of young men and women meet every Sunday to clear a blocked drain and to teach children in a rented room. One of them, Saima, has just finished her intermediate exams. Her father says she should stop. "This is not work," he tells her. "And a girl going door to door is not respectable." Saima argues that the colony has cleaner streets because of the group. Her father answers that the group will never change anything, because people like them do not count.

Saima's father is not a villain, and he is not unusual. He believes two things this topic will test: that social development is the job of government, not of young people, and that some groups take part while others are kept out. This topic looks at who takes part, what stops them, and what the Constitution says about it.

## Explanation

This topic answers three questions. What is social development? How do young people, women and minorities take part in it? And what does the Constitution require?

### Youth, women and minorities in social development

**<Glossary term="Social development">Social development</Glossary>** means improvement in the well-being of people and communities: more schooling, better health, higher income, safer streets and a fairer say in decisions. It is not only government work. It happens when citizens organise, when associations deliver services, and when a community solves a problem itself.

The course guide asks you to look at three groups: young people, women and minorities. Each is large, each takes part, and each meets a different set of barriers.

**Young people.** Pakistan's population is young. The 2023 census counts 18.56 percent of the population in the 15 to 24 age group and 26 percent in the 15 to 29 group, so about two-thirds of the country is under 29 (Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, 2023, Section 4.2.2). Young people already take part through student unions, volunteer groups, sports clubs, religious study circles and online campaigns. Youniss and Levine (2009) collected research on how schools and communities can engage young people in civic life, and their argument is that participation is learned by doing it, not by reading about it.

**Women.** The Constitution says in Article 34 that steps shall be taken to ensure full participation of women in all spheres of national life (Government of Pakistan, 2012). The numbers show how far that is from being met. Literacy among those aged ten and above in 2023 was 68.00 percent for males and 52.84 percent for females (Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, 2023, Table 3.3). Among children aged five to sixteen, 38.99 percent of girls were out of school against 32.44 percent of boys (Table 4.22). Women take part through welfare trusts, health work, teaching, politics and unions, and they meet barriers that are material as well as cultural: unequal pay, limited mobility, safety, and the assumption that public life is not for them.

**Minorities.** Article 36 says the State shall safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of minorities, including their due representation in the Federal and Provincial services. That is a duty on the State, not a gift. Minority citizens take part through associations, local councils, the professions and public service. The barriers they meet include prejudice, under-representation in services, and the sense that they must prove they belong.

**Participation and barriers.** The table above sets them side by side. A useful way to read it is to ask what would change if one barrier were removed. If Saima's colony had a small grant, the group could hire a van for the waste. If it had a woman councillor, the drain would be on the council's list. Participation and barrier are two sides of one problem.

**A worked example: participation or barrier?** Read each description and ask whether it shows a group taking part, or a barrier in its way.

| Description | Participation or barrier? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A young woman tutors Class 3 in her colony every evening | Participation | She is acting for the community, not waiting for the government |
| A girl leaves school after Class 8 because the high school is far away | Barrier | Distance and cost stop her from continuing |
| A minority citizen applies for a government post and is passed over | Barrier | Equal access to service under Article 27 is denied |
| A women's association runs a health camp in a village | Participation | An association delivers a service the state has not reached |
| A pupil says a classmate cannot be a monitor because of his faith | Barrier | Prejudice limits a role, which Article 25 and Article 36 reject |

**Correcting a common misconception: "Minority rights are a favour granted by the majority."** The claim treats a right as a gift. The Constitution does not. Article 25 says all citizens are equal before law and entitled to equal protection of law. Article 36 places a duty on the State to safeguard the rights and interests of minorities. A duty can be enforced; a favour can be withdrawn. The difference matters when a right is refused. A citizen who is refused on the ground of faith is not asking for kindness. They are asking the State to do what Article 36 already requires.

The same point applies to women and young people. Article 34 is a direction to the State, not a reward for good behaviour. The test of a society is not whether it grants these groups permission to take part, but whether it removes what stops them.

## Activity: Participation and barrier audit

Work in groups of four. About 25 minutes.

1. Each member names one group in their own town or village that takes part in social development: a youth group, a women's association, a minority community, a parents' group, a union council member.
2. For each group, the class agrees two things: one form of participation it uses, and one barrier it meets. Write them on the table from this topic, or on a fresh one.
3. Pick one barrier. Discuss whether it could be removed by a constitutional duty (Articles 25, 26, 27, 34 or 36), by a government service, or by the community itself. Say which, and why.
4. Together, write a short reply of four or five sentences to Saima's father, answering both of his claims with evidence from this topic.

Exchange replies with another group and check whether they answered both claims.

## Check your understanding

1. *(Remember)* Without looking back, define social development in one sentence and name the three groups this topic considers.
2. *(Remember)* Give two census figures for 2023 that show the position of young people, and two that show the position of women.
3. *(Understand)* In three sentences, explain the difference between a form of participation and a barrier, with one example of each.
4. *(Understand)* Explain why Article 36 is described as a duty on the State rather than a favour to minorities.
5. *(Apply)* A government school in your district has no female teacher and no pupil from a minority community has ever been chosen as a monitor. Using Articles 25, 26, 27, 34 and 36, say which safeguard each situation raises.

## Summary

Social development is improvement in people's well-being, and it is carried by government and by citizens together. Young people, women and minorities all take part in Pakistan, through volunteering, associations, welfare work, politics and public service. Each group meets barriers: young people face few jobs and little money, women face a literacy gap, distance, unequal pay and social restriction, and minorities face prejudice and under-representation. The Constitution sets duties rather than favours. Articles 25, 26 and 27 protect equal treatment, Article 34 directs steps for the full participation of women, and Article 36 requires the State to safeguard minority rights and their due representation in the services.

## Try this at your practicum school

Find out one way your practicum school includes pupils, parents or community members in its work: a school management committee, a parents' group, a cleanliness drive, a prize day. Ask one teacher who takes part in it and what makes it hard for others to join. Write three lines: the activity, who takes part, and the barrier the teacher named.

## Summative task

Write 300-350 words on the following task.

1. Choose one group: young people, women or minorities.
2. Describe two ways that group takes part in social development in your area, and one barrier it meets.
3. Analyse whether the barrier is best removed by a constitutional duty, by a government service or by the community itself, and justify your choice with evidence from this topic.

*(Analyze)*
