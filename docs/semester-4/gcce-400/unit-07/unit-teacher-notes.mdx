# Diversity, Inclusion and Social Justice - Notes for Teachers

The course guide lists no teaching strategies for this unit, but it suggests practical activities for the course. These notes offer one way to plan the four sections of Week 12 and to use community storytelling and a cultural exchange activity. The unit is sensitive: it touches on prejudice, minorities and injustice in Pakistan. Keep the teaching descriptive and sourced, present more than one perspective where views differ, and name no living private individual.

## Sequencing the four sections

- **Section 12.1 (Topic 7.1).** Start from Zainab's pupil who asks why the class needs so many languages. Let the class list the languages spoken at home before you give the census figures. Settle the difference between a difference and a harm, because everything later depends on it.
- **Section 12.2 (Topic 7.2).** Start from Saima's father. Ask trainees whether the Sunday group is government work, NGO work or neither, before you name social development. Keep the census indicators on the board; they are the evidence the section turns on.
- **Section 12.3 (Topic 7.3).** Start from Bilal's three observations. Ask the class to sort them into private troubles and public injustices before you give the flowchart. The distinction is the hardest idea in the unit.
- **Section 12.4 (Topic 7.4).** Start from Rizwan's school. The equality and equity diagram usually lands at once; spend the time on the routine design, which is the unit's Create task.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"Diversity is a threat to national unity."** A likely wrong answer is, "We should all speak one language so that we are united." Ask what Article 28 protects and what Article 33 tells the State to discourage. The answer is not to remove differences but to remove prejudice.
- **"Minority rights are a favour granted by the majority."** Ask what the difference is between a duty and a gift, and what a citizen can do when a duty is not met. Article 36 is a duty on the State.
- **"Equality means treating everyone in exactly the same way."** Use the diagram with the three children and the boxes. Ask what happens to the shortest child when everyone gets the same box. Then ask why Article 25(3) and Article 37(a) allow special provision.
- **"These are personal problems, so I cannot act."** Ask the class to find the pattern and the duty in one of Bilal's observations. Once both are visible, the problem is public.

**One good discussion prompt.** "Should a school give every pupil the same thing, or give each pupil what they need?" Expect a split. Use it to draw the equality and equity distinction, then ask when each is the right rule.

## Practical work: community storytelling and cultural exchange

The guide suggests four optional practical activities for the course. Two fit this unit: community storytelling and cultural exchange activities.

1. In small groups, trainees collect one story from a community member about a time they felt they belonged, or did not belong, in a school, a workplace or a public place. Written consent is needed, and the storyteller may withdraw at any point. Do not record anyone without permission.
2. Each group prepares a short retelling, in the storyteller's own language where possible, and shares it with the class. The class notes which dimension of diversity the story turns on.
3. For a cultural exchange activity, the class plans a small event that celebrates the diversity of the group itself: a dish, a song, a craft, a festival, a story. The event must not ask anyone to represent a whole community, and it must not rank one culture above another.
4. Each group links what it heard or planned to one idea in this unit: a difference between groups, a harm done to a group, or a practice that includes.

Follow your institution's consent and safety rules, and do not photograph anyone without permission. The activity is not marked against a fixed model answer; judge whether trainees link what they heard or planned to the ideas of the unit.
