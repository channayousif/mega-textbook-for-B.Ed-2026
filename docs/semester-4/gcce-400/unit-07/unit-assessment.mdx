# Diversity, Inclusion and Social Justice - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Diversity is the range of differences among the people of a society. In Pakistan it runs along language, ethnicity, religion, region, economic class and political view. The 2023 census shows the scale of it: Punjabi is the mother tongue of about 37 percent of the population, Pashto 18 percent, Sindhi 14 percent, Saraiki 12 percent and Urdu 9 percent, and no single language is spoken at home by most Pakistanis. Race is a physical marker a society treats as significant; ethnicity is shared culture; and a minority group is defined by subordination and unequal treatment rather than by numbers.

Diversity is not a threat to national unity. Article 28 protects the right to preserve a distinct language, script or culture, and Article 33 tells the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices. The threat comes from prejudice and discrimination, not from difference.

Young people, women and minorities all take part in social development, through volunteering, associations, welfare work, politics and public service. Each group meets barriers. Young people face few jobs and little money. Women face a literacy gap, distance, unequal pay and social restriction: literacy in 2023 was 68.00 percent for males and 52.84 percent for females, and 38.99 percent of girls aged five to sixteen were out of school against 32.44 percent of boys. Minorities face prejudice and under-representation. The Constitution sets duties rather than favours: Articles 25, 26 and 27 protect equal treatment, Article 34 directs steps for the full participation of women, and Article 36 requires the State to safeguard minority rights and their due representation in the services.

Social inequality is a systematic difference in resources, opportunities or treatment, and it can be measured in education, work, gender and region. An injustice is more than a private trouble: it has a pattern that reaches a group and a duty that someone is failing to meet. A citizen responds in sequence: notice and name the problem, gather evidence, try dialogue, escalate through advocacy, and, where a right is clear, seek a legal remedy.

Inclusive citizenship means every citizen belongs and can take part. Equality gives everyone the same treatment; equity gives each person the support they need, which may differ. Equal treatment can hold people back when people start from different places, which is why Article 25(3) and Article 37(a) allow special provision. Small habits at home, in school and in the community are what turn a right into a fact, and harmony follows because an included citizen believes the community is theirs.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Which set names the four dimensions of diversity the course guide asks you to consider? *(Remember)*
   - A) Language, religion, class and region
   - B) Ethnic, cultural, economic and political
   - C) Race, caste, sex and age
   - D) Education, health, income and housing
2. A pupil says, "A minority group means a group with fewer people." Which answer best corrects this? *(Understand)*
   - A) Yes, a minority group is always smaller than the rest of the population
   - B) No, a minority group is defined by subordination and unequal treatment, whatever its size
   - C) No, a minority group means any group that speaks a different language
   - D) Yes, but only in a census
3. According to the 2023 census, which language is reported by the largest share of the population as their mother tongue? *(Remember)*
   - A) Sindhi
   - B) Pashto
   - C) Punjabi
   - D) Urdu
4. Which description best fits social development? *(Remember)*
   - A) Improvement in the well-being of people and communities, carried by government and citizens together
   - B) The work of government departments only
   - C) Economic growth measured by national income
   - D) The work of registered non-governmental organisations only
5. Article 36 is described as a duty on the State rather than a favour to minorities because it: *(Understand)*
   - A) Can be withdrawn at any time
   - B) Requires the State to safeguard minority rights, including due representation in services
   - C) Applies only to citizens who are registered
   - D) Was granted by the majority in Parliament
6. A young woman tutors Class 3 children in her colony every evening. Which is the best classification? *(Apply)*
   - A) A barrier, because she is not paid
   - B) Participation, because she acts for the community rather than waiting for the government
   - C) A barrier, because a girl should not go door to door
   - D) Participation, because it is government work
7. Which indicator shows inequality in education in Pakistan in 2023? *(Remember)*
   - A) About 35.60 percent of children aged five to sixteen were out of school
   - B) About 37 percent of the population speaks Punjabi
   - C) About 23 percent of the population lives in Sindh
   - D) About 18.56 percent of the population is aged 15 to 24
8. A colleague says, "The leaking roof and the absent teachers are personal problems." Which answer best explains why this is not correct? *(Understand)*
   - A) Because the headteacher is responsible for the building
   - B) Because a pattern reaches a group and a public duty is not being met
   - C) Because the government will do what it can
   - D) Because the pupils are poor
9. A road has been reported to the union council three times with no reply. Which step should come next? *(Apply)*
   - A) Gather evidence
   - B) Notice and name the problem
   - C) Legal remedy
   - D) Advocacy
10. A school gives every pupil the same printed examination paper, and a pupil with a visual impairment cannot read it. Which statement is best? *(Apply)*
    - A) The school is equitable because everyone has the same paper
    - B) The school is equal but not equitable, because the same treatment does not meet different needs
    - C) The school is neither equal nor equitable, because the pupil should not be examined
    - D) The school is equitable because the pupil can ask a friend

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Name the four dimensions of diversity the course guide asks about, and give one Pakistani example of each. *(Understand)*
2. In three sentences, explain why the claim that diversity threatens national unity is mistaken, using Articles 28 and 33. *(Analyze)*
3. Define a minority group in one sentence, and explain in two more sentences why numbers alone do not define one. *(Understand)*
4. Describe one form of participation and one barrier for each of youth, women and minorities, in a short list. *(Understand)*
5. A government school has no female teacher and no pupil from a minority community has ever been chosen as a monitor. In three sentences, say which constitutional safeguard each situation raises. *(Analyze)*
6. Give two census indicators from 2023 that show the position of women in education, and say in one sentence why they matter for participation. *(Understand)*
7. Name four forms of inequality in Pakistan and give one indicator for each. *(Understand)*
8. A trainee notices that two of six teachers are often absent. In three sentences, explain whether this is an indicator or an impression, and what should happen next. *(Analyze)*
9. Describe the four stages of a social movement, and give one example of a civic response that is not a movement. *(Analyze)*
10. A pupil who speaks Saraiki at home is told that only Urdu may be used in the classroom. In three sentences, explain whether this is equality or equity, and which article it raises. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. "Diversity in Pakistan is a threat to national unity." Analyse this claim in about 350 words. Use the dimensions of diversity, at least two census indicators, and Articles 28 and 33. *(Analyze)*
2. Choose one group: young people, women or minorities. In about 350 words, analyse how that group takes part in social development in your area, what barrier it meets, and whether the barrier is best removed by a constitutional duty, by a government service or by the community itself. *(Analyze)*
3. A village near Larkana has one teacher for six classes, a leaking school roof, and a Class 4 pupil who cannot climb the stairs to the classroom. In about 350 words, evaluate which of the three problems is the most serious injustice, and justify your judgement with evidence and with the Constitution. *(Evaluate)*
4. Design one classroom routine that makes every pupil, whatever their background, feel included, and write about 350 words justifying it. Your justification must use the equality and equity distinction and at least one article of the Constitution. *(Create)*
5. Write a short talk of about 400 words for Class 9 pupils titled "Belonging in Pakistan". Explain what diversity is, why it is not a threat to unity, how inequality is measured, and what one thing a pupil can do to include someone who is left out. *(Evaluate)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. Short, plain answers can earn full marks when the idea is accurate and supported.

### MCQ answer key

1. B - the guide names ethnic, cultural, economic and political diversity; the others are real dimensions but not the guide's four.
2. B - a minority group is defined by subordination and unequal treatment, not by size.
3. C - Punjabi was reported by about 37 percent of the population in 2023, the largest share.
4. A - social development covers well-being and is carried by government and citizens together; B and D narrow it, and C is only one measure.
5. B - Article 36 places a duty on the State to safeguard minority rights and due representation; a duty can be enforced, a favour cannot.
6. B - she acts for the community without waiting for government, which is participation.
7. A - out-of-school children is an education indicator; the others measure language, province and age.
8. B - a private trouble touches one person; an injustice has a pattern reaching a group and a duty that is not met.
9. D - dialogue has been tried three times without reply, so the next step is to widen the audience.
10. B - the same treatment is equal; it is not equitable because it does not meet the pupil's different need.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Ethnic (Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashtun, Baloch, Saraiki speakers), cultural (shared language, food, dress, festivals), economic (income, occupation, land, city or village), political (party, opinion, provincial interest). **Marks (4):** 1 for each dimension with a fitting example.
2. **Model:** Article 28 protects the right of any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture to preserve and promote it. Article 33 tells the State to discourage parochial, racial, tribal, sectarian and provincial prejudices. So the Constitution protects diversity and names prejudice as the harm. Unity means agreeing on how to live together, not being the same. **Marks (3):** 1 for Article 28; 1 for Article 33; 1 for the conclusion that difference is not the threat.
3. **Model:** A minority group is a group treated as subordinate, or lacking power, because of its physical or cultural characteristics. Numbers do not define it, because a small group may hold power and a large group may be subordinate. The test is unequal treatment, not headcount. **Marks (3):** 1 for the definition; 1 for why numbers do not define it; 1 for the test of unequal treatment.
4. **Model:** Youth: volunteering in a clean-up or tutoring, against few jobs and little money. Women: running a welfare trust or standing for a union council seat, against social restriction, unequal pay and safety. Minorities: serving on a school committee or in public service, against prejudice and under-representation. **Marks (3):** 1 for each group with both a form and a barrier.
5. **Model:** The absence of a female teacher raises Article 34, which directs steps to ensure full participation of women in all spheres of national life, and Article 27, which forbids discrimination in appointment. The refusal to choose a minority pupil as monitor raises Article 25, equality before law, and Article 36, the safeguard of minority rights. **Marks (3):** 1 for each situation with its article; 1 for a reason.
6. **Model:** Literacy among those aged ten and above was 68.00 percent for males and 52.84 percent for females; 38.99 percent of girls aged five to sixteen were out of school against 32.44 percent of boys. They matter because a group that is less educated takes part less in public life. **Marks (3):** 1 for each indicator; 1 for the link to participation.
7. **Model:** Education: 35.60 percent of children aged five to sixteen out of school. Income and work: 38.57 percent of the population employed, with 59.68 percent of males and 16.10 percent of females. Gender: literacy 52.84 percent for females against 68.00 percent for males. Region: literacy 42.01 percent in Balochistan against 66.25 percent in Punjab. **Marks (4):** 1 for each form with an indicator.
8. **Model:** It is an impression until it is counted. Two of six teachers absent is a proportion, so it becomes an indicator once the register is checked. The next step is to gather evidence, by recording attendance, before anything is claimed. **Marks (3):** 1 for the distinction; 1 for the evidence; 1 for the next step.
9. **Model:** Emergence (people notice a problem), coalescence (they organise to publicise it), institutionalisation (it becomes an established body with staff and funding), decline (the issue fades or the change is won). A single petition to the union council, or one meeting with a councillor, is a civic response at the emergence stage and is not a movement. **Marks (4):** 1 for each stage; 1 for the example.
10. **Model:** It is equal treatment, because the rule is the same for everyone. It is not equitable, because it ignores that the pupil thinks in another language and so holds the pupil back. It raises Article 28, which protects the right to preserve and promote a distinct language, and Article 33, which tells the State to discourage prejudice. **Marks (3):** 1 for equality; 1 for equity; 1 for the article.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.
