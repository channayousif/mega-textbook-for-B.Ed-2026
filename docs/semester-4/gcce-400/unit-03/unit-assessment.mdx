# Rights and Responsibilities - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Fundamental rights are constitutional guarantees that protect human dignity, personal liberty, and equality against arbitrary state power. Unlike ordinary statutes that can be altered by simple legislative majorities, fundamental rights are enshrined in the supreme constitution. Under Article 8 of the 1973 Constitution of Pakistan, any law, custom, or administrative practice in derogation of fundamental rights is void. Pakistan's constitutional evolution progressed through the Government of India Act 1935, the 1956 and 1962 constitutions, and culminated in the consensus parliamentary constitution of 14 August 1973. Articles 8 to 28 establish enforceable protections, including security of person (Article 9), fair trial (Article 10A), inviolable human dignity (Article 14), freedom of speech and expression (Article 19), equality before the law (Article 25), and free, compulsory education for children aged five to sixteen (Article 25A).

Citizenship in a constitutional democracy also demands fulfilling duties. Under Article 5(1), loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen. Under Article 5(2), obedience to the Constitution and law is the inviolable obligation of every citizen and resident within Pakistan. Mandatory legal duties, such as paying taxes honestly and complying with statutory regulations, are compulsory and backed by state penalties. Democratic societies also require voluntary civic responsibilities, such as informed voting under Article 51, religious tolerance, protecting minorities, conserving shared public goods, and serving on school management committees. Rights and responsibilities exist in dynamic reciprocity: rights protect citizens from tyranny, while duties protect society from disorder and sustain public institutions.

5. Quote or state accurately the two clauses of Article 5 of the Constitution of Pakistan 1973 and identify who is bound by each clause. *(Understand)*
6. Explain why the payment of taxes is classified as a mandatory legal duty rather than a voluntary civic deed. *(Understand)*
7. A trainee teacher observes that many voters in their neighbourhood vote based solely on clan directives rather than candidate integrity. Explain why informed, independent voting is an essential civic responsibility in a constitutional democracy. *(Apply)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Constitutional Protection and Judicial Enforcement of Fundamental Rights** *(Analyze)*  
   Analyse the mechanism through which the 1973 Constitution protects citizens against unconstitutional laws and executive abuse. In your response:  
   - Explain the rule established in Article 8 regarding laws inconsistent with fundamental rights.  
   - Describe the role of the superior judiciary (High Courts under Article 199 and the Supreme Court under Article 184(3)) in issuing writs to enforce citizen rights.  
   - Illustrate your analysis with a realistic Pakistani example where an unlawful administrative action is challenged and overturned by a court.
2. **The Evolution of Citizen Rights in Pakistan's Constitutional History** *(Evaluate)*  
   Evaluate Pakistan's journey toward establishing a stable constitutional rights framework from 1956 to the 2010 Eighteenth Amendment. In your response:  
   - Assess the strengths and vulnerabilities of the fundamental rights frameworks in the 1956 and 1962 constitutions.  
   - Explain why the consensus adoption of the 1973 Constitution represented a major turning point for parliamentary democracy and citizen rights.  
   - Judge the impact of the Eighteenth Amendment (2010), focusing specifically on Articles 10A, 19A, and 25A.
3. **Legal Obligations and Democratic Vitality under Article 5** *(Analyze)*  
   Analyse the concept of constitutional duties under Article 5 of the 1973 Constitution. In your response:  
   - Examine the legal and civic meaning of "loyalty to the State" and "inviolable obligation" to obey the Constitution and law.  
   - Explain how obeying statutory laws and paying taxes directly enables the state to protect fundamental rights like education and health.  
   - Analyse the consequences for a society when substantial segments of the population evade civic and legal duties.
4. **Designing a Community Civic Engagement Plan for Rights and Responsibilities** *(Create)*  
   Develop a practical community action plan for a secondary school in Sindh that teaches pupils both their constitutional rights and their civic responsibilities. Your plan must include:  
   - An awareness campaign introducing three key constitutional rights relevant to youth (such as Articles 14, 19, and 25A).  
   - Three practical school and community service projects that cultivate matching civic responsibilities (such as anti-littering campaigns, peer conflict mediation, and community library support).  
   - An evaluation method to measure whether pupils demonstrate improved civic dispositions and respect for peers' rights.
5. **The Reciprocal Balance Between Rights and Duties in Contemporary Pakistan** *(Evaluate)*  
   Evaluate the proposition that democratic citizenship in Pakistan is threatened equally by the state's failure to deliver fundamental rights and by citizens' disregard for civic duties. In your response:  
   - Provide two concrete examples where state neglect of fundamental rights (such as failure to enforce Article 25A or judicial delays under Article 10A) weakens citizen trust.  
   - Provide two concrete examples where citizen disregard for duties (such as widespread tax evasion or unlawful public vandalism during protests) undermines state capacity.  
   - Argue how teacher educators can foster a balanced civic mindset in future generations that simultaneously demands state accountability and practices civic duty.

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **B** - Under Article 8(1) and 8(2), any law or custom inconsistent with fundamental rights is void to the extent of such inconsistency.
2. **B** - Article 9 explicitly states that no person shall be deprived of life or liberty save in accordance with law.
3. **B** - Enacted under Ayub Khan, the 1962 Constitution established an authoritarian presidential system with indirect elections and initially omitted an enforceable chapter of fundamental rights until restored by the First Amendment in 1963.
4. **C** - Article 25(1) guarantees equality before the law, and Article 25(2) states there shall be no discrimination on the basis of sex. Fining women more than men for the same offence denies them equal protection of law.
5. **C** - Article 19A, inserted in 2010 by the Eighteenth Amendment, guarantees the right to information in matters of public importance.
6. **A** - Article 5(1) states: "Loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen."
7. **B** - An "inviolable obligation" is an absolute, non-negotiable legal command that applies to all citizens and residents without exception.
8. **B** - Legal duties are mandatory obligations enforced by state sanctions (fines, imprisonment), whereas civic responsibilities are voluntary moral actions guided by civic values.
9. **A** - Article 16 protects the right of citizens to assemble "peacefully and without arms"; violent disruption or harming ambulances violates both constitutional conditions and criminal law.
10. **B** - Property rights under Article 24 are subject to reasonable legal regulation, and lawful tax assessment is a compulsory duty required to fund public infrastructure and state institutions.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model answer:** Fundamental rights are supreme constitutional entitlements that stand above ordinary legislation and bind every organ of the state. They differ from ordinary statutory rights in two ways: first, under Article 8, ordinary statutes that violate fundamental rights are void; second, fundamental rights are directly enforceable in superior courts through constitutional writ jurisdiction (Articles 199 and 184(3)).  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for defining fundamental rights as supreme constitutional guarantees; 1 mark for contrasting their judicial supremacy under Article 8 with ordinary statutes.
2. **Model answer:** Article 14 declares that the dignity of man and the privacy of home are inviolable. Its civic purpose is to establish that every individual possesses inherent human worth that the state cannot violate, explicitly outlawing physical torture or cruel, degrading treatment by law enforcement agencies during investigations.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for naming the inviolability of dignity and home privacy; 1 mark for explaining its safeguard against police torture and arbitrary intrusion.
3. **Model answer:** The constitutional right at stake is Article 25A (Right to education). Under this article, the State is legally obligated to provide free and compulsory education to all children aged 5 to 16. The headmaster cannot deny admission based on poverty or inability to purchase private materials; the child has an enforceable constitutional entitlement to free schooling.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for correctly identifying Article 25A and the age range (5 to 16); 1 mark for explaining the state's mandatory legal obligation to provide free access.
4. **Model answer:** The 1956 Constitution included a formal, justiciable chapter of fundamental rights enforceable through court writs. In contrast, the 1962 Constitution initially omitted an enforceable fundamental rights chapter, framing rights merely as advisory principles of law-making, until widespread public agitation forced the First Constitutional Amendment in 1963 to reinstate enforceable rights.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for noting the enforceable rights chapter in the 1956 Constitution; 1 mark for explaining that the 1962 Constitution initially omitted enforceable rights until the 1963 amendment.
5. **Model answer:** Article 5(1) states: "Loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen" (binding all citizens of Pakistan). Article 5(2) states: "Obedience to the Constitution and law is the inviolable obligation of every citizen wherever he may be and of every other person for the time being within Pakistan" (binding citizens worldwide and all residents within Pakistan).  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for accurately stating or quoting both clauses; 1 mark for identifying who is bound (citizens for 5(1); citizens everywhere plus all domestic residents for 5(2)).
6. **Model answer:** Tax payment is a mandatory legal duty because it is enacted by statutory legislation (such as income and sales tax acts) and carries enforceable legal penalties, including fines and prosecution for non-compliance. It is mandatory because public goods (defence, justice, public education, healthcare) cannot exist without reliable public revenue.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for explaining the legal compulsion and statutory penalties; 1 mark for linking tax revenue to the maintenance of essential public services.
7. **Model answer:** In a democracy, the government derives its authority from the consent of the governed through elections (Article 51). If citizens vote purely on clan directives or narrow coercion, public representatives become accountable to sectional bosses rather than the public interest. Informed, independent voting ensures that representatives are chosen on merit, honesty, and effective public policies.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for connecting the vote under Article 51 to democratic legitimacy; 1 mark for explaining how voting on clan directives undermines public accountability and policy merit.
8. **Model answer:** Loyalty to the State means upholding the sovereign, constitutional framework of Pakistan and defending its territorial integrity. In contrast, loyalty to a government means supporting the political party currently holding executive office. In a democracy, citizens may vigorously oppose, criticise, and vote against the government while remaining completely loyal to the constitutional State. Conflating the two leads to treating democratic opposition as treason.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for distinguishing the permanent constitutional State from temporary elected administrations; 1 mark for explaining why democratic governance requires the right to oppose the ruling government.
9. **Model answer:** Freedom of speech under Article 19 protects the right to express ideas, critique policies, and publish information. However, this freedom is subject to "reasonable restrictions" regarding public order and decency. If speech is used for defamation, slander, or inciting violent sectarian hatred, it destroys the dignity (Article 14) and security (Article 9) of other citizens. Thus, free speech can survive only when citizens exercise the civic responsibility of truthful, non-violent communication.  
   *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for noting that Article 19 contains reasonable restrictions; 1 mark for demonstrating how hate speech or defamation harms other citizens' constitutional rights.
10. **Model answer:** (a) Freedom of speech (Article 19), balanced by the duty to speak truthfully and avoid defamation or insult. (b) Freedom of peaceful assembly (Article 16), balanced by the duty to stay peaceful and unarmed and not to disrupt classes or other pupils. (c) The right to free and compulsory education (Article 25A), balanced by the parents' duty to send the child regularly and support the school, for example through the School Management Committee. (d) Freedom of trade, business or profession (Article 18), balanced by the duty to obey licensing rules, keep the footpath clear and pay lawful taxes. In each case the right protects the citizen from the state (preventing tyranny), while the duty protects other people and shared public goods (preventing disorder), so democratic stability depends on this reciprocity.  
    *Mark scheme (2 marks):* 1 mark for correctly naming the right at stake in all four scenarios (half a mark for two or three); 1 mark for naming a balancing duty for each scenario and linking the pattern to reciprocity between citizens and the state.
