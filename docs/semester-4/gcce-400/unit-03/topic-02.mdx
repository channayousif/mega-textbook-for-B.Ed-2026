# Topic 3.2 - The citizen's duties and responsibilities

## A real classroom situation

Farhana is a B.Ed trainee teaching social studies to Class 8 in a community secondary school in Hyderabad. During a discussion on local government, students conduct a mock election for class monitors. The voting goes smoothly, and two monitors are elected. However, during the lunch break, several students leave juice boxes and plastic wrappers scattered across the veranda.

When Farhana asks the students to pick up the litter, one boy remarks, "Miss, that is the school peon's job. Our parents pay taxes and school fees so that the municipality and the school hire sweepers. A citizen has the right to clean public spaces, but picking up trash is a worker's duty."

Another student adds, "My uncle says ordinary citizens have no duties in Pakistan. Duties are only for police officers, soldiers, and government servants who draw monthly salaries from the treasury."

Farhana realises that her pupils have developed a one-sided understanding of citizenship: they see themselves as consumers who demand rights, services, and conveniences from the state, but they do not see themselves as responsible members who sustain their shared community. By the end of this topic, you will be able to explain the mandatory legal duties every citizen owes under Article 5 of the Constitution, the voluntary responsibilities essential for a democracy, and why rights cannot survive without civic duty.

## Explanation

This topic answers three questions. What are the legal duties of a citizen in Pakistan? What broader responsibilities does a democratic society require? And how do rights and responsibilities balance each other?

### Duties of citizens

A **duty** is a compulsory obligation that a person is required to perform by law or authority. If a citizen fails or refuses to carry out a legal duty, the state can impose penalties, including fines or imprisonment.

In standard civics textbooks such as *Civics Today*, legal duties are distinguished from voluntary civic acts because duties are mandatory for the preservation of public order (Glencoe/McGraw-Hill, n.d.). Without enforceable duties, no legal system could maintain peace, fund public services, or protect life and property.

In the Constitution of Pakistan 1973, citizen duties are not buried in secondary legislation; they are stated right at the beginning of the constitutional text in Part I, Article 5:

1. **Article 5(1) - Loyalty to the State:** "Loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen."
   - Loyalty to the state means standing for the sovereignty, integrity, and defence of Pakistan.
   - It is important to distinguish loyalty to the *State* from unquestioning obedience to a particular *government* or ruling political party. In a constitutional democracy, citizens have every right to criticise government policies, question ministers, and vote for opposition parties. However, loyalty to the State itself means upholding the constitutional order, defending the country against external threats, and refusing to assist foreign aggression or violent insurrections that seek to destroy the constitutional state.
2. **Article 5(2) - Obedience to the Constitution and law:** "Obedience to the Constitution and law is the inviolable obligation of every citizen wherever he may be and of every other person for the time being within Pakistan."
   - The phrase "inviolable obligation" means a duty that cannot be broken, transferred, or set aside by anyone, regardless of wealth, social rank, or official position.
   - Crucially, this clause applies not only to citizens living inside Pakistan, but also to Pakistani citizens residing abroad ("wherever he may be"), as well as to foreign tourists, diplomats, and international residents living temporarily within Pakistan.
   - Obedience to law includes following statutory criminal laws, civil property laws, environmental regulations, and municipal traffic rules.

Beyond Article 5, citizenship entails essential practical legal duties:

- **Paying taxes honestly.** Modern states require financial revenue to build roads, construct hospitals, run government schools, maintain the power grid, and supply clean water. Paying taxes - including income tax, sales tax, and local municipal property taxes - is a mandatory legal obligation. Tax evasion starves public infrastructure and directly deprives vulnerable children of schools and medical clinics.
- **Obeying lawful public orders.** Complying with building regulations, public health directives during disease outbreaks, and environmental sanitation laws protects the collective welfare.
- **Jury or witness service.** When summoned by a court of law, providing truthful testimony in judicial proceedings is a legal duty necessary for delivering fair trials under Article 10A.

**Correcting a common misconception: "Duties apply only to government servants."** As Farhana's student claimed in the opening scenario, many people think duties are restricted to state employees. It is true that public servants take specific oaths of office and are subject to statutory service rules (such as the Government Servants Conduct Rules). However, constitutional duties under Article 5 bind every single human being residing in the country. A private shopkeeper, an engineer, a farmer, a university student, and a teacher all share the exact same constitutional obligation to obey the law, respect the rights of neighbours, and remain loyal to the state.

**A worked example: distinguishing legal duties from civic responsibilities.**

How can a teacher determine whether an action is a mandatory legal duty or a voluntary civic responsibility? Use this two-part test:

| Test question | Legal duty | Civic responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Is the action commanded by a specific law or constitutional article? | Yes. Commanded by statute or Article 5. | No. Encouraged by democratic values, ethics, and civic norms. |
| Does the state impose a formal legal penalty (fine, arrest) for non-performance? | Yes. Non-compliance results in statutory sanctions. | No. Non-compliance may lead to social disapproval or poor civic outcomes, but not arrest. |

*Worked application:*

1. *Filing an annual income tax return:* Required by the Income Tax Ordinance and punishable by financial penalties and legal summons. This is a **legal duty**.
2. *Voting in a general election:* Article 51 gives every eligible adult citizen the right to vote, but Pakistani law does not fine or imprison citizens who choose not to vote. Exercising the ballot thoughtfully is a **civic responsibility**.
3. *Volunteering to plant trees in a barren school courtyard:* There is no statute mandating community gardening. This is an admirable **civic responsibility**.
4. *Halting your motorcycle at a red traffic signal:* Enforced by traffic police and punishable by fine. This is a **legal duty**.

### Responsibilities in a democratic society

While legal duties form the baseline of citizenship, a vibrant democracy cannot survive on bare legal obedience alone. A society where citizens only obey laws when police officers are watching soon decays into apathy or authoritarian control. Democratic life depends on voluntary **civic responsibilities** - the ethical commitments and civic habits that citizens embrace to make their community just, healthy, and self-governing.

Dean (2005) points out that when schools fail to teach active civic responsibilities, pupils grow into passive adults who complain about societal problems but never participate in collective solutions. Ahmad (2004) similarly argues that democratic citizenship in Pakistan requires cultivating mutual tolerance, civic engagement, and community solidarity.

Key civic responsibilities in a democratic society include:

1. **Informed and active political participation.**
   - While voting is not legally compulsory in Pakistan, casting an informed vote in general and local government elections under Article 51 is a fundamental civic responsibility.
   - Responsible participation means studying candidates' track records, understanding party manifestos, resisting vote-buying or ethnic sectarian pressure, and holding elected representatives accountable between elections.
2. **Respecting the rights, faiths, and dignity of others.**
   - In a diverse, multi-ethnic, and multi-religious country like Pakistan, democracy requires active tolerance.
   - Citizens have a civic responsibility to respect the constitutional rights of fellow citizens, particularly religious minorities protected under Articles 20 and 22, linguistic communities protected under Article 28, and women and children protected under Article 25.
   - This includes rejecting hate speech, sectarian discrimination, and vigilantism, and insisting that disputes be resolved through peaceful, lawful judicial processes.
3. **Engaging in community development and public problem-solving.**
   - Citizens have a responsibility to care for shared public spaces: conserving water, keeping neighbourhood streets clean, protecting parks, and preventing damage to public property such as trains and government buildings.
   - Taking part in local community bodies - such as School Management Committees (SMCs), parent-teacher associations, rural water user associations, and neighbourhood dispute resolution committees - strengthens democratic governance from the grassroots up.
4. **Practicing civic vigilance and holding authority accountable.**
   - A responsible citizen does not passively accept administrative corruption or abuse of power.
   - Using lawful mechanisms - such as filing requests under the Right to Information laws (Article 19A), lodging formal complaints through ombudsman offices, and attending public town hall hearings - helps keep public servants honest and responsive.

### The reciprocal balance between rights and responsibilities

Rights and responsibilities are not opposing forces; they are reciprocal and interdependent. As illustrated in the diagram above:

- **Rights create duties.** Every right you enjoy places a corresponding duty upon others. For example, your right to life and security (Article 9) creates a duty for all other citizens not to harm you, and a duty for the state to provide law enforcement. Your right to free speech (Article 19) creates a responsibility to speak truthfully and not violate the reputation or safety of your neighbours.
- **Duties sustain rights.** If citizens refuse their duties, rights quickly vanish. If citizens evade paying taxes, the government cannot fund public schools to fulfill Article 25A (the right to education) or maintain courts to deliver Article 10A (fair trials). If citizens refuse to obey traffic laws, the freedom of movement (Article 15) becomes impossible because roads turn dangerous and chaotic.
- **Avoiding extremes.** A society that emphasizes only rights without responsibilities becomes selfish, chaotic, and ungovernable. Conversely, an authoritarian state that enforces harsh duties upon citizens while denying them fundamental rights reduces free people to oppressed subjects. True constitutional citizenship thrives only when rights and duties remain in dynamic, mutually reinforcing balance.

   - What fundamental right is being claimed or exercised?
   - What corresponding civic duty or responsibility is being neglected or challenged?
   - What harm occurs if the balance between right and duty is broken?
   - What compromise or lawful solution restores the balance?

**Scenarios:**
- *Scenario 1:* A group of neighbourhood residents organises a peaceful demonstration demanding uninterrupted electricity. To draw maximum government attention, they block the main highway connecting a hospital and commercial market for six hours.
- *Scenario 2:* A wealthy industrialist earns substantial commercial profits from a textile mill but under-reports corporate earnings to evade municipal taxes, arguing that private wealth is protected by the Constitution (Article 24).
- *Scenario 3:* A vocal citizen uses an online social media group to expose local corruption, but repeatedly publishes unverified personal insults and false accusations against a teacher's family members.
- *Scenario 4:* A local community demands that the provincial government build a new girls' primary school, but community members refuse to donate a small plot of village land or serve on the volunteer School Management Committee to oversee construction.

## Summary

In a constitutional democracy, rights and responsibilities exist in inseparable balance. Under Article 5 of Pakistan's 1973 Constitution, loyalty to the State is the basic duty of every citizen, and obedience to the Constitution and law is the inviolable obligation of every citizen and resident. Legal duties, such as obeying the law and paying taxes, are compulsory and backed by statutory penalties. Voluntary civic responsibilities, such as voting thoughtfully under Article 51, respecting religious and cultural diversity, conserving public goods, and serving on school management bodies, are essential for democratic vitality. Rights cannot survive without responsibilities: every right enjoyed by one citizen depends upon corresponding duties fulfilled by others and by the state.

## Try this at your practicum school

In your practicum school, conduct a collaborative classroom exercise on rights and responsibilities:

1. **Classroom charter:** Work with a group of pupils in Class 6, 7, or 8 to co-create a two-column "Classroom Rights and Responsibilities Charter."
2. **Column matching:** In the left column, list four fundamental rights the pupils believe every child should enjoy in school (such as the right to be treated with dignity, to ask questions freely, to have a clean desk, and to play safely). In the right column, help pupils write the matching student responsibility for each right (such as not teasing others, listening respectfully to peers, keeping the classroom clean, and playing by fair rules).
3. **Observation:** Observe whether pupils take greater ownership of classroom discipline when they understand that their own freedoms depend on respecting their classmates' rights.
