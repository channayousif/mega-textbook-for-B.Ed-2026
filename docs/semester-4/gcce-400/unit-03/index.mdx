# Rights and Responsibilities

"Can a school turn away a child whose parents cannot pay fees? Does a citizen have to obey a law they disagree with?" Trainee teachers in Sindh hear questions like these whenever families speak about schooling, local taxes, or public rules. This unit examines the constitutional answers. In a democratic society, rights and responsibilities are not separate gifts; they are two sides of a single legal and moral compact. Without enforceable rights, citizens become vulnerable subjects. Without shared duties, freedoms collapse into disorder.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the meaning of fundamental rights and liberties and how they restrict arbitrary state power. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-3-1)*
- Locate key fundamental rights in Articles 8 to 28 of the Constitution of Pakistan 1973, including Articles 9, 10A, 14, 19, 25, and 25A. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-3-1)*
- Trace the main constitutional milestones in Pakistan from 1956 to the 1973 consensus and subsequent rights expansions. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-3-1)*
- Identify which fundamental right is at stake in concrete school and community scenarios and explain how it is protected. *(Apply; SLO:GCCE-400-3-1)*
- Describe the mandatory legal duties of citizens under Article 5 of the Constitution. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-3-2)*
- Distinguish between compulsory legal duties and voluntary civic responsibilities in a democracy. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-3-2)*
- Analyse the reciprocal balance between rights and responsibilities, showing how fulfilling civic duties protects common freedoms. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-3-2)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1 introduced civics, the historical expansion of civic participation, and the dimensions of citizenship. Unit 2 examined the state, democratic government, and civil society. This unit builds directly on those ideas by studying the constitutional text that defines what Pakistani citizens can demand from the state and what they owe to their society.

## In this unit

1. [Fundamental rights in the Constitution of 1973](./topic-01) - the concept of fundamental rights and liberties, constitutional development, and the protections of Articles 8 to 28.
2. [The citizen's duties and responsibilities](./topic-02) - constitutional duties under Article 5, tax paying, law obedience, democratic participation, and balancing rights with duties.

## How to use this unit

Read the two topics in sequence. Each topic opens with a realistic situation from a school or community in Sindh, explains the constitutional concepts in plain language, and provides a worked example before asking you to apply the principles.

Complete the collaborative activity in each topic with your peers. Test your retention with the "Check your understanding" questions before reviewing the text, verify your progress against the self-assessment checklist, and undertake the practicum application task in your placement school.

After finishing both topics, work through the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It contains 10 multiple-choice questions, 10 restricted-response questions, and 5 extended-response tasks. Attempt every question before consulting the model answers and rubrics in the final section. Teacher educators will find instructional sequences and practical suggestions in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
