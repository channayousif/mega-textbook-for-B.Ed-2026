# Advocacy and Activism

<Figure id="fig-U5-7" kind="illustration" src="/img/figures/gcce-400/unit-05/fig-U5-7.webp" alt="A trainee teacher presents an educational advocacy plan on an easel to a group of teachers, parents and community members gathered in a Sindh community hall." />

"If our school lacks clean water or safe transport, who is responsible for changing it? Can teachers and parents do anything, or must we wait for higher authorities?" A B.Ed trainee in Sukkur asked this during a seminar on community participation. Many citizens feel powerless when confronted with community problems, believing that change requires either political connections or sudden street demonstrations.

This unit explores how citizens systematically identify problems, build public support, and achieve lasting improvements. You will examine how public opinion forms through reasoned discussion, how structured advocacy campaigns solve local problems, and how social action movements develop across history to protect democratic and constitutional rights.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Explain the concept of public discourse and how public opinion forms in a democratic society. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-5-1)*
- Distinguish genuine public opinion from temporary social media trends and media sensationalism. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-5-1)*
- Describe the role of advocacy in identifying and addressing community and educational issues. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-5-2)*
- Differentiate clearly among advocacy, lobbying, and protest, giving Pakistani examples of each. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-5-2)*
- Apply the six steps of the advocacy cycle to design a non-violent action plan for a school issue. *(Apply; SLO:GCCE-400-5-2)*
- Describe the four stages of a social movement: emergence, coalescence, bureaucratisation, and decline. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-5-3)*
- Analyse a documented Pakistani social movement, such as the Lawyers' Movement, tracing its development and constitutional impact. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-5-3)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 2 and 4 provide essential grounding on the structure of government, civil society, ethics, and community cohesion. Trainees should also recall the fundamental rights guaranteed by the Constitution of Pakistan (1973), particularly Article 16 (peaceful assembly), Article 17 (freedom of association), Article 19 (freedom of speech), and Article 25A (right to free and compulsory education).

## In this unit

1. [Public discourse and public opinion](./topic-01) - what public discourse is, how public opinion develops through social institutions, and why online trends do not equal public consensus.
2. [Advocacy for social issues](./topic-02) - defining advocacy, distinguishing it from lobbying and protest, and applying the six-step advocacy cycle to local community challenges.
3. [Social action movements](./topic-03) - the nature and lifecycle stages of social movements, non-violent discipline, and historical lessons from Pakistan's Lawyers' Movement.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Each topic starts with a realistic vignette from a Pakistani school or community, explains the core concepts clearly, provides concrete examples, and walks through a worked example before asking you to complete tasks.

Engage in the collaborative activities with your peers. Complete the "Check your understanding" retrieval items before checking reference texts. Use the self-assessment checklists to evaluate your understanding, and try the practicum tasks during your school placement.

After finishing all three topics, complete the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It includes 10 multiple-choice questions, 10 restricted-response questions, and 5 extended-response questions. Comprehensive model answers and rubrics are located in the final section of that page. Instructors will find additional pedagogical suggestions and a practical workshop plan in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
