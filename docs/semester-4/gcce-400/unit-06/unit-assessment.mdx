# Digital Citizenship and Technology - Assessment and Review

## Unit summary

Digital citizenship is taking part in digital society safely, ethically and constructively. It is the online side of the citizenship you met in Unit 1. Digital platforms carry civic engagement in forms such as e-petitions, citizen portals, online campaigns, community groups and online volunteering. These forms use rights the Constitution already gives, such as freedom of speech and expression and the right to form associations, and they make participation faster, wider and cheaper. They also spread misinformation, harden echo chambers, expose personal data and leave out people who cannot get online.

Cyber ethics is ordinary ethics applied online: honesty, respect, responsibility, respect for privacy and self-protection. Misinformation spreads fast, and experiments in Pakistan show that specific, repeated feedback helps people spot it, while general warnings do not. Nothing posted online is truly private, and sharing a false or harmful post is an act for which the sharer is responsible. A five-check routine, source, date, evidence, harm and decision, turns that responsibility into a habit.

The digital divide is the gap between those who can use digital tools and the internet well and those who cannot. It has four kinds: access, usage, socioeconomic and geographic. Studies in Pakistan report that ICT infrastructure and access are the leading determinants, and that online education and services widen an already existing divide. Because so much civic life now runs online, the divide decides who can take part, who can be heard and who can reach public services. Access has grown, but it is still unequal.

## Summative assessment

Attempt every question before you read the final guidance section. Each MCQ has one best answer. For restricted responses, keep to the length stated. For extended responses, build an argument with evidence, not a list of definitions. All names and places in the questions are examples for practice.

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit, digital citizenship is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) Owning a smartphone and a social media account
   - B) Taking part in digital society safely, ethically and constructively
   - C) Spending a fixed number of hours online each week
   - D) Knowing how to make a video go viral
2. What can a citizen do on the Pakistan Citizen's Portal? *(Remember)*
   - A) Register a complaint and track its progress
   - B) Apply for a new identity card
   - C) Pay school fees to a private school
   - D) Vote in a general election
3. Khalid and Ahmad (2026) found that social media use predicted civic engagement among Pakistani university students, and that this link was partly explained by: *(Understand)*
   - A) The cost of mobile data
   - B) Digital literacy
   - C) The number of followers a student has
   - D) The type of phone a student owns
4. A community group posts a video of a blocked drain and tags the district administration, asking residents to report similar problems. Which form of digital civic engagement is this mainly? *(Apply)*
   - A) An online campaign
   - B) An e-petition
   - C) A citizen portal
   - D) Online volunteering
5. Cyber ethics is best described as: *(Remember)*
   - A) The technical rules that stop a phone from crashing
   - B) The principles that guide right and wrong conduct online
   - C) The law that sets the price of mobile data
   - D) The skill of making a post look attractive
6. Which of these is an example of misinformation? *(Remember)*
   - A) A newspaper report that a school won a competition
   - B) A government notice that a road will close for repair
   - C) A forwarded message that a school is closing, with no source
   - D) A teacher's message to parents about a parents' meeting
7. A post about a public issue has no named source. What is the best first step before sharing it? *(Apply)*
   - A) Share it quickly so people can warn others
   - B) Check the source and look for a reliable confirmation
   - C) Change the wording so it sounds more true
   - D) Delete it without reading it
8. Which of these lists the four kinds of digital divide described in this unit? *(Remember)*
   - A) Access, usage, socioeconomic and geographic
   - B) Age, height, weight and income
   - C) Urban, rural, coastal and mountain
   - D) Reading, writing, arithmetic and science
9. Shair et al. (2022) reported that the leading determinants of the digital divide in Pakistan are: *(Understand)*
   - A) ICT infrastructure and access to the technology
   - B) A national ban on social media
   - C) High literacy in rural areas
   - D) Lack of interest in technology among young people
10. A girl in a town has a phone, but her family will not let her use it after Class 8. Which kind of divide is this mainly? *(Apply)*
   - A) Access divide
   - B) Usage divide
   - C) Socioeconomic divide
   - D) Geographic divide

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Define digital citizenship in one sentence, and name three forms of digital civic engagement. *(Understand)*
2. In three sentences, explain two opportunities and two risks of digital platforms for civic participation. *(Understand)*
3. A classmate says, "Posting about a problem online is not real civic engagement." Analyse this claim in three or four sentences, using one Pakistani example. *(Analyze)*
4. Explain in three sentences how Article 19 and Article 17 of the Constitution of Pakistan connect to digital civic engagement. *(Understand)*
5. In three sentences, explain why sharing a false post is an ethical act for which the sharer is responsible. *(Understand)*
6. List the five checks you should make before sharing a post about a public issue, and explain in one sentence what the last check asks. *(Understand)*
7. A WhatsApp message says a government school in your district is closed this week. It has no source, the photo is from another year, and the education office says no closure is planned. Analyse, in three or four sentences, what the five checks show and what you should do. *(Analyze)*
8. Define the digital divide in one sentence and name its four kinds. *(Understand)*
9. According to Shair et al. (2022), what are the leading determinants of the digital divide in Pakistan, and what other factors did they find? *(Understand)*
10. Analyse, in three or four sentences, how the digital divide changes who can take part in civic life. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. **Are digital platforms making civic engagement stronger in Pakistan?** Evaluate this claim in 300-400 words. Use the opportunities and risks from Topic 6.1, the evidence of Khalid and Ahmad (2026), and at least one Pakistani example. Reach a reasoned judgement. *(Evaluate)*
2. **Design a classroom routine for responsible sharing.** Create a 40-minute Class 8 lesson that teaches the five checks and gives pupils one real practice task, such as checking a sample post. In 300-400 words, give the learning aim, the steps, the practice task, and explain how the routine builds one ethical principle. *(Create)*
3. **Is the digital divide the main barrier to citizenship in your area?** Evaluate this claim in 300-400 words. Use the four kinds of divide, the barriers reported by Shair et al. (2022) or Iftikhar et al. (2023), and local evidence you know. Propose one action and defend it. *(Evaluate)*
4. **A town, three citizens.** Read the three short cases and analyse, in 300-400 words, how digital platforms, cyber ethics and the digital divide meet in them. For each case, name the main issue and say what a responsible citizen would do. *(Analyze)*
   - A union council posts a notice about a vaccination camp only on its Facebook page. Rukhsana, a daily-wage worker, has no data package and hears about the camp after it ends.
   - A message in the camp's WhatsApp group says the vaccine is harmful. It has no source, and a health worker asks the group to share it "to warn people".
   - A boy in the town films the empty camp and posts it online, tagging the health department, which then sends a mobile team the next day.
5. **Plan a digital-citizenship activity for your practicum school.** Create a half-day plan that combines one idea from each topic: a digital platform for a real school problem, the five checks for a post about it, and a way to include a family that is offline. In 350-450 words, give the aim, the steps, the roles of pupils and parents, and justify your choices. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

Use this section only after attempting the questions. It explains the reasoning behind each answer, so that you can check and improve your own. Your teacher may use different marks for formal assessment.

### MCQ answer key

1. **B.** Digital citizenship is taking part in digital society safely, ethically and constructively. Owning a phone, hours online and viral videos are not the definition.
2. **A.** The Pakistan Citizen's Portal lets a citizen register a complaint and track it. Identity cards, private fees and voting are handled elsewhere.
3. **B.** Digital literacy partly explained the link between social media use and civic engagement. Data cost, followers and phone type were not the mediator.
4. **A.** A group putting an issue in public view through posts and tags is an online campaign. No petition is signed, no portal is used and no unpaid service is given.
5. **B.** Cyber ethics is the principles that guide right and wrong conduct online. The other options are technical, commercial or visual skills.
6. **C.** A forwarded message with no source is misinformation. The other options are reported or official information.
7. **B.** The first check is the source, so the best step is to check it and look for reliable confirmation. Sharing quickly, changing wording and deleting unread do not help.
8. **A.** The four kinds are access, usage, socioeconomic and geographic. The other lists are not divides.
9. **A.** Shair et al. name ICT infrastructure and access to the technology as the leading determinants. The other options are not their findings.
10. **C.** A family rule that limits a girl's use is a socioeconomic divide, shaped by income and gender. She has a phone, so access is not the main barrier.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

1. **Model:** Digital citizenship is taking part in digital society safely, ethically and constructively. Forms: an e-petition for a school roof, a complaint on the Pakistan Citizen's Portal, an online campaign for clean water, a WhatsApp community group, online volunteering for flood relief. **Marks (4):** 1 for the definition; 3 for three correct forms (1 each).
2. **Model:** Opportunities: fast and wide reach, low cost, photo and video evidence, and a record of who responded. Risks: misinformation spreads quickly, echo chambers harden views, people without access are excluded, and posts can expose personal data. **Marks (4):** 2 for two opportunities (1 each); 2 for two risks (1 each).
3. **Model:** The claim is partly right: a like or share can be shallow, and a post is not the same as showing up or doing the work. But it is wrong to say online action is not civic. Registering a complaint on the Pakistan Citizen's Portal creates a record the government must answer, and a video of a blocked drain can move an official to act. So online action is real civic engagement, but it is often only the first step. **Marks (4):** 1 for the grain of truth; 1 for why it is wrong; 1 for a correct example; 1 for the balanced conclusion.
4. **Model:** Article 19 gives freedom of speech and expression, and Article 17 gives the right to form associations, both subject to reasonable limits set by law. Digital platforms are new ways to use these rights: posting an opinion, forming a group and campaigning online. **Marks (3):** 1 for each article; 1 for linking them to digital platforms.
5. **Model:** Sharing publishes a post to your own network and lends it your name. If the post is false, you spread the falsehood; if it is harmful, you help it reach the people it can hurt. So the sharer is responsible, not only the writer. **Marks (3):** 1 for sharing as publication; 1 for the consequence; 1 for responsibility.
6. **Model:** Check the source, check the date, check the evidence, consider the harm, then decide. The last check asks what the post could do to a person or community before you share it. **Marks (3):** 2 for all five checks (1 for three or four, 2 for all five); 1 for the harm check explained.
7. **Model:** The source check fails because the message has no source. The date check fails because the photo is from another year. The evidence check fails because the education office says no closure is planned. The harm check shows parents may keep children home and panic. The decision is to pause, not share, and to post the office's correction. **Marks (4):** 1 for each check applied; 1 for the decision.
8. **Model:** The digital divide is the gap between people who can use digital tools and the internet well and people who cannot. Its four kinds are access, usage, socioeconomic and geographic. **Marks (4):** 1 for the definition; 3 for the four kinds (1 for three, 3 for all four).
9. **Model:** Shair et al. report that the leading determinants are ICT infrastructure and access to the technology. They also find socioeconomic, demographic, regional and supply-side factors. **Marks (3):** 1 for infrastructure; 1 for access; 1 for the other factors.
10. **Model:** Because so much civic life runs online, a citizen who is offline cannot sign a petition, track a complaint, read a health message or join a school group. The divide follows income, education, gender, age and place, so it deepens existing inequality. **Marks (4):** 1 for one concrete effect; 1 for two effects; 1 for the structured pattern; 1 for the conclusion about inequality.

### ERQ rubrics

Each task uses four criteria worth **0-3 marks each**, totalling 12. Across criteria, 3 means accurate, developed and supported; 2 means mostly accurate with an incomplete link; 1 means a relevant point with little explanation; 0 means absent, wrong or unrelated. Plain, short sentences can earn full marks.

**ERQ 1: Are digital platforms making civic engagement stronger in Pakistan?**

| Criterion | 3 marks | 2 marks | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opportunities and risks | Uses two opportunities and two risks accurately with examples | Uses two or three, or examples are thin | Names one | Absent |
| Use of evidence | Uses Khalid and Ahmad (2026) to support a point, with the dual effect explained | Mentions the study with a weak link | Names the study only | Absent |
| Evaluation (Evaluate) | Reaches a reasoned judgement that weighs stronger engagement against the risks | Gives a judgement with partial reasons | States agreement or disagreement only | No judgement |
| Pakistani examples | Two or more apt, specific examples | One apt example | General examples | None |
