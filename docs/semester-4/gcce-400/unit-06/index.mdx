# Digital Citizenship and Technology

"Sir, if I post a photo of the broken wall, will the education office really come?" A Class 9 pupil in Thatta asked this after her school's boundary wall collapsed. Her question is the question of this unit. Digital tools have become a normal part of public life in Pakistan, from school WhatsApp groups to government complaint apps. They let citizens speak, organise and demand answers faster than before. They also carry new risks: false news travels quickly, personal data can leak, and millions of Pakistanis still cannot get online at all.

This unit has three topics. The first looks at how digital platforms carry civic engagement, and what they make easier and harder. The second looks at cyber ethics: how to use social media responsibly, and how to decide what to share. The third looks at the digital divide in Pakistan, and asks who is included in digital citizenship and who is left out.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Define digital citizenship and describe how digital platforms are used for civic engagement. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-6-1)*
- Explain two opportunities and two risks of digital platforms for civic participation. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-6-1)*
- Explain what cyber ethics means and apply it to responsible use of social media. *(Apply; SLO:GCCE-400-6-2)*
- Use a short checklist to decide whether a post about a public issue is reliable enough to share. *(Apply; SLO:GCCE-400-6-2)*
- Describe the access, usage, socioeconomic and geographic digital divides in Pakistan and their effect on citizenship. *(Understand; SLO:GCCE-400-6-3)*
- Analyse how the digital divide includes some citizens and excludes others. *(Analyze; SLO:GCCE-400-6-3)*

## Prerequisite knowledge

GICT-300 Applications of ICT in Semester 1 gives the basic skills: using a smartphone or computer, going online, and handling files and messages. Units 1 and 5 of this course give the civic base: what citizenship means, the forms of civic engagement, and how advocacy and social action work. Bring your own experience of WhatsApp, Facebook, YouTube or a government app, and be ready to look at it with a citizen's eye rather than only a user's eye.

## In this unit

1. [Digital platforms and civic engagement](./topic-01) - what digital civic engagement is, the forms it takes, and the opportunities and risks of digital platforms.
2. [Cyber ethics and responsible social media](./topic-02) - the principles behind responsible online conduct, and a five-check routine for deciding what to share.
3. [Digital divides and citizenship in Pakistan](./topic-03) - the access, usage, socioeconomic and geographic divides, and what they do to citizenship.

## How to use this unit

Read the three topics in order. Each one opens with a real situation from a school or community in Sindh, explains the ideas, and shows a worked example before it asks you to use them.

Do the group activity in each topic with classmates. Answer the "Check your understanding" questions from memory before you look back. Use the self-assessment checklist honestly, and take the practicum task into a real school.

When you finish all three topics, attempt the [unit assessment](./unit-assessment). It has 10 multiple-choice, 10 short-answer and 5 extended questions. The answers and marking guidance are at the end of that page, so try every question first. Teachers will find planning help and a practical activity in the [notes for teachers](./unit-teacher-notes).
