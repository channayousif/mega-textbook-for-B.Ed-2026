# Digital Citizenship and Technology - Notes for Teachers

## Teaching strategies

- **Start from the learners' own screens.** Ask pupils which apps they use and what they have seen spread in a family group. The unit then gives names to what they already know.
- **Use the five checks as a routine, not a rule to memorise.** Write source, date, evidence, harm, decision on the board and run it on one real post in every class for a week.
- **Pair the online and the offline.** For every digital example, ask what the same action would look like in person. This keeps the unit from treating digital life as a separate world.
- **One discussion prompt that works:** "A post can be civic and still be careless. What is the difference?" Let pupils argue both sides before you summarise.
- **One likely wrong answer to work with:** "If I only shared it, it is not my fault." Ask the class what the writer would say, and what the person who received it would say. Then connect it to the idea that a share is publication.

## Sequencing

1. Topic 6.1 establishes what digital civic engagement is and the forms it takes.
2. Topic 6.2 turns to the learner's own conduct: ethics, privacy and the five checks.
3. Topic 6.3 widens the lens to who is left out, so pupils see that the first two topics depend on access.

## Likely misconceptions and how to handle them

- **"Anything posted online is private."** Have pupils test it: how many people could see a post after one forward? Then connect it to consent and to the class photo example.
- **"Sharing is not the same as saying it."** Use a short role-play: one pupil writes a false message, another shares it, and the class traces who spread it and who was harmed.
- **"Everyone in Pakistan now has internet access."** Ask pupils to name someone in their family or street who is offline, and why. Then use the four divides to classify the reasons.

## Practical work

The course guide suggests service-learning with a local organisation. For this unit, a short digital-literacy service task fits: pupils help parents or neighbours with one real task, such as reading an official message, checking a source, or using a government portal, with the school's consent rules followed. Any activity involving pupils or community members needs the school's permission and the consent of the people involved. Do not post anyone's name, photo or data without consent.
