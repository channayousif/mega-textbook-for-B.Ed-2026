# D01. Assessment, evaluation and testing defined

## What the test asks

A CRQ on this objective usually asks you to **define** two or more of the four terms
(measurement, testing, assessment, evaluation) and then **differentiate** or **illustrate**
them with a classroom example. Stronger questions ask you to **explain** how the terms are
related, for example "Every test is an assessment, but not every assessment is a test.
Explain."

Candidates lose marks in three predictable ways:

- giving four dictionary definitions with no example, so the examiner cannot tell whether
  you understand the difference;
- treating "assessment" and "testing" as synonyms, which is the exact confusion the course
  was written to correct;
- forgetting that **evaluation involves a judgement of value**, which is its defining feature.

- [EFMP-301 Topic 9.1 - Types and purposes of assessment](/semester-1/efmp-301/unit-09/topic-01)
  defines assessment as evidence gathered for a decision and shows that tests are only one
  kind of evidence. Use its "name the decision first" idea in every answer.
- [EFMP-301 Topic 9.2 - Psychological tests and measurement](/semester-1/efmp-301/unit-09/topic-02)
  explains what a test is (fixed tasks, fixed conditions, fixed scoring) and why a score is a

What the degree does not give you is a side-by-side set of definitions for all four terms,
the idea that they nest inside one another, and the definition of **evaluation**. That residue
is taught below.

## Explanation

### Four terms, four different jobs

The 2012 HEC Classroom Assessment guide opens its course by asking student teachers to
separate these four words, and it makes one point that is worth repeating in an exam: the
definitions of measurement and testing are fairly standard, but definitions of assessment and
evaluation vary from one expert to another (HEC, 2012, Unit 1). So in your answer, give a clear
working definition and then show you can use it.

**Measurement** is the process of assigning numbers to a characteristic according to a rule.
Counting that Sana answered 14 of 20 sums correctly is measurement. So is timing how long a
Class 2 child takes to read a passage aloud. Measurement answers the question *how much?* It
says nothing about whether 14 is good or bad.

**A test** is one particular instrument for obtaining a measurement: a set of tasks given
under set conditions and scored by set rules. **Testing** is the act of giving it. A test
produces a sample of behaviour, so a score is always an estimate of what the pupil can do, not
a full picture (Miller, Linn and Gronlund, 2013).

**Assessment** is the wider process of gathering and interpreting evidence about what pupils
know and can do, using any suitable method. Tests are one method. Others include observation,
questioning, oral work, projects, portfolios, homework and pupils' self-assessment. The guide's
central message is that assessment is a process that is more comprehensive than testing (HEC,
2012, Unit 1). Assessment answers the question *what has this pupil learned, and what should
happen next?*

**Evaluation** is the act of making a **judgement of worth or value** on the basis of the
evidence. Deciding that Sana's 14 out of 20 is "satisfactory for the end of the unit", or that a
new reading programme in a Hyderabad school "has worked", is evaluation. Evaluation can be about
a pupil, a lesson, a programme, a textbook or a whole school system.

### How the four terms nest

A useful way to remember the relationship is to picture four boxes, one inside another.

1. **Evaluation** is the outermost box: a judgement about value.
2. Inside it sits **assessment**: the collection and interpretation of evidence that the
   judgement rests on, by any method, with or without numbers.
3. Inside assessment sits **measurement**: the part of the evidence expressed as numbers by a
   rule, whether from a test, a rating scale, a checklist or a count of observed behaviour.
4. At the centre is **testing**: one particular, formal way of obtaining a measurement.

The nesting explains the model sentence examiners like: *every test is an assessment, but not
every assessment is a test.* A teacher who listens to a group explaining a science experiment
is assessing without testing. The reverse sentence also holds: *not all assessment involves
numbers.* A teacher's written note that a pupil "can now explain why the Moon seems to change
shape" is assessment evidence with no measurement in it.

### One lesson, four terms

Take one Class 4 mathematics lesson on fractions in a government primary school in Sukkur.

- The teacher gives a ten-item quiz at the end of the lesson. That is **testing**.
- She counts the right answers and records 6/10 for Asif. That is **measurement**.
- She also watched Asif during group work and noticed he could shade halves and quarters but
  confused thirds and fifths. Combining that observation with the quiz, she concludes that he
  understands unit fractions with small denominators only. That is **assessment**.
- At the end of the term she decides that Asif's progress in fractions is "below the expected
  level" and recommends extra practice. That is **evaluation**.

Notice that the same lesson produced all four, but each term names a different step.

### Why the precision matters in Pakistani classrooms

The guide frames the whole course as part of a slow transition "from an entirely test-based
culture for evaluating learning in school to an assessment-based culture" (HEC, 2012, Unit 1).
If a teacher thinks assessment simply means the monthly test, she will collect evidence only
once a month and only on paper. If she understands assessment as a continuous process, she
will also use questioning, observation and short tasks every day, and she will reserve formal
evaluation for the points where a real judgement has to be made. That is the practical reason
the licence syllabus asks you to define these words: the definitions change what a teacher
does.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

**Define** measurement, testing, assessment and evaluation, and **explain** with one classroom
example how the four are related. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 2

"Every test is an assessment, but not every assessment is a test." **Justify** this statement
with two examples of assessment that do not involve testing. *(15 marks)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Accurate definition of measurement (numbers assigned by a rule; answers "how much") | 2 |
| Accurate definition of a test/testing (set tasks, set conditions, set scoring; a sample of behaviour) | 2 |
| Accurate definition of assessment (wider process of gathering and interpreting evidence by many methods) | 3 |
| Accurate definition of evaluation (a judgement of worth or value based on evidence) | 3 |
| Explains the relationship (nesting: evaluation contains assessment, which contains measurement, of which testing is one formal method) | 3 |
| One coherent classroom example that shows all four steps | 2 |

A full-mark answer defines all four terms precisely, states that evaluation is the only one
that involves a value judgement, shows the nesting, and walks one realistic Pakistani classroom
example through all four steps. Award no more than 8 marks for correct definitions with no
relationship or example.

### CRQ 2

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Explains why every test is an assessment (a test is one method of gathering evidence of learning) | 3 |
| Explains why assessment is wider (many methods; process, not event) | 3 |
| First example of non-test assessment, described clearly (e.g. observation checklist during group work) | 3 |
| Second, different example (e.g. oral questioning, portfolio, self-assessment, project) | 3 |
| Shows what decision each example could feed, or links to the test-based versus assessment-based culture | 3 |

Full marks require two genuinely different non-test methods, each tied to what the teacher
learns from it. Two examples of the same method (for example, two kinds of oral question) earn
the second example's marks only once.
