# D03. Formative and summative assessment

## What the test asks

The two key types of classroom assessment are **formative** and **summative**. Expect command
words such as **distinguish**, **compare**, **explain with examples**, or a scenario asking
you to **identify** which type a teacher is using and **justify** your answer.

Marks are usually lost by:

- defining the types by timing alone ("formative is during, summative is at the end") and
  missing the real test, which is **purpose and use of the results**;
- claiming that a particular instrument (a quiz, a test) is always one type;
- describing formative assessment with no step in which teaching actually changes.

## Key points

**Formative assessment** is assessment *for* learning. It happens while learning is still
under way, and its results are used to adjust teaching and to help pupils improve. The HEC
guide puts it simply: formative assessment is conducted during a lesson to get evidence about
learning that gives feedback to students and the teacher, which is then used to improve
teaching and learning (HEC, 2012, Unit 2). Typical tools: questioning, exit tickets, short
quizzes, observation, mini whiteboards, peer and self-assessment.

**Summative assessment** is assessment *of* learning. It comes after a period of instruction
and reports how well pupils mastered the material, whether they are ready for the next unit,
and what grades should be given (Seifert and Sutton, 2009). Typical tools: end-of-unit tests,
term examinations, board examinations, final projects.

**The one difference that matters.** The guide stresses that although experts list many
differences, there is only one significant one: the purpose for which the assessment is
undertaken and the way its data are used. Summative scores certify competence; formative
scores improve instruction and learning (HEC, 2012, Unit 1).

**Same instrument, different type.** A five-item quiz is formative if its results change
tomorrow's lesson, and summative if the marks go straight into the term record. A summative
test can later be turned into a formative assessment (by going through errors with the
class), but a formative assessment cannot be turned into a summative test (HEC, 2012, Unit 1).

**Quick comparison for a CRQ.**

| Point | Formative | Summative |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Improve learning and teaching | Certify, grade, report |
| Timing | During instruction | After instruction |
| Feedback | Descriptive, next steps | A mark, grade or rank |
| Stakes for the pupil | Low | Often high |
| Who acts on it | Teacher and pupil, immediately | School, parents, boards, later |

**Why it matters in Pakistan.** The National Professional Standards for Teachers give formative
and summative assessment a strand each within Standard 5 (Assessment), which the guide
reproduces (HEC, 2012, Unit 1). A teacher at the highest level of the standard uses formative
checks to change teaching during the lesson, and uses a variety of fair summative tools,
including self-made ones.

**Model example.** A Class 5 teacher in Mirpurkhas asks three questions on place value before
moving on, sees that half the class cannot read five-digit numbers, and re-teaches with place
value cards (formative). At the end of the month she gives a unit test and enters the marks in
the result register (summative).

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

**Distinguish** between formative and summative assessment, and **explain** why the same test
can serve either purpose. Use one classroom example. *(15 marks)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Accurate definition of formative assessment (during learning, used to improve teaching and learning) | 3 |
| Accurate definition of summative assessment (after instruction, used to certify, grade or report) | 3 |
| At least three further points of contrast (timing, feedback type, stakes, who uses results) | 3 |
| Explains that purpose and use of results, not the instrument, decide the type | 3 |
| Classroom example showing one instrument used both ways, or a summative test turned formative | 3 |

A full-mark answer makes purpose the deciding factor, shows a concrete instrument changing type
according to how its results are used, and notes that formative use needs a step where teaching
or learning actually changes. Timing-only answers earn at most 8.
