# D. Practice: assessment and test development

This page gives a full practice set for heading D in the format of the licence paper: constructed
response questions (CRQs) of 15 marks each and one case-study extended response question (ERQ) of
100 marks. Answer under timed conditions: about 5 minutes per CRQ and 50 minutes for the ERQ.
Then mark your work with the rubrics at the end.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

**Differentiate** between assessment and evaluation, and **explain** with one example why a teacher
needs both. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 2

A teacher returns Class 5 mathematics tests with only a mark out of 20 on each paper. **Critique**
this practice using research on feedback and **suggest** three changes. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 3

**Explain** the purpose of a table of specifications and **construct** one for a 20-item Class 4
science test covering three topics taught for 5, 3 and 2 periods, using the levels Remember,
Understand and Apply. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 4

For an item on a Class 8 test, 7 of the 10 pupils in the upper group and 7 of the 10 pupils in the
lower group answered right. **Calculate** the difficulty and discrimination indices, **interpret**
them, and **recommend** action. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 5

**Compare** analytic and holistic rubrics, and **justify** which you would use to give feedback on
Class 6 science project reports. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 6

The odd-even split-half correlation of a 30-item test is 0.60. **Calculate** the reliability of the
full test with the Spearman-Brown formula and **explain** two ways the teacher could raise it.
*(15 marks)*

### ERQ 1

**Case study: Government Boys and Girls Primary School, near Hyderabad**

Mr Imran teaches Class 5 in a government primary school near Hyderabad. His class has 54 pupils,
many of whom speak Sindhi at home and study science in Urdu. The school holds a monthly test and a
final examination; the monthly test counts towards promotion.

Mr Imran writes each monthly science test the night before. His latest test had 25 true-false
items, most copied word for word from the first chapter of the textbook, although he had taught three
chapters. The directions said only "Attempt all questions." He marked the papers, wrote a total on
each, and read the marks aloud in class from highest to lowest. Eleven pupils scored below 10 out of
25. Several parents complained that their children "knew the work but could not understand the
questions." Mr Imran keeps no record of which questions pupils got wrong, so he cannot say which
topics need re-teaching, and pupils receive no comments on their papers, only the total.

The head teacher has attended a district workshop on the National Professional Standards for
Teachers and wants the school to move towards "assessment for learning". She asks Mr Imran to
redesign next month's assessment of the unit on plants, which he will teach over eight periods. She
also asks him to suggest how the school could judge pupils' progress through the year more fairly
than by a single monthly score. He has no printing budget beyond the usual test paper, and the
classroom has no cupboards.

Answer all parts.

(a) **Identify** the weaknesses in Mr Imran's current test and marking practice, naming the principle
of testing each one violates. *(20 marks)*

(b) **Design** a table of specifications for a 20-item test on the plants unit, and **explain** how it
improves the test's validity. *(20 marks)*

(c) **Explain** how Mr Imran should reduce the language barrier for his pupils without lowering the
level of the science being tested. *(15 marks)*

(d) **Propose** two formative assessment strategies Mr Imran can use during the eight periods, with
the feedback he will give. *(20 marks)*

(e) **Evaluate** whether a portfolio or regular performance tasks would help the school judge progress
more fairly, given its constraints, and **recommend** a practical approach linked to NPST Standard 5.
*(25 marks)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Assessment defined: gathering and interpreting evidence to understand and improve learning | 3 |
| Evaluation defined: a judgement of worth or value based on evidence | 3 |
| Three distinct points of difference (purpose, timing, feedback type, scope, use of results) | 4.5 |
| One example showing both in the same classroom | 3 |
| Explains the need for both: evaluation depends on assessment evidence; assessment alone does not certify | 1.5 |

### CRQ 2

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| A bare mark gives no information on how to improve | 3 |
| Butler's finding: comments alone improved later performance, grades and grades-plus-comments did not | 3 |
| Three changes, each consistent with effective feedback (comment-only on some work, focus on two or three points linked to the objective, time in class to act, task-focused wording, group common errors), 3 marks each | 9 |

### CRQ 3

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Purpose: blueprint ensuring content validity, balanced coverage of content and thinking levels, guide for writing items | 4 |
| Row totals proportional to teaching time: 10, 6 and 4 items | 4 |
| Column totals stated and justified (e.g. Remember 6, Understand 8, Apply 6) | 3 |
| Cells filled so that every row and column adds up to 20 | 3 |
| Percentages or a note on professional judgement | 1 |

Any consistent distribution is accepted if the row totals follow teaching time and the grid adds up.

### CRQ 4

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| p = (7 + 7) / 20 = 0.70 | 3 |
| Interprets p: fairly easy item | 2 |
| D = (7 - 7) / 10 = 0.00 | 3 |
| Interprets D: no discrimination; strong and weak pupils did equally well | 3 |
| Recommends reviewing the item: check for clues, ambiguity or content everyone has mastered; revise or replace if the aim is to separate levels, keep if it checks an essential mastered objective in a criterion-referenced test | 4 |

### CRQ 5

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Analytic rubric: criteria scored separately, detailed feedback, slower | 3 |
| Holistic rubric: one overall judgement, faster, less diagnostic | 3 |
| Two further contrasts or appropriate uses | 3 |
| Justified choice: analytic for feedback on project reports, because it shows pupils which criterion to improve | 4 |
| Example criteria for the report (e.g. method, data, explanation) | 2 |

### CRQ 6

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Formula: r(full) = 2r / (1 + r) | 3 |
| Substitution: 2 × 0.60 / 1.60 = 1.20 / 1.60 | 3 |
| Result: 0.75 | 3 |
| Two valid ways to raise reliability (add more good items, remove ambiguous items, clearer scoring guides, standard conditions, items of moderate difficulty for the group), 3 marks each | 6 |

### ERQ 1

**(a) Weaknesses and violated principles (20 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Only one of three chapters sampled: violates alignment and representative sampling (content validity) | 4 |
| Items copied from the textbook and only true-false: recall only, guessing at 50 per cent, item type not matched to objectives | 4 |
| Vague directions: violates the principle of clear directions | 3 |
| Language load for Sindhi-speaking pupils: violates clarity and fairness; scores measure reading, not science | 4 |
| Marks read aloud in rank order with no feedback: violates ethical use and the use of results to improve learning | 3 |
| Test written in haste, no review: no planning or item review | 2 |

**(b) Table of specifications (20 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Content areas of the plants unit listed with teaching periods summing to eight | 4 |
| Items per content area proportional to periods, totalling 20 | 5 |
| Thinking levels as columns, with a justified distribution beyond recall | 5 |
| Every row and column adds up | 2 |
| Explains the gain in content validity: coverage matches teaching, levels match objectives | 4 |

**(c) Reducing the language barrier (15 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Simple sentences and familiar vocabulary; key science terms kept and taught beforehand | 4 |
| Directions read aloud, and in Sindhi where allowed, with a worked example item | 4 |
| Diagrams and labelled pictures to carry meaning | 3 |
| Explains that the science demand stays the same while irrelevant reading load is removed (validity) | 4 |

**(d) Formative strategies and feedback (20 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| First strategy suited to 54 pupils (e.g. exit slips, thumbs up/down, mini whiteboards, targeted questioning), with timing and link to a learning target, 6 marks each strategy | 12 |
| Feedback that is specific, actionable and task-focused for each strategy | 4 |
| States how the evidence will change teaching (regrouping, re-teaching) | 4 |

**(e) Portfolio or performance tasks, and recommendation (25 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Benefits of a portfolio for judging progress (growth over time, self-assessment, evidence for parents) | 5 |
| Benefits of performance tasks (skills tests miss, e.g. observing plant growth or measuring) | 4 |
| Constraints weighed honestly: 54 pupils, no cupboards, no printing budget, marking time | 5 |
| A practical recommendation (e.g. a slim growth portfolio of three dated pieces per term kept in a plastic sleeve or notebook, one short performance task per unit scored with a simple rubric, combined with monthly tests) | 6 |
| Explicit link to NPST Standard 5 strands (linked to outcomes, formative, varied fair summative tools, feedback and self-assessment) | 5 |

A 100-mark answer addresses every part, keeps to the case facts, and gives recommendations that can be
carried out in this school without new resources.
