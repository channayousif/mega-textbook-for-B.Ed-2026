# A. Practice: Methods of Teaching and Foundations of Education

This page follows the format of the licence paper's Part II: constructed-response questions (CRQs) worth
15 marks each and one extended-response case study (ERQ) worth 100 marks. Attempt each question under
time pressure before reading the rubric: about 5 minutes per CRQ and 50 minutes for the ERQ.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) A Class 6 teacher in Sukkur says: "Student-centred teaching is the modern way; direct
instruction should be abandoned." **Evaluate** this statement with reference to the purposes of
teacher-centred and student-centred instruction and the learning theories behind them. *(A01, A04)*

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) **Design** a 10-minute questioning sequence for a Class 5 lesson on "Why we should save
water". Include at least two closed and two open questions, label each with its Bloom's level, and
**explain** how you will use wait time and redirecting to involve the whole class of 50. *(A02, A05)*

### CRQ 3

(15 marks) **Explain** the five essential elements of cooperative learning and **show** how the STAD
model builds individual accountability and a fair incentive structure. *(A03)*

### CRQ 4

(15 marks) **Explain** John Dewey's principle of active learning and **compare** the role of the teacher
in a pragmatist classroom with the role of the teacher in an idealist classroom. *(A08)*

### CRQ 5

(15 marks) Using the functionalist, conflict and interactionist perspectives, **analyse** why children
from poor rural families in Sindh are more likely to leave school early. **Suggest** one action a teacher
can take in response to each perspective. *(A09)*

### CRQ 6

(15 marks) **Discuss** the obligations of the state and of the teacher toward religious minority pupils
in Pakistan, with reference to Islamic teaching and at least three articles of the Constitution. *(A07)*

### CRQ 7

(15 marks) **Write** a teaching philosophy statement of about 200 words for an elementary teacher. Name at
least one educational philosophy and one psychological perspective, and **justify** how they shape your
aims, methods and assessment. *(A08, A10)*

### ERQ 1

(100 marks) **Read the case and answer all parts.**

Government Boys and Girls Primary School, in a village near Badin, has 310 pupils and six teachers. Mr
Aslam has taught Class 5 for eighteen years. His class has 58 pupils. Every lesson follows the same
pattern: he reads the textbook aloud, explains difficult words, writes questions and answers on the
board, and the pupils copy them. Pupils who cannot recite the answers the next day are made to stand at
the back. He calls on the same six pupils in the front row because "the others never know". Eight of
his pupils are Hindu; during the Islamiat period they sit silently at the back because there is no
other arrangement.

A new teacher, Miss Samina, joins the school after completing her B.Ed. She organises her Class 4 into
groups of four, starts each unit with a question from the pupils' lives, and uses short quizzes and
pair work. Some parents complain that her class is noisy and that "children are playing, not studying".
The head teacher, who must prepare the school for a district assessment in three months, asks both
teachers to present a plan for improving learning. Mr Aslam says he has no time for "new methods"
because the syllabus must be finished. Miss Samina admits that some groups waste time and that one or
two pupils do all the work in each group.

The head also reminds staff that under the provincial licensing policy they will need to show evidence
of their professional development and practice against the national standards.

Answer the following:

**(a)** (15 marks) **Identify** the instructional approach Mr Aslam uses and the philosophy and learning
theory it reflects. **Evaluate** its strengths and weaknesses for his class.

**(b)** (20 marks) **Analyse** Mr Aslam's questioning and his treatment of pupils who cannot recite
answers, using ideas from questioning strategy and the interactionist perspective. **Recommend**
specific improvements.

**(c)** (20 marks) Miss Samina's group work has problems. **Diagnose** them using the essential elements
of cooperative learning and **design** one improved cooperative lesson (name the model and its steps)
for her Class 4.

**(d)** (15 marks) **Discuss** the situation of the Hindu pupils during Islamiat in the light of the
Constitution of Pakistan and Islamic teaching, and **state** what the school should do.

**(e)** (15 marks) **Propose** a plan that combines direct and indirect instruction so that both
teachers prepare pupils for the district assessment without abandoning higher-order learning. Include
two objectives at different Bloom's levels.

**(f)** (15 marks) **Explain** which National Professional Standards for Teachers each teacher should
focus on, and what evidence each could collect for licence renewal.

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Purposes | 4 | Direct instruction for knowledge and skills (lower-order); student-centred for understanding (higher-order). |
| Theories | 4 | Behavioural theory behind direct instruction; constructivist theory behind indirect instruction. |
| Evaluation | 5 | Rejects the claim with reasons: both are legitimate; choice depends on purpose and pupils; they can be combined. |
| Example | 2 | A realistic Class 6 example showing the two used together. |

### CRQ 2

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Question sequence | 6 | At least two closed and two open questions on the topic, rising from lower to higher order. |
| Bloom's labels | 3 | Accurate labels using one version of the taxonomy consistently. |
| Wait time | 3 | Pause of at least three seconds, longer for higher-order questions; think time or think-pair-share. |
| Redirecting and whole-class participation | 3 | Redirecting answers to other pupils; random selection after the pause; slates or hand signals. |

### CRQ 3

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Five elements | 7 | Positive interdependence, individual accountability, promotive interaction, social skills, group processing, each explained (1-2 marks each, maximum 7). |
| STAD steps | 4 | Teacher presentation, team study, individual quiz, improvement points, team recognition. |
| Accountability and incentive | 4 | Individual quizzes make each pupil accountable; improvement points let every pupil contribute fairly, so helping others pays. |

### CRQ 4

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Dewey's principle | 6 | Learning by doing and reflecting; education as living, not preparation; educative experience leads to growth; real problems. |
| Pragmatist teacher | 4 | Guide who arranges experiences and asks questions. |
| Idealist teacher | 3 | Moral and intellectual model who transmits great ideas through lecture and dialogue. |
| Explicit comparison | 2 | At least two direct contrasts. |

### CRQ 5

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Functionalist analysis | 3 | School sorts pupils; early leaving seen as a failure of socialisation or placement. |
| Conflict analysis | 3 | Poverty, language and cultural capital disadvantage poor pupils; schooling reproduces inequality. |
| Interactionist analysis | 3 | Labelling and low expectations push pupils out. |
| Three teacher actions | 6 | Two marks each, one matched to each perspective (for example, linking school to community roles; supporting language and resources; high expectations and fair questioning). |

### CRQ 6

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Islamic teaching | 4 | For example 2:256, 60:8, 109:6, 5:8; Bukhari 3166. |
| Constitution | 6 | Two marks each for three accurate articles, such as Article 20, Article 22(1), Article 22(3)(b), Article 36, or the Objectives Resolution under Article 2A. |
| Teacher's obligations | 5 | Equal respect and attention, no compulsory instruction in another faith, stopping mockery, fair assessment. |

### CRQ 7

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Named philosophy used accurately | 3 | Idealism, realism, pragmatism, critical theory or a justified mix. |
| Named psychological perspective used accurately | 3 | Behaviourist, constructivist, social cognitive or humanist. |
| Aims, methods and assessment | 6 | Each clearly derived from the named beliefs (2 marks each). |
| Coherence and context | 3 | A consistent, personal statement suited to an elementary classroom in Pakistan. |

### ERQ 1

Award up to 100 marks across the six parts.

**(a) 15 marks.**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Identifies teacher-centred direct instruction (recitation and copying) | 3 |
| Links it to behaviourist theory and to realist or idealist transmission of knowledge | 4 |
| Strengths: covers the syllabus, orderly, suits basic knowledge | 3 |
| Weaknesses: little higher-order thinking, passive pupils, memorisation without understanding, humiliation harms motivation | 5 |

**(b) 20 marks.**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Questioning faults: recall only, same six pupils, no wait time, no probing or redirecting | 6 |
| Interactionist analysis: labelling ("the others never know"), self-fulfilling prophecy, standing at the back as a public label | 6 |
| Recommendations: mix of question levels, wait time, random selection, think-pair-share, probing and prompting, ending the punishment, specific feedback | 8 |

**(c) 20 marks.**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Diagnosis: weak individual accountability, uneven positive interdependence, social skills not taught, no group processing | 8 |
| Named model with correct steps (Jigsaw, STAD or Think-Pair-Share) | 6 |
| Lesson design addresses the diagnosed problems (roles, individual quiz, quiet signal, checklist, reflection) and fits Class 4 | 6 |

**(d) 15 marks.**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Article 22(1): no pupil required to receive religious instruction of another faith; also Articles 20, 36 or the Objectives Resolution | 6 |
| Islamic teaching on freedom of belief and just treatment of non-Muslims | 4 |
| School action: a meaningful alternative activity, dignity and inclusion, communication with parents | 5 |

**(e) 15 marks.**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| A sequence combining direct instruction for core knowledge with indirect or cooperative work for understanding | 6 |
| Two objectives at different Bloom's levels, observable and realistic | 4 |
| Link to the district assessment without narrowing to rote learning | 5 |

**(f) 15 marks.**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Mr Aslam: for example standard 4 (instructional planning and strategies), 6 (learning environment), 5 (assessment), with reasons | 5 |
| Miss Samina: for example standard 6 (learning environment), 8 (collaboration and partnership, for parents' concerns), 9 (continuous professional development), with reasons | 5 |
| Evidence for renewal: lesson plans, observation records, pupil work samples, CPD records (the Sindh policy asks for evidence of CPD courses) | 5 |

A high-scoring answer uses the case details throughout rather than general statements, and keeps each
part within its marks.
