# A06. National Professional Standards for Teachers in Pakistan

## What the test asks

CRQs on the standards ask you to **list** or **explain** the ten standards, **describe** how each
standard is organised, **apply** a standard to a teacher's practice (which standard, which part, what
evidence), or **evaluate** how standards are used for licensing and appraisal. A case study may
describe a teacher and ask which standards she meets or falls short of, and what she should do next.

Marks go to naming the standards accurately, to the three-part structure (knowledge and understanding,
dispositions, performance and skills), and to linking a standard to observable classroom evidence.
Candidates lose marks by listing standards with no example, by confusing a standard with a code of
conduct or a curriculum, and by treating "meeting the indicator" as the goal rather than a sign of
good practice.

## Key points

- **What and when.** The National Professional Standards for Teachers in Pakistan were issued by the
  Ministry of Education in 2009, developed with support from UNESCO and USAID. The HEC course guide's
  foreword names them, with the National Curriculum and the 2010 scheme of studies, as a foundation of
  the B.Ed (Hons) and ADE programmes (its foreword dates them to 2008). They define what teachers
  should know, value and be able to do.
- **The ten standards.** (1) Subject matter knowledge; (2) human growth and development; (3) knowledge
  of Islamic ethical values and social life skills; (4) instructional planning and strategies; (5)
  assessment; (6) learning environment; (7) effective communication and proficient use of information
  and communication technologies; (8) collaboration and partnership; (9) continuous professional
  development and code of conduct; (10) teaching of English as a second or foreign language.
- **Three parts in every standard.** *Knowledge and understanding* (what the teacher knows),
  *dispositions* (attitudes and values), and *performance and skills* (what the teacher can do). The
  split matters because a teacher can know and care about assessment yet still be unable to write a
  good test item: three different shortfalls with three different remedies.
- **Indicators and evidence.** Beneath the parts sit indicators: specific, observable statements. A
  good indicator is observable, specific to its standard and achievable under real conditions. Evidence
  such as a seating map of who answered, samples of marked work or a lesson plan is collected against
  an indicator.
- **The trace.** For any practice, name the standard, the part, the indicator and the evidence. Example:
  only four pupils answer questions; standard 6 (learning environment); performance and skills;
  indicator "pupils across the room contribute"; evidence, a seating map with contributions marked.
- **Uses.** Standards guide teacher education programmes, self-evaluation, professional development,
  certification, licensing and appraisal. Certification asks whether a person was prepared; licensing
  asks whether the person is currently fit to practise; appraisal asks how the teacher is doing and
  what comes next.
- **Link to the Sindh licence.** Sindh's Teaching License Policy 2023 makes the licence valid for five
  years, renewable for another five on evidence of at least 5 credit hours of STEDA-approved
  continuous professional development courses (STEDA, 2023). The
  standards describe the practice the licence is meant to assure.
- **The caution.** An indicator is a sign of good practice, not the practice itself; chasing the sign
  empties the standard of meaning. One instrument used for both development and high-stakes decisions
  loses its developmental value, because teachers stop admitting weaknesses.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) **Explain** the three-part structure shared by every National Professional Standard for
Teachers in Pakistan. Choose **one** standard and, for a Class 5 teacher, give one example each of the
knowledge, disposition and performance it requires, and one piece of evidence the teacher could collect.

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) Miss Rubina, a Class 3 teacher in Thatta, plans every lesson from the textbook alone, never
checks what pupils understood before moving on, and seldom meets parents. **Identify** three
National Professional Standards her practice falls short of, **justify** each choice, and **suggest**
one improvement for each.

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Three parts explained | 6 | Knowledge and understanding, dispositions, performance and skills, each explained accurately (2 marks each). |
| Choice of a named standard | 1 | One of the ten, named correctly. |
| Three examples | 6 | A fitting example for each part in a Class 5 context (2 marks each). |
| Evidence | 2 | An observable piece of evidence tied to an indicator (for example marked work samples, a seating map, a lesson plan). |

### CRQ 2

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Three standards identified | 3 | For example standard 4 (instructional planning and strategies), standard 5 (assessment), standard 8 (collaboration and partnership). One mark each. |
| Justification | 6 | Each standard linked to the specific behaviour in the case (2 marks each). |
| Improvements | 6 | A practical, specific improvement for each (2 marks each), such as planning with objectives and varied strategies, using quick checks of understanding, and holding parent meetings. |

Accept other standards (for example 6, learning environment) where the justification fits the case.
