# E08. Working context of Pakistani teachers

## What the test asks

A CRQ on this objective asks you to **describe** the conditions Pakistani teachers work in, to
**differentiate** a teacher's traditional classroom role from non-traditional roles in the school and
community, or to **explain** how a teacher can act as a change agent, social activist, leader or link
between stakeholders. The HEC guide calls the teacher's role in school and community "the most important
unit of the course", so expect scenario questions: a village where girls leave school early, an SMC
that is run by one influential member, a teacher asked to lead an enrolment drive.

Marks are usually lost by:

- describing only classroom duties when the question asks about the community;
- treating "leader" as meaning the head teacher only;
- writing about change agency as grand reform instead of realistic steps a teacher can take;
- ignoring the conditions (workload, resources, competing demands) that limit what a teacher can do.

Those topics describe the working context and the teacher's roles mainly inside the school. The guide's

community, as a social activist, as a leader and as a link between stakeholders. That residue is taught
below.

## Explanation

### Traditional and non-traditional roles

The guide asks the instructor "to distinguish between teachers' traditional roles within the classroom
from non-traditional roles outside the classroom, such as the teacher as a community mobilizer, as a
social activist, or in a formal and informal leadership role" (HEC, 2012, Unit 7).

| Traditional roles (inside the classroom) | Non-traditional roles (school and community) |
|---|---|
| Planning and teaching lessons | Integral member of the community |
| Assessing and recording progress | Change agent in the community |
| Managing the class | Role model in community life |
| Caring for pupils' welfare in school | Social activist and community mobiliser |
| | Formal and informal leader |
| | Link between stakeholders |

### The teacher as an integral part of the community

In many Pakistani towns and villages the teacher is one of the few educated adults the whole community
knows. The guide asks student teachers to consider the "multidimensionality of community and community
membership": a teacher belongs to the local community, to a professional community of teachers, to an
academic community and, sometimes, to policy circles (Unit 6, session 25). Belonging brings trust, but
also scrutiny: a teacher's conduct outside school is watched and imitated.

### The teacher as a change agent in the community

EFMP-302 Topic 3.5 treats change agency inside the school. The guide extends it to the community, with a
focus on **inclusion**: student teachers prepare "a plan for promoting inclusion in schools" and
recommendations stressing "the role of teachers" (Unit 6, session 26). Its learning goal is to recognise
how "culture, gender, special needs, and equity and equality issues affect the school and the community".
Realistic community change includes persuading a family to let a daughter continue to middle school,
getting a child with a disability admitted, or encouraging the community to support a school safety
repair. It starts from standing and trust, not from confrontation.

### The teacher as a role model

The guide builds the profile of a good teacher from student teachers' memories of their own teachers and
links it to participation in community activities (session 27). Its culturally responsive pedagogy
reading adds that teachers "can be role models, demonstrating fairness and reminding students that
difference is normal" (Richards et al., 2006, in HEC, 2012). The guide's session 28 asks how teachers and
schools affect **individual and group behaviour**: a teacher who treats every community fairly, keeps
promises and takes part respectfully in local occasions models the behaviour the school hopes children
will learn.

### The teacher as a social activist and community mobiliser

A **social activist** teacher works beyond the classroom on issues that block children's education:
enrolment, girls' schooling, child labour, sanitation, or the functioning of the SMC. The guide assigns
Buzdar and Ali's study of primary teachers in Punjab as community mobilisers (Unit 7, session 31) and
asks student teachers to present cases covering "the community's socio-economic profile", "the issue
involved", "the teacher's role in identifying support, organizing groups, and finding resources", and
the role of community institutions. Those four headings are a strong structure for a CRQ answer.

The guide's session 29 adds a practical tool. Student teachers observe a PTA or SMC meeting, or read its
minutes, and draw a **sociometric diagram** of who speaks to whom, who leads, who decides and who sets
the agenda, and "consider the role of women in the meeting". Mapping the real pattern of influence is
the first step to changing it.

### The teacher as a leader

York-Barr and Duke (2004) define teacher leadership as "the process by which teachers, individually or
collectively, influence their colleagues, principals, and other members of school communities to improve
teaching and learning practices with the aim of increased student learning and achievement". The key
word is **influence**: leadership is a role, not only a position. The guide asks student teachers to
consider "if there is a difference between leadership positions and roles" (session 30).

- **Formal leadership**: head teacher, subject coordinator, SMC member, cluster trainer.
- **Informal leadership**: the teacher colleagues consult, the one who starts a reading club, organises
  an enrolment drive, or speaks for the school at a community gathering.

### The teacher as a link between stakeholders

The stakeholders around a school (pupils, parents, SMC, head, district officials, masjid and community
elders, NGOs, health workers) rarely meet each other. The teacher meets most of them. The guide's
session 31 calls this "establishing linkages among stakeholders", and Unit 6 invites a teachers'
association speaker on the role of "establishing linkages between and among major stakeholders, and
promoting home-school linkages". EFMP-302 Topic 5.2 shows why these stakeholders want different
things; the linking role is to bring them to a shared aim for the child.

**A Sindh illustration.** In a village school near Thatta, Miss Nasreen notices that several Class 5
girls do not continue to the middle school in the next village. She collects the facts (distance, no
safe transport, parents' concerns), raises them at the SMC, draws the head, two parent members and a
local elder into a small group, and with the district office's permission arranges for the girls to
travel together with an older relative. She has no formal title, yet she has acted as change agent,
social activist, informal leader and link between stakeholders, all within the working conditions
EFMP-302 Unit 5 describes.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

**Differentiate** between a teacher's traditional and non-traditional roles, and **explain** three
non-traditional roles with a Pakistani example of each. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 2

A primary teacher in rural Sindh finds that many pupils leave school after Class 5. **Explain** how she
can act as a social activist and a link between stakeholders to address this, considering the working
conditions that may limit her. *(15 marks)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Traditional roles described (classroom planning, teaching, assessing, managing) | 2 |
| Non-traditional roles described as roles outside the classroom in school and community | 2 |
| Clear point of difference (where the role is exercised; whom it serves; formal duty vs initiative) | 2 |
| Three non-traditional roles (integral community member, change agent in community, role model, social activist or mobiliser, formal or informal leader, link between stakeholders), each explained, 2 marks each | 6 |
| A relevant Pakistani example for each, 1 mark each | 3 |

A full-mark answer uses the guide's distinction explicitly and gives realistic examples within a
teacher's reach.

### CRQ 2

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Social activist role applied: identifies causes, gathers facts, raises the issue | 3 |
| Linking role applied: names at least three stakeholders (parents, SMC, head, elders, district, NGO) and how she brings them together | 4 |
| Concrete actions (home visits, SMC agenda item, working group, transport or timing solution, follow-up) | 4 |
| Working conditions considered (workload, lack of authority or resources, competing demands) with a realistic response | 2 |
| Uses the idea of leadership as influence rather than position | 2 |

Answers that only say "she should motivate parents" cannot score above 5.
