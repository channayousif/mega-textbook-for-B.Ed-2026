# E04. Main characteristics of culture

## What the test asks

A CRQ on this objective asks you to **define** culture and **explain** its characteristics, or to
**differentiate** between the elementary concepts of culture (trait, complex, pattern, lag,
diversity) with examples. Higher-mark questions ask you to **analyse** a school situation using these
concepts, for example a school that bought computers that nobody uses (cultural lag), or a class where
children from several communities are treated differently (cultural diversity).

Marks are usually lost by:

- confusing a cultural trait with a cultural complex;
- defining cultural lag as "backwardness" instead of a gap between material and non-material change;
- giving foreign examples when the question asks for Pakistani ones;
- treating cultural diversity as a problem to be removed rather than a condition to be managed fairly.

## Explanation

### What culture is

A classic definition, quoted in Encyclopaedia Britannica, is Tylor's: culture is "that complex whole
which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits
acquired by man as a member of society". OpenStax puts it more simply: a culture "represents the
values, beliefs, norms, language, symbols, and practices of a group, while society represents the
people who share a culture" (Conerly et al., 2021, Ch. 3).

Culture has two broad parts (Conerly et al., 2021, Section 3.1):

- **Material culture**: things people make and use, which can be touched. Examples: a charpai, an
  ajrak, a school building, a mobile phone, a textbook.
- **Non-material culture**: the ideas, attitudes and beliefs of a society. Examples: respect for
  elders, the value placed on hospitality, beliefs about the education of girls, the rule that pupils
  stand when a teacher enters.

### Main characteristics of culture

Drawing on the sources above, the characteristics a licence answer should cover are:

1. **Culture is learned, not inherited.** Children acquire it through socialization in the family,
   school and community (see E03). A child of Sindhi parents raised in Lahore learns Punjabi ways.
2. **Culture is shared.** It belongs to a group, not to one person. A private habit is not culture;
   a custom a whole community follows is.
3. **Culture is transmitted from generation to generation**, mainly through language. OpenStax calls
   language the symbol system "through which culture is transmitted". Schools are one of the main
   channels of transmission.
4. **Culture is symbolic.** Gestures, objects and words carry shared meanings; the national flag or a
   greeting only mean what the culture has agreed they mean.
5. **Culture is dynamic.** It changes through discovery, invention and diffusion (borrowing from other
   cultures), although different parts change at different speeds.
6. **Culture is integrated.** Its parts fit together, so a change in one part (for example, mobile
   phones) affects others (family communication, how pupils study).
7. **Culture varies from society to society**, and within a society. Judging another culture by one's
   own standards is **ethnocentrism**; assessing a culture by its own standards is **cultural
   relativism** (Conerly et al., 2021, Section 3.1).
8. **Culture regulates behaviour** through values and norms. **Values** are what a culture regards as
   good and important; **norms** are the rules of behaviour that follow from them. Norms may be
   **formal** (written rules, such as a school's uniform policy) or **informal** (unwritten
   expectations), and include **mores** (with moral weight) and **folkways** (everyday customs without
   moral weight) (Conerly et al., 2021, Section 3.2).

### Elementary concepts of culture

The HEC guide names five concepts and asks student teachers to illustrate each with Pakistani
examples (HEC, 2012, Unit 3, sessions 11-12).

**Cultural trait.** The trait is "generally regarded as the unit of culture" (Encyclopaedia Britannica). A
trait may be an object, a way of doing something, a belief or an attitude. Examples: an ajrak; eating
with the right hand; the belief that knowledge is a virtue; greeting with *Assalam-o-Alaikum*.

**Cultural complex.** A "distinguishable and relatively self-contained cluster of traits is
conventionally called a culture complex" (Encyclopaedia Britannica). The traits hang together around one
activity. Example: a wedding (mehndi, baraat, walima, the clothes, the food, the songs and the roles
of relatives) is a complex, not a single trait. The school morning assembly is another: recitation,
national anthem, announcements, lining up by class.

**Cultural pattern.** When complexes are organised into a meaningful whole, they form a pattern that
gives a way of life its overall shape. Britannica notes Clark Wissler's "universal culture pattern":
every culture has the same general categories, such as language, art, social organisation, religion
and technology, though each fills them differently. In classroom terms, the pattern is the typical way
of life of a community, for example the daily and seasonal rhythm of a farming village, in which work,
family roles, religious observance and schooling are fitted together.

**Cultural lag.** Following Ogburn (1922), "culture lag refers to the time that passes between the
introduction of a new item of material culture and its social acceptance" (Conerly et al., 2021,
Section 3.3). Material culture usually changes faster than the non-material culture that has to make
sense of it. School examples:

- A school receives a computer lab, but the timetable, teacher skills and rules about pupils touching
  equipment do not change, so the machines stay locked.
- Pupils have smartphones, but the norms for responsible use and online safety have not yet been
  developed in the family or school.

**Cultural diversity.** Diversity is the presence of different cultures (languages, religions,
ethnicities, customs, social classes) within one society or one classroom. The guide asks student
teachers whether there was diversity in their own classrooms, whether students were "treated
differently by teachers and school staff", and whether boys and girls were treated differently (Unit
3, session 12). Its reading warns that a school culture that does not welcome difference can make
pupils of "another race, tribal group, religion, or social class" feel inferior "in hundreds of small
ways" (Schoonmaker, in HEC, 2012, Faculty Resources).

### Culture and the school

The same reading describes each school as having its own **school culture**: "the norms, values,
procedures, assumptions, beliefs, traditions, and expectations of those within". Two schools in the
same community can have different cultures. Teachers are shaped by the school's culture and can also
change it. This links the concepts above to teaching:

- recognising traits and complexes helps a teacher understand what pupils bring from home;
- recognising cultural lag helps a teacher plan the norms and training that new technology needs;
- recognising diversity, and avoiding ethnocentrism, helps a teacher treat every pupil fairly.

**A Sindh illustration.** A Class 7 teacher in Sukkur asks pupils to bring one object that belongs to
their family's culture. One brings an ajrak, one a Balochi embroidered cap, one a Christmas card from a
neighbour, one a photograph of an Eid gathering. She sorts them on the board into traits, then asks
which ones belong to a larger complex (Eid, a wedding). The lesson ends with the class naming a
cultural lag in their own school.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

**Explain** five characteristics of culture, giving a Pakistani example for each. *(15 marks)*

### CRQ 2

**Differentiate** between a cultural trait, a cultural complex and cultural lag, and **analyse** one
situation in a Pakistani school that shows cultural lag. *(15 marks)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Five distinct characteristics (learned, shared, transmitted, symbolic, dynamic, integrated, varies, regulates behaviour), each accurately explained, 2 marks each | 10 |
| A relevant Pakistani example for each, 1 mark each | 5 |

Full marks need explanation, not only a heading. "Culture is learned" earns 1 mark alone and 2 marks
when the answer says how (through socialization in family, school and community).

### CRQ 2

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Cultural trait defined as the smallest unit of culture, with example | 2.5 |
| Cultural complex defined as a cluster of related traits around one activity, with example | 2.5 |
| Clear distinction between trait and complex | 1 |
| Cultural lag defined as the gap between change in material culture and adjustment of non-material culture (Ogburn) | 3 |
| Analysis of a school situation: names the material change, the non-material part that has not changed, the consequence, and a way to close the gap | 6 |

An answer that defines cultural lag as "backwardness" earns no marks for that criterion.
