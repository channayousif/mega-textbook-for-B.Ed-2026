# C. Practice: Classroom Management

These questions follow the format of Part II of the Sindh Teaching Licence test: constructed-response
questions (CRQs) worth 15 marks each, and one extended-response case study (ERQ) worth 100 marks.
Attempt each question in writing, under time, before you read the marking guidance at the end. Allow
about 12 minutes per CRQ and 45 minutes for the ERQ.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) A teacher believes that "learning is the teacher transferring knowledge to the pupil."
**Analyse** how that belief would shape the teacher's management of routines, seating and responses to
pupils' errors, and **identify** which learning theory it draws on most heavily. *(C01)*

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) A trainee's observation record reads: "The teacher praised the pupils who answered
correctly and moved on when a pupil gave a wrong answer." **Write** two defensible inferences from this
observation, **explain** why each might be wrong, and **describe** what further data you would collect to
decide between them. *(C03)*

### CRQ 3

(15 marks) Take the objective "pupils will be able to add two-digit numbers with carrying." **Plan** it
for three settings: a single-grade class of 25, a multigrade room with Classes 2 to 4, and a single-grade
class of 60. For each, **state** one curriculum or management decision that changes because of the room
and **explain** why. *(C05, C10)*

### CRQ 4

(15 marks) A teacher says: "I told my class the routine on the first day, so now they know it."
**Explain** what this overlooks, using the course guide's account of how routines are established, and
**design** a routine for the transition from whole-class explanation to group work, including how you would
teach it. *(C06)*

### CRQ 5

(15 marks) A school in Badin invites parents to an annual prize day and asks them for donations, but
never consults them about anything else. **Distinguish** between community participation and community
involvement, and **propose** two practices that would move this school towards involvement, **justifying**
each. *(C07)*

### CRQ 6

(15 marks) **Evaluate** the claim that "a caring response to classroom disruption is a weak response."
Refer to the difference between punitive and restorative consequences and to the six-step response to a
behaviour breakdown. *(C07, C09)*

### ERQ 1

(100 marks)

**Case study.** Miss Shazia has just been posted to a government primary school in a village near
Mirpurkhas. She teaches Classes 3 and 4 together in one room: 52 pupils on fixed benches built for 35,
one blackboard, a locked cupboard of science materials, and walls with a few faded commercial charts.
The previous teacher kept order mainly by shouting and by making pupils stand outside the room. On her
first day Miss Shazia notices that the same five Class 4 boys in the front row answer every question,
while the back benches, most of them Class 3 girls, say nothing all day. When she tries group work in
her second week, pupils argue over who holds the single textbook, noise rises quickly, and two boys
start pushing each other. A Class 3 girl who has recently moved from Karachi finishes every task within
minutes and then chats with her neighbours. Some parents have told the head teacher they are unhappy
that their daughters are "not learning anything" and are thinking of keeping them at home. The head
teacher has asked Miss Shazia for a written plan to improve management in her room over the next term.
She wants to avoid returning to the shouting and exclusion her pupils are used to, but she is not sure
where to start, and she has no budget.

Answer all parts.

**(a)** Using the course guide's definition of classroom management, **explain** why the previous
teacher's classroom was not well managed even if it was quiet. (15 marks)

**(b)** **Identify** four features of Miss Shazia's physical and social environment that are causing
management problems, and **propose** a no-cost change for each. (20 marks)

**(c)** **Design** a schedule for one 40-minute lesson for her multigrade room and **describe** two routines,
one of them for group work, that would make it run. (20 marks)

**(d)** **Explain** how Miss Shazia could differentiate one lesson objective for the early finisher and
for pupils who are struggling, without lowering the target. (15 marks)

**(e)** Two boys push each other during group work. **Apply** a caring, accountable response and
**justify** it against sending them outside. (15 marks)

**(f)** **Suggest** how Miss Shazia could involve the concerned parents so that they become partners
rather than critics. (15 marks)

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

Points-based rubric, 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| Routines | 3 | Teacher-centred routines for attention and silence; little pupil movement or talk |
| Seating | 3 | Rows facing the teacher; pupils as audience |
| Responses to error | 3 | Correction by the teacher and re-practice; errors treated as failures rather than information |
| Theory identified | 3 | Mainly behaviourist (teacher as stimulus source, reinforcement of correct responses), with some cognitivist transmission features |
| What the belief leaves out | 3 | Pupil construction, discussion, checking understanding; notes the guide's view that the best-managed classroom engages each learner in constructing knowledge |

A full-mark answer shows the belief producing a consistent pattern across all three areas and names
the theory with reasons.

### CRQ 2

Points-based rubric, 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| Two inferences | 4 | Plausible, cautiously worded (e.g. "the teacher may value correct answers over reasoning"; "the teacher may have chosen not to embarrass the pupil") |
| Why each might be wrong | 4 | Alternative explanations for the same behaviour; one lesson is limited evidence |
| Further data | 5 | Specific tools: tally of teacher responses to errors across several lessons, interview with the teacher, a second observer or classroom |
| Observation vs inference | 2 | States that the record is evidence and the inferences are interpretations |

A full-mark answer keeps inference separate from evidence and names concrete further data that would
test the inferences.

### CRQ 3

Points-based rubric, 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| Single-grade class of 25 | 4 | e.g. small groups with concrete materials; teacher can circulate to every group |
| Multigrade Classes 2-4 | 5 | Same broad skill at levels suited to each class; anchor activity for the groups not with the teacher; peer tutoring; adapted timetable |
| Class of 60 | 4 | Whole-class anchor problem with tiered follow-up; pair work; quick checks with slates |
| Target kept constant | 2 | Explicitly keeps the objective while changing the route |

A full-mark answer shows a real change in decisions for each room and explains why the room forces it.

### CRQ 4

Points-based rubric, 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| What the claim overlooks | 5 | Routines must be taught, demonstrated, practised with feedback and re-taught until habitual, especially in the first two weeks |
| Transition routine designed | 6 | Clear signal, time limit, specific steps (e.g. turn to the bench behind, one pupil collects materials, roles assigned), a start prompt |
| How it is taught | 4 | Model, rehearse, give feedback, re-practise, praise success, re-teach after holidays |

A full-mark answer corrects the claim with the guide's process and gives a routine precise enough to
teach.

### CRQ 5

Points-based rubric, 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | 5 | Participation is presence or contribution (attending, donating); involvement is having a voice or role in decisions or learning |
| Diagnosis of the school | 2 | Prize day and donations are participation without involvement |
| Practice 1 | 4 | e.g. parents help plan a school event or classroom project; justified by voice and ownership |
| Practice 2 | 4 | e.g. a community member contributes to a lesson through the six-step visit routine; home visits; justified |

A full-mark answer applies the distinction to the described school and proposes realistic practices.

### CRQ 6

Points-based rubric, 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| Punitive vs restorative | 4 | Punitive makes the pupil pay; restorative requires the pupil to repair harm and rebuild the relationship |
| Six-step response | 5 | Calm stop, check harm, hear both sides, agree repair, follow up, restore relationship |
| Evaluation | 4 | Argues that restorative responses are demanding, build accountability and reduce repeat behaviour; care includes firm expectations |
| Example | 2 | A concrete classroom incident |

A full-mark answer takes the claim seriously, then shows why a caring response is firmer in its
demands, not weaker.

### ERQ 1

Marked out of 100 across six parts. Within each part, award marks for accurate use of course ideas,
application to the details of the case, and realistic proposals.

**(a) 15 marks.** Guide definition: management places student learning at its heart; the best-managed
classroom engages each learner in constructing knowledge (5). Quiet is not learning; the previous
teacher controlled behaviour but not learning (4). Shouting and exclusion are reactive and punitive, not
proactive management; quote or paraphrase the guide's misconceptions about quiet classrooms and threats
and punishment (4). Link to the case (girls say nothing; parents report no learning) (2).

**(b) 20 marks.** Four features, 5 marks each (feature identified 2, no-cost change 2, justification 1).
Credit: sightlines and back benches unseen (teach from the aisle, rotate seats); participation dominated
by front-row boys (pair talk before answers, name-based questioning); single textbook causing conflict
(roles such as a reader per group, shared reading routine); locked materials (routine with the head for
access, monitors); bare or faded walls (pupil work, target corner); crowding and movement (clear aisle,
movement routine); no norms for group work (taught norms).

**(c) 20 marks.** Schedule (8): whole-class opening, alternating teacher-led and independent blocks for
Classes 3 and 4, whole-class close, realistic timings. Two routines (6 each): one group-work routine with
set-up, run and close (roles, time limit, noise signal, reporting) and one further routine (anchor
activity with stuck and finished procedures, or a switch signal). Credit must go to routines described
precisely enough to teach.

**(d) 15 marks.** One stated objective (3). Extension for the early finisher on the same target
(e.g. applying the skill in a harder problem, or a peer-helper role with guidance) (5). Scaffold for
strugglers on the same target (worked example, number line, picture cues, partner) (5). Explicit statement
that the target stays the same (2).

**(e) 15 marks.** Six-step response applied to the pushing (calm stop, check harm, hear both, agree repair,
follow up, restore) (9). Justification against exclusion: learning time lost, no repair, relationship
damage, repeats the previous teacher's pattern (4). Reference to reviewing the group-work routine that
allowed the conflict (2).

**(f) 15 marks.** Distinguishes involvement from participation (3). Concrete practices: meeting parents
to listen to concerns and show pupils' work, inviting a parent or community member into a lesson using
the planned-visit routine, a class event planned with parents, home visits where appropriate (3 marks
each, up to 9). Sensitivity to rural Sindh context, including girls' attendance and parents who may
feel unwelcome in school (3).
