# B10. Individual differences

## What the test asks

The guide's Unit 3 moves from "the concept of individual differences" through "how do people differ?",
"why do people differ?" and "an overview of physical, emotional, social, and mental differences" to "how
to deal with individual differences", then children with special needs: physical and learning
disabilities, emotional disturbance, social and economic disadvantage, and gifted learners (HEC, 2012,
Educational Psychology, Unit 3). Its outcomes include recognising differences "in approaches to learning"
and planning teaching for varied abilities. CRQs ask you to **define** the concept, **explain** its
dimensions and causes, or **suggest** strategies for a mixed class.

Marks go to a sorted set of dimensions, causes that go beyond "heredity and environment" in one line, and
strategies that are specific and feasible in a large class. Candidates lose marks by listing differences
without saying what a teacher does about them.

## Explanation

### The concept

**Individual differences** are the ways in which people differ from one another in physical, mental,
emotional and social characteristics, and so in how they learn. The guide's starting point is that
teachers meet many students "who are not only physically different, but have mental, social, cultural,
and emotional differences", and "must be aware of the differences in their students in order to teach
effectively" (HEC, 2012, Unit 3). Two principles follow: differences are normal and expected, and most
characteristics vary along a continuum, with most people near the middle and fewer at either end.

### How people differ: the dimensions

- **Physical**: height, strength, health, motor skill, vision and hearing, energy.
- **Mental (intellectual)**: general ability, specific aptitudes (verbal, numerical, spatial), memory, speed of learning, creativity.
- **Emotional**: temperament, anxiety, confidence, emotional control.
- **Social**: sociability, leadership, cooperation, family and community background.
- **Interests, attitudes and motivation**: what a pupil cares about and how hard they try.
- **Achievement and prior knowledge**: what they already know.
- **Language and culture**: home language, values, ways of speaking and learning.

### Approaches to learning

The guide asks you to recognise differences "in approaches to learning". Felder and Brent (2005) describe
three: a **deep** approach (seeking meaning and connections), a **surface** approach (memorising in order
to reproduce) and a **strategic** approach (doing whatever earns the best marks). These are not fixed
traits; pupils shift approach with the task and the assessment. An examination that rewards recitation
pushes pupils towards the surface approach. Asking pupils to explain, apply and connect pushes them towards
depth.

### Why people differ: the causes

- **Heredity**: the range of possibilities for height, temperament and some aspects of ability.
- **Environment**: nutrition, health, home language, stimulation, schooling and relationships. Heredity and environment act together, not separately.
- **Age and maturation**: pupils of the same class can differ by more than a year in age and further in maturity.
- **Sex and gender**: some physical differences are biological; many differences in interests and confidence reflect how boys and girls are treated and what is expected of them.
- **Socio-economic status**: family income, parents' education and the resources of the home.
- **Culture and language**: what the community values and the language of learning versus the language of the home.

### Social and economic disadvantage

The guide lists "social and economic disadvantages" among special needs. Disadvantage affects learning
through many routes: hunger and poor health, crowded homes with no quiet place to study, child labour or
household work, fewer books and less school-related talk at home, and learning in a language not spoken at
home. None of these is a limit on ability. They are barriers, and a school can lower some of them: free
reading time and books at school, homework that does not need resources at home, a respectful
relationship with parents, and never mocking a pupil's clothes, fees or home.

### How to deal with individual differences

1. **Know the learners**: short diagnostic activities, observation, talking with pupils and families.
2. **Differentiate** content, process, product and environment within one lesson (Tomlinson, 2014).
3. **Flexible grouping**: mixed-ability groups for discussion, similar-level groups for targeted practice, changed often so no group becomes a label.
4. **Pace and support**: extra time and smaller steps for some, extension tasks for others.
5. **Varied assessment**: oral, written, practical and project work, so different strengths can show.
6. **Remedial and enrichment work**: re-teaching for those who have not grasped a key idea; deeper challenge for those who have.
7. **A climate that values difference**: public statements that people learn at different speeds, and praise for effort and progress.

**A Pakistani example.** Mr Imran's Class 6 in a Khairpur government school has 55 pupils, from a boy
already reading English novels to a girl who arrived this year from a village school where Sindhi was the
only language. He begins each unit with a five-minute oral check, gives a three-level worksheet (the same
core question at each level), pairs pupils across levels for discussion, and meets the new girl's family
to learn what she can already do in Sindhi.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) **Explain** the concept of individual differences and **describe** the physical, mental,
emotional and social dimensions, with a classroom example of each.

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) **Analyse** the causes of individual differences among pupils in a rural Sindh school and
**suggest** four strategies a teacher can use to deal with them.

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | 3 | Ways people differ in physical, mental, emotional and social characteristics; differences are normal and vary along a continuum. |
| Four dimensions | 8 | Each dimension correctly described (2 marks each). |
| Examples | 4 | A believable classroom example for each dimension (1 mark each). |

### CRQ 2

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Causes | 6 | At least four causes explained, e.g. heredity, environment, age, gender expectations, socio-economic status, language and culture; heredity and environment interacting. |
| Rural Sindh context | 1 | Causes linked plausibly to the setting (e.g. home language, household work), without stereotyping. |
| Four strategies | 8 | Specific and feasible in a large class, e.g. diagnostic checks, differentiation, flexible grouping, varied assessment, remedial and enrichment work (2 marks each). |
