# B08. Psychology and educational psychology compared

## What the test asks

The first outcome of the Educational Psychology guide is to "differentiate between psychology and
educational psychology", alongside the nature and scope of the field (HEC, 2012, Educational Psychology,
Unit 1). A CRQ will ask you to **define** both, **differentiate** them on several points, and **explain**
with an example why a teacher needs educational psychology.

Marks go to a clear definition of each, three or four distinct points of difference (not the same point
reworded), and a classroom example. Candidates lose marks by saying educational psychology is "just
psychology used in schools", and by forgetting that the two also share methods and feed each other.

## Key points

- **Psychology** is the scientific study of mind and behaviour in general: memory, emotion, personality, development, social behaviour, in any setting.
- **Educational psychology** is the branch that studies mind and behaviour **in educational settings**: how people learn and develop, what helps and hinders learning, and what follows for teaching (Seifert & Sutton, 2009).

| Point of difference | Psychology | Educational psychology |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | All human (and animal) behaviour | Learners, the learning process and the learning situation |
| Aim | To understand and explain behaviour | To understand learning **in order to improve** teaching and learning |
| Nature | Largely pure (basic) science, with many applied branches | An applied branch, judged partly by usefulness |
| Setting | Laboratory, clinic, community, workplace | Classrooms and other places of learning |
| Typical question | How does memory work? | What should a teacher do differently because of how memory works? |
| Context | Seeks general laws | Must ask whether a finding holds in this school, with these pupils |

**What they share.** Both use observation, experiment and case study, and both demand evidence rather than
opinion. The relationship runs both ways: psychology supplies tested theories, methods and a precise
vocabulary; classrooms test those theories in real conditions and raise new questions.

**Why a teacher needs it.** Every teaching decision rests on some belief about learning. Educational
psychology replaces staff-room guesses ("she is just weak") with explanations that can be checked, and it
carries an ethical weight because teachers act on other people's children.

**Example.** A general psychologist studies how anxiety affects recall. An educational psychologist asks
whether a low-stakes weekly quiz helps a nervous Class 6 in Hyderabad remember fractions, and under what
conditions it might harm them.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) **Define** psychology and educational psychology and **differentiate** between them on four
points, with a classroom example.

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) "Educational psychology is only common sense applied to schools." **Evaluate** this statement.

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Definitions | 4 | Psychology as the scientific study of mind and behaviour; educational psychology as its study in educational settings to improve learning (2 marks each). |
| Four differences | 8 | Four distinct points, e.g. scope, aim, pure versus applied, setting, context (2 marks each). |
| Classroom example | 3 | An example that shows the same topic asked from each side. |

### CRQ 2

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| What the field is | 3 | Scientific, applied study of learning in educational settings. |
| Against "common sense" | 6 | Common sense gives contradictory answers; the field states claims that can be tested; uses observation, experiment and case study; findings can be wrong and so checked. |
| Fair concession | 3 | Teacher experience is a source of good questions; some findings do match intuition. |
| Reasoned conclusion | 3 | A clear judgement supported by the points above. |
