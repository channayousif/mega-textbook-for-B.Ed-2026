# B. Practice questions

This page provides practice questions covering the objectives in Child Development and Education Psychology (Heading B). The questions use the same format and command words as the Sindh Teaching Licence test.

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) **Explain** the difference between Piaget's concept of assimilation and accommodation. **Illustrate** your answer with an example of a Class 2 pupil learning about different types of animals.

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) **Describe** Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems theory. **Analyse** how a sudden change in a child's mesosystem could impact their academic performance in a primary school in Sukkur.

### CRQ 3

(15 marks) A teacher notices that a student only completes homework when promised a tangible reward. **Compare** intrinsic and extrinsic motivation in this context, and **justify** two strategies the teacher could use to foster intrinsic motivation.

### CRQ 4

(15 marks) **Analyse** the role of heredity and environment in shaping a child's intelligence. **Explain** why an educator must consider both factors when designing instructional activities for a diverse classroom.

### CRQ 5

(15 marks) **Design** a brief classroom activity for early childhood education (ECE) that promotes fine motor skills. **Justify** your design choices using principles of physical development.

### ERQ 1

(100 marks) **Case Study:** 
You have recently been appointed as a Class 4 teacher in a government school in a rural district of Sindh. The classroom consists of 35 students with diverse backgrounds. You notice a student named Ali who consistently struggles with reading comprehension. Ali is often restless, frequently interrupts others, and becomes easily frustrated when asked to read aloud. During break times, he is observed sitting alone, and other students occasionally tease him. His parents work long hours in agriculture and are rarely able to attend school meetings.

You decide to develop a comprehensive plan to support Ali's learning and social-emotional development.

**a)** (20 marks) **Analyse** Ali's situation using Maslow's hierarchy of needs. Identify which levels of needs are currently unmet and explain how this impacts his classroom behaviour.

**b)** (25 marks) Drawing on Erikson's psychosocial theory, **explain** the developmental crisis Ali is likely facing at this age (Industry vs. Inferiority). **Discuss** how his current experiences might affect the resolution of this crisis.

**c)** (25 marks) **Design** an intervention plan using Vygotsky's concept of the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) to improve Ali's reading comprehension. Provide specific examples of scaffolding you would use.

**d)** (30 marks) **Evaluate** the role of the microsystem and mesosystem (from Bronfenbrenner's ecological model) in Ali's case. **Propose** practical steps you can take to strengthen the mesosystem despite his parents' work commitments.

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Definition of assimilation | 4 | Explains assimilation as integrating new information into existing cognitive schemas without changing the schema. |
| Definition of accommodation | 4 | Explains accommodation as altering existing schemas or creating new ones when new information cannot fit into existing schemas. |
| The Class 2 animals example, covering both processes | 7 | One worked illustration that shows assimilation and then accommodation in the same pupil: calling a horse a "big dog" because the child only has a schema for four-legged pets (assimilation), then learning that a horse is a different animal and creating a new "horse" schema (accommodation). Award 4 for assimilation and 3 for accommodation if only one process is illustrated. |

### CRQ 2

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Description of ecological theory | 5 | Briefly outlines the nested systems (micro, meso, exo, macro, chrono) that influence a child's development. |
| Definition of mesosystem | 3 | Accurately defines the mesosystem as the interactions between two or more microsystems (e.g., home and school). |
| Analysis of change | 7 | Believable scenario of a mesosystem change (e.g., conflict between parents and teacher, or breakdown in communication) and a logical explanation of how this reduces support, causing a drop in academic performance. |

### CRQ 3

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Comparison of motivation types | 5 | Clearly contrasts extrinsic (driven by external rewards/punishments) with intrinsic (driven by internal satisfaction/interest). |
| Contextual application | 4 | Connects the definitions to the student relying on tangible rewards for homework. |
| Strategy 1 | 3 | A feasible strategy to foster intrinsic motivation (e.g., relating tasks to student interests, offering choices), with justification. |
| Strategy 2 | 3 | A second feasible strategy (e.g., focusing on mastery goals rather than performance), with justification. |

### CRQ 4

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Role of heredity | 4 | Explains that genetics provide a baseline or potential range for cognitive abilities. |
| Role of environment | 4 | Explains that environmental factors (nutrition, stimulation, education) determine where within that range the child's intelligence develops. |
| Interaction | 3 | Articulates that heredity and environment constantly interact; neither acts alone. |
| Implication for educators | 4 | Explains that since teachers cannot change heredity, they must maximize the environment by providing rich, varied activities that allow all potentials to flourish. |

### CRQ 5

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Activity design | 6 | Clear, age-appropriate ECE activity (e.g., threading beads, using playdough, cutting paper). |
| Focus on fine motor skills | 4 | Explicitly links the activity to the development of small muscle movements (hands, fingers). |
| Justification | 5 | Uses principles of physical development (e.g., proximodistal development, transitioning from gross to fine motor control) to justify why the activity is suitable. |

### ERQ 1

Award up to 100 marks.

**Part a) Maslow's Hierarchy (20 marks)**
| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Identification of needs | 10 | Identifies belongingness/love (sitting alone, teased) and potentially esteem needs (frustration with reading) as unmet. |
| Impact on behaviour | 10 | Explains that because these lower-level deficiency needs are unmet, Ali is unable to focus on cognitive/growth needs (learning to read), leading to restlessness and frustration. |

**Part b) Erikson's Psychosocial Theory (25 marks)**
| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Industry vs. Inferiority | 10 | Correctly identifies the crisis for middle childhood: the need to develop a sense of competence in valued skills (industry) versus feeling inadequate (inferiority). |
| Application to Ali | 15 | Discusses how struggling with reading and being teased risks resolving the crisis towards inferiority, impacting his self-esteem and future motivation. |

**Part c) Vygotsky and ZPD (25 marks)**
| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| ZPD concept | 5 | Defines ZPD as the difference between what a learner can do independently and what they can do with guidance. |
| Intervention plan | 10 | Proposes a plan to assess Ali's current independent reading level and target instruction just above it. |
| Examples of scaffolding | 10 | Provides specific, practical scaffolding techniques (e.g., paired reading, using graphic organizers, providing vocabulary cues) and explains how they will be gradually removed. |

**Part d) Ecological Systems (30 marks)**
| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Microsystem analysis | 10 | Evaluates the immediate environments: the classroom (where he faces peer teasing and academic struggle) and the home (where parents work long hours). |
| Mesosystem analysis | 10 | Evaluates the weak link between home and school; the lack of communication exacerbates the lack of support. |
| Practical steps | 10 | Proposes realistic ways to build the mesosystem despite challenges (e.g., sending audio notes via WhatsApp if parents are illiterate/busy, scheduling brief meetings during times parents might be near the school, using community liaisons). |
