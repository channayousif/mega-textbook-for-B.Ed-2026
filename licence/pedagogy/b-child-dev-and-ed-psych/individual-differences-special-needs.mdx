# B04. Individual differences and special needs

## What the test asks

The guide's Unit 5 covers "differences in student learning and performance strengths", recognising
emotional and behavioural, language, physical and sensory disabilities and learning disorders,
"cognitive differences: delays and giftedness", differentiated instruction, and "school resources and
support services for special-needs students", together with "perspectives on national educational policy
in Pakistan" (HEC, 2012, Child Development, Unit 5). CRQs ask you to **identify** signs of a need,
**explain** how to differentiate for it, **describe** support services, or **justify** inclusion.

Marks go to observable signs described without labelling the child, to specific classroom adjustments,
and to knowing when and to whom to refer. Candidates lose marks by "diagnosing" pupils, by vague advice
("give extra attention"), and by quoting policy they cannot name.

## Explanation

### Normal variation and special needs

The guide's starting point is that "no child develops in exactly the same way as another. Some difference
is normal", while "some children develop outside of normal limits, and education works to accommodate
those children, as well" (HEC, 2012, Summary of essential knowledge). A special educational need is a
difference large or lasting enough that the ordinary classroom must be adjusted for the child to learn.

### Categories and what a teacher may notice

A teacher does not diagnose. The teacher **observes and records** what the child does, and refers. The
categories below follow the guide's Unit 5 and Seifert and Sutton (2009, Chapter 5).

| Category | What a teacher may notice | First classroom adjustments |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional and behavioural | Frequent outbursts or withdrawal, trouble with relationships, behaviour far outside what peers show, lasting over time | Predictable routines, calm clear limits, a trusted adult, praise for specific positive behaviour |
| Language and communication | Very limited vocabulary for age, unclear speech, difficulty following spoken instructions | Short instructions with gestures and pictures, time to answer, speaking models |
| Physical | Difficulty moving, writing or sitting for long; tiring quickly | Accessible seat and route, adapted writing tools, oral or shorter written tasks |
| Sensory: vision | Holding books close, squinting, copying errors from the board | Front seat, large clear print, reading aloud what is written |
| Sensory: hearing | Not responding when spoken to from behind, watching faces closely, speech errors | Seat near the teacher, face the class when speaking, write key words |
| Specific learning disorder | Marked, lasting difficulty in reading, writing or number despite adequate teaching, while other abilities are average or better | Multisensory practice, extra time, alternative ways to show learning |
| Cognitive delay | Slower progress across most areas, needs many repetitions and concrete support | Small steps, concrete materials, frequent review, realistic goals |

**Developmental delay** means a child reaches milestones (walking, talking, self-care, early literacy)
noticeably later than the usual range. The guide pairs "delays and giftedness" as two ends of cognitive
difference. A delay is a signal to observe closely and refer early, not a verdict on the child's future.

### Giftedness: a model

Your degree treats giftedness as an educational need. A widely used model is Renzulli's (1978)
**three-ring conception**: gifted behaviour arises where **above-average ability**, **creativity** and
**task commitment** overlap. The model reminds teachers that high marks alone are not the whole picture,
and that commitment can be developed by giving able pupils real challenges.

### Differentiated instruction

Differentiation means planning one lesson so that different pupils can reach the goal by different
routes. Tomlinson (2014) describes four things a teacher can vary:

- **Content**: what is learned, or how pupils get access to it (a simpler text, a recording, pictures).
- **Process**: how pupils make sense of it (concrete materials, pair work, a worked example).
- **Product**: how pupils show learning (oral answer, drawing, written paragraph).
- **Learning environment**: seating, grouping, noise and time.

In a Class 3 of 50 in Sukkur, a reading lesson may give one group the full passage, another the same
passage with key words pictured, and a third the passage read aloud by the teacher, with everyone
answering the same central question in the way they can.

### School resources and support services

Support usually runs in steps: the class teacher observes and adjusts; concerns are shared with the head
teacher and parents; the child is referred for assessment (health, vision and hearing checks, or a
specialist); and a plan with specific goals is agreed and reviewed. Other resources include peer helpers,
a resource room or special educator where one exists, assistive devices, and links with health services
and community organisations. The guide asks student teachers to study working models of inclusive
education in Pakistani schools (HEC, 2012, Unit 5).

### The Pakistani and Sindh position

Article 25-A of the Constitution requires the State to provide free and compulsory education to all
children aged five to sixteen. In Sindh, the Sindh Empowerment of 'Persons with Disabilities' Act, 2018
defines inclusive education as "a system of education wherein students with and without disabilities
learn together and the system of teaching and learning is suitably adapted to meet the learning needs of
different types of students with disabilities" (section 2), and section 9, "Equity in education", addresses
accessible, inclusive provision in educational institutions. The guide notes that Pakistan's policy has
moved towards including more children with disabilities in mainstream classrooms while also developing
separate specialist facilities (HEC, 2012, Summary of essential knowledge).

## Practice questions

### CRQ 1

(15 marks) A Class 4 pupil often copies wrongly from the board and holds his book very close. **Identify**
the possible need, **explain** what the teacher should and should not do, and **describe** three
classroom adjustments.

### CRQ 2

(15 marks) **Explain** differentiated instruction and **design** a differentiated activity for a mixed
Class 5 science lesson that includes a gifted pupil and a pupil with a specific learning disorder in
reading.

## Answers and marking guidance

### CRQ 1

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Possible need | 3 | Possible visual difficulty, stated as a possibility based on observed signs, not a diagnosis. |
| Should do | 4 | Observe and record, inform head teacher and parents, refer for a vision check, follow up. |
| Should not do | 2 | Label, scold for carelessness, or assume low ability. |
| Three adjustments | 6 | E.g. front seat, large clear writing, reading board work aloud, a partner, printed copies (2 marks each). |

### CRQ 2

Award up to 15 marks.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiation explained | 4 | One goal, different routes; varies content, process, product and environment. |
| Activity design | 5 | A coherent Class 5 science activity with a shared goal and at least two tiers or options. |
| Gifted pupil | 3 | Genuine challenge (open problem, extension, leadership), not merely more of the same. |
| Reading disorder | 3 | Access without heavy reading: oral instructions, pictures, a reading partner, oral or drawn product. |
