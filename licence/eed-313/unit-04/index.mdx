# Creating shared values and community

Unit 3 asked how routines buy instructional time. This unit asks a different question: what kind
of community makes a classroom worth learning in? It moves from the management of time to the
management of relationships - between teacher and pupils, among pupils, and between the school and
the world outside its walls.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain what **community** means inside and outside a school, and distinguish **participation**
  from **involvement**.
- Describe the typical practices of **community participation**, and explain how they differ in
  **multigrade** settings.
- Explain what an **ethic of care** is, and argue why it is not the same as never correcting a
  child.
- Analyse how a caring classroom builds **personal accountability** and **responsible action**.
- Evaluate a described response to a **behaviour breakdown**, using the distinction between a
  punitive response and a caring, accountable one.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 to 3, in particular the definition of classroom management as maximising learning, the
management/discipline distinction, and the routines that carry a well-managed classroom through a
day. This unit assumes you have met those ideas and applies them to the relational side of
management; it does not re-teach the earlier units.

## How to use this unit

The four topics follow the logic of an expanding circle. Topic 4.1 asks what community means;
Topic 4.2 asks how to bring it into the classroom; Topic 4.3 asks what kind of relationships a
caring classroom is built on; Topic 4.4 asks what happens when those relationships are tested.

Each topic is one complete learning cycle. The question bank at the end of the unit covers all
four topics together. Its answers and mark schemes are in a clearly marked section at the foot of
that page; try each item before you read them.
