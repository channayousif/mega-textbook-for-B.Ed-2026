# Topic 4.1 - What community means for a school

## A real classroom situation

<Figure id="fig-U4-1" src="/img/figures/eed-313/unit-04/fig-U4-1.svg" alt="Concept map showing the circles of community around one classroom: the classroom at the centre, then the school, then the parents and families, then the local community, each linked by labelled relationships." width={780} height={470} />

Miss Farzana teaches Class 3 in a government school in Ghotki. Her classroom does not end at the
door. Parents visit once a term. A local shopkeeper lends materials for science lessons. The imam
at the nearby mosque talks to the class about respect. This topic is about what community means for
a school, and about the difference between asking the community to help and genuinely involving it.

## Explanation

The guide treats community not as a vague aspiration but as a set of relationships that a school can
build, neglect or damage. The management question is: who is part of the community that supports
learning, and how does the school relate to them?

### The circles of community

<Figure id="fig-U4-5" src="/img/figures/eed-313/unit-04/fig-U4-5.svg" alt="Table comparing participation against involvement with an example of each: participation is attending a school event, involvement is helping to decide what happens there." width={780} height={470} />

The figure above maps the circles of community around one classroom. The innermost circle is the
classroom itself - the relationships between teacher and pupils and among pupils. The next circle
is the school - the headteacher, other teachers, the support staff. The next is the parents and
families. The outermost is the local community - neighbours, local leaders, businesses, places of
worship.

Each circle has a different relationship with the classroom, and the management demand of each is
different. The classroom circle is managed directly; the school circle through collaboration with
colleagues; the parent circle through communication and invitation; the community circle through
partnership and reciprocity. Wong and Wong (1998) argue that a well-managed classroom is one where
all four circles are deliberately tended, not just the innermost one.

### Participation and involvement distinguished

The figure above maps the distinction this topic makes. The terms are often used interchangeably,
but they mean different things, and the difference matters for management.

**Participation** means being present: attending a school event, signing a form, contributing
money, showing up. A parent who attends a parent-teacher meeting is participating.

**Involvement** means having a voice in what happens: helping to decide the agenda, contributing
to a classroom activity, being asked for advice and acting on it. A parent who helps plan the
meeting is involved.

Marzano, Marzano and Pickering (2003) found that the quality of a school's relationship with its
community depends less on how many people participate and more on how many are genuinely involved.
A school with fifty parents at a meeting but no voice in what happens has participation without
involvement. A school with ten parents who help make decisions has involvement, and involvement is
what changes the classroom.

**Common misconception.** "Community involvement means asking parents for money." It does not.
Asking for money is a transaction; involvement is a relationship. A parent who raises funds is
participating; a parent who helps decide how the funds are spent is involved.

### Typical practices of community participation

The guide describes several typical practices, and the management demand of each:

- **Parent-teacher meetings** - the most common practice, and the one most likely to become
  participation without involvement if the agenda is set entirely by the school.
- **Home visits** - the teacher visits the family, which builds a relationship that no meeting can
  replace. The management demand is time and cultural sensitivity.
- **Community events** - the school hosts an event and the community attends. The management
  demand is planning, and the risk is that the event becomes a performance rather than a
  partnership.
- **Community members in the classroom** - a parent or local professional contributes to a lesson.
  The management demand is preparation: the visitor needs to know what to do, and the pupils need
  to know why they are there.

## Activity: Participation or involvement?

Work in pairs. Read the following five scenarios and classify each as participation or involvement.
For each, explain your reasoning in one sentence.

1. A parent attends a parent-teacher meeting where the teacher reports on pupil progress.
2. A parent helps design the science fair and judges the entries.
3. The school asks each family to contribute towards a new water tap.
4. A local farmer talks to the class about how crops grow, at the teacher's invitation.
5. A parent sits on the school management committee and votes on the budget.

Allow 15 minutes. Then compare with another pair and identify one scenario where the boundary is
unclear, and explain why.

   account, explain what that claim might overlook. *(Analyze)*
3. State the difference between participation and involvement, and give one example of each.
   *(Remember)*
4. A headteacher says, "We do not need community involvement; we need the parents to pay their
   fees." Evaluate that claim using this topic's account of what involvement achieves. *(Evaluate)*
5. For a described school that wants to move from participation to involvement, propose one
   specific first step and explain why it is realistic. *(Apply)*

## Summary

Community is not a single thing but a set of circles around a classroom, each with a different
relationship to the school. Participation is being present; involvement is having a voice. The
quality of a school's community relationships depends less on how many people participate and more
on how many are genuinely involved.

## Try this at your practicum school

Over one week, note every interaction between the school and the community (parents, local
leaders, visitors). Classify each as participation or involvement, and write half a page on which
dominates and what the cost is for the school's relationship with its community.

Do not name the school or the teacher in your write-up.
