# Unit 4 teacher notes

## Sequencing note

The four topics follow the logic of an expanding circle. Topic 4.1 asks what community means; Topic
4.2 asks how to bring it into the classroom; Topic 4.3 asks what kind of relationships a caring
classroom is built on; Topic 4.4 asks what happens when those relationships are tested.

A useful session order is to teach the topics in numerical order across the four weeks the guide
allows (Weeks 12 to 15), using the first session of the unit to map the community circles around
the trainees' own practicum schools and the last to rehearse the integrative assessment. The unit
is designed so that a tutor who needs to compress it can merge the activity time of Topics 4.1 and
4.2 into a single community-mapping workshop without losing the conceptual spine.

## Common misconceptions to probe

These are the misconceptions the unit names explicitly. Surface them early and return to them;
trainees often assent to the correct version in a discussion and then revert when they plan.

1. **"Community involvement means asking parents for money."** This is the most persistent one.
   Probe it by asking: "If a parent gives money but never visits the school, is that involvement?"
   Trainees who equate community support with financial contribution will find the distinction
   counter-intuitive. The figure in Topic 4.1 (participation vs involvement) is designed to make
   the distinction visible; use it as a poster or handout.

2. **"An ethic of care means never correcting a child."** Probe it by asking: "If a pupil is about
   to make a dangerous mistake, does care mean letting them?" The answer is no, and the six-step
   model in Topic 4.4 is designed to show how correction and care coexist.

3. **"A caring classroom has no consequences."** Probe it by asking: "If a pupil hurts a classmate
   and nothing happens, is that caring - for whom?" The answer is that the hurt classmate deserves a
   response, and the six-step model shows what a caring consequence looks like.

## Managing the activities

**Topic 4.1 - Participation or involvement? (15 minutes).** This is a quick classification task.
The key teaching move is the debrief: ask trainees to identify the scenario where the boundary is
unclear, and use it to make the point that the boundary is a matter of voice, not presence.

**Topic 4.2 - Plan a community visit (25 minutes).** The trap is that trainees produce a vague
invitation rather than a six-phase plan. The difference: a vague invitation says "come and talk";
a six-phase plan says what the need is, who the person is, what the first contact says, what the
visit looks like, how it is hosted, and what the follow-up is. The debrief question - "identify one
moment where the visit could become a performance" - is designed to catch this.

**Topic 4.3 - Trace one incident twice (20 minutes).** This is the most emotionally demanding
activity. The key teaching move is to make the comparison explicit: write the two traces side by
side and ask, "What is the message to the class in each?" The divergence is usually at step 2: the
punitive trace skips "check for harm" and goes straight to blame.

**Topic 4.4 - Evaluate a response (20 minutes).** The trap is that trainees evaluate the outcome
(quiet class) rather than the process (relationship rebuilt). Push them back: "The class is quiet
in both responses. What is different?" The answer is the relationship, and the six-step model is the
tool for making it visible.

## Practical work and practicum links

Each topic carries a "Try this at your practicum school" task. The unit works best when the tutor
sets these up explicitly before trainees leave for practicum and debriefs them when trainees
return.

- **Topic 4.1 practicum task** (classify school-community interactions) produces a tally. Use it
  to open Topic 4.2: ask trainees which interactions are participation-only, and use their examples
  to motivate the six-phase model.

- **Topic 4.2 practicum task** (plan a community visit) produces a plan. Use it to make the point
  that community involvement is a routine, not a one-off event.

- **Topic 4.3 practicum task** (note caring teacher moves) produces a list. Use it to make the
  point that care is a set of practised routines, not a personality trait.

- **Topic 4.4 practicum task** (trace a behaviour incident) produces a six-step analysis. Use it to
  make the point that the unexpected is the moment when the classroom's culture is most visible.

## A note on the figures

The unit carries eight figures: two concept-map figures that make a relational structure visible
(fig-U4-1, fig-U4-3), two flowchart figures that sequence a process (fig-U4-2, fig-U4-4), and four
tables that compare options against a criterion (fig-U4-5, fig-U4-6, fig-U4-7, fig-U4-8). The
tables are the ones trainees most often try to read as prose. Before you project any of them, ask
trainees to read the column headers first and say what the table is comparing.

## Assessment note

The 10/10/5 bank is designed to be used in two passes: the MCQs and RRQs as formative checks at
the end of Topics 4.2 and 4.4, and the ERQs as a summative task at the end of the unit. ERQ 5 (the
integrative item) is the one that most reliably separates trainees who have understood the unit
from those who have memorised its definitions. If you use only one ERQ, use that one.
