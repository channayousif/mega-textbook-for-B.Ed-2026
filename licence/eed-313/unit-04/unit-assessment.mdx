# Unit 4 assessment

## Unit summary

Community is not a single thing but a set of circles around a classroom, each with a different
relationship to the school. Participation is being present; involvement is having a voice. An ethic
of care is built from attentiveness, responsiveness, competence and mutual respect, and it is not the
same as being permissive: a caring classroom has consequences, but they are restorative rather than
punitive. When behaviour breaks down, a caring, accountable response follows six steps and rebuilds
the relationship rather than damaging it.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. In this unit's account, the innermost circle of community around a classroom is: *(Remember)*
   a) the parents and families
   b) the local community
   c) the classroom itself
   d) the school

2. A parent attends a parent-teacher meeting. This is an example of: *(Understand)*
   a) involvement
   b) participation
   c) partnership
   d) accountability

3. Marzano, Marzano and Pickering (2003) found that the quality of a school's community
   relationships depends most on: *(Remember)*
   a) how many people participate
   b) how many people are genuinely involved
   c) how often the school holds events
   d) how much money the community contributes

4. An ethic of care is built from attentiveness, responsiveness, competence and: *(Remember)*
   a) discipline
   b) mutual respect
   c) punishment
   d) permissiveness

5. A teacher who notices a pupil is struggling and acts on that notice is demonstrating which
   component of an ethic of care? *(Apply)*
   a) Attentiveness
   b) Responsiveness
   c) Competence
   d) Mutual respect

6. A teacher who sends a disruptive pupil out of the room is using a: *(Understand)*
   a) restorative consequence
   b) responsive consequence
   c) punitive consequence
   d) proactive consequence

7. The first step in a caring, accountable response to a behaviour breakdown is: *(Remember)*
   a) hear both sides
   b) stop the behaviour calmly
   c) check for harm
   d) agree a repair

8. A pupil who says "I did that, and I will make it right" is demonstrating: *(Apply)*
   a) participation
   b) attentiveness
   c) personal accountability
   d) mutual respect

9. The unexpected-event decision aid prioritises, in order: *(Remember)*
   a) safety, learning, relationship
   b) learning, safety, relationship
   c) relationship, safety, learning
   d) safety, relationship, learning

10. Henley (2009) argues that mutual respect is the foundation of: *(Remember)*
    a) a punitive classroom
    b) a permissive classroom
    c) a proactive classroom
    d) a provisional classroom

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Name the four circles of community around a classroom, starting from the innermost. *(Remember)*

2. A teacher says, "Two hundred parents came to our event, so we have strong community involvement."
   Using Topic 4.1, explain what that claim might overlook. *(Analyze)*

3. State the difference between participation and involvement, and give one example of each.
   *(Remember)*

4. Name the four components an ethic of care is built from. *(Remember)*

5. A teacher corrects a pupil's mistake in front of the class. Using Topic 4.3, explain what message
   that sends and what a respectful alternative would be. *(Analyze)*

6. State the difference between a punitive consequence and a restorative one, and give one example of
   each. *(Understand)*

7. Name the six steps of a caring, accountable response to a behaviour breakdown, in order.
   *(Remember)*

8. A teacher sends a disruptive pupil out of the room and the lesson continues. Using Topic 4.4,
   explain what that response costs in relational terms. *(Analyze)*

9. A school wants to move from participation to involvement. Propose one specific first step and
   explain why it is realistic. *(Apply)*

10. Evaluate the claim that "a caring response to disruption is a weak response." Use the six-step
    model from Topic 4.4 in your answer. *(Evaluate)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A teacher believes that "community involvement means asking parents for money." Analyze what
   that belief overlooks, using Topic 4.1's distinction between participation and involvement, and
   argue which single practice would most improve the relationship between a described school and
   its community. *(Analyze)*

2. A teacher says, "I am not a caring teacher; I am a strict one." Using Topic 4.3's account of
   what an ethic of care is built from, evaluate whether strictness and care are opposed, and justify
   your argument by reference to a described classroom. *(Evaluate)*

3. A pupil knocks over a classmate's materials and the class laughs. Using Topic 4.4, trace the
   incident through the six-step model and argue which step matters most for the classroom's
   culture. Justify the choice by reference to the described pupils. *(Analyze)*

4. Evaluate the claim that "a caring classroom has no consequences." Use the distinction between
   punitive and restorative consequences from Topics 4.3 and 4.4, the six-step model, and at least
   one concrete example of a described incident. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative.** You are placed in a government primary school. A teacher manages mainly through
   rules and exclusion: pupils who misbehave are sent out, and the community is asked for money but
   never invited in.

   Write the community-and-care section of a classroom management plan for that teacher that does
   all three of the following: proposes one practice that moves the school from participation to
   involvement, explains how an ethic of care would change the teacher's response to a described
   behaviour breakdown, and justifies each choice by reference to the constraint the described
   room imposes. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **c** - the classroom itself is the innermost circle.
2. **b** - attending a meeting is participation; helping to decide what happens there is
   involvement.
3. **b** - Marzano et al. found that involvement matters more than participation for the quality of
   community relationships.
4. **b** - the four components are attentiveness, responsiveness, competence and mutual respect.
5. **b** - responsiveness is acting on what you notice; noticing without acting is attentiveness.
6. **c** - sending a pupil out is a punitive consequence.
7. **b** - the first step is to stop the behaviour calmly.
8. **c** - owning the action and committing to repair is personal accountability.
9. **a** - the priority order is safety, learning, relationship.
10. **c** - Henley frames mutual respect as the foundation of a proactive classroom.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** One mark per circle, in order: the classroom, the school, the parents and
families, the local community.

**2. (4 marks)** Explains that attendance is participation, not involvement (1); distinguishes
between being present and having a voice (1); links this to Marzano et al.'s finding about the
quality of community relationships (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**3. (4 marks)** Participation is being present (1); involvement is having a voice in what happens
(1); the participation example is genuinely attendance or contribution (1); the involvement example
is genuinely a voice in decisions (1).

**4. (4 marks)** One mark per component: attentiveness, responsiveness, competence, mutual respect.

**5. (5 marks)** Identifies the message: the pupil's dignity is less important than the correction
(1); names the respectful alternative: correcting privately (1); links this to Topic 4.3's
respectful-relations moves (1); explains the cost: the pupil learns that mistakes are public (1);
the reasoning is coherent (1).

**6. (4 marks)** A punitive consequence punishes the person (1); a restorative consequence repairs
the harm (1); the punitive example is genuinely exclusion or shaming (1); the restorative example is
genuinely a repair or making things right (1).

**7. (6 marks)** One mark per step in order: stop the behaviour calmly, check for harm, hear both
sides, agree a repair, follow up, restore the relationship.

**8. (5 marks)** Identifies the cost: the relationship between teacher and pupil is damaged (1);
explains that exclusion stops the behaviour but does not rebuild the relationship (1); links this to
the six-step model: steps 2 to 6 are missing (1); argues that the pupil learns compliance, not
accountability (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**9. (5 marks)** Proposes a realistic first step (e.g. a parent sitting on a planning committee)
(1); explains why the step is realistic (1); links the step to the participation/involvement
distinction (1); the proposal is concrete, not generic (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**10. (5 marks)** Takes the claim at its strongest (1); uses the six-step model to show that a
caring response is structured, not weak (1); distinguishes between permissive and caring (1); argues
a verdict with reasons (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 on the four criteria below.
