# Routines, schedules, and time management

Unit 2 asked how curriculum decisions function as management acts. This unit moves from
planning to the daily machinery that carries a plan through a lesson and across a week. It asks:
what are routines and structures, how do they buy instructional time, and how do you design them
for the rooms you actually teach in - including rooms with two grades, or with pupils who need
different kinds of support?

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Distinguish **routines**, **structures**, **procedures** and **rules**, and explain how each
  buys instructional time.
- Explain why a routine that works in a single-grade class may need to be redesigned for a
  **multigrade** or **mixed-need** classroom.
- Describe subject-specific routines for **mathematics**, **science** and **literacy**, and
  explain what each one protects.
- Design a set of routines that build **co-operation** and **collaborative learning**, and
  distinguish the two.
- Take a described classroom and propose a routine set that fits its constraints.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Units 1 and 2, in particular the definition of classroom management as maximising learning, the
plan-motivate-teach-assess cycle, and the management tactics for multigrade and overcrowded
classrooms. This unit assumes you have met those ideas and applies them to the design of
routines; it does not re-teach the earlier units.

## How to use this unit

The four topics follow the logic of a single week. Topic 3.1 asks what routines are and why they
matter; Topic 3.2 asks how to adapt them when the room makes one-size-fits-all impossible;
Topic 3.3 asks what subject-specific routines look like in practice; Topic 3.4 asks how to design
routines that build the social skills a well-managed classroom runs on.

Each topic is one complete learning cycle. The question bank at the end of the unit covers all
four topics together. Its answers and mark schemes are in a clearly marked section at the foot of
that page; try each item before you read them.
