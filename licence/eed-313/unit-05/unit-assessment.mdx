# Unit 5 assessment

## Unit summary

A classroom management philosophy draws on four layers: the definition and theories of Unit 1, the
curriculum and differentiation of Unit 2, the routines of Unit 3, and the community and care of
Unit 4. The peer-critique protocol evaluates a plan for clarity, consistency and coverage. The
seven closing misconceptions are the guide's own; the evidence against each is drawn from the four
units. The culminating artefact is the classroom management plan itself.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. The peer-critique protocol evaluates a plan for clarity, coverage and: *(Remember)*
   a) consistency
   b) creativity
   c) compliance
   d) complexity

2. A plan holds a constructivist philosophy but describes a transmission-plan. The peer-critique
   protocol would flag this as a failure of: *(Understand)*
   a) clarity
   b) consistency
   c) coverage
   d) creativity

3. The guide names seven closing misconceptions. Which of the following is one of them? *(Remember)*
   a) The best-managed classroom is one where the teacher is in control of learning
   b) The best-managed classroom is one where the pupils are engaged in intellectually demanding work
   c) Management is proactive and acts on the learning environment
   d) Differentiation adjusts the route without lowering the target

4. A trainee says, "My reviewer noted an inconsistency between my philosophy and my routines. I
   will revise the routines to match the philosophy." This is an example of: *(Apply)*
   a) receiving feedback and revising in light of it
   b) defending the plan against criticism
   c) dismissing the feedback as opinion
   d) changing the philosophy to match the routines

5. Which of the following best captures the course's closing argument about classroom management?
   *(Understand)*
   a) Management is about keeping pupils quiet and compliant
   b) Management is the proactive work of creating the conditions in which learning happens
   c) Management is a set of tips that work in every classroom
   d) Management is the same as discipline with a nicer name

6. A teacher presents a well-sequenced lesson but pupils are disruptive during the transition between
   activities. The most likely routine failure is: *(Apply)*
   a) no materials routine
   b) no checking routine
   c) no transition routine
   d) no anchor routine

7. Wong and Wong (1998) argue that the first weeks of term should be spent: *(Remember)*
   a) establishing consequences for every rule
   b) teaching the plan, then teaching the curriculum
   c) letting pupils discover the routines themselves
   d) focusing on relationship-building before any routines

8. A teacher in a multigrade classroom says, "I just teach two lessons at once." Using Unit 3,
   explain what that claim overlooks. *(Understand)*
   a) The need for an anchor routine
   b) The need for a materials routine
   c) The need for a checking routine
   d) The need for a safety routine

9. Which feature most distinguishes a well-managed classroom from a merely orderly one?
   *(Understand)*
   a) The pupils are silent for most of the lesson
   b) The pupils are engaged in intellectually demanding work for most of the lesson
   c) The teacher gives instructions only once
   d) No pupil ever breaches a rule

10. Marzano, Marzano and Pickering (2003) found that the effectiveness of group work depends
    most on: *(Apply)*
    a) the structure chosen
    b) the size of the group
    c) the routines that carry the structure
    d) the subject being taught

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. Explain why the peer-critique protocol uses three criteria (clarity, consistency, coverage)
   rather than a single overall judgement. *(Understand)*

2. A reviewer says, "I do not agree with your philosophy, so I cannot use it." Using Topics 5.2 and
   5.3, explain what that response overlooks and propose how the peer could give useful feedback.
   *(Analyze)*

3. Explain what the seven closing misconceptions have in common, and why a teacher might hold them.
   *(Understand)*

4. A teacher says, "I have been teaching for twenty years; I do not need a philosophy." Using the
   closing synthesis from Topic 5.4, explain what that claim overlooks. *(Analyze)*

5. Analyse the difference between protocol-based feedback and unstructured opinion, using the
   peer-critique protocol from Topics 5.2 and 5.3. *(Analyze)*

6. A teacher says, "I do not need routines; I just manage as I go." Using Topic 5.1, explain what
   that claim costs in instructional time. *(Analyze)*

7. Explain why an ethic of care is not the same as being permissive, using the four components from
   Topic 5.3. *(Understand)*

8. A teacher sends a disruptive pupil out of the room and the lesson continues. Using Topic 5.4,
   explain what that response costs in relational terms. *(Analyze)*

9. Describe the six phases of a community visit, in order, and explain why the match between person
   and topic matters. *(Understand)*

10. A school wants to move from participation to involvement. Using Unit 4, argue which single
    practice would most improve the relationship and justify the choice. *(Analyze)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A peer's classroom management plan says, "I believe learning is the pupil's active construction
   of knowledge," but describes a transmission-plan with teacher explanation then practice. Using
   the peer-critique protocol, identify the inconsistency, explain why it matters, and propose a
   specific revision. *(Evaluate)*

2. A trainee says, "The course has given me a set of tips for managing a classroom." Using the
   closing synthesis from Topic 5.4 and the four-layer model from Topic 5.1, evaluate the claim and
   argue what the course has actually given the trainee. *(Evaluate)*

3. **Culminating task: the classroom management plan.** Take a classroom you have observed during
   practicum (or one from your own schooling). Write a classroom management plan for that room
   that does all four of the following: states a philosophy of learning and connects it to at least
   two of the three theories from Unit 1; describes at least one curriculum decision and explains
   how it functions as a management act; proposes a routine set that fits the constraint the room
   names; and explains how the plan builds an ethic of care and personal accountability. Justify
   each choice by reference to the pupils or the setting. *(Create)*

4. Evaluate the claim that "a caring classroom has no consequences." Use the distinction between
   punitive and restorative consequences from Topics 5.3 and 5.4, the six-step model, and at least
   one concrete example of a described incident. *(Evaluate)*

5. A teacher at your practicum school manages mainly through rules and exclusion: pupils who
   misbehave are sent out, and the community is asked for money but never invited in. Using the
   four-layer model from Topic 5.1, propose one specific change to the teacher's philosophy, one to
   their routines, and one to their community relationships, and justify each by reference to the
   described pupils or setting. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **a** - the three criteria are clarity, coverage and consistency.
2. **b** - a mismatch between philosophy and plan is a consistency failure.
3. **a** - "The best-managed classroom is one where the teacher is in control of learning" is one of
   the seven closing misconceptions.
4. **a** - the trainee is receiving feedback and revising in light of it, which is the revision
   step of the cycle.
5. **b** - the course's closing argument is that management is proactive and centred on learning.
6. **c** - a transition routine governs how pupils move between activities; without it, the transition
   leaks time and produces disruption.
7. **b** - Wong and Wong's order is routines first, then procedures, then rules.
8. **a** - the anchor routine is the task one grade does while the teacher works with the other; a
   teacher who "teaches two lessons at once" has no anchor.
9. **b** - a well-managed classroom is defined by the quality of pupil engagement, not by the
   absence of noise or breaches.
10. **c** - Marzano et al. found that the routines that carry the structure matter more than the
    structure itself.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (6 marks)** Explains that a single overall judgement is vague (1); names the three criteria
and explains what each catches that a single judgement would miss (1 each); the reasoning is
coherent (1).

**2. (5 marks)** Explains that feedback evaluates the plan, not the philosophy (1); names a specific
protocol-based move the peer could make (1); links the move to the giving-and-receiving model from
Topic 5.3 (1); the proposal is concrete and actionable (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**3. (7 marks)** Identifies the common thread (e.g. all confuse control or compliance with
learning) (1); explains why a teacher might hold these beliefs (drawing on the four units) (2);
names at least five of the seven misconceptions correctly (1 each up to 4).

**4. (5 marks)** Explains that every teacher has a philosophy, whether examined or not (1); links
this to the closing synthesis (1); argues that the course has given the tools to examine and
defend that philosophy (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 5.4 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**5. (5 marks)** Distinguishes opinion from protocol-based feedback (1); names the three criteria
(1); explains that the protocol makes feedback specific and evidence-based (1); uses the protocol
to structure the analysis (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**6. (5 marks)** Explains that a teacher without routines spends the day negotiating every
transition (1); links this to the time-recovery argument from Unit 3 (1); quantifies, even roughly,
the time lost (1); argues that the teacher's time is being spent on management rather than
teaching (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**7. (4 marks)** Names the four components of an ethic of care (1); explains that care includes
consequences, not their absence (1); distinguishes permissive from caring (1); the reasoning is
coherent (1).

**8. (5 marks)** Identifies the cost: the relationship between teacher and pupil is damaged (1);
explains that exclusion stops the behaviour but does not rebuild the relationship (1); links this to
the six-step model: steps 2 to 6 are missing (1); argues that the pupil learns compliance, not
accountability (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**9. (4 marks)** Names the six phases in order: identify a need, find a community member, make
first contact, plan the visit, host the visit, follow up (1); explains that the match between
person and topic is what makes the visit serve the curriculum (1); links this to the six-phase model
(1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**10. (5 marks)** Proposes a specific practice (e.g. a parent sitting on a planning committee) (1);
explains why the practice constitutes involvement, not just participation (1); links the choice to
Unit 4's distinction (1); the proposal is concrete and realistic (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 on the four criteria below.

**ERQ 1 - Identifying an inconsistency**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identification of the inconsistency | Names the inconsistency vaguely | Identifies the mismatch between philosophy and plan | States the inconsistency precisely and links it to the consistency criterion |
| Explanation of why it matters | Asserts it matters | Explains the cost of inconsistency | Explains the cost and links it to the course principles |
| **Proposed revision (Evaluate)** | **Asserts a revision** | **Proposes a revision with a reason** | **Proposes a revision and explains why it makes the plan consistent** |
| Use of the protocol | Generic feedback | Refers to the criteria | Uses the criteria to structure the feedback |

**ERQ 2 - "The course has given me a set of tips"**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the four-layer model | Names the layers vaguely | Lists the layers | Shows how the layers connect in a philosophy |
| Analysis of the claim | Asserts the claim is wrong | Explains what the claim overlooks | Takes the claim at its strongest, then shows what it leaves out |
| **Evaluative reasoning (Evaluate)** | **Asserts a verdict** | **Argues a verdict with reasons** | **Argues a verdict using the four-layer model and the closing synthesis** |
| Use of the closing synthesis | Not referenced | Referenced | Used to explain what the course has actually given |

**ERQ 3 - The classroom management plan**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage of the four layers | Fewer than three layers addressed | Three layers addressed | All four layers addressed with evidence from the units |
| Quality of the justification | Asserts choices | Links choices to the described room | Links choices to specific features of the described pupils or setting |
| **Integration of the layers (Create)** | **Lists four separate ideas** | **Connects them in a coherent plan** | **Shows how the layers reinforce each other in the described room** |
| Realism and register | Ignores the constraints | Acknowledges them | The plan survives the constraints named; writes for a colleague |

**ERQ 4 - "A caring classroom has no consequences"**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the punitive/restorative distinction | Conflates the two | Distinguishes punitive from restorative | Shows what each is for and what each cannot do |
| Use of the six-step model | Names the steps without linking them to consequences | Links at least two steps to the distinction | Shows how steps 4 and 5 are what make a consequence restorative |
| **Evaluative reasoning (Evaluate)** | **Asserts a verdict** | **Argues a verdict with reasons** | **Takes the claim at its strongest, then shows what it leaves out using the full model** |
| Quality of example | Generic | A described incident with a consequence | The example genuinely illustrates the difference |

**ERQ 5 - Improving a rules-and-exclusion teacher's practice**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality of the proposed changes | Vague or generic | Specific and described | Specific, described, and justified by reference to the described pupils or setting |
| Use of the four-layer model | Names the layers vaguely | Applies at least two layers | Applies all three named layers (philosophy, routines, community) |
| **Justification by constraint (Create)** | **Asserts the changes are good** | **Links each change to the described room** | **Shows how the changes shift the teacher from reactive exclusion toward proactive management** |
| Realism and register | Ignores the constraints | Acknowledges them | The proposals survive the constraints named; writes for a colleague |
