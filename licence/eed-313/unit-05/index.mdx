# Course review

Units 1 to 4 gave you the pieces: a definition of classroom management centred on learning, the
theories that explain how learning happens, the curriculum and routines that carry a plan through
a day, and the relationships that make a classroom worth learning in. This unit asks you to pull
those pieces into one usable philosophy, to test it against the work of your peers, and to close
the course with a plan you can use in your own classroom.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Synthesise the content of **Units 1 to 4** into a single, coherent **classroom management
  philosophy**.
- Apply a structured **peer-critique protocol** to a described classroom management plan.
- Evaluate a described plan against the principles of the course.
- Revise a plan in light of structured feedback.

## Prerequisite knowledge

The full content of Units 1, 2, 3 and 4. This unit assumes you have worked through the theories,
the curriculum and differentiation, the routines, and the community and care units, and applies
that material to a single culminating artefact.

## In this unit

1. [Topic 5.1 - One philosophy from four units](./topic-01) - pulling the four preceding units
   into one classroom-management philosophy.
2. [Topic 5.2 - Peer critique of final projects](./topic-02) - the peer-critique protocol and how
   to use it.
3. [Topic 5.3 - Presenting and improving the plan](./topic-03) - presenting the plan, giving and
   receiving structured feedback, and revising in light of critique.
4. [Topic 5.4 - Summary and close](./topic-04) - the course's closing synthesis and the beliefs
   the course asks you to leave behind.

## How to use this unit

This is the lightest unit in the course by design. The work here is synthesis and application of
material already covered, not new content. The question bank at the end of the unit is shorter
than the others and focuses on integration. The summative task is the **classroom management
plan** itself, the course's culminating artefact.
