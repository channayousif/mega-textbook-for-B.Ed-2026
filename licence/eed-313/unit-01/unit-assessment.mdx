# Unit 1 assessment

## Unit summary

Learning is explained by three major theories, and a fluent teacher moves between them as the
learning target demands. Behaviourism treats learning as a change in visible behaviour shaped by
reinforcement; it is the right lens for building automatic routines and practised skills.
Cognitivism treats learning as the reorganisation of mental structures; it is the right lens for
making new information processable and connectable. Constructivism treats learning as the active
construction of personal meaning; it is the right lens for deep understanding and transfer.

Classroom management, as this course uses the phrase, is the proactive work of creating and
maintaining the conditions in which student learning happens. It is not the same as discipline,
which is the reactive response to a breach of norms. A teacher's personal philosophy of
teaching shapes what they treat as the management problem, and a well-managed classroom is marked
by practised routines, high intellectual demand, teacher withitness, and an environment that
supports the learning.

Reading a classroom requires a protocol: decide what to look for, record what you actually saw
in a left column and what you think it means in a right column, and keep the two apart. The
physical environment (sightlines, traffic paths, displays, resources) and the social
environment (participation patterns, norms of help, responses to error, belonging) are the two
landscapes a managed classroom shapes.

Designing the environment on purpose starts with a learning goal, not with furniture. The four
design acts are: identify the resources already in the room, make displays that keep teaching,
match the seating arrangement to the learning it needs to serve, and build the social norms that
make the physical design actually work.

## Summative assessment

### Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)

1. Behaviourism says that learning is best described as: *(Remember)*
   a) the reorganisation of mental structures
   b) the active construction of personal meaning
   c) a change in observable behaviour shaped by reinforcement
   d) the transfer of understanding to a new context

2. A teacher activates pupils' prior knowledge before introducing a new topic. This move is best
   read through which theoretical lens? *(Understand)*
   a) Behaviourism
   b) Cognitivism
   c) Constructivism
   d) None of the three

3. Which feature most distinguishes a well-managed classroom from a merely orderly one?
   *(Understand)*
   a) The pupils are silent for most of the lesson
   b) The pupils are engaged in intellectually demanding work for most of the lesson
   c) The teacher gives instructions only once
   d) No pupil ever breaches a rule

4. In a two-column observation record, the sentence "the teacher used proximity to redirect the
   talking pupils" belongs in: *(Apply)*
   a) the left column, because it records what was seen
   b) the right column, because it is an inference about what the action meant
   c) either column, because it is both an observation and an inference
   d) neither column, because it is a judgement

5. A teacher arranges desks in clusters for a discussion lesson, then is frustrated that the
   class is noisy. The most likely design failure is: *(Apply)*
   a) choosing clusters for a task that needs rows
   b) not establishing a norm of listening to disagreement before the discussion
   c) using constructivism when behaviourism was needed
   d) placing the display wall on the wrong side of the room

6. Wong and Wong (1998) argue that a teacher with a consistent management plan spends the first
   weeks of term: *(Remember)*
   a) establishing consequences for every rule
   b) teaching the plan, then teaching the curriculum
   c) letting pupils discover the routines themselves
   d) focusing on relationship-building before any routines

7. Which pair correctly matches a learning theory with the kind of learning target it serves
   best? *(Understand)*
   a) Behaviourism - interpreting a poem
   b) Cognitivism - building a new scientific concept from scratch
   c) Constructivism - practising letter formation until it is automatic
   d) Behaviourism - drilling a routine until it is automatic

8. A teacher in a low-resource government school cannot move the benches. According to Topic
   1.4, what can she still change this week? *(Apply)*
   a) Nothing, because the physical environment is fixed
   b) Only the social environment, because the physical one is fixed
   c) How the room is used, how the walls are read, how pupils orient, and how they treat each
      other
   d) Only the displays, because seating is fixed

9. "Withitness" refers to a teacher's: *(Remember)*
   a) ability to teach three theories in one week
   b) accurate awareness of what is happening across the whole room and willingness to act early
   c) skill at designing displays from scrap materials
   d) habit of separating observation from inference

10. A trainee observes a lesson and writes only conclusions in her notebook, with no record of
    the evidence they are based on. Which observation skill has she not yet developed? *(Apply)*
    a) Choosing a focus
    b) Separating observation from inference
    c) Using the four lenses
    d) Completing the five-step cycle

### Restricted-response questions (RRQs)

1. In no more than 80 words, distinguish what behaviourism says learning is from what
   constructivism says learning is, and give one classroom example that belongs to the first and
   not the second. *(Understand)*

2. Explain why a silent classroom is not necessarily a well-managed one, using this unit's
   definition of classroom management. *(Understand)*

3. Name the three features of the social environment that carry management weight, and give one
   concrete indicator a observer could record for each. *(Understand)*

4. Explain the difference between classroom management and classroom discipline, and give one
   example of each from a classroom you have observed or experienced. *(Understand)*

5. A teacher always uses rows. Using this unit's account of seating, explain what kind of
   learning the row arrangement serves well and what it costs. *(Apply)*

6. Analyze why a teacher who relies almost entirely on discipline is likely to spend more time
   reacting than a teacher who invests in management. Refer to the proactive/reactive
   distinction. *(Analyze)*

7. A trainee writes in the left column of her observation record: "the teacher praised the
   pupils who answered correctly and moved on when a pupil gave a wrong answer." Rewrite this as
   a defensible inference for the right column, and explain why the inference might be wrong.
   *(Analyze)*

8. Explain what a resource audit is, and argue why it is a more useful first design step than
   buying new materials for a low-resource government school. *(Apply)*

9. A teacher says, "I don't have a philosophy of teaching, I just teach." Using Topic 1.2,
   explain what that claim overlooks and what the cost is for their management. *(Analyze)*

10. Describe the five-step observation cycle from Topic 1.3, and explain why the step
    "separate observation from inference" matters for a trainee's professional development.
    *(Understand)*

### Extended-response questions (ERQs)

1. A teacher believes that "learning is the teacher transferring knowledge to the pupil". Analyze
   how that belief would shape their management of routines, seating and responses to error,
   and argue which of the three learning theories it draws on most heavily. *(Analyze)*

2. Take the Class 4 classroom described in Topic 1.3 (44 pupils, benches for 30, one textbook
   per four pupils, back benches in shadow, no pupil speaks unless asked). Analyze that room
   against two features of the physical environment and two features of the social environment,
   and propose one change to each. Justify each change by reference to a specific learning
   goal. *(Analyze)*

3. Using Ertmer and Newby's (1993) continuum, explain why a skilled teacher moves between the
   three learning theories across a single week rather than choosing one. Illustrate with one
   concrete week-plan for a lower-primary topic of your choice, showing at least one lesson
   move drawn from each theory. *(Create)*

4. Evaluate the claim that "classroom management is just discipline with a nicer name". Use the
   management/discipline distinction from Topic 1.2, the research findings of Marzano, Marzano
   and Pickering (2003), and at least one concrete classroom example of your own. *(Evaluate)*

5. **Integrative.** You are placed in a low-resource government primary school. The headteacher
   asks you to improve learning in a Class 5 room with fixed benches, one cracked blackboard,
   no budget, and a teacher who currently manages mainly through reprimand.

   Design a management plan for that room that does all three of the following: draws on at
   least two of the three learning theories, includes at least one change to the physical and
   one to the social environment, and explains how your plan shifts the teacher's balance from
   reactive discipline toward proactive management. Justify each choice. *(Create)*

## Answers and marking guidance

### MCQ answer key

1. **c** - behaviourism defines learning as a change in observable behaviour shaped by
   reinforcement; (a) is cognitivism, (b) is constructivism.
2. **b** - activating prior knowledge is a cognitivist move, organising new information against
   existing mental structures.
3. **b** - a well-managed classroom is defined by the quality of pupil engagement, not by the
   absence of noise or breaches.
4. **b** - the sentence interprets the teacher's action; it is an inference, not a verifiable
   observation.
5. **b** - clusters serve discussion, but without a norm of listening to disagreement the noise
   is unmanaged rather than a sign of learning.
6. **b** - Wong and Wong's management-plan approach invests the first weeks in teaching the
   plan, then frees the teacher to teach the curriculum.
7. **d** - behaviourism best explains the drilling of a routine until it is automatic; (a), (b)
   and (c) each pair a theory with a target it serves poorly.
8. **c** - Topic 1.4 argues that even a fixed-bench, no-budget room leaves the teacher multiple
   levers: use, displays, pupil orientation and social norms.
9. **b** - withitness is Marzano, Marzano and Pickering's term for accurate whole-room awareness
   and early action.
10. **b** - recording conclusions without evidence is the failure to separate observation from
    inference, which is the core discipline of the two-column protocol.

### RRQ model answers and mark schemes

**1. (4 marks)** Behaviourism says learning is a change in observable behaviour shaped by
reinforcement (1); constructivism says learning is the active construction of personal meaning by
the learner (1); the example belongs to behaviourism rather than constructivism (1); the example
is a genuine practised response, not a meaning-building task (1).

**2. (4 marks)** States the unit's definition of management as maximising student learning (1);
explains that silence is a behavioural state, not a learning state (1); gives an example of a
silent class that is not learning (e.g. copying without understanding) (1); concludes that
management is judged by the quality of engagement, not the level of noise (1).

**3. (6 marks)** One mark per feature correctly named from: participation patterns, norms of
help, responses to error, belonging (any three). One mark per indicator that is concrete and
recordable (e.g. for participation: "the same six hands went up in a 20-minute lesson").

**4. (4 marks)** Management is proactive and acts on the learning environment (1); discipline is
reactive and acts on a pupil's breach (1); the management example is genuinely proactive (1);
the discipline example is genuinely reactive (1).

**5. (4 marks)** Rows serve whole-class instruction well because every pupil can see the board
and the teacher can see every pupil (1); the cost is that group work is awkward and the pupils
nearest the teacher get more attention (1); the explanation is grounded in the Topic 1.4 account
of seating (1); the example is concrete (1).

**6. (5 marks)** Names the proactive/reactive distinction (1); explains that a reactive teacher
responds after each breach rather than preventing the conditions for breach (1); links this to
the finding that management investment in the first weeks reduces later disruption (1); argues
that the reactive teacher spends more time on discipline and less on teaching (1); the reasoning
is coherent (1).

**7. (5 marks)** The inference is a plausible interpretation of the observed action, not a
restatement of it (1); it uses language like "the teacher may have been" or "this suggests"
rather than stating it as fact (1); names at least one alternative inference the same evidence
could support (e.g. the teacher did not hear the wrong answer, or chose not to embarrass the
pupil) (1); explains that without asking the observer cannot know which is correct (1); the
reasoning is coherent (1).

**8. (4 marks)** A resource audit is a list of everything in the room that could support learning,
marked by whether pupils can actually use it (1); argues that buying new materials is often
impossible in a low-resource government school and does not address the use of what is already
there (1); links the audit to the Topic 1.4 principle that the design question is "what is
already here that I am not using?" (1); the argument is coherent (1).

**9. (5 marks)** Explains that every teacher has a philosophy, whether examined or not, and that
an unexamined philosophy is shaped by how they were taught (1); argues that a teacher without a
conscious philosophy manages by habit (1); links this to the cost: the management serves the
teacher's default assumptions rather than the current pupils' needs (1); the explanation draws
on Topic 1.2 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

**10. (4 marks)** Names the five steps in order: decide what to look for, record what you saw,
separate observation from inference, ask why it might be happening, plan one change (1);
explains that without the separation step the observer confuses what they saw with what they
hope it means (1); links this to professional development: a trainee who cannot keep the two
apart will not learn from observation (1); the explanation is coherent (1).

### ERQ rubrics

Each ERQ is marked out of 20 on the four criteria below. A response that does not reach
"Adequate" on the analysis criterion cannot be awarded more than 10 overall, whatever its other
merits.

**ERQ 1 - A teacher who believes learning is transfer**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the belief and its theoretical home | Names the theory vaguely | Identifies behaviourism as the primary lens | Shows what behaviourism makes visible and what it leaves out in this case |
| Analysis of the three management domains | Lists routines, seating and error-response without linking them to the belief | Links at least two of the three to the transfer belief | Shows how the same belief produces a consistent pattern across all three |
| **Argument about the dominant theory (Analyze)** | **Asserts a theory without reasons** | **Argues for behaviourism with one reason** | **Argues for behaviourism and explains why the teacher is also drawing, more or less consciously, on cognitivism** |
| Use of evidence | Generic | A concrete classroom example | The example is specific and genuinely illustrates the pattern |

**ERQ 2 - Analysing the Class 4 room**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage of the four features | Fewer than two physical and one social | Two physical and two social, correctly identified | The four are drawn from the described room, not generic |
| Quality of the proposed changes | Vague or undoable | Specific and doable this week | Specific, doable, and within a teacher's realistic control |
| **Justification by learning goal (Analyze)** | **Asserts a change is good** | **Links each change to a goal** | **Links each change to a goal and explains why the current feature works against that goal** |
| Use of the described room | Generic classroom | Refers to the description | The evidence is drawn from specific details in the description |

**ERQ 3 - A week-plan across the three theories**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the continuum | Theories are treated as competing | Theories are treated as complementary | The response explains what each theory makes visible that the others do not |
| Quality of the week-plan | Labelled but not genuinely different | At least one move from each theory, concrete | The moves are genuinely different in what the teacher does first, what the pupils do, and what success looks like |
| **Design of the integrated week (Create)** | **Lists three separate lessons** | **Builds a coherent week with a reason for each move** | **The week has a clear learning arc and the theory choices are justified by the learning target of each lesson** |
| Use of Ertmer and Newby | Not referenced | Referenced | The continuum is used to explain why the theories are distributed as they are |

**ERQ 4 - "Management is just discipline with a nicer name"**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grasp of the distinction | Conflates the two | Distinguishes proactive management from reactive discipline | Shows what each is for and what each cannot do |
| Use of research | No reference to Marzano et al. | References the finding | Uses the finding to argue that management and discipline address different problems |
| **Evaluative reasoning (Evaluate)** | **Asserts a verdict** | **Argues a verdict with reasons** | **Takes the claim at its strongest, then shows what it leaves out using the full distinction** |
| Quality of example | Generic | Concrete classroom example | The example genuinely illustrates the difference, not just one side of it |

**ERQ 5 - Integrative: a management plan for a low-resource room**

| Criterion | Limited (1-2) | Adequate (3) | Strong (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use of the two theories | Named but not applied | Applied to specific moves | The moves are genuinely different because of the theory they draw on |
| Coverage of physical and social change | One or the other missing | Both present | Both are specific, doable this week, and within the described constraints |
| **Shift from reactive to proactive (Create)** | **Asserts the plan is proactive** | **Shows at least one proactive move** | **Explains how the plan reduces the need for reprimand by building the conditions in which the wanted behaviour is more likely** |
| Justification and realism | Ignores the constraints | Acknowledges them | The plan survives the constraints named: fixed benches, one blackboard, no budget |
