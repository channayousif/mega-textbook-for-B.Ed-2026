# Learning theories and classroom management

This unit asks a question most teacher-training courses leave until the end: before you manage
a classroom, what do you believe about how learning happens? Your answer shapes every management
choice you make, whether you have thought about it or not.

The unit starts there. It sets out the three main accounts of how humans learn, not so you can
pick a side, but so you can see what each one makes visible and what each one leaves out. From
that base it builds a definition of classroom management centred on learning rather than on
quiet, then takes you into a real classroom to practise reading what is actually going on, and
closes with the deliberate design of the space your pupils will learn in.

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Name the three major learning theories, state what each says learning *is*, and give one
  classroom example of each in action.
- Explain why all three theories still have classroom validity, and what a teacher loses if
  they rely on only one.
- Define **classroom management** as a means of maximising student learning, and distinguish it
  from **discipline**.
- Use a simple observation protocol to record the physical and social features of a described
  classroom, and separate what you saw from what you inferred.
- Identify the key features of a well-managed classroom, and analyse a described environment well
  enough to propose one grounded change.
- Propose a small, purposeful change to a classroom's physical or social environment and justify
  it by reference to a learning theory.

## Prerequisite knowledge

None beyond intermediate-level study skills. The unit assumes you have sat in classrooms as a
pupil but have not yet studied learning theory. Every technical term is defined where it first
appears.

## In this unit

1. [Topic 1.1 - How humans learn: three theories](./topic-01) - what learning is, and what
   behaviourism, cognitivism and constructivism each say about it; why a teacher needs all three.
2. [Topic 1.2 - What classroom management is, and is not](./topic-02) - management as maximising
   learning rather than producing compliance; how a teacher's philosophy shapes their management
   beliefs; the discipline/management distinction.
3. [Topic 1.3 - Reading a real classroom](./topic-03) - classroom observation, collecting and
   recording data, and the physical and social features that carry management weight.
4. [Topic 1.4 - Designing the environment on purpose](./topic-04) - resources, displays, seating
   arrangements, and using the physical and social environment to support learning.

## How to use this unit

The four topics are designed to be worked through in order. Topic 1.2's definition of classroom
management draws on the three theories from Topic 1.1; Topic 1.3's observation protocol assumes
that definition; and Topic 1.4's design work applies everything that came before.

Each topic is one complete learning cycle and takes roughly one sitting. The six hours of
classroom observation the guide asks for in Week 2 are built into Topic 1.3 as a structured
observation protocol you can carry out in a practicum school, a peer's micro-teach, or a recorded
lesson if a live classroom is not available to you.

The question bank at the end of the unit covers all four topics together. Its answers and mark
schemes are in a clearly marked section at the foot of that page; try each item before you read
them.
