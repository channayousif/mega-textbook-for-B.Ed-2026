# Curriculum and classroom management

Unit 1 gave you a definition of classroom management centred on learning and the three
theories that explain how learning happens. This unit moves from theory to the teacher's
daily planning work. It asks: once you know what learning is, how do you build a teaching
plan that is consistent with your philosophy, and how do you make that plan work for every
pupil in the room - including when the room holds two grades, or sixty children?

## Unit learning outcomes

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

- Explain how **curriculum** decisions - what to teach, in what order, at what pace -
  function as management acts that reduce disruption before it starts.
- Describe the **plan, motivate, teach, assess** cycle and name the management decision at
  each stage.
- Build a teaching plan that is consistent with a stated philosophy of teaching.
- **Differentiate** instruction by pupil need, interest and level, and distinguish
  genuine differentiation from simply making some pupils do easier work.
- Propose workable management tactics for **multigrade** and **overcrowded** classrooms,
  given the constraints each reality imposes.
- Take a single curriculum objective and plan it three ways: for a well-resourced
  single-grade class, for a multigrade class, and for a class of sixty.

## Prerequisite knowledge

Unit 1, in particular the definition of classroom management as maximising learning, the
three learning theories, and the management/discipline distinction. This unit assumes you
have met those ideas and applies them to planning; it does not re-teach Unit 1.

## How to use this unit

The three topics follow the logic of a single plan. Topic 2.1 asks what your plan is
anchored in; Topic 2.2 walks through the cycle that carries it out; Topic 2.3 asks how to
adapt it when the pupils or the room make one-size-fits-all impossible.

Each topic is one complete learning cycle. The question bank at the end of the unit covers
all three topics together. Its answers and mark schemes are in a clearly marked section at
the foot of that page; try each item before you read them.
