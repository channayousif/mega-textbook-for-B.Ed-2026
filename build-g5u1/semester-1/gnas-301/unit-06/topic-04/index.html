Topic 6.4 - The greenhouse effect and global warming

A real classroom situation​

A Class 9 pupil looks up from his textbook and says, "Sir, if the greenhouse effect is natural, why is everyone shouting about it?" The teacher walks to the window. "This glass," he says, "keeps the room warm in winter. That is the natural effect, and we need it. Now imagine someone seals the vents and adds another pane every year. The problem is not the glass. The problem is the extra panes."

This topic is the physics of the glass and the extra panes, stated simply enough for a Class 9 pupil to carry home.

Explanation​

The greenhouse gas effect and global warming: causes and effects​

The mechanism is short and physical. Sunlight arrives as short-wave energy and warms the Earth's surface. The warm surface radiates the energy back as long-wave heat. Greenhouse gases, water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane and others, let the short-wave sunlight through but absorb part of the outgoing long-wave heat, returning some of it downward. The trapped part warms the lower atmosphere. Without this natural effect the Earth would average far below freezing; the glass keeps the room alive.

The problem is the enhancement. Burning coal, oil and gas, clearing forests, and raising livestock add carbon dioxide and methane to the atmosphere faster than the natural sinks absorb them, so the gas layer thickens: the extra panes. The observed results NASA summarises: global average surface temperature has risen about one degree Celsius since the late nineteenth century, carbon dioxide has increased about 250 times faster than the natural rises after the last ice age, the ice sheets are losing hundreds of billions of tonnes a year, and the sea has risen about 20 centimetres over the last century (NASA, 2024).

A worked example. Pakistan's own connection to the gases, in one paragraph. The country's emissions per person are low by world standards, but its energy, still coal-and-gas heavy, its deforestation pressures, and its growing livestock all add to the shared layer; and the floods and heatwaves of Topic 6.6 are what the shared layer, thickened by everyone's extra panes, hands back. The justice question, smallest emitters, heaviest losses, is real, and it belongs in the classroom honestly: it explains the global negotiation of Unit 5.4, and it does not exempt anyone's next kilowatt.

Common misconception. "The greenhouse effect is itself a problem." The effect is natural and life-sustaining; the enhancement is the problem. The distinction is not pedantry: it tells the pupil that the remedy is not to remove the glass but to stop adding panes, which is why mitigation means cutting emissions, not banning a natural process.

Activity: The pane model​

Work in pairs. Your teacher gives each pair a lamp, a thermometer, and two glass or clear plastic sheets. Measure the temperature under one pane, then two, at one-minute intervals for five minutes. Record the two curves and answer: what does the second pane change, and what in the real atmosphere plays its role? Allow 20 minutes.

Check your understanding​

  1. Explain the greenhouse mechanism in three sentences, sunlight, heat, gases. (Understand)
  2. Why would the Earth freeze without the natural greenhouse effect? (Understand)
  3. What does "250 times faster" mean for the carbon cycle? (Analyze)
  4. Pakistan's per-person emissions are low. Give the honest two-sided statement of its position on the shared layer. (Evaluate)

Summary​

The greenhouse effect is natural and physical: short-wave sunlight in, long-wave heat out, gases returning part of it down, keeping the Earth warm enough for life. The enhancement is the problem: fossil fuels, deforestation and livestock thicken the gas layer, and the observed results include about one degree of warming since the late nineteenth century, rapid carbon-dioxide growth, shrinking ice sheets and rising seas. Pakistan's per-person emissions are low and its losses heavy, which frames the justice of the global negotiation without exempting anyone's next kilowatt.

Self-assessment checklist​

  • I can explain the mechanism in three sentences without jargon.
  • I can state the natural-versus-enhanced distinction and why it matters.
  • I can name the main greenhouse gases with one source each.
  • I can give the honest two-sided statement of Pakistan's position.

Try this at your practicum school​

Find the school's last electricity bill and ask the clerk what powers the grid, the fuel mix in the bill's notes or the local knowledge. Write three sentences: the school's own panes, the national mix, and the one energy change the school could realistically make.

Summative task​

Write 150 to 180 words on the following.

Explain the greenhouse effect and its enhancement to a Class 9 pupil in your own words, using the pane picture, naming the two gases Pakistan's own activities add most, and ending with the one sentence you want the pupil to remember.
