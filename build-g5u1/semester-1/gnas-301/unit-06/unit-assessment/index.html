Unit 6 assessment

Unit summary​

Toxicology asks four questions, the agent, the route, the effect and the dose, with dose the founding one and its caveat, the no-safe-level agents. Substances classify by origin, anthropogenic against natural, by use and by effect; the origin axis decides the control, banning the made and managing the natural. Effects divide by clock, acute against chronic, then by target: genotoxic, mutagenic, teratogenic, carcinogenic and sensitizing, with the dose-response curve rising from a threshold for most effects and no known safe level for the genotoxic class. Organic pollutants linger in fat and inorganic metals never break down; essentiality and toxicity share one ladder; the three absorption doors deliver different doses; bioaccumulation builds within one organism and bio-magnification up the chain, the multiplier that makes a trace in the water a dose in the family. The greenhouse effect is natural and life-sustaining; its enhancement, fossil fuels, deforestation, livestock, is the problem, with about one degree of warming observed. Smog is polluted fog, sulfurous in winter and photochemical in summer, and its controls act at the source and the receptor. Mitigation cuts the causes for the shared future; adaptation softens the arriving present's harms; the two are partners, and the course's tools, the chain, the hierarchy, the governance stack, are how a school answers.

Summative assessment​

Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)​

  1. The founding question of toxicology is: (Remember) a) where was it made b) how much reaches the site c) who sells it d) what colour is it

  2. Arsenic in Sindh's groundwater is a poison that is: (Understand) a) anthropogenic and bannable b) natural and only manageable c) synthetic and replaceable d) biological and treatable

  3. Cutting emissions and installing cool shelters are, respectively: (Understand) a) adaptation and mitigation b) mitigation and adaptation c) both mitigation d) both adaptation

  4. A teratogen's harm depends on: (Understand) a) dose only b) timing as well as dose c) the season only d) nothing measurable

  5. Genotoxic carcinogens are regulated with no safe level because: (Analyze) a) they are always immediately fatal b) their dose-response shows no known threshold c) they cannot be measured d) they only harm workers

  6. Iron is essential at one dose and toxic at another. This illustrates: (Apply) a) the origin axis b) the essentiality ladder c) bio-magnification d) sensitization

  7. A farm worker takes in the same pesticide by skin, breath and hand-to-mouth. What changes across the three? (Apply) a) the substance's chemistry b) the dose reaching the blood c) the effect class d) nothing at all

  8. The natural greenhouse effect: (Understand) a) is the problem to remove b) keeps the Earth warm enough for life c) began with industry d) exists only in cities

  9. Lahore's winter grey is mostly: (Understand) a) photochemical smog b) ordinary fog c) sulfurous smog under an inversion d) desert dust alone

  10. Heat-action plans and flood-proof schools are examples of: (Apply) a) mitigation b) adaptation c) bioactivation d) source control

Restricted-response questions (RRQs)​

  1. Name toxicology's four questions, in any order. (Remember)
  2. Why do controls differ for anthropogenic and natural poisons? (Analyze)
  3. Distinguish acute from chronic effects with the ward's two patients. (Understand)
  4. Why does a sensitizer's harm appear only after years of safe use? (Analyze)
  5. Name the three absorption doors and rank them for a spray drift exposure. (Apply)
  6. Distinguish bioaccumulation from bio-magnification in one sentence each. (Understand)
  7. Explain the greenhouse mechanism in three sentences. (Understand)
  8. Name one mitigation and one adaptation a school could adopt this term, and say which protects the present and which the future. (Apply)
  9. Why can nothing be done at the pathway link during a smog episode? (Analyze)
  10. Mitigation and adaptation answer different questions. State both questions. (Understand)

Extended-response questions (ERQs)​

  1. A parent says the school should "ban every chemical and go natural". Using the classification and the dose idea, write the honest reply with one real example per side. (Analyze)
  2. The district health office asks for the two clocks brief: acute and chronic effects, the different plans they need, and the one special class every pregnant staff member must know. (Evaluate)
  3. The fisheries office asks why it tests big fish, not only water. Using the ladder and the multiplier idea, write the page, ending with advice for the fishing families. (Evaluate)
  4. Warming and the winter grey are one air story. Explain to a Class 9 pupil how the greenhouse effect's enhancement and Lahore's smog grow from the same human activities yet harm by different routes, using the pane picture and the two smog types, and name one control at the source they share. (Analyze)
  5. Course capstone: the principal asks, "Heat and floods, which should we prepare for?" Write the answer: both responses, why both, one adaptation and one mitigation to start this term, who checks, and the sentence the staffroom should remember. (Create)

Answers and marking guidance​

MCQ answer key​

  1. b - the dose makes the poison is the discipline's first law.
  2. b - arsenic dissolves naturally from rock; it can only be managed, tested, treated.
  3. b - mitigation cuts the causes for the shared future; adaptation softens the arriving present's harms.
  4. b - the unborn child's stage of development decides the teratogen's harm.
  5. b - the no-threshold dose-response is the reason for no-level strictness.
  6. b - the essentiality ladder: required at one rung, poisonous higher up.
  7. b - the route changes the dose that reaches the blood; the substance is the same.
  8. b - without it the Earth would average far below freezing.
  9. c - coal and waste burning under an inversion, with photochemical leftovers.
  10. b - adaptation softens the arriving present's harms.

RRQ model answers and mark schemes​

  1. Model: what is the agent; how does it enter (route); what does it do (effect); how much does it take (dose). Marks (4): 1 per question.
  2. Model: anthropogenic poisons are made or concentrated by people, so they can be banned or replaced; natural poisons cannot be banned, only managed, tested, treated, avoided. Marks (2): 1 per half.
  3. Model: the sprayer's collapse an hour after exposure is acute, the antidote clock; the thirty-year well drinker's biopsy is chronic, the testing clock. Marks (2): 1 per patient correctly classed.
  4. Model: the first exposure teaches the immune system; later exposures trigger the trained overreaction, so the harm appears only after the lesson is given. Marks (2): 1 for the teaching; 1 for the later attack.
  5. Model: lung, gut, skin; for spray drift, skin first (the wet spray on the body), lung second (the mist breathed), gut third (hand-to-mouth). Marks (3): 1 for the doors; 2 for a defensible ranking.
  6. Model: bioaccumulation is the build-up within one organism, entry faster than exit; bio-magnification is the rise in concentration up the food chain as each predator eats many prey. Marks (2): 1 per sentence.
  7. Model: short-wave sunlight in and warms the surface; the surface radiates long-wave heat out; greenhouse gases absorb part of the heat and return some downward, warming the lower atmosphere. Marks (3): 1 per sentence.
  8. Model: any real pair, for example mitigation, joining a tree-planting drive or shifting a generator's load hours, which protects the future by cutting emissions; adaptation, shade shelters or a heat-day plan, which protects the present by softening the arriving harms. Marks (2): 1 for a correct mitigation-adaptation pair; 1 for the present-versus-future placement.
  9. Model: the pathway is the air under the lid; dispersion cannot be increased while the inversion sits, so the only honest action is at the source and the receptor. Marks (2): 1 for the physics; 1 for the consequence.
  10. Model: mitigation answers how we stop feeding the future crisis (cut the causes); adaptation answers how we soften the harms already arriving (protect the exposed). Marks (2): 1 per question.

ERQ rubrics​

  1. Analytic rubric. Classification used, both axes honestly (0-3, Analyze) · Dose idea on a real same-substance pair (0-3, Apply) · One natural and one synthetic example, both real (0-2, Understand) · Clarity (0-2). Total 10.
  2. Analytic rubric. Acute and chronic defined with the ward's examples (0-3, Understand) · Plans matched to clocks, antidote versus testing (0-3, Apply) · Teratogen named with its timing logic (0-2, Understand) · Clarity (0-2). Total 10.
  3. Analytic rubric. Ladder arithmetic shown, water to family (0-3, Apply) · Multiplier distinguished from mirror (0-3, Analyze) · One real pollutant named correctly (0-2, Understand) · Advice respectful and practical (0-2, Evaluate). Total 10.
  4. Analytic rubric. Greenhouse mechanism physical, sunlight, heat, gases (0-2, Understand) · Both smog types placed with seasons and causes (0-2, Understand) · The one-story link shown, shared activities, different harm routes (0-3, Analyze) · One shared source control named (0-1, Apply) · Register for a Class 9 pupil (0-2). Total 10.
  5. Analytic rubric. Both responses defined and kept distinct (0-3, Understand) · Capstones placed, heatwave to adaptation, floods to both (0-3, Analyze) · One adaptation and one mitigation, school-scale and real (0-2, Apply) · Who checks named (0-1) · Memorable closing sentence (0-1, Create). Total 10.
