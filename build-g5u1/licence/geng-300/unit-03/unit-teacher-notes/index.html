Unit 3 teacher notes

Sequencing note​

The three topics move from principle to practice. Topic 3.1 sets out the six Cs and the three-part document structure that govern all effective communication. Topic 3.2 applies those principles to speaking, with attention to inclusivity and confidence. Topic 3.3 asks trainees to combine everything in presentations and informal settings. Teach them in order across the four weeks the content-spec allows.

Common misconceptions to probe​

  1. "More words means better communication." This is the most persistent one. Probe it by asking: "If a parent receives a half-page email and a one-line email with the same information, which one will they act on faster?" Trainees who equate length with quality will find the conciseness section counter-intuitive.

  2. "Public speaking is a natural talent, not a skill." Probe it by asking: "Do you know a teacher who seemed terrified of speaking at first and now does it confidently? What changed?" The goal is to normalise the anxiety-confidence curve and to stress that practise, not talent, is what changes performance.

  3. "Informal communication requires no structure." Probe it by asking: "If you meet a senior teacher at a workshop and say nothing, has communication happened?" The goal is to make the case that small talk, networking, turn-taking and politeness all follow learnable conventions.

Managing the activities​

Topic 3.1 - Rewrite for the six Cs (20 minutes, pairs). The trap is that trainees shorten the message without improving clarity. Push them back: "You have made it shorter. Is it also clearer? Can I tell from the first sentence what you want?" Circulate and listen for explanations that link a change to a specific C.

Topic 3.2 - Inclusive-language audit and mini-speak (30 minutes, groups of four). Part 1 (audit) is analytical; Part 2 (mini-speak) is the one that generates the most anxiety. Set clear ground rules: the audience gives one positive and one constructive comment, and the goal is practise, not perfection. Debrief Part 2 by asking: "What did you notice about your own nerves? Did the anxiety-confidence curve ring true?"

Topic 3.3 - Plan and deliver a mini-presentation (35 minutes, groups of three). The trap is that trainees spend all their time on the slides and none on rehearsal. Remind them: "A five-minute talk needs at least one rehearsal out loud. The slides support you; they do not give the talk for you." If possible, have groups present to each other rather than to the whole class, to reduce the pressure.

Practical work and practicum links​

Each topic carries a "Try this at your practicum school" task. The unit works best when the tutor sets these up explicitly before trainees leave for practicum and debriefs them when trainees return.

  • Topic 3.1 practicum task (analyse a school document) produces a concrete example. Use it to open Topic 3.2: ask trainees which of the six Cs the document handled well, and link courtesy and consideration to the inclusive-language section.

  • Topic 3.2 practicum task (observe a speaker) produces a voice-and-body-language analysis. Use it to open Topic 3.3: ask trainees how the speaker managed the room, and connect that to the delivery and Q&A stages of a presentation.

  • Topic 3.3 practicum task (observe a meeting or break) produces examples of informal moves. Use it to make the point that communication skills are not only for formal situations; the staffroom and the workshop break are where professional relationships are built.

A note on the figures​

The unit carries six figures: one concept-map figure (fig-U3-1, the six Cs), one diagram figure (fig-U3-2, the anxiety-confidence curve), one flowchart figure (fig-U3-3, the presentation stages), and three table figures (fig-U3-5, document structure; fig-U3-6, inclusive language; fig-U3-7, conversational moves). The anxiety-confidence curve (fig-U3-2) is the one trainees most often misread as "anxiety is bad". Before you project it, ask trainees to read the three zones and say what the curve claims about moderate anxiety.

Assessment note​

The 10/10/5 bank is designed to be used in two passes: the MCQs and RRQs as formative checks at the end of Topics 3.2 and 3.3, and the ERQs as a summative task at the end of the unit. ERQ 5 (the integrative item) is the one that most reliably separates trainees who have understood the unit from those who have memorised its definitions. If you use only one ERQ, use that one.
