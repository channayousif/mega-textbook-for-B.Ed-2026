Unit 2 assessment

Unit summary​

Purpose, audience and context shape every text. Tone, bias, stereotypes, assumptions and inferences are the features a critical reader identifies. Reading strategies (skimming, scanning, SQ4R, critical reading) are tools that good readers choose according to their purpose. Active listening requires focus, strategy and engagement. Note-taking formats (linear, mind map, Cornell) help you record and review what you hear.

Summative assessment​

Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)​

  1. In this unit's account, "purpose" refers to: (Remember) a) the main idea of a text b) why a text was written c) the audience of a text d) the context of a text

  2. A reader who wants to decide whether a text is relevant should use: (Understand) a) scanning b) SQ4R c) skimming d) critical reading

  3. A pupil says, "The text assumes that all parents value education." This is an example of: (Understand) a) a bias b) an inference c) an assumption d) a stereotype

  4. Which step comes first in the SQ4R strategy? (Remember) a) Read b) Recite c) Survey d) Review

  5. A news article describes a protest as "a small group of troublemakers". This is primarily an example of: (Apply) a) tone b) bias c) inference d) assumption

  6. Cornell notes are characterised by: (Remember) a) branches radiating from a central idea b) a divided page with cues, notes and a summary c) headings and bullet points d) a linear sequence of numbered points

  7. A pupil reads every word of a chapter three times but remembers little. The most likely problem is: (Apply) a) the text is too difficult b) the pupil is using an inefficient strategy c) the pupil has a poor memory d) the text is too long

  8. The difference between what a text says explicitly and what the reader infers is: (Understand) a) explicit meaning is stated; inference is concluded from evidence b) explicit meaning is implied; inference is stated c) explicit meaning is biased; inference is neutral d) explicit meaning is critical; inference is surface-level

  9. Active listening is best described as: (Remember) a) hearing without distraction b) listening with the intention to understand c) taking notes on everything the speaker says d) agreeing with the speaker

  10. A text that says "obviously, everyone agrees" is making: (Apply) a) an inference b) a stereotype c) an assumption d) a tone

Restricted-response questions (RRQs)​

  1. Explain the difference between purpose and audience, and give one example of each. (Remember)

  2. A pupil reads a three-page text three times but cannot answer questions on it. Using this unit's account, explain what is going wrong and propose two specific strategies. (Analyze)

  3. Name the four reading strategies and state when each is most useful. (Remember)

  4. A text says, "Naturally, everyone agrees that education is important." Identify the assumption and explain why it is an assumption, not a fact. (Analyze)

  5. Explain the difference between skimming and scanning, and give one example of each. (Understand)

  6. Describe the six steps of SQ4R in order. (Remember)

  7. A news article describes a protest as "a small group of troublemakers". Identify the bias, explain what a more neutral description would be, and explain what the original description reveals about the writer's purpose. (Analyze)

  8. Explain why "listening is a passive skill" is a misconception, and describe two specific strategies for active listening. (Understand)

  9. A friend says, "Critical reading means finding faults with everything." Using this unit's account, explain what that claim overlooks. (Evaluate)

  10. Describe two note-taking formats and explain when each is most useful. (Remember)

Extended-response questions (ERQs)​

  1. A teacher gives pupils a textbook passage and asks them to read it. The pupils read every word but cannot answer the questions. Using this unit's account of reading strategies, explain what is going wrong, propose two specific strategies the teacher could teach, and justify each by reference to the purpose of the reading. (Analyze)

  2. Read the following text: "The government has announced a new initiative to improve girls' education in rural areas. Under the programme, schools will receive additional funding and female teachers will be given priority in recruitment. Officials say the initiative will help bridge the gender gap in literacy." Apply the four moves for analysing a complete text to this passage: identify surface features, identify hidden features, evaluate the writer's choices, connect to your Pakistani classroom context. (Evaluate)

  3. A pupil says, "I understand the text but I do not know if it is good." Using this unit's account, explain what skill the pupil needs to develop, describe the four moves for analysing a complete text, and apply them to a described short text. (Analyze)

  4. Evaluate the claim that "critical reading means finding faults." Use this unit's account of purpose, audience, context, tone, bias, assumptions and inferences to argue for or against the claim. (Evaluate)

  5. Integrative. You are placed in a government primary school. A Class 5 teacher asks you to help pupils who can decode words but cannot understand what they read. Using this unit's account of purpose, audience, context, reading strategies and active listening, design a 30-minute lesson that teaches two specific strategies and explains why each strategy works. Justify each choice. (Create)

Answers and marking guidance​

MCQ answer key​

  1. b - purpose is why a text was written.
  2. c - skimming is for deciding whether a text is relevant.
  3. c - the text takes something for granted without stating it.
  4. c - survey is the first step of SQ4R.
  5. b - "troublemakers" is a loaded term that favours one perspective.
  6. b - Cornell notes use a divided page with cues, notes and a summary.
  7. b - reading without a strategy is inefficient; the problem is the strategy, not the memory.
  8. a - explicit meaning is stated; inference is concluded from evidence.
  9. b - active listening is listening with the intention to understand.
  10. c - "obviously, everyone agrees" takes something for granted; it is an assumption.

RRQ model answers and mark schemes​

1. (4 marks) Purpose is why a text was written (1); audience is who the text is written for (1); the purpose example is genuinely about the writer's goal (1); the audience example is genuinely about the intended reader (1).

2. (5 marks) Explains that reading without a strategy is inefficient (1); explains that the pupil is reading passively (1); proposes two specific strategies (e.g. SQ4R, critical reading) (1 each); justifies each by reference to the purpose (1).

3. (4 marks) Names all four strategies: skimming, scanning, SQ4R, critical reading (1 each up to 4). One mark per correct use case (up to 4).

4. (5 marks) Identifies the assumption: that everyone agrees (1); explains that an assumption is taken for granted without evidence (1); notes that the word "naturally" signals the assumption (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 2.1 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

5. (4 marks) Skimming is reading quickly for the main idea (1); scanning is reading quickly for specific information (1); the skimming example is genuinely about overview (1); the scanning example is genuinely about finding specific detail (1).

6. (6 marks) One mark per step in order: survey, question, read, recite, review, reflect.

7. (5 marks) Identifies the bias: "troublemakers" favours one side (1); provides a more neutral description (e.g. "a group of protesters") (1); explains that the original description reveals a hostile purpose or audience alignment (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 2.1 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

8. (4 marks) Explains that listening requires active engagement, not just hearing (1); describes two specific strategies (e.g. focused listening, note-taking, overcoming barriers) (1 each up to 2); the explanation is coherent (1).

9. (5 marks) Takes the claim at its strongest (1); explains that critical reading means understanding how a text works (1); notes that evaluation includes judgement of quality, not just fault-finding (1); argues a verdict with reasons (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

10. (4 marks) Describes two formats (e.g. linear, mind map, Cornell) (1 each up to 2); explains when each is most useful (1 each up to 2).
