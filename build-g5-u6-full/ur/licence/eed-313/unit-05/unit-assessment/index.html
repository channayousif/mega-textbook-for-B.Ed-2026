Unit 5 assessment

Unit summary​

A classroom management philosophy draws on four layers: the definition and theories of Unit 1, the curriculum and differentiation of Unit 2, the routines of Unit 3, and the community and care of Unit 4. The peer-critique protocol evaluates a plan for clarity, consistency and coverage. The seven closing misconceptions are the guide's own; the evidence against each is drawn from the four units. The culminating artefact is the classroom management plan itself.

Summative assessment​

Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)​

  1. The peer-critique protocol evaluates a plan for clarity, coverage and: (Remember) a) consistency b) creativity c) compliance d) complexity

  2. A plan holds a constructivist philosophy but describes a transmission-plan. The peer-critique protocol would flag this as a failure of: (Understand) a) clarity b) consistency c) coverage d) creativity

  3. The guide names seven closing misconceptions. Which of the following is one of them? (Remember) a) The best-managed classroom is one where the teacher is in control of learning b) The best-managed classroom is one where the pupils are engaged in intellectually demanding work c) Management is proactive and acts on the learning environment d) Differentiation adjusts the route without lowering the target

  4. A trainee says, "My reviewer noted an inconsistency between my philosophy and my routines. I will revise the routines to match the philosophy." This is an example of: (Apply) a) receiving feedback and revising in light of it b) defending the plan against criticism c) dismissing the feedback as opinion d) changing the philosophy to match the routines

  5. Which of the following best captures the course's closing argument about classroom management? (Understand) a) Management is about keeping pupils quiet and compliant b) Management is the proactive work of creating the conditions in which learning happens c) Management is a set of tips that work in every classroom d) Management is the same as discipline with a nicer name

  6. A teacher presents a well-sequenced lesson but pupils are disruptive during the transition between activities. The most likely routine failure is: (Apply) a) no materials routine b) no checking routine c) no transition routine d) no anchor routine

  7. Wong and Wong (1998) argue that the first weeks of term should be spent: (Remember) a) establishing consequences for every rule b) teaching the plan, then teaching the curriculum c) letting pupils discover the routines themselves d) focusing on relationship-building before any routines

  8. A teacher in a multigrade classroom says, "I just teach two lessons at once." Using Unit 3, explain what that claim overlooks. (Understand) a) The need for an anchor routine b) The need for a materials routine c) The need for a checking routine d) The need for a safety routine

  9. Which feature most distinguishes a well-managed classroom from a merely orderly one? (Understand) a) The pupils are silent for most of the lesson b) The pupils are engaged in intellectually demanding work for most of the lesson c) The teacher gives instructions only once d) No pupil ever breaches a rule

  10. Marzano, Marzano and Pickering (2003) found that the effectiveness of group work depends most on: (Apply) a) the structure chosen b) the size of the group c) the routines that carry the structure d) the subject being taught

Restricted-response questions (RRQs)​

  1. Explain why the peer-critique protocol uses three criteria (clarity, consistency, coverage) rather than a single overall judgement. (Understand)

  2. A reviewer says, "I do not agree with your philosophy, so I cannot use it." Using Topics 5.2 and 5.3, explain what that response overlooks and propose how the peer could give useful feedback. (Analyze)

  3. Explain what the seven closing misconceptions have in common, and why a teacher might hold them. (Understand)

  4. A teacher says, "I have been teaching for twenty years; I do not need a philosophy." Using the closing synthesis from Topic 5.4, explain what that claim overlooks. (Analyze)

  5. Analyse the difference between protocol-based feedback and unstructured opinion, using the peer-critique protocol from Topics 5.2 and 5.3. (Analyze)

  6. A teacher says, "I do not need routines; I just manage as I go." Using Topic 5.1, explain what that claim costs in instructional time. (Analyze)

  7. Explain why an ethic of care is not the same as being permissive, using the four components from Topic 5.3. (Understand)

  8. A teacher sends a disruptive pupil out of the room and the lesson continues. Using Topic 5.4, explain what that response costs in relational terms. (Analyze)

  9. Describe the six phases of a community visit, in order, and explain why the match between person and topic matters. (Understand)

  10. A school wants to move from participation to involvement. Using Unit 4, argue which single practice would most improve the relationship and justify the choice. (Analyze)

Extended-response questions (ERQs)​

  1. A peer's classroom management plan says, "I believe learning is the pupil's active construction of knowledge," but describes a transmission-plan with teacher explanation then practice. Using the peer-critique protocol, identify the inconsistency, explain why it matters, and propose a specific revision. (Evaluate)

  2. A trainee says, "The course has given me a set of tips for managing a classroom." Using the closing synthesis from Topic 5.4 and the four-layer model from Topic 5.1, evaluate the claim and argue what the course has actually given the trainee. (Evaluate)

  3. Culminating task: the classroom management plan. Take a classroom you have observed during practicum (or one from your own schooling). Write a classroom management plan for that room that does all four of the following: states a philosophy of learning and connects it to at least two of the three theories from Unit 1; describes at least one curriculum decision and explains how it functions as a management act; proposes a routine set that fits the constraint the room names; and explains how the plan builds an ethic of care and personal accountability. Justify each choice by reference to the pupils or the setting. (Create)

  4. Evaluate the claim that "a caring classroom has no consequences." Use the distinction between punitive and restorative consequences from Topics 5.3 and 5.4, the six-step model, and at least one concrete example of a described incident. (Evaluate)

  5. A teacher at your practicum school manages mainly through rules and exclusion: pupils who misbehave are sent out, and the community is asked for money but never invited in. Using the four-layer model from Topic 5.1, propose one specific change to the teacher's philosophy, one to their routines, and one to their community relationships, and justify each by reference to the described pupils or setting. (Create)

Answers and marking guidance​

MCQ answer key​

  1. a - the three criteria are clarity, coverage and consistency.
  2. b - a mismatch between philosophy and plan is a consistency failure.
  3. a - "The best-managed classroom is one where the teacher is in control of learning" is one of the seven closing misconceptions.
  4. a - the trainee is receiving feedback and revising in light of it, which is the revision step of the cycle.
  5. b - the course's closing argument is that management is proactive and centred on learning.
  6. c - a transition routine governs how pupils move between activities; without it, the transition leaks time and produces disruption.
  7. b - Wong and Wong's order is routines first, then procedures, then rules.
  8. a - the anchor routine is the task one grade does while the teacher works with the other; a teacher who "teaches two lessons at once" has no anchor.
  9. b - a well-managed classroom is defined by the quality of pupil engagement, not by the absence of noise or breaches.
  10. c - Marzano et al. found that the routines that carry the structure matter more than the structure itself.

RRQ model answers and mark schemes​

1. (6 marks) Explains that a single overall judgement is vague (1); names the three criteria and explains what each catches that a single judgement would miss (1 each); the reasoning is coherent (1).

2. (5 marks) Explains that feedback evaluates the plan, not the philosophy (1); names a specific protocol-based move the peer could make (1); links the move to the giving-and-receiving model from Topic 5.3 (1); the proposal is concrete and actionable (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

3. (7 marks) Identifies the common thread (e.g. all confuse control or compliance with learning) (1); explains why a teacher might hold these beliefs (drawing on the four units) (2); names at least five of the seven misconceptions correctly (1 each up to 4).

4. (5 marks) Explains that every teacher has a philosophy, whether examined or not (1); links this to the closing synthesis (1); argues that the course has given the tools to examine and defend that philosophy (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 5.4 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

5. (5 marks) Distinguishes opinion from protocol-based feedback (1); names the three criteria (1); explains that the protocol makes feedback specific and evidence-based (1); uses the protocol to structure the analysis (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

6. (5 marks) Explains that a teacher without routines spends the day negotiating every transition (1); links this to the time-recovery argument from Unit 3 (1); quantifies, even roughly, the time lost (1); argues that the teacher's time is being spent on management rather than teaching (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

7. (4 marks) Names the four components of an ethic of care (1); explains that care includes consequences, not their absence (1); distinguishes permissive from caring (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

8. (5 marks) Identifies the cost: the relationship between teacher and pupil is damaged (1); explains that exclusion stops the behaviour but does not rebuild the relationship (1); links this to the six-step model: steps 2 to 6 are missing (1); argues that the pupil learns compliance, not accountability (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

9. (4 marks) Names the six phases in order: identify a need, find a community member, make first contact, plan the visit, host the visit, follow up (1); explains that the match between person and topic is what makes the visit serve the curriculum (1); links this to the six-phase model (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

10. (5 marks) Proposes a specific practice (e.g. a parent sitting on a planning committee) (1); explains why the practice constitutes involvement, not just participation (1); links the choice to Unit 4's distinction (1); the proposal is concrete and realistic (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).
