Unit 3 assessment

Unit summary​

Effective communication rests on six principles: clarity, coherence, conciseness, correctness, courtesy and consideration. Documents (emails, reports, letters) follow a three-part structure: introduction, body, conclusion. Inclusive language avoids stereotypes and respects cross-cultural differences. Public speaking anxiety follows an anxiety-confidence curve, and strategies such as preparation, rehearsal, voice modulation and body language help keep anxiety in the helpful zone. Presentations follow seven stages from purpose and audience definition through to follow up. Informal communication, far from being structureless, follows conventions of small talk, networking, turn-taking, conversational repair and politeness strategies.

Summative assessment​

Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)​

  1. In this unit's account, "conciseness" means: (Remember) a) using as few words as possible, even if the meaning suffers b) saying what you need to say without unnecessary words c) writing in short sentences only d) avoiding formal language

  2. A message that uses "he" to refer to a person of unknown gender fails which of the six Cs? (Understand) a) Clarity b) Conciseness c) Courtesy and consideration d) Coherence

  3. The three-part document structure recommended in this unit is: (Remember) a) title, body, references b) introduction, body, conclusion c) greeting, message, signature d) heading, paragraph, footnote

  4. A teacher replaces "chairman" with "chairperson". This is an example of: (Apply) a) voice modulation b) gender-neutral language c) conversational repair d) managing time

  5. On the anxiety-confidence curve, performance is highest when anxiety is: (Understand) a) zero b) very high c) moderate d) rising

  6. Which stage comes first when planning a presentation? (Remember) a) Design visual aids b) Rehearse c) Define purpose and audience d) Handle Q&A

  7. A presenter does not know the answer to an audience question. The recommended response is: (Apply) a) change the subject b) guess and move on c) say you do not know and offer to follow up d) repeat the question back to the audience

  8. "Turn-taking" in conversation refers to: (Remember) a) the order in which speakers alternate b) the topic of the conversation c) the volume of the speakers d) the length of the conversation

  9. A speaker who varies their volume, pace and pitch to hold attention is using: (Apply) a) conversational repair b) small talk c) voice modulation d) turn-taking

  10. A teacher says, "Even the slowest pupils finished the task." The most inclusive rewrite is: (Apply) a) "All pupils finished the task." b) "Some pupils finished the task." c) "The task was too easy." d) "The teacher finished the task."

Restricted-response questions (RRQs)​

  1. Name the six principles of effective communication (the six Cs) and give one example of each. (Remember)

  2. A colleague writes a three-paragraph email in which the purpose is stated only in the last sentence. Using this unit's account of the three-part structure, explain what is wrong and how to fix it. (Analyze)

  3. Explain the difference between courtesy and consideration, with one example of each. (Understand)

  4. A trainee says, "I cannot do public speaking because I am shy." Using the anxiety-confidence curve, explain what this claim overlooks and propose two specific strategies. (Analyze)

  5. Explain the difference between small talk and networking, and state the purpose of each. (Understand)

  6. Describe the seven stages of planning and delivering a presentation, in order. (Remember)

  7. A notice says, "Every teacher should make sure his classroom is clean." Identify the gender-exclusive language and rewrite the sentence inclusively. (Apply)

  8. A friend says, "Informal communication is just chatting, so there is nothing to learn." Using this unit's account, explain what that claim overlooks. (Evaluate)

  9. Name three strategies for managing stage fright and explain how each one helps. (Remember)

  10. Describe two politeness strategies and give one example of each. (Remember)

Extended-response questions (ERQs)​

  1. A colleague sends the following email to a headteacher: "I am writing to you because I wanted to bring to your kind attention that there are some problems with the classroom furniture and I think something should be done about it." Using this unit's account of the six Cs and the three-part structure, rewrite the email and explain four specific changes you made. (Analyze)

  2. A trainee says, "I have to give a presentation tomorrow and I am terrified." Using this unit's account of the anxiety-confidence curve and the seven-stage planning process, write a response that (a) explains why some nervousness is normal, (b) proposes three specific strategies, and (c) justifies each strategy. (Analyze)

  3. A school notice says, "The headmaster and his lady teachers are invited to the workshop." Analyse this sentence for gender-exclusive and stereotypical language. Rewrite it inclusively and explain each change by reference to the six Cs. (Analyze)

  4. Evaluate the claim that "good communicators are born, not made." Use this unit's account of the six Cs, the anxiety-confidence curve and the conventions of informal communication to argue for or against the claim. (Evaluate)

  5. Integrative. You are placed in a government school where the headteacher asks you to (a) deliver a ten-minute presentation on inclusive language to the teaching staff and (b) network with visiting teachers at the tea break afterwards. Using this unit's account of presentations, informal communication and the six Cs, plan both activities. Justify each choice. (Create)

Answers and marking guidance​

MCQ answer key​

  1. b - conciseness means saying what you need to say without unnecessary words.
  2. c - using "he" for a person of unknown gender fails courtesy and consideration.
  3. b - the three-part structure is introduction, body, conclusion.
  4. b - "chairperson" is a gender-neutral alternative to "chairman".
  5. c - on the anxiety-confidence curve, performance peaks at moderate anxiety.
  6. c - defining purpose and audience is the first stage of planning a presentation.
  7. c - the recommended response is to say you do not know and offer to follow up.
  8. a - turn-taking is the order in which speakers alternate.
  9. c - varying volume, pace and pitch is voice modulation.
  10. a - "All pupils finished the task" describes what happened without labelling any group.

RRQ model answers and mark schemes​

1. (6 marks) Names all six Cs: clarity, coherence, conciseness, correctness, courtesy, consideration (1 each up to 6). One mark per genuinely illustrative example (up to 6).

2. (5 marks) Identifies the problem: the purpose is buried in the last sentence (1); explains the fix: state the purpose in the introduction (1); links to the three-part structure (1); the reasoning draws on Topic 3.1 (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

3. (4 marks) Courtesy is respecting the reader or listener (1); consideration is tailoring the message to the audience (1); the courtesy example is genuinely about respect (1); the consideration example is genuinely about audience adaptation (1).

4. (5 marks) Explains that shyness is not a fixed barrier (1); explains the anxiety-confidence curve (1); proposes two specific strategies (e.g. preparation, rehearsal, deep breathing, eye contact) (1 each); links the strategies to the curve (1).

5. (4 marks) Small talk is brief, non-controversial conversation to build rapport (1); networking is building professional relationships (1); the small-talk purpose is genuinely about rapport (1); the networking purpose is genuinely about professional connection (1).

6. (7 marks) One mark per stage in order: define purpose and audience, research and organise content, design visual aids, rehearse, deliver, handle Q&A, follow up (1 each up to 7).

7. (4 marks) Identifies "his" and "lady teachers" as gender-exclusive (1); rewrites inclusively (e.g. "All teachers are invited to the workshop" or "Headteacher and teachers are invited ...") (1); the rewrite avoids gendered language (1); the explanation is coherent (1).

8. (5 marks) Takes the claim at its strongest (1); explains that informal communication follows conventions (small talk, networking, turn-taking, repair, politeness) (1); notes that these conventions can be learned (1); argues a verdict with reasons (1); the reasoning is coherent (1).

9. (6 marks) Names three strategies (e.g. preparation, rehearsal, deep breathing, eye contact, making eye contact with one friendly face) (1 each up to 3); explains how each one helps (1 each up to 3).

10. (4 marks) Describes two strategies (e.g. indirectness, hedging, showing appreciation) (1 each up to 2); gives one genuine example of each (1 each up to 2).
