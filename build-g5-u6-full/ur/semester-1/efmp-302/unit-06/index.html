Sustaining Professionalism through Continuous Learning

This is the last unit of the course, and it is the one that decides whether the other five were worth your time.

Unit 1 said a profession rests on specialised knowledge that keeps developing. Unit 3 described the teacher as a researcher of their own practice and a life-long learner. Unit 4 gave you a self-evaluation cycle. Unit 5 ended on what remains within your control when conditions are hard. Every one of those was a promissory note. This unit is where they are paid, or not.

The honest position is that most teachers stop developing. Not through laziness: through the ordinary operation of a hard job in which nothing external requires it, the available development is often poor, and the first years are survived rather than examined. Knowing that is the beginning of not doing it.

Unit learning outcomes​

By the end of this unit you should be able to:

  • Define continuous professional development, distinguish it from a training course and from an appraisal requirement, and explain why continuing development is not optional for a professional.
  • State the principles that distinguish development which changes practice from development which does not.
  • Describe the preservice, new teacher and experienced teacher stages, the characteristic difficulty of each, and the support each actually needs.
  • Compare conferences, workshops, online platforms, professional learning communities and reflective tools against cost, reach and what each can and cannot deliver.
  • Explain reflective practice as a structured professional method rather than as keeping a diary, and describe journals, portfolios, peer feedback and lesson study.
  • Build a personal professional development plan and defend it against the conditions that ordinarily defeat one.

Prerequisite knowledge​

Units 1 to 5. This unit assumes Unit 3's researcher role, Unit 4's self-evaluation cycle and Unit 5's control-and-influence distinction, and its job is to turn those three into something you do deliberately across a career rather than once during training.

In this unit​

  1. Topic 6.1 - What continuing development is, and why it is not optional - the concept, the need, and the principles that separate development that works from development that does not.
  2. Topic 6.2 - The stages of a teaching career - preservice, new teacher and experienced teacher, and what each stage actually needs.
  3. Topic 6.3 - The ways teachers actually develop - conferences, workshops, online platforms, professional learning communities, and reflective tools including lesson study.
  4. Topic 6.4 - Building a plan that survives the year - what a plan contains, and what usually destroys one by March.

How to use this unit​

Topic 6.4 is the point of the unit and the point of much of the course. Do not treat it as a summary. It asks you to produce something real, and the plan you write there is intended to outlive this course.

Topic 6.3 is the longest and most practical. If you are choosing where to spend effort, spend it there and in 6.4.

The question bank covers all four topics, and its final extended item asks you to build and defend a development plan drawing on the whole course. That item is the closest thing this course has to a final examination of whether any of it was understood.
