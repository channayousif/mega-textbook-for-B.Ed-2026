Unit 6 assessment

Unit summary​

Profit is takings minus total costs, its percentage always against costs; loss is the same subtraction flipped. Simple interest is principal x rate x time, straight-line for savers and borrowers alike, and an instalment can hide a rate four times a bank's. A budget plans income, fixed costs, variable costs, savings (before spending) and review. Media numbers persuade by construction: the truncated axis, the convenient base, the missing comparison, the hidden rate - and the five questions restore the arithmetic. A data-informed decision runs question, evidence, options, trade-offs; the standard indicators (literacy 57.54 percent of persons 10+, out-of-school 46.29 percent of ages 5-16 in Sindh, 2023) each answer one question and hide another; data informs the decision, never replaces the decider.

Summative assessment​

Multiple-choice questions (MCQs)​

  1. The fair stall took 7,200 rupees against costs of 5,600. Its profit is: (Apply) a) 7,200 rupees b) 1,600 rupees c) 2,800 rupees d) 5,600 rupees

  2. Profit percent is always computed against: (Remember) a) takings b) total costs c) the price per plate d) last year's profit

  3. 10,000 rupees at 5 percent simple interest for 3 years earns: (Apply) a) 500 rupees b) 1,000 rupees c) 1,500 rupees d) 15,000 rupees

  4. In the five-part budget, savings are subtracted: (Understand) a) after variable costs, from what is left b) before variable costs, right after fixed costs c) only at year end d) never, if income is tight

  5. A bar chart whose axis starts at 80 instead of 0: (Remember) a) is the truncated-axis move b) is the convenient-base move c) is the hidden-rate move d) is honest if the bars are equal width

  6. "Now with 50 percent more fruit" hides: (Understand) a) the survey size b) the base of the percentage c) the axis start d) the instalment count

  7. A 20,000-rupee item at 2,000 rupees a month for 12 months carries a hidden first-year interest of: (Apply) a) 0 rupees b) 2,000 rupees (10 percent) c) 4,000 rupees (20 percent) d) 24,000 rupees

  8. School A passes 225 of 300; school B passes 54 of 60. The firmer rate belongs to: (Apply) a) B, because 90 is higher b) A, because 300 candidates make the rate steadier c) neither, rates cannot be compared d) both equally

  9. The 46.29 percent out-of-school figure for Sindh counts: (Understand) a) persons 10 years and above b) children ages 5 to 16 c) enrolled pupils d) teachers

  10. The four stages of the decision loop, in order, are: (Remember) a) evidence, question, options, trade-offs b) question, evidence, options, trade-offs c) options, trade-offs, question, evidence d) question, options, evidence, trade-offs

Restricted-response questions (RRQs)​

  1. Compute the profit and profit percent: takings 9,000 rupees, costs 7,500 rupees. (Apply)
  2. Compute the simple interest on 15,000 rupees at 4 percent for 5 years, and the total in the account. (Apply)
  3. A stipend of 25,000 rupees has fixed costs of 12,000 and a savings target of 3,000. State the variable-costs allowance and why savings come out before variable costs. (Understand)
  4. A friend says the fair stall "made 7,200 rupees". Correct the statement in two sentences using cost and percentage. (Analyze)
  5. Name the two questions the claim "9 out of 10 dentists recommend it" needs answered before you repeat it. (Analyze)
  6. A 20,000-rupee item at 2,000 a month for 12 months: compute the total paid and the hidden rate as a percentage. (Apply)
  7. Explain in two sentences why correct arithmetic can still mislead. (Understand)
  8. School A passes 225 of 300; school B passes 54 of 60. Compute both rates and state which rests on the firmer base, with a reason. (Apply)
  9. State what a pupil-teacher ratio of 30 can hide, with a concrete example. (Analyze)
  10. Name the four stages of the decision loop, and say which stage Unit 1's four-step strategy grew into. (Understand)

Extended-response questions (ERQs)​

  1. A school cooperative wants to lend 50,000 rupees for a photocopy machine earning 3,000 rupees a month, while a bank charges 6 percent simple interest per year. Compute the yearly earnings and the yearly interest; state whether the machine pays for its loan and by how much; and identify one cost the comparison ignores, with a rough estimate showing how it could change the conclusion. (Analyze)
  2. A canteen supplier's poster reads: "9 out of 10 pupils prefer our samosas - and now 30 percent bigger!" State the two questions each claim needs answered; compute what "30 percent bigger" means if the old samosa was 80 grams; and write the honest one-sentence version a headteacher could act on. (Analyze)
  3. A district committee must spend 500,000 rupees either on two new teachers for a school with a pupil-teacher ratio of 50, or on a bus for 40 pupils who walk 5 kilometres, in a district whose out-of-school rate (ages 5 to 16) is 46.29 percent (PBS, 2023). Run the decision loop - question, evidence, options with costs, trade-offs; recommend one option with the computation that supports it; and state one piece of evidence that would change your recommendation. (Analyze)
  4. Using the canteen ledger from your Topic 6.1 practicum task (or the mock ledger from the fair stall audit), present the week's profit or loss with its percentage; then write the two-sentence recommendation for next week's stock order, and evaluate what the percentage adds that the rupee figure alone does not. (Evaluate)
  5. Return to your Unit 1 self-assessment checklist. Using one decision from your practicum reconstruction (Topic 6.3) and one advertisement audit (Topic 6.2), evaluate how your quantitative reasoning has changed across the course: name two habits you now have that you lacked in Unit 1, one situation where the numbers changed your first instinct, and one thing you still want to strengthen. (Evaluate)

Answers and marking guidance​

MCQ answer key​

  1. b - 7,200 - 5,600 = 1,600 rupees.
  2. b - the percentage base is total costs.
  3. c - 10,000 x 0.05 x 3 = 1,500 rupees.
  4. b - savings come out before variable spending, or they do not happen.
  5. a - starting the axis near the data is the truncated-axis move.
  6. b - "50 percent more" hides more than what.
  7. c - 24,000 paid on 20,000 for a year is 4,000 interest, a 20 percent rate.
  8. b - 300 candidates give A's rate a steadier base than B's 60.
  9. b - the out-of-school rate counts children ages 5 to 16.
  10. b - question, evidence, options, trade-offs.

RRQ model answers and mark schemes​

  1. Model: profit = 1,500 rupees; 1,500 / 7,500 = 20 percent. Marks (2): 1 - value; 1 - percentage.
  2. Model: 15,000 x 0.04 x 5 = 3,000 rupees; total 18,000. Marks (2): 1 - interest; 1 - total.
  3. Model: 25,000 - 12,000 - 3,000 = 10,000 rupees; savings come out first because what is left after spending is usually nothing. Marks (2): 1 - allowance; 1 - the reason.
  4. Model: the 7,200 is takings, not profit; costs of 5,600 leave a profit of 1,600 rupees, about 28.6 percent of costs. Marks (2): 1 - takings vs profit; 1 - the corrected figure with percentage.
  5. Model: how many dentists, chosen how (the base); and who ran and paid for the survey (the source). Marks (2): 1 each.
  6. Model: total paid 24,000; interest 4,000 on 20,000 for one year = 20 percent. Marks (2): 1 - total; 1 - rate.
  7. Model: the arithmetic can be correct while the question, base or comparison was engineered to persuade; the construction, not the calculation, carries the misleading. Marks (2): 1 - construction vs calculation; 1 - an example or restatement.
  8. Model: A = 75 percent, B = 90 percent; A's base of 300 candidates makes its rate steadier (B's 60 means each pupil moves B's rate by more than a point). Marks (2): 1 - both rates; 1 - the base judgement with reason.
  9. Model: a ratio of 30 can hide one class of 80 and three of 20 - the same average, opposite realities. Marks (2): 1 - the hiding named; 1 - a concrete example.
  10. Model: question, evidence, options, trade-offs; the plan step grew into options and trade-offs. Marks (3): 1 - four stages; 1 - the growth named; 1 - a sentence on why (real decisions choose between incomparable goods).

ERQ rubrics​

  1. Photocopy loan. Analytic rubric: Yearly figures (0-2: 36,000 earned; 3,000 interest); Comparison (0-2: the machine earns 33,000 more than the loan costs); Ignored cost (0-2, Analyze: a real cost with a rough estimate and its effect on the conclusion). Total 6.
  2. Samosa poster. Analytic rubric: Questions (0-2: the survey's who-and-how-many; the size claim's base); Computation (0-2: 104 grams); Honest version (0-2, Analyze: one sentence carrying both numbers with their bases). Total 6.
  3. Committee grant. Analytic rubric: Loop run (0-2: all four stages present and separated); Recommendation with computation (0-2, Analyze: e.g. two teachers cut a 100-pupil school's ratio from 50 to 25 (100/4), while the bus moves 40 pupils; the support names which population each option serves); Changing evidence (0-2: a concrete item that would flip the choice). Total 6.
  4. Canteen ledger. Analytic rubric: Profit with percentage (0-2: correct computation against costs); Recommendation (0-2: two sentences tied to the numbers); Percentage's added value evaluated (0-3, Analyze: what the percentage enables that the rupee figure cannot - comparison across weeks or stalls, size independence). Total 7.
  5. Course reflection. Analytic rubric: Two habits named (0-2: real, specific habits like the total check or the five questions); Numbers vs instinct (0-3, Analyze: a concrete case where data changed the first instinct); Strength to build (0-2: honest and specific); Clarity (0-1). Total 8.
