Topic 5.3 - Measures of central tendency and interpreting statistics

A real classroom situation​

Two teachers compare their classes' test results. Miss Farhana's class of 11 scored: 20, 45, 50, 55, 60, 62, 62, 70, 70, 70, 96. Mr. Qurban's class also averaged "about 60". The headteacher, who has this topic, asks one question of each marksheet: "Which average are you quoting?" Miss Farhana's mean is 60 (the total 660 divided by 11); her median is 62 (the sixth mark when sorted); her mode is 70 (the only mark appearing three times). Three honest averages of one class, three different stories - and the diagram above shows all three living in different places on the same data. A class where the mean sits below the mode is a class with a long tail of low scorers pulling the mean down: that is a teaching fact, not just an arithmetic one.

Explanation​

Measures of central tendency​

The three measures of central tendency each answer "what is typical?" differently, and introductory statistics texts teach all three side by side for exactly this reason (Marecek, Anthony-Smith and Mathis, 2020, Section 5.5).

  • The mean is the fair-share value: add all values, divide by how many. It uses every value, so one extreme mark can drag it - the 20 and the 96 in Miss Farhana's class pull in opposite directions.
  • The median is the middle value when the data are sorted. Half the class scored at or below 62, half at or above. The median ignores how far the extremes sit, so it is the honest average for data with outliers - incomes, house prices, marks with one disaster.
  • The mode is the most frequent value: 70, the mark the class "decided" to get. It is the only average that works for categories too - the modal travel mode, the modal shoe size.

Worked example. A shop's five daily takings are 4,000, 4,200, 4,100, 4,300 and 39,000 rupees (one wedding order). Mean = 55,600 / 5 = 11,120 rupees - a figure no ordinary day resembled. Median = 4,200 rupees - the honest "typical day". When a headteacher reports "average class size", the same choice applies: a few tiny sections can make the mean flatter the reality most pupils live.

Interpretation of statistical information​

Published statistics arrive ready-dressed, and reading them is a skill of questions. The table above lists the five that carry most of the weight. Apply them to a real figure: the 2023 census counted Sindh's literacy rate (10 years and above) at 57.54 percent - 64.23 percent for males and 50.21 percent for females (PBS, 2023).

  • Who collected it? The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, the national statistical office, in the country's first digital census - a strong provenance.
  • How? Door-to-door enumeration on tablets, March to May 2023, approved by the Council of Common Interests.
  • Compared to what? The same measure in 2017 was 54.57 percent - so the rise is about 3 percentage points, small and slow.
  • What is the base? "10 years and above" - a child of nine who cannot read is not counted in this rate at all; a different base (say, 15 and above) gives a different number.
  • What is missing? The gap: about 14 percentage points between male and female literacy (64.23 minus 50.21), and an out-of-school rate (ages 5 to 16) of 46.29 percent - almost half of Sindh's school-age children. The single headline number hides both.

Tout (2020) traces how international assessments learned the same lesson: what counts as "literacy" or "numerate" depends on the tasks asked, and cross-comparing numbers without reading their construction misleads. A teacher who can read a statistic this way - base, comparison, collector, missing gap - is a teacher a parent can trust with a marksheet.

Activity: Marksheet clinic​

Work in groups of three. Your tutor gives each group two anonymised marksheets of 11 scores each (one with an outlier, one without). Each group computes the mean, median and mode of both, then writes three sentences: which average each teacher should quote to parents and why. The debrief question: what does the gap between mean and median say about a class? About 25 minutes.

Check your understanding​

  1. Compute the mean, median and mode of: 20, 45, 50, 55, 60, 62, 62, 70, 70, 70, 96. (Apply)
  2. A shop's takings are 4,000, 4,200, 4,100, 4,300, 39,000 rupees. Which average describes the typical day, and why is the other one misleading here? (Apply)
  3. Sindh's literacy rate (10 years and above) is 57.54 percent. State what changes if the base were "15 years and above", and why the base must be read with the number. (Analyze)
  4. Name the only average that works for categories, with one example. (Understand)
  5. A news report says "literacy rose to 57.54 percent". Write the two questions you would ask before repeating the claim, and what each guards against. (Analyze)

Summary​

The mean is the fair share that outliers can drag, the median is the middle that ignores them, and the mode is the most frequent value - three honest averages that can tell three different stories about one class. Reading a published statistic means asking who collected it, how, compared to what, on what base, and what it leaves out - and the Sindh literacy figure shows how much a single number can hide.

Self-assessment checklist​

  • I can compute the mean, median and mode of a small data set and say what each hides.
  • I can choose the honest average for data with an outlier, with a reason.
  • I can interrogate a published statistic with the five interpretation questions.

Try this at your practicum school​

Take one class's latest test (anonymised, with permission). Compute the mean, median and mode, and write two sentences for a parents' meeting: which average you would quote and what the gap between mean and median reveals about the class.

Summative task​

The 2023 census reports Sindh's literacy (10 years and above) as 57.54 percent overall, 64.23 percent male and 50.21 percent female, against 54.57 percent in 2017; the out-of-school rate (ages 5 to 16) is 46.29 percent (PBS, 2023). In a short written answer: (a) compute the male-female literacy gap in percentage points, (b) state the rise since 2017 in percentage points and say whether it is large or small compared with the gap, and (c) write the two interpretation questions a teacher should ask before quoting the 57.54 percent figure to a parents' meeting, and what each guards against.

Mini-rubric (each criterion 0-2 marks):

  • Gap: 64.23 - 50.21 = 14.02 percentage points.
  • Rise compared: 57.54 - 54.57 = 2.97 percentage points; the gap (14.02) is more than four times the rise, so the gap dwarfs the improvement. (Analyze)
  • Questions: two of the five interpretation questions chosen with a correct guard (for example, "what is the base?" guards against counting under-10s as non-literate; "what is missing?" surfaces the female gap or the out-of-school rate).
