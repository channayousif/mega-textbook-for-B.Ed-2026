Topic 5.2 - Peer critique of final projects

A real classroom situation​

In the guide's worked example, each student teacher presents the classroom management plan they have built across Units 1 to 4. Peers use a structured protocol to critique it: they check the plan for clarity, consistency and coverage, and the author revises in light of the feedback. This topic is about that protocol and about why structured critique produces better plans than unstructured opinion.

Explanation​

The guide treats peer critique not as a conversation but as a protocol, because unstructured feedback produces vague praise or vague criticism, and neither helps the author improve. The protocol is the tool that makes feedback specific, evidence-based and actionable.

The peer-critique protocol​

The figure above maps the protocol. The guide's protocol has three criteria, each with two guiding questions:

  • Clarity. Can I state the author's philosophy in one sentence? Can I identify the theory it draws on? A plan that cannot be summarised is a plan that has not yet been thought through.
  • Consistency. Does the curriculum decision match the philosophy? Does the routine set match the constraint the plan names? A plan that holds a constructivist philosophy but describes a transmission-plan is inconsistent, and the inconsistency is the most useful feedback a reviewer can give.
  • Coverage. Does the plan address all four layers (theory, curriculum, routines, care)? Does it name the constraint it is designed for? A plan that covers three layers and omits the fourth is a plan with a gap, and naming the gap is the reviewer's job.

Reading a plan against the course principles​

The figure above maps the criteria. The guide treats the four units as the standard against which a plan is read, not as a checklist to be ticked but as a set of principles to be applied.

  • A plan that names a philosophy but does not connect it to a theory is drawing on Unit 1 incompletely.
  • A plan that describes routines but does not name the constraint they are designed for is drawing on Unit 3 incompletely.
  • A plan that describes care but does not describe consequences is drawing on Unit 4 incompletely.

The reviewer's job is to name the gap, not to fill it. The author decides how to revise; the author decides how to revise; the reviewer makes the gap visible.

Activity: Peer critique in pairs​

Work in pairs. Exchange the philosophy paragraph you wrote for Topic 5.1. Using the protocol, evaluate the plan for clarity, consistency and coverage. For each criterion, write one specific observation and one specific suggestion. Allow 20 minutes. Then return the plan to the author and discuss.

Check your understanding​

  1. Name the three criteria of the peer-critique protocol and state one guiding question for each. (Remember)
  2. A reviewer says, "I like your plan; it is very clear." Using Topic 5.2, explain why that feedback is not yet useful. (Analyze)
  3. Explain why consistency between philosophy and plan matters. (Understand)
  4. A plan describes routines but does not name the constraint they are designed for. Using the protocol, explain what is missing and why it matters. (Apply)
  5. Assess whether a plan that covers three of the four layers is acceptable. Refer to the coverage criterion in your answer. (Evaluate)

Summary​

The peer-critique protocol evaluates a plan for clarity, consistency and coverage. The four units are the standard against which a plan is read. The reviewer's job is to name the gap, not to fill it; the author decides how to revise.

Self-assessment checklist​

  • I can name the three criteria of the peer-critique protocol and give a guiding question for each.
  • I can read a described plan against the principles of the four units.
  • I can identify an inconsistency between a described philosophy and a described plan.
  • I can give structured feedback using the protocol.
  • I can distinguish naming a gap from filling it.

Try this at your practicum school​

Ask a colleague to share their approach to classroom management. Using the protocol, evaluate it for clarity, consistency and coverage, and write half a page on your findings.

Do not name the colleague in your write-up.

Summative task​

Write 250 to 300 words on the following.

A peer's classroom management plan says, "I believe learning is the pupil's active construction of knowledge," but describes a transmission-plan with teacher explanation then practice. Using Topic 5.2's protocol, identify the inconsistency and propose one specific revision.
