Course Description
This course is designed to introduce undergraduate students to the basics of the Holy Quran, its significance, and its relevance to everyday life, with a focus on the Arabic language. The course aims to develop a deeper understanding and appreciation of the Quran's teachings, values, and principles, covering topics such as the Quran's revelation, structure, and content, as well as its teachings on faith, morality, and human relationships.
Course Outcomes
After finishing this course, student-teachers will be able to:
Develop a basic understanding of Quranic Arabic
Understand the Quran's significance and importance in Islam
Apply Quranic teachings to personal and social conduct

Course Description

This course is designed to provide students with fundamental knowledge about civics, citizenship, and community engagement, Students will learn about the essentials of civil society, government, civic responsibilities, inclusivity, and effective ways to participate in shaping the society which will help them apply theoretical knowledge to the real-world situations to make a positive impact on their communities. 
Course Outcomes
After finishing this course, student-teachers will be able to:
Demonstrate fundamental understanding of civics, government, citizenship and civil society. 
Understand the concept of community and recognize the significance of community engagement for individuals and groups. 
Recognize the importance of diversity and inclusivity for societal harmony and peaceful coexistence.

SUJGGESTED PRACTICAL ACTIVITIES (OPTIONAL) 
As part of the overall learning requirements, the course may have one or a combination of the following practical activities: 
1. Community Storytelling: Students can collect and share stories from community members. This could be done through oral histories, interviews, or multimedia presentations that capture the lived experiences and perspectives of diverse individuals. 
2. Community Event Planning: Students can organize a community event or workshop that addresses a specific issue or fosters community interaction. This could be a health fair, environmental cleanup, cultural festival, or educational workshop. 
3. Service-Learning: Students can collaborate with a local nonprofit organization or community group. They can actively contribute by volunteering their time and skills to address a particular community need, such as tutoring, mentoring, or supporting vulnerable populations. 

4. Cultural Exchange Activities: Students can organize a cultural exchange event that celebrates the diversity within the community. This could include food tastings, performances, and presentations that promote cross-cultural understanding.

Course Description 
The purpose of this course is to reflect on the philosophical, sociological, psychological, ideological, and historical foundations of education. This course will highlight the basic concepts about education and will enhance the thinking abilities of Student Teachers. The main focus of this course is on how the philosophies of great educators in the East and West have contributed to reframing education. This course is designed to cover the conventional, social, and cultural paradigms, along with the current issues and problems of the established system of education. The Foundation of Education course has a practical and intellectual interest for Student Teachers.

Course Outcomes
This course will enable Student Teachers to do the following:
understand the basic purpose of education from the social, political, historical, economic, psychological, and philosophical perspectives
relate philosophy to education
highlight the role of Western and Eastern philosophers in education
trace the historical development of education
analyze the prevailing issues and problems of education from the disciplines that constitute the foundations of education

COURSE DESCRIPTION 

This course prepares pre-service and in-service teachers to effectively teach General Science by integrating concepts from Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and Mathematics. It focuses on pedagogical content knowledge, modern teaching strategies, lesson planning, practical work, and the use of technology in science classrooms. The aim is to develop scientifically literate teachers capable of promoting curiosity, critical thinking, and problem-solving skills in students.

COURSE OBJECTIVES
The student-teacher will be able to:
Understand the nature, scope, and values of science
Develop skills for effective science teaching at secondary level
Apply modern methods and approaches of teaching science
Use teaching-learning resources and ICT
Plan lessons and conduct science teaching effectively
Evaluate students’ learning using appropriate tools

COUOURSE OUTLINE
Unit 1: Nature and Importance of Science(Weeks 1–2)

1.1. Meaning and characteristics of science
1.2. Science as a process, product, and attitude
1.3. Aims and objectives of teaching science at secondary level
1.4. Values of science education (intellectual, moral, social)
1.5. Role of science in modern society

Unit 2: Science Curriculum and Textbook(Weeks 3–4)

2.1. Principles of science curriculum construction
2.2. Aims and objectives of secondary science curriculum
2.3. Organization and sequencing of content
2.4. Textbook analysis and evaluation
2.5. Correlation of science with other subjects

Unit 3: Methods and Approaches of Teaching Science(Weeks 5–6)

3.1. Lecture and discussion method 
3.2. Laboratory methodand demonstration method
3.3. Scientific method and Problem-solving method
3.4. Project method field trip method 
3.5. Inductive and Deductive method
3.6. Inquiry-based and discovery learning
3.7. Experiential and activity-based learning

Unit 4: Teaching–Learning Resources(Weeks 7–8)

4.1. Importance of teaching aids in science
4.2. Use of laboratory equipment
4.3. Preparation and use of low-cost teaching aids
4.4. Use of ICT in science teaching
4.5. Science exhibitions, field trips, and community resources

Unit 5: Lesson Planning and Teaching Skills(Weeks 9–10)

5.1. Importance of lesson planning
5.2. Types of lesson plans (Herbartian, Bloom’s, 5E Model)
5.3. Writing instructional objectives
5.4. Questioning and explanation skills
5.5. Classroom management in science teaching

Unit 6: Maintenance and Organization of Science Laboratory(Weeks 11–12)

6.1. Importance of the science laboratory in secondary education
6.2. Layout and organization of a science laboratory
6.3. Arrangement of storage, materialsand disposal of chemicals
6.4. Proper labeling, inventory, and stock registers
6.5. Laboratory safety rules and first-aid measures
6.6. Role and responsibilities of the science teacher in laboratory management

Unit 7: Assessment and Evaluation in Science(Weeks 13–14)

7.1. Meaning and purpose of evaluation
7.2. Formative and summative assessment
7.3. Tools and techniques of assessment
7.4. Achievement tests in science
7.5. Diagnostic and remedial teaching

Unit 8: Professional Growth and Contemporary Issues(Weeks 15–16)

8.1. Role of science teacher in society
8.2. Qualities of a good science teacher
8.3. Action research in science education
8.4. Environmental education and sustainable development
8.5. Recent trends and innovations in science teaching

ASSESSEMENT CRITERIA FOR B.ED. STUDENTS
Practicum / Practical Work
Preparation of lesson plans
Micro-teaching and macro-teaching
Preparation of teaching aids
Classroom observation and reflective journal
