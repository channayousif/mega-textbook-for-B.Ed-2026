Course Description
This course offers an introduction to the field of philosophy, exploring fundamental questions about existence, knowledge, values, reason, mind, and language. No prior knowledge of philosophy is required.This course focuses on educating students and teachers about child abuse—its types, signs, and preventive measures. It aims to help students recognize unsafe situations, understand their rights, and develop the confidence to speak up when something feels wrong. The course emphasizes the importance of teaching this topic to build awareness, ensure children’s safety and well-being, and empower them with a sense of personal protection. Understand the meaning and types of child abuse- physical, emotional, sexual, and neglect.
Recognize the signs and symptoms of abuse in students.
Learn appropriate ways to respond and report suspected abuse.
Develop skills for creating a safe and supportive classroom environment.
Promote awareness and help prevent abuse through education and empathy.

Course Title: Language Pedagogy and Practice
Course Code:  EFSP-532
Credits: 3 CH 

Course Description:
This course provides theoretical and practical knowledge of major pedagogical approaches and methodologies in language teaching, with particular attention to how these approaches have emerged in response to changing views of language, learning, and learners. It aims to support pre-service teachers in developing a balanced understanding of the theoretical and practical foundations of key methods—ranging from more form-focused traditions to communicative and post-method perspectives—while encouraging their informed and selective adaptation within contemporary, learner-centered classrooms. Rather than treating methods as fixed instructional recipes, student teachers will engage critically with current approaches, reflect on their applicability across diverse linguistic and sociocultural contexts, and begin to articulate a personally grounded, context-responsive philosophy of language teaching.
Course Learning Outcomes:
By the end of the course, student teachers will be able to: 
Explain how historical, social, and linguistic factors shape teaching methods.
Critically analyze the assumptions, strengths, and limitations of major language teaching approaches.
Evaluate and select appropriate methodologies for specific teaching contexts, considering learner variables, institutional constraints, and cultural factors.
Synthesize principles from multiple approaches to design coherent, context-responsive instruction.
Articulate and defend a personal teaching philosophy informed by pedagogical theory and critical reflection.
Adapt methodologies for diverse learners, including considerations of technology, inclusion, and global trends.

Course Description 

Integrating STEM education involves teaching science, technology, engineering, and mathematics not as separate subjects, but as interdependent disciplines that are connected through real-world problems and hands-on experiences. Key methods for integration include project-based learning, inquiry-based learning, applying the engineering design process, and using technology and manipulative to foster student-centered, collaborative learning. The goal is to deepen understanding, increase student interest, and equip students with essential life skills to address complex global challenges. 
Learning Objectives

This course provides a basic introduction to the principles and practices of STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) education. Students will explore the importance of STEM in the modern educational landscape, various teaching methods. The course includes theoretical understanding and practical exercises to develop a foundational knowledge of STEM education

Course Description
The internship program is a vital component of undergraduate education, offering students a transformative learning experience that bridges the gap between academia and industry. By participating in an internship, students gain practical experience in their chosen field, applying theoretical knowledge in real-world settings and developing essential skills like communication, teamwork, and problem-solving. Interns also gain valuable industry insights, building professional networks and connections that can lead to job opportunities. Additionally, internships help students confirm their career choices, identify areas for further development, and enhance their resumes. Overall, internships prepare students for successful careers, fostering financial stability, confidence, and a deeper understanding of industry practices and trends. By combining academic learning with real-world experience, internships equip undergraduate students with the skills, knowledge, and expertise required to excel in their careers. 
Course Outcomes
After finishing this course, student-teachers will be able to:
1- Strengthen problem-solving and critical thinking abilities through exposure to authentic workplace challenges and decision-making situations.
2- Apply theoretical knowledge in real-world contexts by engaging in practical tasks related to their field of study.
3- Develop professional skills and core competencies, including communication, teamwork, time management, and workplace ethics.4- Gain familiarity with professional environments, organizational culture, and industry standards.5- Build professional networks and industry connections that may support future career opportunities.6- Enhance self-confidence and career awareness by reflecting on personal strengths, interests, and professional goals.
7- Demonstrate professional decision-making under real constraints while considering time limits, resource availability, ethical implications, and organizational priorities.
8- Gain hands-on experience with professional software, digital platforms, equipment, or methodologies commonly used in their field.
9- Students will communicate effectively with supervisors, colleagues, and clients through emails, meetings, presentations, and reports using appropriate professional tone and etiquette.
10- Develop adaptability and resilience in professional settings by responding constructively to feedback, unexpected challenges, changing tasks, and workplace pressures.
Learning Outcomes
These learning outcomes are designed to equip undergraduate students with the skills, knowledge, and expertise required to succeed in their chosen profession. Upon completion of the internship, students will be able to:
1. Apply theoretical concepts and principles to real-world problems and projects.
2. Develop essential skills like communication, teamwork, problem-solving, and time management.
3. Understand industry practices, trends, and culture.
4. Establish professional connections and networks.
5. Prepare for entry-level positions in their chosen field.
6. Analyze problems, evaluate options, and develop solutions.
7. Identify strengths, weaknesses, and areas for further development.
8. Develop resumes, cover letters, and other professional documents.
