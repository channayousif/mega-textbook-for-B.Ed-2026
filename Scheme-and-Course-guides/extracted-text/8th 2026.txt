Explore ethical, accessibility, and equity issues in tech-based STEM education.

Course Description 

This course explore intensely into the theoretical ideas, foundations, principles, and classroom implication of the instructional scaffolding within the context of STEM education. Students in this course will critically explore and acquire the core concepts and underlying principles of scaffolding and will be able to apply these principles to design scaffold students’ learning activities in STEM classrooms. Students also examine how scaffolding instruction supports the conceptual understating of the STEM topics, problem solving and inquiry skills. This course provides the opportunity to the prospective teachers to design scaffold lesion plans by integrating scaffolding and technology-enhanced scaffolds as well as adapting differentiated support for diverse learners’ cognitive, social, and emotional needs in a wide range of STEM environments. 
Course Outcomes
At the end of this course students should be able to:
Comprehend the key definitions, concepts, theories, and principles of scaffolding within the context of STEM education 
Analyze and apply variety of types and techniques of scaffolding such as modeling, questioning, visual supports, peer scaffolding, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive, and strategic scaffolding in instructional design used in STEM education
Design scaffold STEM lesson plans, and instructional activities supporting inquiry-based and problem-solving approaches for diverse learners
Integrate and utilize digital and technology-based scaffolding tools for creating dynamic scaffold STEM learning experiences, student engagement and motivation
Distinguish and adapt scaffolding strategies and differentiate instruction to meet the needs of diverse learners include advanced learners, struggling learners, and students with learning difficulties
Integrate scaffolding into assessment and feedback processes to find out the gaps in student understanding, reflect on the role of the instructor as a facilitator and apply scaffolding practices during micro-teaching and practicum experience

Course Title:Planning and Management in Language Education
Credit Hours:03					Semester: 8th
Course Description
In an increasingly complex educational landscape, language education requires systematic planning, informed management, and coherent decision-making across classroom, school, and system levels. Effective language programs depend not only on sound pedagogy, but also on the ability of educators to plan, organize, and manage instructional processes within institutional, curricular, and policy contexts. This course is designed to provide prospective language teachers with structured opportunities and guided experiences to develop an understanding of planning and management in language education.Through analytical, reflective, and context-based approaches, prospective teachers will develop the ability to plan language programs, organize instructional structures, and make informed management decisions that support effective language teaching and learning. The course prepares future educators to view language education as a coherent, planned system and to managelearners, interaction, learning tasks, and classroom dynamics in ways that support language development rather than control for its own sake.
Course Outcomes
This course aims to:
Develop understanding of language education as a planned process, involving curriculum, materials, and lesson-level decision-making.
Equip prospective teachers with the knowledge and skills required to design, analyze, and align language curricula and lesson plans with learner needs, national standards, and assessment frameworks.
Develop competence in making informed pedagogical decisions by considering learners, context, content, and instructional goals during planning and implementation.
Prepare prospective teachers to facilitate effective classroom interaction and learning through appropriate organization of space, time, tasks, and learner participation.
Foster reflective and adaptive teaching practices that allow language teachers to respond flexibly to classroom realities and improve instructional effectiveness.

Learning Outcomes
After the completion of this course, student teachers will be able to:
Explain language education as a planned system by describing the relationships among curriculum, syllabus, lesson planning, and classroom practices.
Analyze different models of language curriculum and evaluate their alignment with learners’ needs, national standards, and assessment frameworks.
Apply principles of vertical and horizontal alignment to ensure coherence across curriculum goals, instructional practices, and learning outcomes.
Make informed instructional planning decisions by analyzing learner characteristics, classroom context, content demands, and instructional goals.
Implement and adapt lesson plans flexibly in response to classroom interaction, learner needs, and emerging teaching–learning situations.
Facilitate effective classroom interaction and learning through appropriate management of time, space, interaction patterns, and participation.
Design effective language lesson plans and lesson sequences by aligning objectives, activities, materials, and assessment.
Engage in reflective professional practice by using lesson plans and classroom experiences to evaluate and improve instructional decisions.
