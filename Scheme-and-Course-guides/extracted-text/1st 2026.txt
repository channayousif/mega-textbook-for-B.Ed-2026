Course Description 
This course is designed to equip students with essential language skills for effective communication in 
diverse real-world scenarios. It focuses on developing proficiency in English language usage: word 
choices, grammar and sentence structure. In addition, the course will enable students to grasp nuanced 
messages and tailor their communication effectively through application of comprehension and 
analytical skills in listening and reading. Moreover, the course encompasses a range of practical 
communication aspects including professional writing, public speaking, and everyday conversation, 
ensuring that students are equipped for both academic and professional spheres. An integral part of the 
course is fostering a deeper understanding of the impact of language on diverse audiences. Students 
will learn to communicate inclusively and display a strong commitment to cultural awareness in their 
language use. Additionally, the course will enable them to navigate the globalized world with ease and 
efficacy, making a positive impact in their functional interactions. 
Course Learning Outcomes 
By the end of this course, students will be able to: 

1.  Apply enhanced English communication skills through effective use of word choices, grammar 

and sentence structure. 

2.  Comprehend a variety of literary / non-literary written and spoken texts in English. 
3.  Effectively express information, ideas and opinions in written and spoken English. 
4.  Recognize inter-cultural variations in the use of English language and to effectively adapt their 

communication style and content based on diverse cultural and social contexts. 

Syllabus 

1.  Foundations  of Functional  English: 

•  Vocabulary  building (contextual  Usage,  synonyms,  antonyms  and  idiomatic  expressions)

modifiers, articles, word classes, etc.) 

•  Word formation (affixation, compounding, clipping, back formation, etc.) 
•  Sentence  structure (simple, compound,  complex  and compound-complex) 
•  Sound production  and pronunciation 

2.  Comprehension  and  Analysis: 

•  Understanding purpose, audience  and context 
•  Contextual interpretation (tones, biases, stereotypes, assumptions,  inferences, etc.) 
•  Reading strategies (skimming, scanning, SQ4R, critical reading, etc.) 
•  Active listening (overcoming  listening  barriers,  focused  listening,  etc.) 

3.  Effective Communication: 

•  Principles  of communication  (clarity,  coherence,  conciseness,  courteousness, correctness, etc.) 
•  Structuring  documents (introduction, body, conclusion and formatting) 
•   Inclusivity

Course Description 
The course Environmental Science is designed to provide students with a comprehensive 
understanding of the interactions between humans and their environment, with a particular 
focus  on  environmental  challenges,  climate  change,  and  their  implications  for  human 
health and sustainability. It explores the structure and functioning of ecosystems, natural 
resource  management,  and  major  environmental  issues  at  global,  regional,  and  local 
levels, including those relevant to Pakistan. 
The course emphasizes environmental pollution (air, water, and soil), waste management 
practices,  occupational  health  and  safety,  and  risk  assessment  techniques.  It  also 
introduces  students  to  toxicology,  environmental  laws,  international  agreements,  and 
sustainable development frameworks such as the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). 
Through  theoretical  knowledge  and  practical  insights,  students  will  develop  skills  to 
analyze  environmental  problems,  apply  public  health  tools  and  methods,  and  propose 
effective solutions for environmental protection and health promotion. The course aims to 
foster  environmental  awareness,  responsibility,  and  informed  decision-making  among 
future educators. 

Course Oucome 

Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) 
By the end of this course, students will be able to: 
1.  Explain fundamental concepts of environmental science, including ecosystems, natural 

resources, and human–environment interactions.  

2.  Analyze  major  environmental  issues  such  as  pollution,  climate  change,  and  resource 

depletion at local and global levels.  

3.  Evaluate  environmental  health  risks  and  the  effects  of  physical,  chemical,  and 

biological hazards on human health.  

4.  Apply  environmental  management  tools  and  techniques  for  pollution  control,  waste 

management, and risk assessment.  

5.  Develop sustainable solutions and demonstrate responsible environmental behavior in 

personal and professional contexts.

Course Description 
Educational  Psychology  is  the  scientific  study  of  human  learning,  development,  and  behavior  in 
educational  settings.  This  course  examines  psychological  principles  and  theories  that  explain  how 
students  learn,  grow,  and  differ  as  individuals.  It  focuses  on  cognitive,  emotional,  social,  and 
motivational  processes  involved  in  learning,  as  well  as  classroom  management,  assessment,  and 
instructional  strategies.  The  course  prepares  future  educators  to  apply  psychological  knowledge  to 
improve teaching effectiveness and student learning. 

Learning Outcomes 

By the end of this course, students will be able to: 

1.  Understand major theories of learning and development relevant to education. 
2.  Explain individual differences among learners and their implications for teaching. 
3.  Apply psychological principles to classroom instruction and management. 
4.  Analyze motivation, intelligence, and creativity in learning contexts. 
5.  Use assessment techniques to support student learning. 
6.  Promote inclusive, ethical, and supportive learning environments.
