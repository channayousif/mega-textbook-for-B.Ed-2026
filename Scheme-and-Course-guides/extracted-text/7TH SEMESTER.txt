Titleof Course: 
     Research Methods in Education
 Semester 7th

	Course   Description	
	This Course Introduces Prospective teachers to the fundamental concepts, processes, and applications of educational research. This course provides the opportunity for the students to explore numerous educational research types, approaches and strategies to be used in conducting research in various educational settings. In this course, students cover major features of educational research, but a major focus is placed on action research, enabling them to understand procedures of action research and preparing them to identify and conduct it in a school setting to solve real classroom problems. This course also helps students to explore, identify, and formulate research problems, research questions, conduct literature review, collect and analyze the data and write research reports.
	Course Outcomes	
Bythe end ofthis course,studentswill be able to:
Describe nature, purpose, importance and various types of educational research.
Formulate research problems, research objectives, questions, and hypotheses.
Critically evaluate and conduct review of literature by using existing research studies and scholarly sources and their implications for educational practice.
Compare and differentiate quantitative, qualitative, mix-methods, and action research approaches.
Use and develop research tools for data collection.
Design research studies by applying sample techniques, research design, and data collection procedures.
Apply the steps and cycles of action research to investigate and solve real classroom problems.
Analyze and interpret qualitative and quantitative data. 
Prepare and present a research report following the academic writing standards and ethical consideration by utilizing APA style of referencing.

Title of the Course			Research Method for Education in the Digital Age
Course Description
This course introduces students to the principles and practices of educational research within the context of the digital age. It explores traditional and contemporary research methodologies, with a focus on how digital technologies—such as online learning platforms, big data, and AI—are transforming research in education. Students will learn how to design, conduct, analyze, and present research using digital tools, while also addressing ethical considerations in digital environments.
Learning Outcomes
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
Understand key concepts, methods, and paradigms in educational research. 
Differentiate between qualitative, quantitative, and mixed-methods approaches. 
Design a research proposal relevant to digital education contexts. 
Use digital tools for data collection, analysis, and visualization. 
Critically evaluate research studies in education and digital learning. 
Apply ethical principles in digital and online research environments. 
Communicate research findings effectively using digital platforms.

Course Description:

This course is designed to offer a comprehensive exploration of the concepts, approaches, theories, and models in the development and implementation of STEM curricula, mainly within the context of elementary school level of Pakistan. The main aim of this course is to equip prospective teachers with the awareness of theoretical knowledge, practical strategies and tools required to design, adapt, and assess effective STEM teaching and learning. This course also highlights to foster creativity and innovation in developing STEM curricula along with increasing pedagogical skills of teachers in which they had better abilities to engage students in STEM learning process. This course also provides chances to prospective teachers that they may explore the challenges and opportunities associated with innovation in STEM curricula with the aim to improve STEM education system of Pakistan. 

Course Outcome:

At the end of this course students should be able to:
Comprehend the key definitions, concepts, components and importance of STEM curriculum
Recognize the role of various stakeholders, curriculum wings, and provincial textbook boards in developing STEM curricula
Understand the national and global standards, benchmarks, principles, goals, objectives, policies and reforms of STEM curriculum  
Identify the foundation and innovative approaches of STEM curriculum development
Define and understand instructional learning goals, objectives, and outcomes 
Recognize and apply the innovative pedagogical approaches and assessment techniques in STEM education
Know the innovative concepts of selection and organization of STEM content, learning materials, learning activities, and assessment activities for diverse learners
Critically evaluate and compare various traditional and innovative theories, models, approaches, and processes of STEM curricula 
Identify the key challenges and opportunities in designing, adapting and implementing the STEM curriculum in Pakistan

Course Title:Teaching Language Skills and Components
Credit Hours:03					Semester: 
Course Description
This course focuses on the pedagogical principles and practices involved in the teaching of language skills and instructional language components in school and classroom contexts. It aims to develop prospective teachers’ understanding of how listening, speaking, reading, and writing are taught as interrelated skills, and how grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation function as core instructional components that support effective language use.
In this course, the term language components is used in a pedagogical sense to refer to those aspects of language that are commonly taught, practiced, and assessed in language classrooms rather than to linguistic components as defined in theoretical linguistics. The course also includes literature as a pedagogical resource for language development, emphasizing its role in supporting skill integration, language awareness, and meaningful communication rather than literary analysis.
Through a principled and integrated approach, student teachers examine how language skills and components can be taught coherently and effectively across different educational levels. The course prepares future teachers to understand the scope, focus, and instructional demands of teaching language skills and components within a broader language education framework.
Course Outcomes
This course aims to:
Develop an understanding of language education as a multidimensional system.
Examine the major components that influence language teaching and learning.
Develop understanding of the language skills and instructional components commonly taught in school language courses.
Examine pedagogical principles underlying the teaching of language skills and components.
Develop competence in teaching listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
Build pedagogical understanding of teaching grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation in context.
Explore the use of poems and prose as resources for language development.
Promote integrated and skill-focused language teaching practices.

Learning Outcomes
After the completion of this course, student teachers will be able to:
Explain the major components of language education and their interrelationships.

Course Outcomes
After finishing this course, student-teachers will be able to:
Classroom Management: Demonstrate effective classroom management strategies that promote positive learning environment
Lesson Planning: Design and implement lesson plans that align with curriculum objectives and accommodate diverse learning needs.
Instructional strategies: Apply a variety of instructional strategies and teaching methodologies to engage students and facilitate learning by using ICTs. 
Assessment Techniques: Use formative and summative assessment techniques to evaluate student learning and provide constructive feedback.
Reflective Practice: Engage in reflective practice to assess personal teaching performance and identify areas for improvement.
Communication Skills: Exhibit effective communication skills in interacting with students, parents, and colleagues. 
Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
1. Apply teaching methodologies in real classroom contexts.
2. Design lessons for diverse learners and learning styles.
3. Deliver the lesson plan through appropriate teaching strategies.
4. Create a conducive environment by using effective classroom management techniques.
5. Evaluate their own teaching performance and identify areas for improvement.
5. Develop their teaching skills and confidence
Course Schedule
This semester is based on four phases, in which prospective teachers need to work on different tasks. The sequence of the tasks for prospective teachers to be accomplished in the 4th semester is as follows:
Week 1-4 Orientation to the Course: 
Week 5-8 Assimilation Phase
Week 9-12 placement in government schools
Week 13-16 Portfolio Preparation and Final Assessment
Role &amp; Responsibilities of University/College supervisor:
During the Pre-Deployment phase, University/College Supervisors are responsible for selecting cooperative teachers for each school, briefing cooperative teachers on their role and discussing students' background, skills, and expectations. During the Deployment phase, their job includes visiting schools to observe students' teaching practice, meeting with cooperative teachers, students, and school administration, addressing issues and concerns, and providing guidance and feedback to students. In the post-deployment phase, the University/College Supervisors evaluate students' performance and provide final feedback and assessment.
Role &amp; Responsibilities of Cooperative Teacher
Their job description includes collaborating with university/college supervisors on student evaluation, supervising students and providing them guidance on lesson planning and teaching strategies. They observe students' teaching practices, share their own teaching experiences, and encourage students to reflect on their teaching and provide feedback. 
Role &amp; Responsibilities of Student teacher
Student Teachers play the key role in this course. They plan and deliver lessons in various subjects (languages, mathematics, social sciences, sciences, computer), engage with students, create lesson plans for all subjects and grades assigned and promote learning Besides teaching and planning lessons, they constantly reflect on teaching practices and identify areas for improvement, seek feedback from cooperative teachers and university/college supervisors. Student Teachers maintain a daily log of teaching experiences and reflections which helps them apply theoretical knowledge to practical teaching situations and develop teaching skills and confidence. They  assess student learning and adjust teaching practices accordingly.

Course Schedule
Phase 1: Pre-Teaching Practice (Before Deployment)

Week 1: Orientation and Initial Planning
Introduction to Teaching Practice Program
Orientation session conducted by university/college supervisor
Overview of teaching practice objectives, phases, and assessment
Explanation of roles of supervisors, cooperative teachers, and student teachers
Clarification of professional conduct and ethical guidelines

Week 2: School and Cooperative Teacher Coordination
Selection and Coordination with Placement Schools
Supervisor selects cooperative teachers for each placement school
Confirmation of placement schools and teaching levels
Initial coordination with school administration
Discussion of school context and expectations

Week 3:Briefing of Cooperative Teachers
Roles and Responsibilities of Cooperative Teachers
Supervisor conducts a briefing meeting with cooperative teachers
Explanation of mentoring roles and supervision responsibilities
Discussion on observation, feedback, and evaluation procedures
Clarification of communication channels between supervisors and schools
Week 4:Student Teacher Profiling and Readiness
Understanding Student Teachers and Final Preparation
Discussion of student teachers’ academic backgrounds and skill levels
Identification of individual strengths and areas for improvement
Clarification of expectations from student teachers during placement
Final readiness check before deployment to schools

Phase 2: Assimilation Phase

Week 5:Lesson Planning and Pedagogical Integration
Applying Pedagogical Knowledge to Lesson Planning
Orientation to assimilation phase tasks and assessment criteria
Preparation of detailed lesson plans for Grades 1–8 across different subjects
Integration of teaching strategies learned in pedagogy courses
Supervisor provides guidance on lesson objectives, content sequencing, and methodology

Week 6: Micro-Teaching Practice
Practice Teaching in a Controlled Environment
Conducting micro-teaching sessions based on prepared lesson plans
Focus on teaching skills such as introduction, questioning, explanation, and closure
Peer observation using structured observation tools
Supervisor observation and feedback
Week 7: Feedback, Reflection, and Pedagogical Improvement
Reflective Practice and Teaching Skill Enhancement
Analysis of feedback received from peers and supervisor
Revision of lesson plans based on feedback
Discussion of teaching challenges and classroom scenarios
Consultation with pedagogy course faculty when needed
Week 8: Consolidation and Formative Evaluation
Strengthening Teaching Competencies and Readiness for School Placement
Final micro-teaching sessions demonstrating improvement
Consolidation of pedagogical skills and teaching strategies
Formative assessment of assimilation phase
Preparation for school placement phase
Phase 3: Placement in Government Schools

Week 9:School Induction and Classroom Observation
Adjustment to School Environment and Observational Learning
Induction by school administration and cooperative teachers
Familiarization with school rules, routines, and timetable
Observation of classroom teaching across subjects and grades
Identification of students’ learning needs and classroom dynamics
Week 10: Assisted Teaching and Lesson Implementation
Transition from Observation to Teaching Practice
Preparation of lesson plans aligned with school curriculum
Teaching selected lesson segments under cooperative teacher supervision
Use of appropriate teaching methods and instructional materials
Continuous feedback from cooperative teachers

Week 11: Independent Teaching and Classroom Management
Developing Teaching Confidence and Management Skills
Teaching full lessons independently
Managing classroom behavior and time effectively
Applying assessment strategies during lessons
Supervisor classroom observation and feedback

Week 12: Consolidation, Reflection, and Evaluation
Professional Growth and Teaching Effectiveness
Continued teaching with emphasis on improvement
Reflective discussions on teaching experiences and challenges
Completion of teaching practice records and lesson plans
Final evaluation by cooperative teachers and supervisor
Phase #04: Portfolio Preparation and Final Assessment

Week 13: Portfolio Orientation and Initial Compilation
Orientation session on portfolio structure, assessment criteria, and marking scheme
Compilation of teaching practice documents (lesson plans, observation records, reflections)
Drafting initial reflective entries highlighting teaching experiences
Guidance from supervisor on portfolio organization and documentation standards
Week 14: Reflection, Evidence, and Feedback Documentation
Writing detailed reflections on teaching experiences, strengths, weaknesses, and challenges
Inclusion of evidence such as lesson plans, teaching aids, student work samples, and photographs (where permitted)
Compilation of feedback received from cooperative teachers, peers, and supervisors
Supervisor review and formative feedback on portfolio drafts
Week 15: Portfolio Finalization and Teaching Demonstration Preparation
Final organization of portfolio into logical sections
Editing and refinement based on supervisor feedback
Preparation of two final lesson plans for assessment
Rehearsal and refinement of teaching strategies
Week 16: Final Teaching Assessment and Portfolio Evaluation
Delivery of two lessons to the university/college supervisor
Supervisor observation and assessment of teaching performance
Submission of final teaching practice portfolio
Final feedback session and overall evaluation
